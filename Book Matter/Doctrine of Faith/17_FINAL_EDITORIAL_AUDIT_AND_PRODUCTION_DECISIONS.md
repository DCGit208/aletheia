# Book I — Final Editorial Audit and Production Decisions

**Book:** *The Doctrine of Faith*  
**Author:** Dr. Coach Achu Gustave  
**Publisher:** Aletheia Publications  
**Audit date:** 6 November 2026  
**Controlling source:** *Aletheia Libraries 1* (2015), “Faith toward God”  
**Current controlled manuscript:** `14_THE_DOCTRINE_OF_FAITH_BOOK_I_PUBLICATION_MANUSCRIPT.md`

## Executive decision

**The manuscript is doctrinally substantial and structurally established, but it is not yet a world-class finished book. It must not be described as final or publication-ready.**

The previous technical and source-coverage checks were useful, but they did not establish the standard now required: a compelling, coherent, carefully edited, fully reader-facing book. Exact synchronization between Markdown and the app reader is a technical integrity check, not proof of literary quality.

This audit therefore supersedes any earlier statement that the prose was fully publication-ready or that the editorial pass had removed all manuscript-internal language.

## What is already established and must be preserved

1. The ten-chapter foundation-first architecture is the current approved working structure.
2. The 2015 Aletheia leaflet remains the controlling doctrinal source for the “Faith toward God” teaching.
3. Hebrews 11:1 and *hypostasis* establish the foundation of Book I; Book II, *Faith Is Evidence*, develops the evidence dimension.
4. The central doctrinal convictions include faith as our state by design and Jesus representing who we are by Design.
5. The argument holds together Christ's person and what He accomplished: representation is not used to minimize redemption, and redemption is not used to erase representation.
6. The source's terminology and doctrinal force must not be diluted. Distinctions among source wording, biblical wording, lexical evidence, and doctrinal synthesis are attribution controls, not attempts to weaken the source.
7. The existing Aletheia World book record and cover artwork are to be preserved. No duplicate title or unnecessary cover redesign is authorized.

## Substantive editorial findings

### 1. The existing prose is still too manuscript-conscious

A full-text scan of manuscript 14 found 52 occurrences of “this book” and 38 occurrences of “the book.” Many are legitimate, but the aggregate reveals that the prose repeatedly explains its own architecture instead of consistently carrying the reader through the doctrine.

Examples include repeated statements about what a chapter or book “establishes,” what the following chapters will do, and how the argument has been assembled. These statements must be reduced where they interrupt the exposition.

### 2. Chapter 10 contained internal architecture and audit language

The previous ending included:
- a ten-item outline of the chapters;
- repeated summaries of the book's architecture;
- a section titled “What Book I Has—and Has Not—Established”;
- a statement that the argument remained “subject to scholarly verification and editorial proof.”

That material was not suitable as the book's final reader-facing conclusion. It also contradicted the intended authority and closure of the finished doctrinal argument.

**Correction made:** the internal chapter outline and audit-style ending were replaced with a reader-facing conclusion, “The Design Revealed in Christ,” followed by “The Foundation and the Evidence.” The revised ending preserves the doctrinal synthesis and the Book II handoff without presenting production status as part of the doctrine.

### 3. Chapter lengths and depth are uneven

The shorter chapters are not automatically defective, and the book should not be padded to equalize word counts. However, Chapters 4, 5, 6 and 8 require a depth-and-progression review. Each must do enough explanatory work to carry its doctrinal responsibility, answer the reader's likely questions, and connect its evidence to the governing argument.

### 4. Chapter 1 requires a high-level literary and conceptual edit

Chapter 1 carries the heaviest conceptual burden. It currently includes repeated explanations of:
- the difference between lexical range and contextual meaning;
- the distinction between *hypostasis* and *pistis*;
- the relation between Hebrews 11:1 and the Aletheia leaflet;
- the division of responsibility between Book I and Book II;
- the status of the book's doctrinal synthesis.

These distinctions are important and must remain accurate. The problem is repetition and the risk that the reader experiences a research memorandum rather than a compelling opening chapter. The chapter needs a clear narrative line: the question, the biblical text, the lexical investigation, the contextual decision, the connection to the Aletheia teaching, and the doctrinal consequence. Each point should be made fully once and then advanced.

### 5. Source provenance must not interrupt the reader's experience

Some passages narrate what the leaflet recommends, what the source note says about other titles, or how the source material is being classified. Source provenance belongs in editorial notes, endnotes, or a concise source note where needed. It should not interrupt the doctrinal exposition unless the reader needs that information to understand the teaching.

No source teaching is to be removed merely because its presentation is inconvenient. The task is to preserve the teaching while placing source history where it belongs.

### 6. Pedagogy is not yet consistently integrated

The manuscript uses study questions and a “Forward link” in uneven ways. Some questions test whether the reader can distinguish source wording from synthesis; those are useful in an audit or advanced seminar but can feel like examiner instructions in a general reader book.

Production decision:
- retain only questions that deepen understanding, reflection, or practical reasoning;
- remove questions that expose editorial classification work rather than teach the reader;
- standardize end-of-chapter treatment across all ten chapters, or move the full question sets to a companion study guide;
- remove production labels such as “Forward link” and make transitions part of the prose.

### 7. Front matter and metadata are not yet aligned

The manuscript title page currently presents *The Doctrine of Faith* without the catalogue subtitle. The existing Aletheia World record uses the subtitle **The Gospel That Restores Our True Nature**. The production edition must use one approved title/subtitle combination consistently across the title page, catalogue, reader, and metadata.

The preface and introduction also need to be rewritten for readers. They currently explain source hierarchy and chapter architecture more than they establish the book's purpose, significance, intended reader, and invitation into the argument. The source hierarchy should remain binding in editorial practice; it need not dominate the reader-facing opening.

### 8. Back matter requires a real publication treatment

The bibliography currently mixes formal bibliographic entries, bare URLs, and a general editorial note. It needs a consistent citation style and complete publication details where available. The Scripture Reference Guide is useful, but it needs to be designed as a reader-friendly index, not left as an unpaginated Markdown list. A glossary should be considered for the Greek and Hebrew terms used throughout the book.

## Required production sequence

### A. Developmental edit
- Review the complete argument as a reader experiences it, not as a repository of source notes.
- Remove repetition and circular explanation.
- Expand underdeveloped chapters only where the doctrine or reader's understanding requires it.
- Improve the opening of each chapter, transitions, illustrations, examples, and conclusions.
- Keep the ten-chapter architecture unless the argument itself demonstrates a structural defect.

### B. Theological and source-fidelity edit
- Recheck every material teaching from the 2015 leaflet against its chapter treatment.
- Preserve the full force of the source and the author's established doctrinal directions.
- Check that no synthesis is falsely presented as a direct quotation from Scripture or the leaflet.
- Ensure that no external commentary silently becomes a higher authority than the Aletheia source.

### C. Line edit and copy edit
- Remove repetitive framing, awkward constructions, filler, unnecessary qualifications, generic religious clichés, and over-explaining.
- Improve sentence rhythm, paragraph logic, clarity, and consistency of terminology.
- Check spelling, punctuation, capitalization, Greek/Hebrew transliteration, verse references, and quotation formatting.
- Maintain a confident, accessible, intellectually serious authorial voice.

### D. Reader-facing pedagogy
- Make the book readable by a serious general reader without sacrificing depth.
- Explain technical terms when first introduced; use them consistently thereafter.
- Use questions and examples to advance understanding, not to expose the editorial workflow.
- Make chapter endings carry the argument forward naturally.

### E. Book design and production
- Confirm final title and subtitle against the existing catalogue record.
- Prepare professional title page, copyright page, dedication, preface, introduction, contents, chapter openers, bibliography, Scripture reference guide, and glossary as appropriate.
- Apply a consistent typographic system for body text, headings, Scripture quotations, Greek/Hebrew text, notes, and running furniture.
- Preserve the existing cover artwork and existing Aletheia World book record.
- Produce the actual platform-compatible publication artifact only after confirming the Aletheia World admin workflow and accepted format.
- Proof the final designed pages against the approved source manuscript.

### F. Final acceptance
The book is not final until:
- all ten chapters have passed the developmental, theological, line and copy edits;
- all Scripture quotations and references have passed final proof;
- Greek/Hebrew text renders correctly;
- the bibliography, copyright and TLB attribution are complete;
- the designed publication has been inspected page by page;
- the live entitled reader has been tested with a real entitled account;
- the author has approved the final proof.

## Publication decision

**Current status: NOT FINAL. NOT READY FOR FINAL PUBLICATION SIGN-OFF.**

The source base and doctrinal architecture are substantial. The digital deployment pipeline is functional. But the manuscript still requires a genuine book-level edit and the complete production pass described above.

No one should represent the existence of ten synchronized chapters as proof that the book is world-class. The standard is the quality of the reading experience and the integrity of the finished publication.
