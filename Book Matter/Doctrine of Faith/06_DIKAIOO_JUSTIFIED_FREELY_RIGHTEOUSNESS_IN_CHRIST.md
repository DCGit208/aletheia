# Chapter 6 — Δικαιόω (*dikaioō*): Justified Freely; Righteousness in Christ

## VI. *Dikaioō* — justified freely

Justified: Greek δικαιόω (*dikaioō*, G1344) — the Aletheia source glosses it “render just or innocent, free from evil or guilt.” It relates this term to δικαιοσύνη (*dikaiosynē*): righteousness, justification. The source describes equity as impartiality to and conformity with the nature of God, and gives the conceptual formulation “original: back on course.” Return to the course order.

The owner's additional working formulation for *dikaioō* is “to render righteous or such as he ought to be.” This is preserved alongside the leaflet's printed gloss as a separate owner-supplied formulation. It is not silently substituted for the source wording; the relationship between the two remains a deliberate point for review.

Has man been justified? Yes. And freely. Through Greek ἀπολύτρωσις (*apolutrōsis*, G629) — redemption; ransom in full.

Romans 3:24 says we are “being justified freely by his grace through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus.” Freely. By His grace. Through the redemption. The redemption is in Christ Jesus.

There is no unpaid remainder on that ransom. *Apolutrōsis* is glossed by the Aletheia source as “redemption; ransom in full.” Do not preach a justification you must finish by labour. Labour belongs to study. The source's claim here is that innocence before God is free in Christ.

*Dikaioō* is not a project you complete on a good week. The source's printed gloss is a rendering: God renders just; God renders innocent; free from evil or guilt. The agent is grace. The means is the ransom in full.

Romans 3:27–28 — “Where is boasting then? It is excluded. By what law? of works? Nay: but by the law of faith. Therefore we conclude that a man is justified by faith without the deeds of the law.”

Boasting is excluded. The law of faith excludes it. A man is justified by faith without the deeds of the law. *Pistis* again: credence, not a wage.

The distinction is sharp. Free justification is not earned innocence. A man who is still trying to buy what the source calls a ransom in full is not standing on this stone. He may be busy; he is not crediting the ransom.

## VII. You are the *dikaiosynē* of God in Christ

### Reconciliation and the new creature

2 Corinthians 5:17–19 — “Therefore if any man be in Christ, he is a new creature: old things are passed away; behold, all things are become new. And all things are of God, who hath reconciled us to himself by Jesus Christ, and hath given to us the ministry of reconciliation; To wit, that God was in Christ, reconciling the world unto himself, not imputing their trespasses unto them; and hath committed unto us the word of reconciliation.”

The earlier course material already taught the new creature and the passing of old things. This chapter opens the next sentence of the same passage. God has reconciled us to Himself by Jesus Christ. God was in Christ, reconciling the world unto Himself, not imputing their trespasses unto them. The word of reconciliation is committed unto us.

### Made the righteousness of God in Him

2 Corinthians 5:20–21 — “Now then we are ambassadors for Christ, as though God did beseech you by us: we pray you in Christ’s stead, be ye reconciled to God. For he hath made him to be sin for us, who knew no sin; that we might be made the righteousness of God in him.”

He made Him to be sin for us, who knew no sin, so that we might be made the righteousness of God in Him. The source's teaching emphasizes the present-tense force: right now, you are the δικαιοσύνη (*dikaiosynē*) of God in Christ.

The Aletheia gloss for *dikaiosynē* is righteousness/justification; equity, impartiality to and conformity with the nature of God; “original: back on course.” You are that in Him, now, according to this teaching.

This is not presented as an aspiration visited on a good day. The source's interpretation is that *pistis* credits the state named by the verse. The new creature is in Christ; the righteousness of God is “in Him.” Keep the location. Do not detach the claim from “in Him.”

The teaching therefore rejects postponing the statement of 2 Corinthians 5:21 as though it were a ladder that has not been completed. It also insists that the claim not be made by dropping the verse's words “in Him.” Both parts belong in the argument.
