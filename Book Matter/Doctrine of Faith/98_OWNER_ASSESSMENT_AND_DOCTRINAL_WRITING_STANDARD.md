The failure is not adequately described as poor editing, weak organization, or a need for sentence polishing. The deeper failure is that the doctrine was treated as material to arrange into chapters before it had been understood and reconstructed well enough to teach.

This document is a control against repeating that failure. It must be consulted before further assessment, outlining, rewriting, or editing of the Introduction and any chapter. It is intended to guide actual work, not to become another administrative exercise that substitutes for writing the book.

## 4. The required method: understand before composing

### 4.1 Recover the evidence base

Before writing, read the actual relevant source materials and owner notes, not only summaries or prior assistant-generated audits:

- The five-page 2015 *Aletheia Libraries 1* leaflet, especially its exact “Faith toward God” section.
- The supplied *BIBLE INTRODUCTION.pdf*, used according to its actual contents and role as a supplementary Bible-study method reference.
- The author's notes, corrections, and explicit decisions in the project files and conversation.
- The existing chapter/source manuscripts and argument reconstructions.
- The current controlled manuscript, while remembering that current prose is under review and is not proof of doctrinal correctness.

Do not silently fill gaps with generic theological knowledge. Where a source does not support a claim, identify the gap. Where external lexical, grammatical, historical, or scholarly evidence is needed, distinguish it from the author's source and formulation.

### 4.2 Extract propositions before arranging passages

Create a proposition inventory. For every proposition, record:

- Its exact content.
- Its source: Scripture, 2015 leaflet, lexical/grammatical evidence, owner note, or doctrinal synthesis.
- The passage or source location that supports it.
- Its role in the doctrine.
- What it depends on.
- What follows from it.
- What it does not establish by itself.
- Where it belongs in the book.

Do not treat a list of Bible references as an argument. A passage belongs in a chapter only when its role in the chapter's reasoning is clear and can be explained.

### 4.3 Reconstruct the reasoning

Build an explicit argument map before drafting. Show the reader's path through the doctrine:

- What is the governing question?
- What foundational proposition answers or frames it?
- What evidence or teaching establishes that proposition?
- What intermediate conclusions are necessary?
- Why does each next proposition follow?
- What distinctions prevent a false inference?
- What conclusion has now been earned?
- What question or dependency properly leads into the next chapter?

A connection is not established by shared vocabulary, thematic resemblance, or placing two references in the same paragraph. The reasoning connecting them must be stated and defended.

### 4.4 Establish chapter responsibility and boundaries

For each chapter, define:

- The exact doctrinal question it must answer.
- The propositions it must establish.
- The source material and Scripture necessary to establish them.
- The prior chapter's conclusions it depends upon.
- The next chapter's question to which it hands off.
- Material that is related but does not advance its argument and therefore belongs elsewhere.

Do not introduce a topic simply because it is important elsewhere in the book. Do not prematurely summarize a major subject that deserves its own chapter. Do not leave a necessary premise undeveloped merely to preserve a short chapter.

### 4.5 Develop, do not compress

A major teaching must receive the space and explanation its complexity requires. Do not reduce substantial doctrinal facts to two or three sentences, then move on as though the subject has been exhausted.

Development means explaining the proposition, its textual basis, its conceptual significance, its relationship to other propositions, the implications that follow, and the distinctions required to understand it correctly. Length is governed by the work the argument needs—not by an arbitrary word count.

### 4.6 Compose only after the argument is sound

The final prose must read as a coherent, authoritative doctrinal book written in the author's voice. It must not read like:

- A list of proof-texts.
- A condensed theological overview.
- An annotated outline.
- A source audit or research report.
- A sequence of disconnected explanations.
- A set of editorial warnings about what the chapter is not doing.
- A summary that announces conclusions without demonstrating them.

The prose must carry the intellectual weight of the subject. It must explain facts in their proper place, develop the author's ideas, and make the logic visible without repeatedly narrating the writing process.

## 5. Scripture and source reasoning controls

1. Read each passage in its immediate literary, grammatical, historical, and subject context, following the actual method supported by the supplied Bible Introduction.
2. Explain why a passage is introduced at that point in the argument.
3. Do not jump from one passage to another without stating and establishing the connection.
4. Distinguish direct biblical wording from lexical observation, source teaching, owner interpretation, and cumulative doctrinal synthesis.
5. Do not claim a single verse says a conclusion that is actually reached by connecting several passages.
6. Do not turn a lexical gloss into a complete doctrinal definition.
7. Do not use etymology as a substitute for contextual meaning.
8. Do not make grammar prove more than it establishes.
9. Do not use Scripture as decoration or as a proof-text attached after a conclusion has already been asserted.
10. Do not introduce unrelated material, even if it is doctrinally important elsewhere.
11. Preserve the source's actual wording, sequence, framing, and level of detail when representing it. Do not silently correct, reconcile, expand, or replace source content with general knowledge.
12. Where the author's synthesis goes beyond the explicit wording of one source, develop the synthesis transparently and rigorously; do not weaken it merely because it is a synthesis.

## 6. Chapter 1: immediate working requirement

Chapter 1 must not be rewritten until its doctrine and argument have first been reconstructed in a form that can be evaluated independently of polished prose.

The current controlled working responsibility of Chapter 1 is to establish the foundation of Book I through Hebrews 11:1 and *hypostasis*, and to make the relationship to the Aletheia leaflet's *pistis* / credence / state-of-God's-children teaching intelligible. The chapter must be understood in relation to the full approved book architecture and the author's notes—not treated as an isolated essay.

The following distinctions must remain intact:

- Hebrews 11:1 and *hypostasis* are the foundation assigned to Book I by the author's direction.
- The 2015 leaflet's “Faith toward God” section begins from Hebrews 6:1, identifies *pistis* as “credence,” and says faith defines the state of God's children; it does not itself quote Hebrews 11:1 or use *hypostasis*.
- *Hypostasis* and *pistis* are distinct Greek terms and must not be treated as synonyms.
- Book I develops the *hypostasis* foundation; Book II, *Faith Is Evidence*, develops the *elenchos* dimension. Both clauses of Hebrews 11:1 must be acknowledged.
- The relationship between *hypostasis*, the source's *pistis* teaching, faith as state, and the author's Design thesis must be demonstrated as an argument, not declared as a slogan.
- The role of Hebrews 1:3, Hebrews 3:14, Hebrews 10:32–39, Hebrews 11:1–6, and the relevant owner-note passages must be determined by their actual contribution to that argument.
- Christ's representation and His redemptive accomplishment must not be separated or reduced to passing remarks.
- Material assigned to Chapter 2 or later chapters must not be imported wholesale into Chapter 1; necessary foundations must not be omitted merely because they are developed further later.

This section does not prescribe a predetermined conclusion for every interpretive question. It requires the argument to be reconstructed from the actual source material and the author's directions before prose is written.

## 7. The Introduction is not exempt

The author's dissatisfaction specifically identifies the Introduction as a serious failure. It must be assessed and developed as a substantive part of the book, not treated as routine front matter.

The Introduction must establish the real intellectual and doctrinal undertaking of the book, orient the reader to the argument, and prepare the reader to follow its reasoning. It must not be generic, promotional, vague, repetitive of the preface, or a compressed contents summary. Its claims must be supported by the actual book's developed argument. Do not attempt to fix a weak Introduction by polishing sentences before the book's governing argument is understood.

## 8. Quality gates before presenting any new draft

A chapter or Introduction must not be presented as ready for author review until it passes these questions:

### Doctrinal reasoning
- Can the argument be stated as a sequence of propositions and inferences?
- Is each proposition grounded in its actual source?
- Is every connection explained rather than asserted?
- Does each Scripture advance the governing argument?
- Have unrelated topics and premature excursions been excluded?
- Are the author's doctrinal formulations preserved and properly developed?

### Development and explanation
- Has each major fact received adequate explanation?
- Are important terms and distinctions explained in context?
- Does the prose show how the conclusion follows?
- Has a complex subject been reduced to a superficial summary?
- Can a serious reader understand the teaching without the author having to supply missing reasoning orally?

### Authorial and literary quality
- Does the prose sound like a substantial doctrinal book?
- Is the language precise, mature, coherent, and appropriate to the intellectual weight of the subject?
- Are the facts positioned and explained in the right order?
- Does each section flow naturally from the previous one?
- Has editorial narration, generic filler, and disconnected summary been removed?

### Whole-book integrity
- Does the chapter perform its assigned responsibility?
- Does it depend correctly on prior chapters and prepare the next?
- Does it avoid duplicating or displacing another chapter's central work?
- Does the Introduction accurately prepare readers for the book that follows?

### Author satisfaction
- Would the author recognize his doctrine, reasoning, and intended voice?
- Does the work meet the author's expectations rather than asking the author to lower them?
- Is the work being presented for genuine substantive review, rather than asking the author to repair a draft that should have been better understood before writing?

A failed doctrinal-reasoning or authorial-quality gate cannot be offset by correct formatting, a high numerical score, complete citations, technical deployment, or a polished contents page.

## 9. Required sequence of work from this point

1. **Read:** Review the actual sources, notes, corrections, and existing argument documents relevant to the Introduction and Chapter 1.
2. **Extract:** Build the complete proposition inventory with provenance and dependencies.
3. **Reconstruct:** Produce the logical argument map, including every necessary intermediate inference.
4. **Test:** Identify unsupported links, missing premises, irrelevant passages, unresolved ambiguities, and chapter-boundary problems.
5. **Demonstrate understanding:** Present the reconstructed doctrine and argument for evaluation before writing new chapter prose. This is the required proof of understanding, not another generic audit.
6. **Architect:** Establish the section sequence from the argument, with a clear purpose and conclusion for each section.
7. **Compose:** Write full, developed, reader-facing exposition in the author's voice.
8. **Evaluate:** Apply the author's standards and the established scorecard only after the argument and prose exist. Numerical scoring is diagnostic, never a substitute for judgment.
9. **Revise:** Correct the actual reasoning and exposition, not merely surface wording.
10. **Submit:** Present a coherent review copy and a concise change record. Do not publish, synchronize, or deploy revised reader content without the appropriate approval.

## 11. Governing principle

**Understand the doctrine before attempting to teach it. Reconstruct the reasoning before composing the prose. Develop every major teaching adequately. Make every Scripture and every fact earn its place in the argument. Write the book the author intends—not a summary of the materials supplied.**

Understanding is demonstrated by correctly reconstructing and explaining the author's reasoning. The manuscript must meet the author's expectations; the author is not expected to lower those expectations to accommodate the manuscript.
