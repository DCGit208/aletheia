## I. ’Ĕlōhîm — image and likeness

This acceptance or approval is on the basis of our true nature from creation with respect to God.

Genesis 1:26 — “And God said, Let us make man in our image, after our likeness: and let them have dominion over the fish of the sea, and over the fowl of the air, and over the cattle, and over all the earth, and over every creeping thing that creepeth upon the earth.”

God, in the Hebrew of this verse, is ’ĕlōhîm. This teaching reads ’ĕlōhîm as plural: Trinity. “Let us make” is not a committee of angels inventing a lesser thing. God speaks as God. The “us” and the “our” are the speech of God concerning God. Man is made in that image, after that likeness.

Do not send the plurality to a courtroom of created beings. The Maker is speaking. The thing made is man.

Image. Likeness. Dominion. Those are not ornaments. They are the design. Man is who he is by how he was designed and by Who designed him.

Genesis 1:27 — “So God created man in his own image, in the image of God created he him; male and female created he them.”

The Living Bible (TLB) gloss, kept as a gloss beside the KJV, not as a rival Bible: “So God made man like his Maker. Like God did God make man; Man and maid did he make them.”

Like his Maker. That is the use of the gloss. Mark it TLB. Keep the KJV as the text you teach. The gloss is there so you will not miss the sentence: God made man like his Maker.

Genesis 1:28 — “And God blessed them, and God said unto them, Be fruitful, and multiply, and replenish the earth, and subdue it: and have dominion over the fish of the sea, and over the fowl of the air, and over every living thing that moveth upon the earth.”

He blessed them. He spoke to them. Fruitful. Multiply. Replenish. Subdue. Have dominion. The blessing and the charge are one speech. You will not be allowed, in this school, to keep “image” as a pious word and throw away dominion as if it were optional poetry.

This school will not put a softer sentence in the place of Genesis 1:26–28. Teach the verses as written. Man is who he is by design and by Who designed him.

This chapter keeps a Scripture set that must sit with these verses. You will not detach the Scripture set. You will not use the Scripture set to empty the verses. You will hold them together.

John 14:12 — “Verily, verily, I say unto you, He that believeth on me, the works that I do shall he do also; and greater works than these shall he do; because I go unto my Father.”

He that believeth on Me. The works that I do shall he do also. Greater works than these shall he do. Because I go unto My Father. The going of the Son is the reason the believer is not left as a spectator of works he can never touch. Do not reduce the verse to a fundraising slogan. Do not empty it because you are afraid of it. Teach it as written, with Genesis 1:26–28.

1 John 4:17 — “Herein is our love made perfect, that we may have boldness in the day of judgment: because as he is, so are we in this world.”

As He is, so are we in this world. Not as He will be, so we hope to be after we die. In this world. The love is made perfect toward boldness in the day of judgment on that ground. Do not turn the sentence into a compliment you pay yourself. Do not refuse the sentence because it is large. It is written.

Psalm 82:6 — “I have said, Ye are gods; and all of you are children of the most High.”

Romans 8:17 you have already heard: if children, then heirs; heirs of God, and joint-heirs with Christ.

John 17:20–23 will be opened in its own section. It belongs in this Scripture set now as the prayer of oneness and of glory given. Hold the address. Do not wait until section IX to admit that the prayer exists.

This is the image-and-likeness file. You will not be taught a hedge that undoes Genesis 1:26–28. You will be taught the writing.

If you came to this chapter hoping the school would apologize for the text, you came to the wrong house. If you came hoping the school would invent a title the Scripture set does not grant, you also came to the wrong house. The text is enough. Reason from it.

Further reading at this knot, directed, not as a seventh course: *The Doctrine of Man*. *Why Men Die*.

## II. Jesus as representation of humanity's design — an owner-supplied inquiry

The owner's developing thesis is:

> **Jesus represents who we are by Design.**

The question is therefore not limited to “What did Jesus accomplish for us?” It must also ask, “What does Jesus represent?” The intended investigation is that Jesus reveals, in perfect manifestation, the reality toward which humanity was designed and to which redemption restores us.

This is a cumulative thesis to investigate through the Scripture set, not a conclusion to assign to Genesis 1:26–28 or any single verse by itself. The connected passages include:

- **Genesis 1:26–28:** image, likeness, blessing, fruitfulness, and dominion.
- **Romans 8:17:** children, heirs of God, and joint-heirs with Christ; the same verse also names suffering with Him and being glorified together.
- **1 John 4:17:** “as he is, so are we in this world,” in the context of love being made perfect and confidence in the day of judgment.
- **John 17:20–23:** Jesus' prayer concerning the oneness of believers with the Father and the Son.
- **Hebrews 1:2:** the Son appointed heir of all things, through whom God made the worlds/ages.
- **Ephesians 1:7 and Colossians 1:14:** redemption through Christ.
- **Romans 3:23–24:** coming short of the glory of God and being justified freely through the redemption in Christ Jesus.

### Questions that must remain visible

**Mark 11:22.** If this verse is used to connect humanity's design with the state of God, its wording and Greek construction must be examined carefully. Do not assert “God's faith” as a grammatical conclusion without evidence.

**Matthew 5:48.** The command to be perfect as the heavenly Father is perfect must be read in its context, including Jesus' teaching about love toward enemies. The design/nature reading is a theological synthesis to be demonstrated, not the only explicit statement of the verse.

**Psalm 82:6 and John 10:34–35.** The phrase “as Gods” is the owner's framing. Genesis 1:26–28 explicitly says image, likeness, and dominion; Psalm 82:6 and Jesus' appeal in John 10 form a related but distinct Scripture argument. Keep the wording and context of each passage intact.

The claim that Jesus represents humanity's design must be developed by showing how these passages relate. Hebrews 1:2 alone does not establish the entire thesis, and 1 John 4:17 must not be detached from its context of love and confidence.

## Charge of this chapter

Read Genesis 1:26–28 aloud and list the actual terms in the passage: image, likeness, blessing, fruitfulness, multiplication, replenishment, subduing, and dominion. Then distinguish those explicit terms from the owner's synthesis about humanity's design.

Read John 14:12, Psalm 82:6, Romans 8:17, and 1 John 4:17 alongside Genesis. For each passage, write what it explicitly says and what additional conclusion you believe follows from the combined Scripture set. Keep the distinction visible.

Finally, investigate the thesis **“Jesus represents who we are by Design”** through the full cluster named in this chapter. Do not make a single verse carry the whole argument, and do not omit the context of any passage used.
