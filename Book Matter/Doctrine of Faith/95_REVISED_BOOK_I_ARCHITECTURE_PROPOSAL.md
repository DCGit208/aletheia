# 95 — Revised Book I Architecture Proposal: Foundation Before Evidence

**Book I:** *The Doctrine of Faith*  
**Book II:** *Faith Is Evidence*  
**Date:** 2026-10-18  
**Status:** Revised architecture proposal arising from the owner's explicit correction; requires further review before chapter rewrites are approved.  
**Supersedes:** The sequencing assumptions in 89_BOOK_I_ARCHITECTURE_REVIEW_PROPOSAL.md that reserved the core Hebrews 11:1 / hypostasis treatment for Book II. The older document remains as a record of the first proposal and its rationale.

## 1. Governing correction

Book I must establish faith's foundation through Hebrews 11:1 and hypostasis in relation to substance and the owner's thesis of underlying nature/state. Book II builds upon that foundation to develop faith as evidence. The foundational subject must not be deferred merely because Hebrews 11:1 contains both “substance” and “evidence.”

The 2015 Aletheia leaflet does not itself explicitly discuss Hebrews 11:1 or hypostasis. It explicitly begins its Faith Toward God section from Hebrews 6:1 and says pistis / credence defines the state of children of God, connecting this to true nature, adoption, image/likeness, divine nature, redemption, righteousness, heirship, and oneness. The revised book must reason across these sources without falsely attributing the Hebrews 11:1 / hypostasis argument to the leaflet.

## 2. Architecture principle

The book's sequence must follow conceptual dependency:

1. Establish the governing statement about faith as hypostasis in Hebrews 11:1.
2. Investigate the lexical range and contextual use of hypostasis, including Hebrews 1:3 and 3:14; do not use etymology as a substitute for exegesis.
3. Relate that foundation to the Aletheia source's explicit claim that pistis defines the state of children of God.
4. Develop what the source and owner notes say about God's nature, image/likeness, design, Sonship, heirship, divine nature, and pleasing God.
5. Examine deviation / coming short of glory, Christ's person and work, redemption, justification, and righteousness.
6. Develop the questions of heirship, oneness, present reality, and the image/glory revealed in Christ.
7. Close Book I with a clear statement of the foundation established and a controlled handoff to Book II's distinctive treatment of evidence.

This sequence is a proposed logic, not an automatic verdict on chapter count. The eleven-chapter arrangement below is a working recommendation to test against the source material and existing prose.

## 3. Proposed reader-facing structure (11 working chapters)

### Chapter 1 — Faith as Hypostasis: Substance, Reality, and the Foundation of the Doctrine

**Purpose:** Establish Hebrews 11:1 as the foundational scriptural proposition and investigate hypostasis in the sentence. Examine “substance,” “assurance,” “reality,” and “nature” as documented lexical options; compare Hebrews 1:3 and 3:14; read Hebrews 10:32–39 and 11:1–6 in context. Set out the owner's thesis that faith as substance explains faith as the person's underlying nature/state, identifying it as the governing doctrinal argument to be demonstrated.

**Required distinction:** Hebrews 11:1 and hypostasis are not explicitly in the 2015 leaflet's Faith Toward God section. The chapter must say so and then establish why the wider Scripture/lexical foundation governs the book.

**Required questions:** What does the sentence actually say? What is the range of hypostasis? What does its use in Hebrews 1:3 contribute? How does Hebrews 3:14 control our interpretation? Why does the author connect faith to things hoped for and not seen? How does the Aletheia claim that pistis defines our state as children relate to this?

### Chapter 2 — Pistis: Credence That Defines the State of Sons

**Purpose:** Develop the leaflet's explicit faith-toward-God teaching from Hebrews 6:1, pistis / credence, acceptance / true nature, adoption, and Sonship. Connect Galatians 4:5–7, Romans 8:14–17, and Ephesians 1:3–5. This chapter must now explicitly connect the source's pistis / state claim to the foundation established in Chapter 1, rather than treating “state” as an independent slogan.

**Owner-note integration:** Introduce Hebrews 11:2,5–6 and Matthew 3:17 as a controlled inquiry into faith, good report, pleasing God, and the Father's declaration over the Son. The claim that Sonship itself is the state of faith remains a synthesis to demonstrate.

### Chapter 3 — God, Elohim, Image, Likeness, and Human Design

**Purpose:** Develop Genesis 1:26–28 as the design foundation: image, likeness, blessing, fruitfulness, and dominion. Preserve the leaflet's stated reading of elohim as “plural: Trinity,” identifying it as the source's interpretation. Keep the KJV wording and TLB “like his Maker” gloss distinct.

**Owner-note integration:** Investigate “Jesus represents who we are by Design” as a cumulative thesis. Include John 14:12, Mark 11:22, and Matthew 5:43–48 / 5:48 with their contexts. Do not assert “God's faith” as a grammatical conclusion without verification.

### Chapter 4 — Psalm 82 with John 10: “Ye Are Gods” and the Judgment Context

**Purpose:** Study Psalm 82:1–8 as a whole and Jesus' argument in John 10:30–39. Keep Psalm 82:6–7 together. Preserve the source's claim and Jesus' appeal to Scripture without letting the verse alone carry every later claim about divine nature.

**Cross-links:** Genesis 1:26–28; John 10:35; 2 Peter 1:4; Sonship and design.

### Chapter 5 — True Nature and Partaking of Divine Nature

**Purpose:** Develop 2 Peter 1:2–8 in its sequence: promises, partaking of divine nature, escape from corruption, diligence, and the listed qualities. Connect it to the source's “true nature from creation” claim and to the owner's design/restoration thesis, clearly marking synthesis.

**Cross-links:** Genesis 1:26–28; Psalm 82:5–7; Romans 8:17; 1 John 4:17.

### Chapter 6 — Faith, Sonship, Good Report, and Pleasing God

**Purpose:** Give the Hebrews 11:2,5–6 and Matthew 3:17 cluster its own coherent treatment if the chapter-level audit confirms it needs more than a subsection. Ask what the text says about good report, Enoch's testimony, pleasing God, and the Father's declaration over the Son. Develop—not assume—the synthesis connecting the state of Sonship and the state of faith.

**Architecture note:** This chapter is provisionally separate so the central state/pleasing-God question is not buried inside a lexical chapter. If the source and prose audit shows it can be integrated more coherently into Chapter 2 without losing the reasoning, this chapter may be merged. Do not preserve eleven chapters merely to preserve the number.

### Chapter 7 — Why Did Jesus Come? Glory, Deviation, Accomplishment, and Representation

**Purpose:** Develop Romans 3:21–26 and 1 Corinthians 11:7 in the source's terms: coming short of glory as deviation from true nature, the image/glory language, and the question “Why did Jesus come?” Bring Hebrews 1:2–3 and 2 Corinthians 4:4–6 into the cumulative representation inquiry. Keep Christ's accomplishment and representation together.

**Owner thesis:** “Jesus represents who we are by Design.” The working synthesis “Christ's work restores what Christ's person reveals” must be demonstrated, not presented as a direct biblical quotation.

### Chapter 8 — Redemption, Free Justification, and Righteousness in Christ

**Purpose:** Develop dikaioo, dikaiosyne, and apolutrosis from Romans 3:23–28, 2 Corinthians 5:17–21, Ephesians 1:7, and Colossians 1:13–14. Preserve the leaflet's lexical formulations and the owner's additional formulations as distinct. Explain which part of “back on course” is the source's conceptual gloss and which part is synthesis.

**Non-negotiable wording control:** Keep “freely,” grace, redemption, and “in Him” / “in Christ” visible. Do not turn the ransom analogy into a broader theory unsupported by the source.

### Chapter 9 — The Son, Hebrews 1:8, and the Heir

**Purpose:** Work through the leaflet's questions: “According to Hebrews 1:8, what does that make you?”, “Who is heir of God?”, and “What is an heir?” Show the complete reasoning chain through Hebrews 1:1–8, Galatians 4:1–7, Romans 8:14–17, 2 Corinthians 5:21, and Genesis 1:26–28. Do not invent a title or make an unexamined jump from the Father's address to the Son to every believer.

**Status:** Final doctrinal answer remains an owner decision. The chapter must expose the argument and the exact point at which synthesis enters.

### Chapter 10 — Oneness and Glory: Liken John 17:20–23

**Purpose:** Answer the leaflet's question through Jesus' prayer's own progression: the disciples, later believers, being one as the Father and Son are one, indwelling, glory given, the world's knowledge that the Father sent the Son, and the stated purpose. Preserve the prayer's “as” comparisons.

**Cross-links:** Genesis 1:26–28; Romans 8:17; 1 John 4:17; 2 Corinthians 5:21. Do not reduce oneness to a feeling or organizational merger.

### Chapter 11 — The Image in the Face of Jesus Christ: Glory, Knowledge, and Present Reality

**Purpose:** Develop 2 Corinthians 4:1–6 and close the cumulative Book I argument. Connect the image of God in Christ, the light shining in hearts, and the knowledge of God's glory in the face of Jesus Christ to the book's established foundation and the source's teaching on design, true nature, redemption, righteousness, Sonship, and oneness.

**Closing function:** State what Book I has established about faith as hypostasis / substance and the person's state/nature, with all synthesis labels intact. Then identify the next question: how faith is evidence. Do not fully write Book II inside Book I.

## 5. Required review gates before prose rewrites

1. **Source fidelity:** Verify every explicit leaflet claim remains represented, in its force and terminology.
2. **Hebrews 11:1:** Verify the Greek text, sentence structure, key lexical options, and context in Hebrews 10:32–39 and 11:1–6.
3. **Hypostasis cross-use:** Compare Hebrews 1:3, 3:14, and 11:1 without assuming identical nuance.
4. **Owner thesis:** State clearly how the owner's “underlying nature/state” formulation follows from the combined argument; mark each inferential step.
5. **Scripture context:** Preserve immediate contexts for Mark 11:22, Matthew 5:48, Psalm 82:6–7, 1 Corinthians 11:7, 1 John 4:17, Romans 8:17, and John 17:20–23.
6. **Lexical decisions:** Preserve source and owner formulations for dikaioo, dikaiosyne, and apolutrosis until resolved.
7. **Hebrews 1:8:** Keep the owner's doctrinal decision open; the chapter's reasoning must be explicit.
8. **Series boundary:** Book I establishes the nature/state/substance foundation; Book II develops faith as evidence.
9. **Pedagogy:** Move from scriptural/source questions to reasoning and then to formulation; do not begin with a slogan and force verses to fit.
10. **Preservation:** Do not overwrite src/lib/content/companion-faith.ts or delete existing drafts during restructuring.

## 6. Decision protocol

This proposal is a reconstruction of the architecture, not final owner approval of every doctrinal conclusion. After the source/context audit and relationship map are checked, the owner can approve or adjust the chapter structure. Open questions must be listed with the exact evidence and inference that needs a decision. A chapter title or chapter count must never take precedence over the doctrine's actual logical order.
