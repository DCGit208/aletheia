# Book I Architecture Review Proposal

**Book:** *The Doctrine of Faith*  
**Repository:** `DCGit208/aletheia`  
**Stage:** Gate B — proposed architecture for owner review  
**Status:** Proposed, not yet owner-approved. No existing app prose has been moved, rewritten, or deleted by this proposal.

## 1. Decision sought

Approve or correct the proposed nine-chapter reader-facing structure below. The proposal keeps the existing eight app-companion entries as the source-preservation units while separating the final entry's two distinct argumentative movements: (1) the prayer of oneness in John 17:20–23, and (2) the image/light/face argument in 2 Corinthians 4:4,6. It also gives each existing chapter a clear function and places the additional owner notes where they can deepen the argument without becoming detached appendices.

This is a structural proposal, not a doctrinal ruling. The content remains governed by the 2015 Aletheia source, the Scripture in context, and recorded owner formulations/decisions.

## 2. Why nine chapters

The existing app file contains eight array entries but thirteen internal sections. The main structural issue is concentrated in entries 6–8:

- Entry 6 combines free justification with the present claim that the reader is the *dikaiosynē* of God in Christ.
- Entry 7 combines the question prompted by Hebrews 1:8 with the distinct question of who is heir and what heirship means.
- Entry 8 combines John 17's oneness, 2 Corinthians 4's image/light/face, a book-level recap, course transition language, and further reading.

The proposal does **not** mechanically turn thirteen internal sections into thirteen chapters. It retains the existing first seven chapter functions and separates the final entry into two reader-facing chapters because the oneness argument and the image/light/face argument each need a clear purpose and conclusion. The justification/righteousness and Hebrews 1:8/heirship movements remain integrated because their subparts are tightly connected and can be developed coherently as chapters with clearly marked internal sections.

## 3. Proposed table of contents

### Chapter 1 — Πίστις (*pistis*): Credence That Defines Sons

**Purpose:** Establish the source's account of faith toward God as *pistis* / credence and its relationship to sonship, adoption, and heirship. Establish the governing distinction between a person's state and a passing mood, while demonstrating the distinction from the source and Scripture rather than repeating it as a slogan.

**Primary source material:** Existing app entry 1; source-map S10; Hebrews 6:1; Galatians 4:1–7; Romans 8:14–17; Ephesians 1:3–5.

**Owner-note integration:** A controlled subsection may introduce Hebrews 11:2, 5–6 and Matthew 3:17 as a question about faith, good report, pleasing God, and the Father's declaration over the Son. The claim that Sonship is the state of faith must remain identified as synthesis, not as a direct definition of *pistis* from those verses.

**Superseded boundary note:** This original proposal incorrectly deferred Hebrews 11:1 / *hypostasis* / substance to Book II. The owner has clarified that this is foundational to Book I. See documents 94 and 95 for the corrected direction.

### Chapter 2 — ’Ĕlōhîm: Image, Likeness, and Design

**Purpose:** Establish Genesis 1:26–28 as the design foundation: image, likeness, blessing, fruitfulness, and dominion. Preserve the source's stated reading of *’ĕlōhîm* and distinguish it from any additional grammatical or theological discussion.

**Primary source material:** Existing app entry 2; source-map S10; Genesis 1:26–28; John 14:12; Psalm 82:6; Romans 8:17; 1 John 4:17; John 17:20–23 as a forward link.

**Owner-note integration:** Develop the question of Jesus as representation of humanity's design. Consider Mark 11:22 and Matthew 5:48 only with their own wording and contexts intact; the owner's design/nature reading is a synthesis to be demonstrated. Preserve Matthew 5:48's immediate context of love, including love toward enemies.

**Boundary:** Genesis uses image, likeness, blessing, and dominion language. Do not silently replace those terms with the owner's phrase “as Gods” as if that were the verse's English wording.

### Chapter 3 — Psalm 82 with John 10

**Purpose:** Examine the source's Psalm 82:6 claim in the complete psalmic and Johannine argument. Preserve both the assertion “Ye are gods” and the judgment context, including Psalm 82:7. Show what Jesus says in John 10 and what His appeal to the unbreakable Scripture actually does in the argument.

**Primary source material:** Existing app entry 3; Psalm 82:1–8; John 10:30–39; John 10:34–35.

**Owner-note integration:** Keep this chapter connected to the design/sonship argument without making Psalm 82:6 alone carry every later conclusion about divine nature or present identity.

**Boundary:** Do not detach Psalm 82:6 from its surrounding verses, and do not use the judgment context to erase the verse. Both are in the source's Scripture set.

### Chapter 4 — True Nature: Partakers of the Divine Nature

**Purpose:** Develop 2 Peter 1:3–8 around the source's emphasis on 1:4, keeping the promises, partaking of divine nature, escape from corruption, and the virtues/diligence sequence together. Connect the passage to the source's “true nature from creation” line without pretending that every phrase is identical across passages.

**Primary source material:** Existing app entry 4; 2 Peter 1:2–8; Genesis 1:26–28; Psalm 82:5; the source's *dikaiosynē* / “back on course” formulation as a forward reference.

**Owner-note integration:** Strengthen the design → deviation → restoration line where the cited passages warrant it. Mark “back on course” as the source's conceptual gloss / wider synthesis, not as an uncontested dictionary definition.

**Boundary:** Do not make “partakers of divine nature” a free-standing slogan that ignores 2 Peter 1:4's mention of escaping corruption or verses 5–8's ensuing exhortation.

### Chapter 5 — Why Did Jesus Come? Glory, Deviation, and Representation

**Purpose:** Answer the source's governing question through Romans 3:23–24 and its selected connection to 1 Corinthians 11:7. Develop the distinction between what Christ accomplished and what Christ represents, without letting either question swallow the other.

**Primary source material:** Existing app entry 5; Romans 3:21–26 as context; Romans 3:23–24 as the source anchor; 1 Corinthians 11:7; Hebrews 1:2; Ephesians 1:7; Colossians 1:14.

**Owner-note integration:** Develop “Jesus represents who we are by Design” as a cumulative thesis. Hebrews 1:2 contributes Son/heir/creation language but does not by itself prove the whole thesis. Ephesians 1:7 and Colossians 1:14 should be added to the redemption coverage, not merely listed.

**Boundary:** Acknowledge the immediate context of 1 Corinthians 11:7 concerning head covering/headship in the assembly while preserving the clause selected by the Aletheia source. Do not reduce “come short of the glory” to a generic afterlife statement; preserve the source's “deviation from true nature” formulation and identify it as source teaching.

### Chapter 6 — Δικαιόω (*dikaioō*): Justified Freely; Righteousness in Christ

**Purpose:** Establish the source's free-justification teaching and its relation to *dikaioō*, *dikaiosynē*, and *apolutrōsis*. Develop the present-state claim from 2 Corinthians 5:17–21 while keeping “in Him” integral to the statement.

**Primary source material:** Existing app entry 6, both current internal movements (sections VI and VII); Romans 3:23–28; 2 Corinthians 5:17–21; Ephesians 1:7; Colossians 1:14.

**Required lexical distinctions:**
- Leaflet: *dikaioō* — “render just or innocent (free from evil or guilt).”
- Owner's additional formulation: “to render righteous or such as he ought to be.”
- Leaflet: *dikaiosynē* — righteousness/justification; equity; impartiality to and conformity with God's nature; “original: back on course.”
- Leaflet: *apolutrōsis* — redemption; “ransom in full.”
- Owner's working formulation: “riddance, ransom in full.”

Preserve these formulations distinctly until any additional lexical research and owner decision are recorded. The “restoration/back on course” line is a conceptual argument and must not silently be treated as the sole lexical meaning.

**Boundary:** Do not make the commercial ransom analogy do more work than the source's wording supports. Keep grace, the blood/redemption context, and “in Christ / in Him” visible.

### Chapter 7 — Hebrews 1:8 and the Heir

**Purpose:** Work through the source's question, “According to Hebrews 1:8, what does that make you?” and the linked questions, “Who is heir of God?” and “What is an heir?” Show the argument step by step, not by jumping from the Father's address to the Son to an unqualified title for every believer.

**Primary source material:** Existing app entry 7, both current internal movements (sections VIII and IX); Hebrews 1:1–8; 2 Corinthians 5:21; Genesis 1:26–28; 1 John 4:17; Galatians 4:1–7; Romans 8:17.

**Owner-note integration:** Bring the Son's representation and the present state of sonship/heirship into the reasoning, while retaining the full Romans 8:17 clause about suffering with Christ and being glorified together.

**Required review:** The leaflet asks the Hebrews 1:8 question but does not supply a direct printed answer. The canonical chapter must identify the inference chain and keep the final conclusion subject to owner review. The question must not remain blank, but neither may a title be invented without argument.

### Chapter 8 — Oneness and Glory: Liken John 17:20–23

**Purpose:** Answer the source's question about how to liken Jesus' prayer of oneness. Work from the prayer's own progression: the immediate disciples, those who believe through their word, oneness in the Father and Son, glory given, “I in them, and thou in me,” and the world's knowledge that the Father sent the Son.

**Primary source material:** Existing app entry 8, current section X; John 17:1, 18–23; Genesis 1:26–28; Romans 8:17; 1 John 4:17; 2 Corinthians 5:21 as connected context.

**Owner-note integration:** Develop the line of oneness, glory, representation, and present reality. The connection between “the glory which thou gavest me I have given them” and the glory/true-nature argument in Romans 3:23 and 1 Corinthians 11:7 is a synthesis that must be demonstrated.

**Boundary:** Do not reduce oneness to organizational merger or a feeling of togetherness. Also do not make the “as” in John 17 disappear. Preserve the passage's own relational wording and purpose clauses.

### Chapter 9 — The Image in the Face of Jesus Christ: Light, Knowledge, and Evidence's Horizon

**Purpose:** Develop 2 Corinthians 4:1–6 around the source's selected verses 4 and 6: Christ as the image of God, the blinding of minds, God's light shining in hearts, and the knowledge of God's glory in the face of Jesus Christ. Close Book I by gathering the doctrine established, then establish a disciplined forward bridge to Book II.

**Primary source material:** Existing app entry 8, current section XI, plus only the necessary parts of sections XII–XIII; 2 Corinthians 4:1–6; 2 Corinthians 5:21; John 17:20–23; Genesis 1:26–28; the source's *pistis* / sonship / image / justification / oneness sequence.

**Owner-note integration:** This is where the cumulative question “what does Jesus represent?” can be gathered with “what did Jesus accomplish?” The close should show how Book I's established claims generate the next inquiry into faith, substance, and evidence without pretending Book I has already fully developed Hebrews 11:1 or *hypostasis*.

**Boundary:** Remove or adapt the app/course transition about the next baptism chapter. The standalone book should not end as if it were Course 2 in a six-principle sequence. Keep further-reading titles only where they serve the book's editorial design and source trace.

## 6. Book I / Book II boundary

Book I should conclude after it has developed the source's faith/sonship, design, true nature, Christ's work and representation, free justification/righteousness, heirship, oneness, and image/light/face cluster.

Book II, *Faith Is Evidence*, should carry the sustained inquiry into Hebrews 11:1, *hypostasis*, substance, and evidence. Book I may leave a clear question at the threshold, but should not duplicate Book II's central argument or use “evidence” in the proposed Chapter 9 title as permission to write the full Book II thesis here.
