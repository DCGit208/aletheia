## 1. Review method

The review uses four distinct categories:

1. **Leaflet/source teaching:** what the 2015 Aletheia leaflet states in its “Faith toward God” section.
2. **Scripture wording/context:** what the cited passage says, including relevant surrounding context.
3. **Synthesis:** a conclusion developed by connecting multiple passages.
4. **Owner-supplied formulation:** a developing thesis or interpretation supplied in the owner's notes.

The leaflet's statements and glosses are preserved as source data. Preserving them does not mean that every interpretation in the leaflet is declared beyond examination; equally, the manuscript must not silently replace the source's claims with generic theology or outside assumptions. Any tension or open question remains visible for owner decision.

## 5. Remaining review items

1. **Hebrews 1:8 — highest priority.** The chapter's provisional answer must be confirmed or corrected by the owner. The audit does not settle the doctrine on the owner's behalf.
2. **Lexical decisions.** The relationship between the leaflet's *dikaioō* gloss and the owner's “render righteous or such as he ought to be” formulation remains open. The source's *dikaiosynē* and *apolutrōsis* formulations remain preserved.
3. **Mark 11:22.** The wording and Greek construction behind “faith in God” versus “God's faith” have not been externally verified in a specialist lexical source. No grammatical conclusion is claimed here.
4. **Matthew 5:48.** The context of love toward enemies is now included; the broader ontological/design interpretation remains a synthesis requiring owner review.
5. **Source quotation proofing.** The review checked the chapter architecture and key passages, but every quoted Scripture should still receive a final quotation/proofread pass against the chosen Bible text before publication.
6. **Editorial voice.** Several forceful teaching lines in the existing companion are retained. A later line edit may improve transitions and remove unintended repetition, but must not soften the source's claims or change the doctrine without an explicit decision.
7. **Book II boundary (corrected).** Book I must establish the foundational relationship among Hebrews 11:1, *hypostasis*, substance, and faith as the person's underlying state/nature. Book II develops faith as evidence from that foundation.

## 6. Review conclusion

The first-draft manuscript is structurally coherent and covers the main content of the 2015 leaflet's Faith section. Two concrete coverage gaps found during this pass—contextual development of Mark 11:22 and Matthew 5:48, and substantive integration of Ephesians 1:7 / Colossians 1:13–14—have been addressed in Chapters 2 and 5. Two additional interpretive statements have been clarified so the reader can distinguish an inference or companion exposition from the explicit wording of Scripture.

This is **not** a declaration that the manuscript is final or that every source interpretation has been settled. The next review should begin with the owner's decision on the Hebrews 1:8 inference, then proceed through the open lexical and design/representation questions.



## 13. Superseding architecture correction — 2026-10-18

The first-draft review's earlier boundary statement was incorrect. The owner's direction is that Hebrews 11:1 and *hypostasis* form the foundation and core of Book I. The leaflet itself does not explicitly quote Hebrews 11:1 or use *hypostasis*; it explicitly teaches *pistis* / credence as defining the state of children of God. The corrected task is to connect these lines through an explicit, source-grounded argument. See documents 94 and 95. The current nine drafts are preserved working material, not a validated final sequence.
