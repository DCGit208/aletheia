# Chapter 7 — Δικαιόω (*dikaioō*): Redemption, Free Justification, and Righteousness in Christ

**Manuscript status:** Coordinated working draft for the foundation-first manuscript  
**Book:** *The Doctrine of Faith* — Book I  
**Source-preservation note:** This chapter retains the two connected movements in the existing app companion: free justification through redemption, and the present-tense statement of 2 Corinthians 5:21. It preserves the Aletheia leaflet's printed glosses and records the owner's additional formulation separately; it does not silently substitute one for the other.

## The response to deviation: redemption and righteousness

Chapter 6 developed the source's reading of coming short of glory as deviation from true nature, while preserving the explicit language of Romans 3:23–24: all have sinned and come short of the glory of God, and are justified freely by grace through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus. This chapter examines the terms through which the Aletheia source describes that response.

The source's lexical glosses and the owner's additional formulations must remain distinguishable. The source glosses *dikaioō* as “render just or innocent, free from evil or guilt”; the owner has separately proposed “to render righteous or such as he ought to be.” The source's *dikaiosynē* explanation includes equity and conformity with God's nature and the conceptual phrase “original: back on course.” The glossary records *apolutrōsis* as “redemption; ransom in full”; the owner's additional note uses “riddance, ransom in full.” Preserve that distinction rather than silently merging the formulations. These statements must be evaluated and integrated without silently replacing one with another.

## *Dikaioō* — justified freely

Justified: Greek δικαιόω (*dikaioō*, G1344) — the Aletheia source glosses it “render just or innocent, free from evil or guilt.” It relates this term to δικαιοσύνη (*dikaiosynē*): righteousness, justification. The source describes equity as impartiality to and conformity with the nature of God, and gives the conceptual formulation “original: back on course.” The relationship between the source's glosses and the owner's additional formulation is examined below without silently merging them.

The owner's additional working formulation for *dikaioō* is “to render righteous or such as he ought to be.” This is preserved alongside the leaflet's printed gloss as a separate owner-supplied formulation. It is not silently substituted for the source wording; the relationship between the two remains a deliberate point for review.

Has man been justified? Yes. And freely. Through Greek ἀπολύτρωσις (*apolutrōsis*, G629) — redemption; ransom in full.

Romans 3:24 says we are “being justified freely by his grace through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus.” Freely. By His grace. Through the redemption. The redemption is in Christ Jesus.

There is no unpaid remainder on that ransom. *Apolutrōsis* is glossed by the Aletheia source as “redemption; ransom in full.” Do not preach a justification you must finish by labour. Labour belongs to study. The source's claim here is that innocence before God is free in Christ.

*Dikaioō* is not a project you complete on a good week. The source's printed gloss is a rendering: God renders just; God renders innocent; free from evil or guilt. The agent is grace. The means is the ransom in full.

Romans 3:27–28 — “Where is boasting then? It is excluded. By what law? of works? Nay: but by the law of faith. Therefore we conclude that a man is justified by faith without the deeds of the law.”

Boasting is excluded. The law of faith excludes it. A man is justified by faith without the deeds of the law. *Pistis* again: credence, not a wage.

The distinction is sharp. Free justification is not earned innocence. A man who is still trying to buy what the source calls a ransom in full is not standing on this stone. He may be busy; he is not crediting the ransom.

## You are the *dikaiosynē* of God in Christ

### Reconciliation and the new creature

2 Corinthians 5:17–19 — “Therefore if any man be in Christ, he is a new creature: old things are passed away; behold, all things are become new. And all things are of God, who hath reconciled us to himself by Jesus Christ, and hath given to us the ministry of reconciliation; To wit, that God was in Christ, reconciling the world unto himself, not imputing their trespasses unto them; and hath committed unto us the word of reconciliation.”

The preceding exposition has introduced the new creature and the passing of old things. This section continues the same passage. God has reconciled us to Himself by Jesus Christ. God was in Christ, reconciling the world unto Himself, not imputing their trespasses unto them. The word of reconciliation is committed unto us.

### Made the righteousness of God in Him

2 Corinthians 5:20–21 — “Now then we are ambassadors for Christ, as though God did beseech you by us: we pray you in Christ’s stead, be ye reconciled to God. For he hath made him to be sin for us, who knew no sin; that we might be made the righteousness of God in him.”

He made Him to be sin for us, who knew no sin, so that we might be made the righteousness of God in Him. The source's teaching emphasizes the present-tense force: right now, you are the δικαιοσύνη (*dikaiosynē*) of God in Christ.

The Aletheia gloss for *dikaiosynē* is righteousness/justification; equity, impartiality to and conformity with the nature of God; “original: back on course.” You are that in Him, now, according to this teaching.

This is not presented as an aspiration visited on a good day. The source's interpretation is that *pistis* credits the state named by the verse. The new creature is in Christ; the righteousness of God is “in Him.” Keep the location. Do not detach the claim from “in Him.”

The teaching therefore rejects postponing the statement of 2 Corinthians 5:21 as though it were a ladder that has not been completed. It also insists that the claim not be made by dropping the verse's words “in Him.” Both parts belong in the argument.

### Keep the three terms distinct while showing their relationship

The source's sequence brings three Greek terms into relation, but they should not be treated as interchangeable:

- *Dikaioō* is the verb used in the source's question about being justified freely. The leaflet's printed gloss is “render just or innocent (free from evil or guilt).” The owner's additional formulation—“to render righteous or such as he ought to be”—is a separate proposal and remains open for deliberate lexical and doctrinal review.
- *Dikaiosynē* is the related noun the source connects with righteousness/justification, equity, impartiality, and conformity with God's nature. “Original: back on course” is the source's interpretive formulation in this teaching; it should not be presented as a complete dictionary definition of the noun.
- *Apolutrōsis* is the source's term for redemption, glossed as “ransom in full.” The owner's notes also use “riddance, ransom in full.” Preserve the source wording and the owner's expanded formulation as separate records until the intended relationship is decided.

Romans 3:24 explicitly joins free justification with redemption in Christ Jesus. Second Corinthians 5:21 explicitly says believers are made the righteousness of God “in Him.” Those are the textual anchors. The source's broader claim that this means being brought “back on course” with God's nature is the interpretive synthesis connecting the terms and passages; it should be presented with the same force as the source, but with the reasoning visible.
