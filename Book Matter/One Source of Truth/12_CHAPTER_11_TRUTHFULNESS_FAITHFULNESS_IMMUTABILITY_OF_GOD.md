# ONE SOURCE OF TRUTH
## The Doctrine of the Graphē

# MOVEMENT II — GOD, TRUTH, AND REVELATION

# Chapter 11 — The Truthfulness, Faithfulness, and Immutability of God

> **Central question:** If God is the ground of truth, what is it about God's own nature that makes Him a trustworthy ground of truth and revelation?

Chapter 10 established the necessity of moving from the question of an ultimate ground to the nature of that ground.

It also established an important boundary.

It is not enough to say:

> **“God exists.”**

Nor is it enough to say:

> **“God is powerful.”**

If God is to be the ultimate ground of truth, and if a later written revelation is to be regarded as trustworthy because it proceeds from God, then God's relationship to truth must itself be established.

The question is therefore not merely whether God possesses information.

It is not merely whether God has spoken.

It is not even merely whether God has historically made promises.

The deeper question is:

> **What is God like with respect to truth, faithfulness, constancy, and falsehood?**

That question is necessary because the reliability of communication depends upon the reliability of its source.

If a source can deceive, its statements require independent correction.

If a source is capable of error, its testimony cannot simply function as an infallible standard.

But if the source is intrinsically true, faithful, unchanging, and incapable of falsehood, then the epistemological status of what that source communicates is radically different.

This chapter therefore develops the divine attributes that make the next question possible.

Chapter 12 will then take one of them—the impossibility of God lying—and examine it with greater precision.

---

# 1. The Source Must Be Examined Before the Communication

Suppose a person receives a document claiming:

> “God said this.”

The statement contains two distinct claims.

First:

> **God exists and is capable of communicating.**

Second:

> **This particular communication genuinely came from God.**

But even before those questions can be resolved, another issue arises:

> **What kind of source is God?**

If the alleged source were known to be deceptive, the claim would require immediate suspicion.

If the source were known to be unreliable, the claim could not be accepted merely because it was attributed to that source.

If the source were perfectly truthful and incapable of falsehood, however, a genuine communication from that source would carry a very different epistemic status.

Therefore:

> **The character of the source is relevant to the credibility of the communication.**

This does not mean that every document claiming divine origin is automatically genuine.

That would collapse **divine truthfulness** into **documentary authentication**, which the book has deliberately refused to do.

The correct order is:

> **God's truthfulness → possibility of trustworthy revelation → evidence for a claimed revelation → authentication of the written record.**

Each step still has to be established.

---

# 2. Truthfulness Is More Than Accurate Speech

A person can accidentally speak something true.

A person can also communicate a correct statement while misunderstanding the subject.

Truthfulness, however, concerns more than the accidental correspondence of an isolated sentence with reality.

It concerns the character of the communicator.

A truthful person does not intentionally present falsehood as truth.

When the Bible describes God as true, therefore, the claim concerns God's character and reliability.

This distinction matters because the book is not trying to establish merely:

> “God has said some true things.”

It is asking whether:

> **truth belongs intrinsically to God.**

That is a much stronger proposition.

If truthfulness were merely occasional in God, then the human investigator would still require an independent standard for deciding when God's statements could be trusted.

But if God is true in His very nature, the situation changes.

---

# 3. “God Is True”

The biblical record repeatedly attributes truthfulness to God.

The declaration in Mark 12:14 is especially direct:

> **“Master, we know that thou art true, and carest for no man...”**

The statement is addressed to Jesus Christ.

It does not merely praise Him as someone who occasionally teaches correct propositions.

It identifies Him as **true**.

This must be read alongside His own declaration in John 14:6:

> **“I am the way, the truth, and the life.”**

The significance is substantial.

Christ does not merely claim to possess truth as information.

He identifies Himself with **the truth**.

That moves the discussion from:

> “What does God know?”

to:

> **“What is God in relation to truth?”**

This is one reason Christ cannot be treated merely as an evidential footnote later in the book.

The investigation of divine truthfulness ultimately encounters Christ's own identity claims.

---

# 4. Truthfulness and the Nature of God

If God is true, then falsehood cannot be treated as a normal alternative available to God in the way that it is available to human beings.

Human beings can know something to be true and nevertheless lie about it.

They can misunderstand.

They can deceive.

They can change their statements.

They can make promises they later abandon.

They can sincerely report what is false because they are mistaken.

None of these limitations can simply be transferred to God.

The biblical record establishes a fundamentally different picture.

Numbers 23:19 states:

> **“God is not a man, that he should lie; neither the son of man, that he should repent: hath he said, and shall he not do it? or hath he spoken, and shall he not make it good?”**

The argument is not:

> “God has a very good record of honesty.”

The contrast is deeper:

> **“God is not a man, that he should lie.”**

The text connects God's inability to lie with the distinction between God and man.

This does not mean that God lacks the capacity to communicate or act freely.

It means that falsehood is incompatible with who He is.

That distinction becomes decisive when Hebrews 6:17–18 is examined directly in Chapter 12.

---

# 5. Faithfulness: When God Speaks, His Word Corresponds to His Character

Truthfulness concerns what God is.

Faithfulness concerns the reliability with which God acts according to what He has said.

The two belong together.

A being could theoretically tell the truth about the present while failing to keep promises concerning the future.

Biblical faithfulness goes beyond accurate description.

It includes dependable correspondence between God's Word and God's action.

Deuteronomy 7:9 declares:

> **“Know therefore that the LORD thy God, he is God, the faithful God, which keepeth covenant and mercy with them that love him and keep his commandments to a thousand generations.”**

The text does not merely say that God makes covenants.

It identifies Him as **the faithful God** and connects that faithfulness with keeping covenant.

This is important for revelation because revelation is not merely a set of abstract propositions.

The biblical record repeatedly presents God speaking, promising, commanding, warning, acting, and fulfilling.

Faithfulness therefore links:

> **God's character → God's Word → God's action.**

That connection is essential to the reliability of divine communication.

---

# 6. God's Faithfulness Is Not Created by Human Verification

A necessary distinction must be made here.

When human beings investigate whether God is faithful, they are not creating God's faithfulness by their investigation.

They are discovering, testing, or reasoning about a property that is claimed to belong to God.

This is the same distinction used throughout the book:

> **Evidence does not create reality.**

Evidence provides grounds for recognizing reality.

If God is faithful, He is faithful whether a human being recognizes it or not.

If God is true, He is true whether a human institution accepts the proposition or not.

The epistemological task is therefore:

> **How do we rationally establish what is already the case?**

That distinction prevents a common error.

Human consensus does not manufacture divine truthfulness.

The task is to determine whether the evidence and biblical record warrant the conclusion.

---

# 7. Immutability: Why Constancy Matters

Truthfulness and faithfulness would be significantly weakened if God's nature were unstable.

If God were constantly changing in the relevant sense, then the investigator could ask:

> “Which God are we talking about?”

Or:

> “Was what God revealed yesterday still true today?”

The biblical record does not present God in this way.

James 1:17 states:

> **“Every good gift and every perfect gift is from above, and cometh down from the Father of lights, with whom is no variableness, neither shadow of turning.”**

The statement concerns the absence of variability and changing shadow in God.

The significance for the present investigation is not that God never acts differently in different circumstances.

Rather, His character is not subject to the instability that characterizes finite creatures.

His goodness is not temporary.

His truthfulness is not seasonal.

His faithfulness is not dependent upon changing human opinion.

His character does not become true or false according to circumstances.

---

# 8. Immutability Does Not Mean Inactivity

A common misunderstanding must be prevented.

To say that God is immutable does not mean that God cannot act.

The biblical record is filled with divine action.

God creates.

God speaks.

God judges.

God promises.

God delivers.

God sends.

God reveals.

God responds to human actions in history.

Immutability concerns God's **nature and character**, not the proposition that God never acts or that every divine action occurs in exactly the same manner.

This distinction is necessary because otherwise “unchanging” could be misunderstood as philosophical staticness.

The biblical concern is deeper:

> **The God who acts in history does not become a different kind of God while doing so.**

The God who promises remains faithful to His promise.

The God who speaks truth does not become false.

The God who establishes His counsel does not abandon it because His character has changed.

---

# 9. God's Counsel and the Stability of His Purpose

Hebrews 6:17–18 provides an especially important connection between God's purpose, His oath, His immutability, and His inability to lie:

> **“Wherein God, willing more abundantly to shew unto the heirs of promise the immutability of his counsel, confirmed it by an oath:  
> That by two immutable things, in which it was impossible for God to lie, we might have a strong consolation...”**

The text places several ideas together:

1. God has counsel.
2. God's counsel is immutable.
3. God confirms His promise.
4. The things involved are immutable.
5. It is impossible for God to lie.

The order matters.

The writer is not presenting God's truthfulness as an isolated philosophical attribute.

The impossibility of falsehood is embedded in God's unchanging purpose and His trustworthy promise.

This is why Chapter 12 will need to examine the passage closely rather than merely quoting the phrase:

> **“it was impossible for God to lie.”**

The question is:

> **Why is lying impossible for God?**

The answer must be grounded in what God is, not merely in what God happens to do.

---

# 10. God's Word and God's Character

A source's communication cannot be separated indefinitely from the source itself.

If God is true, then God's Word cannot be treated as though it were independent of His nature.

This does not yet prove that every document claiming to be God's Word actually is God's Word.

That remains an authentication question.

But it establishes an important conditional principle:

> **If a communication genuinely proceeds from the God who is true and incapable of falsehood, its truthfulness cannot be treated as an accidental possibility.**

The question then becomes:

> **How do we establish that the communication genuinely proceeds from God?**

That is the transition to revelation and authentication.

The present chapter therefore establishes the **source-side premise**.

Later chapters will examine the **communication-side evidence**.

---

# 11. God Does Not Need an External Moral Correction

Another consequence follows.

If God is intrinsically true and faithful, then God's moral character is not being measured by a higher external human standard.

This is important because a common philosophical formulation asks:

> “Is something good because God commands it, or does God command it because it is good?”

The question can become misleading if it assumes that God is merely an agent who receives moral standards from somewhere else.

The biblical conception presents God Himself as the ultimate reality under investigation.

The relevant issue is not that God arbitrarily manufactures goodness.

Nor is it that God is judged by a superior moral authority.

Rather:

> **God's character is the standard against which His own acts and communications are understood.**

This is another reason the doctrine of divine truthfulness is ontological before it becomes merely ethical.

---

# 12. Truth, Faithfulness, and Immutability Form a Single Epistemic Structure

These attributes should not be studied as disconnected theological labels.

They answer different aspects of one question.

### Truthfulness asks:

> **Is God true rather than false?**

### Faithfulness asks:

> **Can God's Word and commitments be depended upon?**

### Immutability asks:

> **Does His character and counsel remain stable rather than becoming something else?**

Together they yield:

> **God is true, His Word is dependable, and His character does not change into its opposite.**

That combination is precisely what makes trustworthy revelation possible.

If God were true but unreliable in keeping His Word, revelation would remain unstable.

If God were faithful but capable of becoming false, His future communications could not be secured.

If God were immutable but intrinsically false, His constancy would merely make falsehood permanent.

The three concepts therefore work together.

> **Truthfulness gives the content reliability.**

> **Faithfulness gives God's commitments dependability.**

> **Immutability gives the source stability.**

---

# 13. Why This Matters for Divine Revelation

We can now see why the question of revelation could not properly have been placed before this examination.

Suppose God were merely powerful.

Power does not entail truthfulness.

Suppose God were merely intelligent.

Intelligence does not entail moral fidelity.

Suppose God were merely eternal.

Eternity does not entail truth.

Suppose God were merely the Creator.

Creatorship alone, without consideration of God's character, would not establish the reliability of every alleged communication attributed to Him.

Therefore:

> **The existence of God is not the whole argument for trustworthy revelation.**

The nature of God matters.

This is why the book's architecture moves:

> **God → truthfulness → faithfulness → immutability → inability to lie → revelation.**

The sequence is not decorative.

Each stage supplies what the next requires.

---

# 14. Does This Prove Every Claimed Revelation?

No.

This distinction must be protected rigorously.

From:

> **God is truthful**

we cannot validly infer:

> **Therefore every person who says “God told me” is telling the truth.**

That would be an obvious non sequitur.

Likewise:

> **God cannot lie**

does not entail:

> **Every document claiming divine origin is inspired.**

A human being can falsely attribute a statement to God.

A document can contain a false attribution.

A copy can contain a transmission error.

A commentator can misunderstand a text.

An institution can attach God's name to its own doctrine.

Therefore the authenticity of a claimed revelation remains an evidential question.

The correct inference is narrower:

> **If God genuinely communicates something, the truthfulness of that communication is secured by the truthfulness of God.**

The remaining question is whether the alleged communication genuinely came from Him.

---

# 15. God’s Truthfulness Does Not Eliminate the Need for Evidence

This point is crucial for the epistemology of the book.

Someone might reason:

> “If God cannot lie, then there is no need to examine evidence.”

That does not follow.

The impossibility of God lying concerns the truthfulness of God.

Evidence concerns whether the thing before us actually comes from God.

Consider the difference between these propositions:

**P1:** God cannot lie.

**P2:** This document is God's Word.

**P3:** Therefore every proposition in this document is true.

P1 does not establish P2.

But:

> **P1 + P2 → P3**

can become a legitimate form of reasoning once P2 has itself been established.

That distinction will become one of the central epistemological safeguards of the book.

The source can be perfectly truthful while an alleged document falsely claiming to represent that source is not.

---

# 16. Why This Prevents Circularity

The distinction also prevents the argument from becoming circular.

A circular argument would reason:

> “The Bible is God's Word because the Bible says it is God's Word; therefore everything in the Bible is true because the Bible is God's Word.”

That does not independently establish the premise.

The present architecture is different.

It proceeds:

> **What is truth?**

↓

> **What grounds truth?**

↓

> **What can be established concerning God?**

↓

> **What is God's nature with respect to truth and falsehood?**

↓

> **What would follow if God genuinely revealed something?**

↓

> **What evidence establishes that a particular revelation genuinely came from God?**

↓

> **What evidence establishes that a particular written record preserves that revelation?**

This is cumulative reasoning.

The later conclusion does not simply appear at the beginning as an assumption.

---

# 17. God’s Faithfulness in the Biblical Record Is Historical, Not Merely Abstract

The biblical presentation of God's faithfulness is repeatedly connected with events.

God makes promises.

God enters covenants.

God acts.

God delivers.

God judges.

God fulfills.

This is important because the investigation will eventually require historical evidence.

If the Bible merely offered an abstract definition:

> “God is faithful,”

the claim would still require examination.

But the biblical record repeatedly presents God's faithfulness through actions and fulfilled declarations.

This creates a historical dimension to the argument.

The question eventually becomes:

> **Do the events recorded in the biblical writings correspond to reality, and do the patterns of promise and fulfillment support the claim that God is faithful?**

That question belongs to the cumulative authentication process.

The present chapter supplies the conceptual framework within which such evidence becomes intelligible.

---

# 18. God's Character and the Meaning of Revelation

If revelation is communication from God, then revelation cannot be understood independently of the nature of its source.

The source is:

> **true**

therefore His genuine communication is not false.

The source is:

> **faithful**

therefore His promises and declarations are dependable.

The source is:

> **immutable**

therefore His truthfulness is not subject to a later reversal of character.

The source is:

> **incapable of falsehood**

therefore genuine divine communication cannot contain deliberate divine deception.

These conclusions do not yet settle the question of inspiration.

They establish why inspiration, once established, matters.

---

# 19. The Special Significance of 2 Corinthians 1:18

Paul makes a striking statement:

> **“But as God is true, our word toward you was not yea and nay.”**

The phrase begins with:

> **“As God is true...”**

God's truthfulness is being used as the foundation for the reliability of what is being said.

This is important because it shows the biblical pattern itself:

> **God's character → reliability of communication.**

The argument is not a modern philosophical invention imposed upon Scripture.

The biblical record itself connects the truthfulness of God with the reliability of what proceeds from Him.

That connection is essential to the present investigation.

---

# 20. Christ's Truthfulness Brings the Argument Into Focus

The earlier references to Christ can now be brought together.

John 14:6 records:

> **“I am the way, the truth, and the life...”**

Mark 12:14 records the recognition:

> **“Master, we know that thou art true...”**

John 1:1 states:

> **“In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God.”**

John 1:3 continues:

> **“All things were made by him; and without him was not any thing made that was made.”**

These passages place Christ within the investigation at a level that cannot be reduced to “a teacher who said true things.”

The Word is presented as God.

The Word is presented as involved in creation.

Christ identifies Himself with truth.

Christ is described as true.

This will become decisive later when the book examines:

- Christ's treatment of Scripture;
- His use of Scripture in argument;
- His confirmation of the written record;
- His authority over the apostles;
- and the apostolic testimony concerning Him.

The investigation is therefore moving toward Christ not as an incidental religious figure, but as a central axis in the authentication of divine revelation.

---

# 21. What Does “Immutable” Actually Protect?

The importance of immutability can be stated precisely.

If God were capable of becoming morally or epistemically opposite to what He is, then a revelation authenticated at one point would require continual re-evaluation based on whether God's nature had changed.

But the biblical conception denies that instability.

James 1:17 speaks of:

> **“no variableness, neither shadow of turning.”**

Hebrews 6:17 speaks of:

> **“the immutability of his counsel.”**

The issue is therefore not that God cannot interact dynamically with history.

The issue is that His essential character and purpose are not subject to corruption, contradiction, or reversal.

That stability is essential for trustworthy revelation.

---

# 22. The Three Attributes Answer Three Different Threats

It is useful to identify the threats these attributes answer.

### Threat 1 — Falsehood

If God could lie, His communication could not be treated as inherently trustworthy.

**Answer:** truthfulness.

### Threat 2 — Unreliability

If God did not keep His Word, knowing that He had spoken truth would not guarantee that His commitments would be fulfilled.

**Answer:** faithfulness.

### Threat 3 — Instability

If God's character or counsel could fundamentally change, what was reliable yesterday might not remain reliable tomorrow.

**Answer:** immutability.

Together:

> **Truthfulness → no falsehood.**

> **Faithfulness → dependable Word and promise.**

> **Immutability → enduring stability of character and counsel.**

This is the epistemic significance of the three attributes.

---

# 23. The Argument Has Not Yet Reached Scripture

This boundary must remain explicit.

At this point, the book has not yet demonstrated:

- that the Bible is the Word of God;
- that every biblical book belongs to the canon;
- that every biblical writer was inspired;
- that every manuscript transmission was error-free;
- that every interpretation of Scripture is correct;
- or that every statement made by every person recorded in Scripture represents divine speech.

Those are different propositions.

The present conclusion is narrower:

> **The biblical conception of God presents Him as true, faithful, and unchanging; these attributes are directly relevant to whether divine communication can be trustworthy.**

The next chapter will focus specifically on the strongest formulation:

> **“It was impossible for God to lie.”**

That proposition must not be diluted into the weaker statement:

> “God normally tells the truth.”

The stronger claim has stronger consequences and requires stronger reasoning.

---

# 24. Why “God Cannot Lie” Is More Than a Moral Statement

If lying were merely something God chose not to do, the proposition would describe behavior.

But Hebrews 6:18 uses stronger language:

> **“it was impossible for God to lie.”**

The distinction is enormous.

“God does not lie” can be heard as:

> God chooses not to lie.

“It is impossible for God to lie” points toward:

> **falsehood is incompatible with God's nature.**

The epistemological consequence is correspondingly stronger.

If falsehood is intrinsically incompatible with God, then genuine divine revelation cannot be treated as though God might deliberately communicate falsehood.

This is why the question of God's inability to lie deserves its own chapter.

---

# 25. What This Chapter Has Established

The argument can now be summarized without reducing it to slogans.

### 1. The character of a source matters to the credibility of its communication.

A source that is deceptive or unreliable cannot simply function as an infallible authority.

### 2. Biblical truthfulness concerns God's nature.

The issue is not merely whether God has occasionally spoken correct propositions.

### 3. God is presented as true.

The biblical record explicitly attributes truth to God and to Christ.

### 4. God is faithful.

His faithfulness is connected with His covenant, promises, and actions.

### 5. God is immutable.

His character and counsel are not subject to the instability that characterizes finite beings.

### 6. These attributes belong together.

Truthfulness, faithfulness, and immutability jointly explain why genuine divine communication can be trusted.

### 7. These attributes do not authenticate every claim made in God's name.

The authenticity of a particular revelation or document remains an evidential question.

### 8. The distinction prevents circular reasoning.

The book can establish God's truthfulness without simply assuming that every later document claiming divine origin is authentic.

### 9. Christ occupies a central place in the argument.

His identity as truth, His relation to the Word, and His historical treatment of Scripture will become increasingly important.

### 10. The next question is unavoidable.

If falsehood is impossible for God, **why is it impossible?**

---

# 26. What Has Not Yet Been Established?

The following remain open questions:

- Why exactly is it impossible for God to lie?
- What does the impossibility of divine falsehood mean philosophically and biblically?
- Does divine omnipotence include the ability to do what contradicts God's nature?
- How do God's truthfulness and logical coherence relate?
- What does Scripture mean when it says God cannot lie?
- How does God's inability to lie affect divine promises?
- How does it affect revelation?
- How does it affect inspiration?
- How does it affect the claim that Scripture is infallible?
- What evidence is required to move from **“God cannot lie”** to **“this written record genuinely comes from God”**?

These questions must not be prematurely answered here.

They generate the next chapter.

---

# 27. The Next Necessary Question

We have established that the biblical conception of God is not merely:

> **a powerful being who happens to communicate truth.**

God is presented as:

> **true;**

> **faithful;**

> **unchanging;**

and therefore as a source whose genuine communication cannot be treated as ordinary fallible human testimony.

But one statement demands closer examination:

> **“It was impossible for God to lie.”**

Why?

Is the statement merely saying that God has chosen never to lie?

Or does it mean that falsehood is metaphysically and morally incompatible with God's nature?

If so:

> **What does that impossibility entail for God's Word, His promises, His revelation, and eventually the written record that claims to preserve His revelation?**

That is the question of the next chapter.

## Conceptual distinctions

| Concept | What it establishes |
|---|---|
| **Truthfulness** | Falsehood is incompatible with God's truthful nature |
| **Faithfulness** | God's Word, covenant, and commitments can be depended upon |
| **Immutability** | God's essential character and counsel are not unstable or subject to reversal |
| **Revelation** | What God communicates |
| **Authentication** | What establishes that a claimed communication genuinely proceeds from God |
| **Inspiration** | The divine operation concerning the production of Scripture |
| **Infallibility** | The conclusion concerning the truthfulness/reliability of the inspired record |

## Logical guardrails

- Do not infer that every statement attributed to God is genuine merely because God is truthful.
- Do not confuse God's inability to lie with the inability of human beings to falsely claim divine authority.
- Do not use the truthfulness of God as a shortcut around historical authentication.
- Do not treat faithfulness as merely emotional reliability.
- Do not treat immutability as inactivity or inability to act in history.
- Do not reduce “God is true” to “God has accurate information.”
- Do not collapse revelation, inspiration, canon, transmission, authentication, and infallibility into one concept.
- Do not use theological labels without establishing what the underlying biblical propositions actually mean.
- Do not allow a conclusion to outrun its premises.
- Do not replace the biblical record with commentary about the biblical record.
