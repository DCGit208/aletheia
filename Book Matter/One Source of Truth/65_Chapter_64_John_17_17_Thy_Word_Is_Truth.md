# 64.3 Define the Question: The Relationship Between God’s Word and Truth

**The relationship**, in this chapter, is the dedicated account of how God’s Word stands to truth in the wording of John 17:17, given that the Graphē has already been shown to be unbreakable because it is God’s truthful written Word.

The relationship is easily abused. In ordinary speech “thy word is truth” may mean a preacher’s motto, a claim that whatever a later speaker feels is thereby God’s word, a claim that sincerity is truth, a claim that every later tradition is thereby certified, a psychological remark that Scripture is inspiring, or a claim that Chapter 65 is already finished because an identification exists. None of those senses is this chapter’s object.

Four elements belong to the definition.

First, **relationship as identification**. The object is not a catalogue of true sentences that happen to occur in a religious book. The object is the predication the Son makes: ὁ λόγος ὁ σὸς ἀλήθειά ἐστιν — the word which is Thine is truth. Identification names what the word is. It does not name a sample of its contents.

Second, **in the prayer-context**. The sentence occurs in the Son’s prayer to the Father concerning those given Him, in the unit that asks for their sanctification through the truth. Occasion, speaker, addressee, and work named (sanctification) belong to the object. A detached slogan is not the object.

Third, **not a second why, and not the gate**. The why of unloosenability is already in force. The present file connects that why with this identification. It does not re-prove the why. It does not conclude that the Graphē is the primary written authority by which truth is known, tested, reasoned through, taught, discerned, and lived. Connection is not consumption of the next file.

Fourth, **not those other claims**. The definition excludes, as not this object: a retry of Chapter 63; a replacement of John 10 by John 17; a grant that every later interpreter’s claim is truth; a reduction of “word” to private impression; a reduction of truth to sincerity; a treatise on the later ethics of holiness as a separate subject. Those exclusions are part of the definition. They are not a later apology.

Thus:

> **The question of this chapter is not why the scripture cannot be broken, how the a fortiori is drawn, or why the Graphē is the primary written authority in the Movement VIII sense. Those questions have been examined or remain later. The question is the relationship between God’s Word and truth in John 17:17.**

# 64.7 The Prayer Context: John 17 as the High Priestly Prayer

John 17 is prayer. The Gospel locates it after the farewell discourse and before the arrest. The speaker is the Son. The addressee is the Father. The subjects of the petition, in the unit that contains verse 17, are those the Father has given Him out of the world. The conventional designation **High Priestly Prayer** names that act: the Son, about to be consecrated and to consecrate Himself (17:19), intercedes for those given Him. The designation is a traditional name for the chapter as prayer of intercession and consecration. It is not a reconstructed Levitical ceremony invented behind the text. The text itself is the control: He prays; He speaks to the Father; He asks concerning those given Him; He names His own sanctification for their sakes.

Chapter 44 already established what this prayer is as revelation-file: not controversy, and not thereby a weaker claim. Eternal life is knowledge of the only true God and of Jesus Christ whom He has sent (17:3). He has manifested the name unto the men given Him (17:6). He has given them the words the Father gave Him; they have received them, and have known surely that He came out from the Father, and have believed that the Father sent Him (17:8). The world has not known the Father; He has known the Father; these have known that the Father sent Him; He has declared the name and will declare it (17:25–26). Those conclusions remain premises. This chapter does not re-write John 17 as a second Christology-file. It locates verse 17 inside that prayer as the dedicated identification of the Father’s word as truth.

The prayer is not a retreat from John 10. It is a different act. In John 10 He answers a charge by opening the Graphē as court. In John 17 He speaks to the Father of those given Him, of the word already given them, of the world’s hatred, of their being kept, of their being sent, and of their being sanctified through the truth. The two acts belong to one Speaker. They do not collapse into one scene.

The prayer’s first movement (17:1–5) concerns the Son’s glorification and the finished work. The second (17:6–19) concerns those given Him in the ministry: manifestation of the name, giving of the words, keeping, hatred by the world, sanctification through the truth, His own sanctification for their sakes, and their sending as He was sent. The third (17:20–26) concerns those who shall believe on Him through their word, and the declared name. Verse 17 stands in the second movement. It is not an isolated motto that could equally have been spoken in the porch at Dedication or in a later academy. It is a petition and an identification inside the prayer for those who have already received the words.

Thus:

> **John 17 is the Son’s prayer to the Father — conventionally named the High Priestly Prayer — located after the farewell and before the arrest. Verse 17 belongs to the petition for those given Him. Context is the control, not a reconstructed ceremony, and not a slogan detached from the prayer.**

No major verse may become a detached proof text. The sentence of verse 17 stands in a unit.

> **“I have given them thy word; and the world hath hated them, because they are not of the world, even as I am not of the world. I pray not that thou shouldest take them out of the world, but that thou shouldest keep them from the evil. They are not of the world, even as I am not of the world. Sanctify them through thy truth: thy word is truth. As thou hast sent me into the world, even so have I also sent them into the world. And for their sakes I sanctify myself, that they also might be sanctified through the truth.”**
> — John 17:14–19

Six elements of the unit control verse 17.

**The word already given.** “I have given them thy word” (17:14) is prior to the petition of 17:17. The word in view is not an undefined future impression. It is the Father’s word the Son has already given. Verse 8 has already said the same in the plural: “I have given unto them the words which thou gavest me.” Giving is completed as ministry-act. The petition of 17:17 does not invent a word. It identifies the word already given, and asks that they be sanctified through the truth that word is.

**Not of the world.** They are hated because they are not of the world, as He is not of the world. Sanctification through the truth is not an exit from the world. He does not ask that they be taken out of the world. He asks that they be kept from the evil, and that they be sanctified through the truth. Consecration here is setting-apart in the world, not removal from it. The unit itself prevents a flight-spirituality from capturing the verse.

**The petition: sanctify them through thy truth.** ἁγίασον αὐτοὺς ἐν τῇ ἀληθείᾳ. The imperative is addressed to the Father. The object is those given Him. The sphere and instrument is the truth — articular, “the truth.” The first clause is a request for a divine act.

**The identification: thy word is truth.** ὁ λόγος ὁ σὸς ἀλήθειά ἐστιν. The second clause is not a second request. It is predication. It names what the truth is. The petition is not left with an undefined “truth.” The truth through which they are to be sanctified is the Father’s word.

**The sending.** As the Father sent the Son into the world, the Son has sent them into the world (17:18). Sanctification through the truth is ordered to mission, not to private withdrawal. The unit joins consecration and sending. It does not thereby become the apostolic-office file already examined. It locates the identification inside a sending already named.

**His self-sanctification.** “For their sakes I sanctify myself, that they also might be sanctified through the truth” (17:19). Their sanctification through the truth is not independent of His own consecration for their sakes. The unit joins His act and their being set apart through the truth. The join will be examined as relation of consecration to the Word as truth, not as a second atonement-file.

Thus:

> **Verse 17 is a petition plus an identification inside a unit: word already given; not of the world yet kept in it; sanctify them through thy truth; thy word is truth; sent as He was sent; He sanctifies Himself that they may be sanctified through the truth. The sentence is not a fragment.**

# 64.9 Lexical Control: ἁγιάζω — Sanctify

**ἁγιάζω** (*hagiazō*, G37). Basic lexical field: to make holy, to set apart, to consecrate. The aorist active imperative ἁγίασον is a request that the Father perform that act upon “them.”

Chapter 59 already noted ἁγιάζω in John 10:36 — “whom the Father hath sanctified, and sent into the world” — as belonging to the application of the a fortiori, not as a finished consecration-doctrine. That use remains a neighbouring occurrence in the same Gospel, of the same Speaker, of the same verb: the Father set the Son apart and sent Him. John 17:17–19 uses the verb of those given Him, and of the Son’s own act for their sakes. Neighbourhood is not identity of files. John 10:36 named His consecration and sending as the greater case of the dispute. John 17 names their consecration through the truth, and His self-consecration for their sakes. The verb’s field is one. The assignments differ.

Three controls belong to the present use.

**Set apart, not merely improved.** The lexical field is consecration: making holy by setting apart to God. It is not, in the first instance, a synonym for moral self-help. The prayer asks the Father to set them apart. The instrument named is the truth.

**Through / in the truth.** ἐν τῇ ἀληθείᾳ may be read as locative (in the truth as sphere) or instrumental (by/through the truth as means). The English of the received wording, “through thy truth,” captures the instrumental force without forbidding the locative. Either way, the truth is not a decoration of an otherwise independent consecration. The setting-apart is in and by the truth.

**Not a finished ethics treatise.** The verb’s presence does not convert this chapter into a later doctrine of holiness as a complete moral system. The present object is the relation: they are to be sanctified through the truth; the truth is the Father’s word. That relation is load-bearing. Expansion beyond it is theft of later files.

John 17:19 repeats the verb of the Son: ἐγὼ ἁγιάζω ἐμαυτόν — I sanctify myself. His self-consecration is for their sakes, ἵνα καὶ αὐτοὶ ὦσιν ἡγιασμένοι ἐν ἀληθείᾳ — that they also might be sanctified in/through truth. The purpose-clause ties His act to their being set apart in truth. The unit therefore does not treat “sanctify them through thy truth” as a free-floating moral wish. It treats it as a petition whose ground includes His own consecration, and whose instrument is the truth identified as the Father’s word.

Thus:

> **ἁγιάζω in 17:17 is the Father’s requested act of setting them apart, in and through the truth. The field is consecration. The assignment is the relation of that act to the Word as truth, not a second ethics-file.**

# 64.10 Lexical Control: ἀλήθεια — Truth

**ἀλήθεια** (*alētheia*, G225). Basic lexical field: truth, truthfulness, reality as distinguished from appearance, falsehood, and deceit.

Chapter 1 already defined truth as conformity to reality or actuality. Chapter 44 already controlled Johannine ἀλήθεια in John 14:6: articular ἡ ἀλήθεια, identity language, not a vague commendation of candour; not sincerity as a mood; reality as disclosed, over against lie, darkness, and the evil one. Those conclusions remain premises. This chapter does not re-write Movement I or the John 14:6 file. It uses the term as John 17:17 uses it.

Two occurrences stand in the verse, and they are not grammatically identical.

**ἐν τῇ ἀληθείᾳ.** Articular: *the* truth. The petition names a definite truth as sphere and instrument of sanctification. It does not name “truths” as a heap of useful maxims. It does not name sincerity. It names the truth.

**ἀλήθειά ἐστιν.** Predicate, anarthrous in the identification-clause: the word which is Thine *is truth*. The predication is identification of essence, not a list of contents. The article on “the truth” in the first clause and the predicate “is truth” in the second are one doctrine in two grammatical positions: the truth through which they are sanctified is what the Father’s word is.

Johannine usage already in force prevents a mood-reading. John 1:14, 17: the Word full of grace and truth; grace and truth came by Jesus Christ. John 8:32: ye shall know the truth, and the truth shall make you free. John 8:44–46: the devil is a liar; Jesus tells the truth heard from God. John 14:6: I am the way, the truth, and the life. John 17:3: the only true God. “True” there is the reality of God over against idols, not the sincerity of worshippers. The same field governs 17:17. The truth through which they are sanctified is not their candour. It is the Father’s word as reality corresponding to what God is and has said.

Thus:

> **ἀλήθεια in 17:17 is truth as already defined and as Johannine usage already controlled: conformity to reality, not sincerity as a mood. The articular “the truth” of the petition is identified, in the next clause, as the Father’s word.**

---

# 64.11 Lexical Control: ὁ λόγος ὁ σός — Thy Word

**λόγος** (*logos*, G3056). Basic lexical field: word, speech, message, account. **ὁ λόγος ὁ σός**: the word, the one that is Thine — the Father’s word.

Chapter 22 already established that “Word of God” is a broader biblical category whose particular referent is determined by context, and that not every occurrence of λόγος means the Bible. John 1:1’s ὁ λόγος names the Son. The present articular phrase with possessive ὁ σός names the word belonging to the Father. Contextual control is therefore mandatory. The control is supplied by the unit itself.

**Already given as the Father’s.** “I have given them thy word” (τὸν λόγον σου, 17:14). “I have given unto them the words (ῥήματα) which thou gavest me” (17:8). The singular λόγος and the plural ῥήματα belong to one economy: the Father’s communication, given by the Son, received by those given Him. The singular in 17:14 and 17:17 names that communication as a whole — the Father’s word as given. The plural in 17:8 names the words as given. Neither use is John 1:1. Neither use is an empty honorific. The word is the Father’s, given by the Son, already in their possession as given.

**Not private impression.** The possessive ὁ σός / σου attaches the word to the Father. It does not attach it to the disciples’ feelings, nor to a later reader’s inner voice as such. What they have been given is His word. What they may later feel is not, by the fact of feeling, His word. The grammar of the phrase is ownership: the word which is Thine.

**Related to the Graphē without mechanical synonym.** The Graphē is writing that, in biblical usage, designates Holy Writ. The Father’s word is the broader communication of which that writing is the inspired record. John 10:35 has already named those “unto whom the word of God came,” and has located that coming in the writing that cannot be broken. The word that came, written, is Graphē. The word the Son has given, identified as truth in 17:17, is the Father’s word in that same economy. The present file may therefore say, with control: the identification of the Father’s word as truth bears upon the Graphē as that word written. It may not say: every λόγος in John is a bound volume. Chapter 22 forbids the second sentence. The first sentence is this chapter’s join, not Chapter 65’s gate.

Thus:

> **ὁ λόγος ὁ σός in 17:17 is the Father’s word, already given by the Son to those given Him. It is not John 1:1 retried, not every occurrence of λόγος, not private impression, and not yet the Graphē-gate. It is the word whose written form has already been identified as Graphē, now named as truth.**

# 64.12 “Thy Word Is Truth” as Identification

The second clause of John 17:17 is identification.

> **ὁ λόγος ὁ σὸς ἀλήθειά ἐστιν.**
> **“Thy word is truth.”**

**Identification**, in this chapter, is the predication that names what the subject is. The subject is the Father’s word. The predicate is truth. The verb is ἐστιν — is. The clause does not say that the word *has* truth as one property among others, or *contains* truths as a vessel contains objects, or *serves* truth as a tool may serve an end while remaining other than the end. It says that the word *is* truth.

The first clause has asked that they be sanctified through the truth. The second clause tells what that truth is. The two clauses are one sentence in the received wording, and one doctrine in the Greek: petition, then identification of the instrument. Without the second clause, “thy truth” might be left as an undefined divine quality. With the second clause, the truth through which they are sanctified is the Father’s word.

This is the same architecture the book has already earned, now spoken by the Son to the Father. God is true and cannot lie (Chapters 10–12). Genuine divine communication is therefore not His falsehood. The word that is His is, as His, truth. John 17:17 is that conclusion as prayer, not as a second Movement II. The identification is explicit in the text. The warrant for receiving it as identification, rather than as compliment or hyperbole, is the wording itself together with the doctrine of God and of truth already in force.

Three marks of identification belong to the clause.

**The copula.** ἐστιν is not “contains,” “illustrates,” “tends toward,” or “is useful for.” It is “is.”

**The possessive.** ὁ σός locates the word as the Father’s. The identification is of *His* word, not of word as a human genus. Human speech remains human speech. The word which is Thine is truth.

**The articular truth of the petition.** The first clause’s τῇ ἀληθείᾳ is definite. The second clause identifies that definite truth with the Father’s word. Identification here is not a poetic parallel that might as well have run the other way as a mere flourish. It specifies the truth named in the petition.

Thus:

> **“Thy word is truth” is identification: the Word belonging to the Father is truth. The copula, the possessive, and the definite truth of the petition jointly forbid a weaker reading as the primary sense.**

---

# 64.13 Identification Is Not Mere Containment of Truths

A live weaker reading says:

> **The word contains truths. Some sentences in it are true. That is all “thy word is truth” needs to mean.**

Containment is not identification.

A vessel may contain gold and dross. A book may contain true sentences and false sentences. A teacher may utter a mixture. “Contains truths” is compatible with a fallible remainder. “Is truth” is not that claim. The predicate is the thing itself, not a sample of its contents.

The unit refuses the weaker reading on its own terms. If the word merely contained some truths, sanctification “through thy truth” would require a further operation: the disciples would have to winnow the true bits from the rest, and the Father would be asked to sanctify them through a selection the prayer does not name. The prayer identifies the word as truth, and asks that they be sanctified through that truth. The instrument is not a remainder after criticism has saved a few sentences. The instrument is the Father’s word as truth.

Movements I–II already make the weaker reading insufficient as doctrine of God. If God cannot lie, His word is not a mixture of His truth and His falsehood. Containment of “some truths” as the primary sense of 17:17 would imply that the remainder might not be truth, which is to say that the Father’s word, as His, might include what is not true. That implication collides with Chapters 10–12, used here not retried. The collision is a reason the weaker reading fails. It is not a second Movement II.

The Graphē, already identified as God-breathed writing that does not fail as truthful divine writing of what it actually communicates, is the written form of that word. Containment as the primary sense would also collide with inspiration’s guarantees and with infallibility of the record, used here not retried. The present file does not re-prove those files. It notes that identification, not containment, is what coheres with them.

Thus:

> **“Thy word is truth” is not the claim that the word merely contains selected truths. Containment leaves a fallible remainder the identification does not grant. Identification names what the word is.**

---

# 64.14 Identification Is Not Usefulness of a Fallible Word

A second live weaker reading says:

> **The word may be fallible as record, and still be useful for sanctification. “Thy word is truth” names religious usefulness, not identity.**

Usefulness is not identification.

A fallible instrument may be useful. A partially accurate map may still help a traveller. A proverb may edify while misstating a fact. Usefulness answers a different question from “is truth.” Chapter 1 already forbade treating usefulness, popularity, or institutional employment as determinants of truth. That prohibition remains in force. It is used here, not retried.

The prayer does not ask that they be sanctified through a useful approximation. It asks that they be sanctified through the truth, and identifies the Father’s word as that truth. If the word were fallible as the Father’s word, the first clause would ask the Father to set them apart by what is not, in whole, what He is. The second clause would then be a compliment paid to a defective instrument. The wording is not that compliment.

The weaker reading often presents itself as humility: we would not claim too much for a text. The humility is misplaced. The claim is the Son’s, spoken to the Father, of the Father’s word. Reducing it to usefulness is not humility before the text. It is a refusal of the predicate.

Movement VIII’s why already refuses the same reduction in another form. If the Graphē could be loosed as court because it was only a useful fallible book, John 10:35 would not stand as assertion of unloosenable force. The why was: because of what it is. The present identification names that “what it is” from the other side of the locked arc: it is truth. A useful fallible remainder cannot bear either file.

Thus:

> **“Thy word is truth” is not the claim that a fallible word remains useful for consecration. Usefulness is not the predicate. Identity is.**

---

# 64.15 Sanctification Through Truth: The Instrument Named

The first clause is a petition with an instrument:

> **“Sanctify them through thy truth.”**

The act requested is ἁγίασον — set them apart. The instrument and sphere is ἐν τῇ ἀληθείᾳ — in/through the truth. The identification then names the instrument: thy word is truth.

The relation is therefore inspectable.

If they are to be sanctified through the truth, and the truth is the Father’s word, then they are to be sanctified through the Father’s word. The inference is not a later system. It is the two clauses read as one sentence. The chapter’s object includes that relation. It does not include a complete later doctrine of how holiness is grown, staged, lost, or classified in the churches.

Three positive statements belong to the relation at this level.

**The instrument is truth, not a substitute for truth.** They are not asked to be set apart through error that happens to be edifying, through a lie told for a pious end, or through a feeling offered as if it were the Father’s word. The Father is asked to sanctify them through the truth.

**The truth is the Father’s word.** The instrument is not an abstract property detached from what He has said. It is His word, already given by the Son.

**Setting-apart by that word is setting-apart by truth.** Because the word is truth, the consecration requested is not consecration by a religious object other than truth. Word and truth are not two instruments. They are named as one: the truth, which the word is.

The unit’s “not of the world” and “keep them from the evil” locate this setting-apart as holiness in the world, not as extraction from it. The sending of 17:18 locates it as consecration ordered to mission. Those locatings belong to the unit. They do not become a later missiology-file.

Thus:

> **Sanctification through truth is, in this sentence, sanctification through the Father’s word, because that word is truth. The instrument is named. The naming is not a second ethics-book.**

# 64.16 How Sanctification Relates to the Word Being Truth — Without an Ethics Treatise

The relation must be stated with full force and then stopped at the assignment’s edge.

**Why the Word’s being truth matters for sanctification.** If the word were not truth, “sanctify them through thy truth” and “thy word is truth” would part company. One could then imagine a consecration through some other truth than the word, or a use of the word that did not sanctify because the word was not the truth named. The sentence refuses both. The truth through which they are set apart is the word. The word through which they are set apart is truth. Consecration here is truth-wrought because it is Word-wrought, and Word-wrought because the Word is truth.

**What this is not.** It is not the claim that every later moral dispute is settled by quoting the clause. It is not a complete account of regeneration, illumination, filling, or the later vocabulary of holiness movements. It is not Movement IX’s analysis of mishandling, and not Movement X’s analysis of skill. Those files remain later. The present relation is only this: the Father’s act of setting them apart is requested as an act in and through the truth; that truth is His word; therefore the consecration in view is not independent of the Word as truth.

**Neighbouring texts used, not expanded into this file.** Ephesians 5:26 speaks of Christ sanctifying and cleansing the church with the washing of water by the word. 1 Peter 1:22–23 speaks of purifying souls in obeying the truth, being born again by the word of God. John 15:3: “Now ye are clean through the word which I have spoken unto you.” Those texts confirm that the writings themselves join word, truth, cleansing, and setting-apart. They do not licence a second baptism-file, a second regeneration-file, or a second vine-file inside this chapter. Confirmation of the join is the use. Exhaustion of those doctrines is not.

**The limit is the shape of the doctrine.** A chapter on John 17:17 that omitted sanctification would omit half the verse. A chapter on John 17:17 that became a treatise on Christian ethics would omit the assignment. The assignment is the relationship between God’s Word and truth, with sanctification through truth as the petition that identification serves. The petition is kept. The treatise is refused.

Thus:

> **Sanctification through truth relates to the Word being truth as instrument to identity: they are set apart through the truth because the Word is that truth. The relation is load-bearing. A separate ethics treatise is not this file.**

# 64.17 John 17:19: His Self-Consecration and Their Sanctification Through the Truth

Verse 19 belongs to the unit and must not be ignored.

> **“And for their sakes I sanctify myself, that they also might be sanctified through the truth.”**

He sanctifies Himself for their sakes. The purpose is that they also might be sanctified through the truth. Their being set apart through the truth is not a self-contained moral project. It is tied to His own consecration.

Chapter 44 and Chapters 45–46 already identified Him as the Sent One who gives His life and is raised. This chapter does not re-try the death and resurrection as Movement VI’s hinge. It uses that identity as already in force: the One who prays is the One who will be delivered up, as the Gospel’s next chapters record. “I sanctify myself” in this setting is His setting Himself apart to that work for their sakes. The prayer does not pause to lecture on sacrifice. It names the act as consecration, and names its purpose: that they also might be sanctified through the truth.

The join that belongs to this file is therefore twofold, and only twofold.

**His consecration is for their sakes.** Their sanctification through the truth is not independent of Him. The Truth who is the Son (John 14:6) consecrates Himself that they may be sanctified through the truth which is the Father’s word. Person and word are not rivals. The Person gives the word and consecrates Himself. The word is the Father’s, and is truth.

**The purpose-clause keeps the instrument.** Even when His self-consecration is named, the instrument of their sanctification remains “through the truth.” Verse 19 does not replace verse 17’s identification with a wordless mysticism. It confirms it: they are to be sanctified through the truth — the truth already identified as the Father’s word — and this is the purpose of His self-consecration for their sakes.

Thus:

> **John 17:19 ties their sanctification through the truth to His self-consecration for their sakes, without replacing the Word as truth with a wordless act, and without becoming a second atonement-file.**

# 64.18 The Words Already Given as the Same Word Now Identified

The identification does not float above the ministry. It names what has already been given.

Verse 6: He has manifested the Father’s name unto the men given Him; they have kept His word (τὸν λόγον σου). Verse 8: He has given them the words (ῥήματα) the Father gave Him; they have received them. Verse 14: He has given them the Father’s word (τὸν λόγον σου). Verse 17: that word is truth.

The sequence is the ministry’s own. Manifestation of the name, giving of the words, reception, keeping, then petition that they be sanctified through the truth which that word is. The word identified as truth is not an unknown quantity awaiting a later interior event as its definition. It is the word already spoken, given, and received.

This sequence answers a particular abuse in advance. If “thy word” were defined as whatever later impression a person names as God’s speech, verse 17 would baptize private feeling. The prayer has already specified the word: the Father’s, given by the Son, received by those given Him. Identification attaches to that word. It does not attach to an undefined later substitute.

John 17:20 will extend the prayer to those who shall believe on Him through their word (διὰ τοῦ λόγου αὐτῶν). “Their word” is the word of the given men, the word they have received and will speak. The extension belongs to the prayer’s third movement. It is not this chapter’s gate, and it is not an unrestricted licence for every later speaker. Chapter 49 already controlled the sending-formula as His sending, not later self-appointment. That control remains. The present point is narrower: the word identified as truth in 17:17 is the Father’s word given in the ministry; “their word” in 17:20 is that word as spoken by those who received it, not a second rival source. The Graphē-gate will have to ask how the written testimony stands as primary written authority. This file does not ask that question as finished. It notes that the prayer itself already joins the Father’s word, the Son’s giving, and the given men’s word, without making every later claim identical with that word.

Thus:

> **The word identified as truth is the Father’s word already given and received. Identification does not wait upon a later impression to become definite. “Their word” in 17:20 is that word as spoken by the given men, not a blank check for every later speaker.**

# 64.19 Positive Warrant Connecting This Identification with Movement VIII So Far

The locked guidance’s step 10 is connection, not replacement. The connection can now be stated with full force.

Movement VIII has shown that in the Dedication controversy He appeals to what is written as court; that the cited writing is Psalm 82 as written; that the argument is an a fortiori from that writing; that “the scripture cannot be broken” asserts unloosenable court-force; that the why of that incapacity is what the Graphē is: God’s truthful written Word.

John 17:17 identifies the Father’s word as truth.

The join is this:

> **The writing cannot be broken because it is God’s truthful written Word. The Son, speaking to the Father, identifies that Word as truth. Unloosenability and identification are two verdicts upon one reality: the Father’s word, written as Graphē, is truth, and therefore cannot be loosed as if it were not.**

The join does not require that John 17 be relocated into Solomon’s porch. Occasion differs. Act differs: controversy is not prayer. Grammatical object in 10:35 is ἡ γραφή; in 17:17 it is ὁ λόγος ὁ σός. The two are related as writing and word, already distinguished in Chapter 22 and already joined in John 10:35’s own “word of God came” plus “the scripture cannot be broken.” The present connection is the same economy named from the other side: the word that came, written, cannot be broken because of what it is; the word that is the Father’s is truth.

Without this connection, the why of Chapter 63 would remain a doctrine of the writing unjoined to the Son’s dedicated identification of the word as truth. With this connection, step 10 is performed. The performance is not Chapter 65. The performance is the join the locked arc names.

His identity as the Truth remains in force throughout the join. The Truth asserts that the scripture cannot be broken. The Truth prays that the Father’s word is truth. The two sentences are one Speaker’s verdict upon word and writing. They are not two religions.

Thus:

> **Step 10 is performed: the why of unloosenability is connected with “thy word is truth.” The Graphē cannot be broken because it is God’s truthful written Word; the Father’s word is truth. Connection is not replacement of John 10, and not the gate.**

# 64.20 Positive Warrant Connecting This Identification with Movements I–II

The identification also coheres with the doctrine of truth already earned. That coherence is use, not retry.

**Movement I.** Truth is conformity to reality, not assertion, not belief, not institutional consensus, not sincerity as a determinant. If the Father’s word is truth, it conforms to what is actually the case. It is not made truth by the disciples’ reception, by later institutional employment, or by the warmth with which it is heard. Reception is commanded and recorded. Reception does not create the identity. 1 Thessalonians 2:13 will be used below as neighbouring record of right classification: they received it not as the word of men, but as it is in truth the word of God. “As it is in truth” locates classification in what the word is. Movement I is that architecture in advance of this verse. John 17:17 is that architecture as the Son’s identification.

**Movement II.** God is the ground of truth. He is truthful, faithful, immutable. He cannot lie. If the word is His, it is not His falsehood. “Thy word is truth” is what that character yields when the Son names the Father’s word to the Father. The verse is not a second proof that God cannot lie. It is the application of that already-established character to the word in the prayer.

The two movements together prevent two reductions that will be refused later as competing claims: truth as sincerity, and word as private impression. Sincerity is a quality of a subject. Truth is conformity to reality. Impression is a state of a subject. The Father’s word is His communication. The identification attaches to His word, as truth, because of who He is and because of what truth is. It does not attach to the subject’s mood.

Thus:

> **Movements I–II are used, not retried: truth as conformity to reality; God as ground of truth, unable to lie. John 17:17 is the Son’s identification of the Father’s word as that truth. Coherence is warrant, not a second opening of the book.**

# 64.21 The Written Word in View Without Consuming Chapter 65

The identification bears upon the Graphē. It does not finish the gate.

The Graphē has already been identified as the written Holy Writ, God-breathed, non-failing as record of what it actually communicates, standing as the written Word, unbreakable as court because of what it is. If the Father’s word is truth, the writing that is that word written is not a second, lesser kind of speech. Writing does not empty the word of being truth. Chapter 58 already synthesized standing of the written Word at Movement VII’s level. Chapter 63 already named that standing as the identity from which unloosenability follows. The present file adds the dedicated identification: that word is truth.

What the present file does not add is Chapter 65’s conclusion: that the Graphē is the primary written authority by which truth is known, tested, reasoned through, taught, discerned, and lived. That conclusion requires the identification as a premise. It is not identical with the identification. A word can be identified as truth without the investigator having yet drawn, as a dedicated verdict, the full gate of primary written authority for all later testing and living. The TOC places that verdict next. Sequence is the account of the difference.

The investigator is therefore warranted, with full force, in saying:
