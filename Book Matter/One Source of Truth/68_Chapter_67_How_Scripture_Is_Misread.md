# 67.3 Define the Question: How Scripture Is Misread

**How Scripture is misread**, in this chapter, is the dedicated account of the later act of stating sense failing to correspond to what the Graphē actually communicates, examined as inspectable modes rather than as a single mood of “error.”

The phrase is easily abused. In ordinary speech “misreading” may mean any disagreement, any difficulty, any later application, any refusal of a school’s official sense, any admission that a passage is hard, or the entire later investigation of systems, displacement, heresy, aridity, and unskillfulness. None of those senses is this chapter’s object.

Four elements belong to the definition.

First, **failure of correspondence**. Misreading is not the existence of a later act. Chapter 66 already established that stating sense is a real accountable act. Misreading is that act failing of its object: the sense stated is not the sense communicated.

Second, **modes, not one fog**. The locked assignment names five operations: poor perception, deficient understanding, faulty inference, context failure, improper handling. Those operations may combine. They are still distinguishable. A catalog that names only “people err” has not examined how.

Third, **the writing as measure of the failure**. The writing is not rewritten by being misread. Chapter 52 already established that wresting does not unmake standing. Chapter 66 already established compatibility of a fallible reader with a non-failing record. The present definition inherits both: misreading is the reader’s failure of a stable object.

Fourth, **not those other claims**. The definition excludes, as not this object: a retry of Chapter 66; a treatment of theology, philosophy, tradition, and institution as systems; a finished account of secondary sources becoming primary; tergiversation, accepted heresies, aridity, and *apeiros* as finished files; Movement X’s labour-files; the First Principles as this file’s achievement. Those exclusions are part of the definition. They are not a later apology.

Thus:

> **The question of this chapter is not whether text and interpretation are two, why the Graphē is the primary written authority, or how later systems replace the text. Those questions have been examined or remain later. The question is how Scripture is misread in the modes just named.**

# 67.4 Distinguish Objects That Must Not Be Collapsed

The investigation fails at the first step if it treats different objects as one.

**This catalog and Chapter 66’s distinction.** This chapter examines how the later act fails. Chapter 66 named the later act as distinct from the writing. Distinction and catalog neighbour one another. They are not the same file. To treat this file as Chapter 66 retried would hide the modes inside a second distinction. To omit the modes in order to protect the distinction would leave the distinction unjoined to the second word Movement IX assigns.

**Misreading and difficulty.** A passage may be hard to be understood and still not be misread. 2 Peter 3:16 names both hardness and wresting. Hardness is a feature of some writings. Wresting is a handling of those writings. To collapse the two is to baptize wresting as difficulty, or to treat every difficulty as already a crime.

**Misreading and disagreement.** Two readers may disagree, and one or both may be misreading, or the disagreement may still be a labour toward a sense not yet settled. Disagreement is a social fact. Misreading is a failure of correspondence. The first does not, by existing, prove the second of every party.

**The five modes and one another.** Poor perception is not yet deficient understanding, though it yields it. Deficient understanding is not yet faulty inference, though it invites it. Faulty inference is not yet context failure, though isolation often produces it. Context failure is not yet improper handling as a completed wresting, though it is already a mishandling of neighbours. The catalog is a distinction of operations, not a claim that a failed reading uses only one.

**This chapter and later files.** Systems, displacement, tergiversation, accepted heresies, aridity, *apeiros*, scholarly labour, *spoudazō*, and the First Principles remain later. A mode may appear in a later system. Appearance is not those files written now.

Thus:

> **Misreading is failure of correspondence in named modes. It is not the distinction retried, not mere difficulty, not mere disagreement, not one undifferentiated fog, and not the later files under another name.**

# 67.7 Define “Misreading” at This Stage

**Misreading**, in this argument, is the later act of stating the Graphē’s sense in a way that does not correspond to what that writing actually communicates.

Four notes belong to the definition at this stage.

First, **an act with an object**. The object is the writing’s communication. Chapter 57 attached non-failure to what the writing actually communicates, not to every later inference drawn from it. Misreading is failure of that attachment on the reader’s side.

Second, **detectable in principle**. Chapter 66 already used the analogue: an infallible measuring-line does not make every measurement correct; it makes incorrect measurement detectable in principle. Detectability is not automatic detection. It is the reason a catalog of modes is possible. If failure were undetectable, “how Scripture is misread” would name a fog.

Third, **possible without malice, and possible with it**. A reader may fail by dullness, haste, isolation, or invalid inference without intending to corrupt. A reader may also handle the word deceitfully. The catalog includes both. Intention distinguishes culpability. It does not create a second definition of correspondence.

Fourth, **not the writing’s failure**. The Sadducees’ error in Matthew 22 does not unmake Exodus 3. The wresting in 2 Peter 3:16 does not unmake Paul’s epistles or the other scriptures. The first distortion in Genesis 3 does not unmake the command that was given. Misreading leaves the object standing.

Thus:

> **Misreading, at this stage, is failed correspondence of a later statement of sense to the writing’s communication. It is not difficulty as such, not disagreement as such, not the writing’s collapse, and not yet the later files.**

# 67.8 Define the Five Modes Named by the Locked Assignment

The locked assignment does not name a mood. It names five operations. They must be defined before they are used as premises.

**Poor perception** is failure at the first intake of what is there: eyes that do not see what the writing presents, ears that do not hear what it says, attention that does not register the words, the relations, or the fact that a thing has been said. Mark 8:17–18 names the operation: “perceive ye not yet, neither understand? have ye your heart yet hardened? Having eyes, see ye not? and having ears, hear ye not? and do ye not remember?” Perception here is not a later mystical faculty. It is the first reception of what is present.

**Deficient understanding** is failure to grasp the sense of what has been perceived or could have been perceived. Words may be in hand. The sense is not. Matthew 22:29 names the operation as not knowing the scriptures. Acts 8:30 names it as a question: “Understandest thou what thou readest?” Understanding is not a second writing. It is reception of the writing’s sense.

**Faulty inference** is failure in the movement from what the writing says to a conclusion it does not warrant. The text used may be genuine. The step is invalid. Matthew 16:6–12 exhibits disciples concluding “bread” from “leaven.” Matthew 4:6 exhibits a citation of Psalm 91 used to warrant a leap the psalm does not command. Inference is already defined in Chapters 5–6. Faulty inference in this file is that operation performed upon Scripture.

**Context failure** is failure to keep a statement among its neighbours: speaker, audience, occasion, surrounding argument, wording, genre, and the other scriptures that bear upon the same subject. Isolation is the typical form. A sentence is treated as if it had no before and after, no speaker, and no corpus. Context is not a later academic fashion. It is the writing’s own location of its sentences.

**Improper handling** is the completed misuse of the writing as writing: wresting, corrupting, handling deceitfully, changing a literal communication into a second meaning the text does not give, treating figurative language as if it conveyed no literal truth, or using the writing to evacuate the writing. 2 Peter 3:16 names wresting. 2 Corinthians 2:17 and 4:2 name corrupting and deceitful handling. Improper handling may include the other four modes as its materials. It is named separately because it is the writing used against its own communication.

The five may occur together. A hardened heart may refuse to perceive, and therefore not understand, and therefore infer badly, and therefore isolate a sentence, and therefore wrest. Naming five is not claiming five sealed compartments. It is refusing to leave failure as one fog.

Thus:

> **The five modes are operations: intake, grasp, inference, location, and use. They are the catalog’s species. They are not yet systems, displacement, or the labour-files.**

# 67.9 Why Modes Matter: Failure Is Operational, Not Mystical

The catalog exists because failure is operational.

If misreading were a fate, the writings’ rebukes would be unintelligible. “Ye do err” names an act that should not have been done. “Perceive ye not yet” names an operation that should have been performed. “Understandest thou what thou readest?” names a question that can receive a yes. “Which they … wrest” names a handling. Fate does not wrest. An operation does.

If misreading were a mystery of an obscure book, the writings’ simplicity-claims and their hardness-claims would be collapsed into one fog. The Bible Introduction materials will be tested below as the proposition that the writing is given to be understood, and that the ordinary reasons it is not understood are willful operations, not a defect in the Giver. That proposition must be tested, not adopted as a slogan. It already names why modes matter: if the writing communicates, failure is on the receiving operations.

If misreading were only institutional, the catalog would already be Chapter 68. Persons misread. Organizations misread. The operation is the same in kind: a later act failing of a writing. The speaker’s size does not create a new mode. It may later create a system. That is a later question.

Modes matter because they can be examined. Perception can be asked: what is on the page? Understanding can be asked: what does that communicate? Inference can be asked: does this conclusion follow? Context can be asked: who speaks, to whom, in what argument, among which neighbours? Handling can be asked: is the writing being used as what it is, or used to evacuate what it is? Those questions are the reason this chapter is an investigation and not a lament.

Thus:

> **Modes matter because failure is an operation of the later act. The writing remains the object. The operations remain inspectable. Mystery is not the doctrine. Fate is not the doctrine.**

# 67.10 Positive Warrant: The Writing Communicates; Failure Is the Later Act

The first member of the positive warrant is communication already in force.

Because the God who cannot lie has made known, because all Scripture is God-breathed as writing, because inspiration’s guarantees attach to what the writing actually communicates, and because the Father’s word has been identified as truth, the Graphē is a communicating writing. A communicating writing can be received. It can also be failed of. Failure of a communicating writing is not the writing’s silence. It is the later act’s miss.

Chapter 66 already refused despair on this ground: if distinction meant that no later statement can correspond, the writing would have failed as communication. The present member uses the same ground from the other side: if the writing communicates, a later statement can fail of that communication, and the failure can be named. Communication is the reason both a correct reading and a misreading are intelligible.

The first member also locates blame without writing the later pathology-files. The writing is not an amulet that works by mere presence. The reconstructible Introduction proposition that the Bible is not a charm, but a revelation-record to be studied and practiced, coheres here as warrant: presence of a copy is not reception of the communication. Reception can fail. The copy remains a copy of a communicating writing.

Thus:

> **The first member is communication. The writing speaks as writing. Misreading is the later act’s failure of that speech, not the speech’s collapse into silence.**

The second member of the positive warrant is the writings’ own diagnoses.

The Graphē does not leave mishandling as an extra-biblical worry. It names error as not knowing the scriptures. It names perception refused. It names hearing without understanding. It names wresting. It names corrupting the word. It names handling the word deceitfully. It names making the word of God of none effect. It names a citation of what is written used to warrant what the written thing does not command. It names a first distortion that questions, adds, and reverses a given command. Those are not later chapter-titles imported into the text. They are the text’s own account of failed later acts.

The second member is why the catalog is not a theory imposed upon a silent Bible. Chapter 7 already established that the writings invite reason. They do not only supply material to be read. They diagnose the reading.

The diagnoses are modes, not yet systems. “Not knowing the scriptures” is deficient understanding. “See ye not” is poor perception. “They reasoned among themselves” toward a false conclusion is faulty inference. A citation cut from its neighbours is context failure. “Wrest” and “corrupt” and “deceitfully” are improper handling. The writings supply the species. The present file arranges them as the locked assignment requires.

Thus:

> **The second member is the writings’ own diagnoses. The catalog is read out of the record, not lowered onto it as a later scheme.**

The third member of the positive warrant is the remaining possibility of a correct reading.

A catalog of failure that implied the impossibility of success would be despair under another name. Chapter 66 already refused that despair. The present file must refuse it as a misuse of the catalog. The existence of poor perception does not abolish perception. The existence of wresting does not abolish a sense that can be violated. Nehemiah 8:8 already recorded sense given and understanding caused. Luke 24 already recorded understanding opened toward writings that already stood. Acts 17:11 already recorded a search whether those things were so. Acts 8 already recorded a reading that did not yet understand, and then a sense given from the same writing. Those sentences remain premises. They are the catalog’s necessary counterpart: modes of failure are intelligible because modes of reception are real.

The third member is also why the catalog is worth writing. If no correct reading can be had, examining how readings fail is theatre. If a correct reading can be had, examining how readings fail is labour toward the writing. Movement X will examine that labour as dedicated files. The present member only states what the catalog requires: failure is of a reachable object.

Thus:

> **The third member is possibility. The writing can be understood. Therefore it can be misread. The catalog does not abolish knowledge. It locates the operations by which knowledge of the writing is missed.**

# 67.13 Mode I — Poor Perception

Poor perception is the first mode because nothing later can be sound if the writing is not first received as what is there.

Perception, in this argument, is the intake of the presented thing: the words as written, the fact that a statement has been made, the visible relations on the page or in the hearing, the memory of what has already been shown. It is the operation Chapters 4–6 already required as the beginning of knowledge. Applied to Scripture, it is the beginning of knowing the scriptures.

Poor perception is not illiteracy as such. A man may be able to sound the words and still not see what they present. Poor perception is not the absence of a copy. The disciples in Mark 8 had been present for the breakings of bread. They had eyes. They had ears. The rebuke is that those organs were not in use toward what had been done and said. Poor perception is refused intake.

Poor perception may be dullness: the words pass and leave no mark. It may be selective: the eye catches the useful fragment and does not register the neighbour that governs it. It may be hardened: the heart has already decided what must not be seen. Mark 8 joins perception, understanding, hardness, seeing, hearing, and memory in one rebuke. The join is important. Hardness is named as a cause of not seeing. Not seeing is still the first failed operation.

The mode is already a misreading when the writing is not seen as the writing it is. A people may handle a volume and not perceive that a command was given, that a promise was made, that a speaker was Satan, that a question was asked, or that a resurrection was stated in “I am the God of Abraham.” The later modes will fail because the first intake failed.

Thus:

> **Poor perception is refused or neglected intake of what the writing presents. It is the first mode. Later grasp, inference, context, and handling cannot repair what was never seen.**

# 67.14 Biblical Record: Mark 8:17–18

Mark 8:14–21 records a crossing after the feedings. The disciples had forgotten to take bread. Jesus charged them: “Take heed, beware of the leaven of the Pharisees, and of the leaven of Herod.” They reasoned among themselves, saying, “It is because we have no bread.” He, knowing it, said:

> “Why reason ye, because ye have no bread? perceive ye not yet, neither understand? have ye your heart yet hardened? Having eyes, see ye not? and having ears, hear ye not? and do ye not remember?”

He then recalled the five thousand and the four thousand, the baskets taken up, and asked again, “How is it that ye do not understand?”

**Context.** The speaker is Jesus. The audience is disciples who have just seen two feedings. The occasion is a warning about leaven — teaching and corruption, as Matthew 16:12 will state when the same incident is understood. The surrounding argument is not a lesson about bakery. It is a lesson about a kind of influence, given to men who have already been shown that bread is not the limiting fact.

**What is explicit.** He names a failure to perceive, a failure to understand, a hardened heart, unused eyes, unused ears, and unused memory. He treats those as willful in the sense that the organs and the prior evidence were present. He treats their reasoning about bread as the wrong operation toward His sentence.

**What the passage warrants for this mode.** Perception is an operation that can be refused while eyes and ears remain. Memory of what the same speaker has already shown belongs to perception of His present sentence. A warning can be in the ear and not in the intake. The first mode is live among possessors, not only among outsiders.

**What the passage does not warrant here.** That disciples are *apeiros* as a finished diagnosis. That remaining labour-files have been written. That every later failure is hardness. Hardness is named in this scene. It is not the whole catalog.

Thus:

> **Mark 8:17–18 warrants poor perception as a named operation: eyes and ears present, intake refused, memory unused, a sentence in the ear and not received as what it is.**

# 67.15 Biblical Record: Matthew 13:13–15 and Isaiah 6:9–10

Matthew 13:13–15 gives Jesus’ reason for speaking to the multitude in parables:

> “Therefore speak I to them in parables: because they seeing see not; and hearing they hear not, neither do they understand.”

He then cites Isaiah 6:9–10: hearing but not understanding, seeing but not perceiving, a heart waxed gross, ears dull of hearing, eyes closed, lest they be converted and He heal them.

**Context.** The speaker is Jesus, after the parable of the sower, answering why He speaks as He does. The cited writing is Isaiah’s commission, where the prophet is sent to a people who will hear and not understand. The audience of Matthew 13 includes a multitude and, separately, disciples who ask and receive explanation. The same chapter records, “Who hath ears to hear, let him hear,” and the explanation that the wicked one catches away what was sown in the heart when a man “understandeth it not” (Matthew 13:19).

**What is explicit.** Seeing without seeing and hearing without hearing are named. Understanding is named as what does not occur. The heart, ears, and eyes are named as the organs of the failure. Conversion and healing are named as what the closed organs refuse.

**What the passage warrants for this mode.** Poor perception is not a modern psychological label laid on the text. It is the text’s own pairing of organs with refused intake. The pairing is moral and volitional in Isaiah’s wording: eyes closed, ears dull, heart gross. The reconstructible Introduction proposition that the reasons the Bible is not understood are willful operations finds correspondence here: the organs are not missing; they are unused toward the communication.

**What the passage does not warrant here.** That parables make the writing a book of remaining mysteries. Matthew 13 also records explanation given to those who ask. That dullness of hearing has been examined as Hebrews 5’s dedicated diagnosis. Neighbourhood is not arrival. That every later reader who finds a sentence hard has already closed his eyes in Isaiah’s sense. Hardness of a passage and closing of eyes are two.

Thus:

> **Matthew 13:13–15 and Isaiah 6:9–10 warrant poor perception as seeing without seeing and hearing without hearing — organs present, intake refused, understanding therefore not reached.**

# 67.16 Biblical Record: John 8:43

John 8:43: “Why do ye not understand my speech? even because ye cannot hear my word.”

**Context.** The speaker is Jesus, in dispute with hearers who claim Abraham as father and who have not believed. The surrounding argument includes “If ye continue in my word, then are ye my disciples indeed; and ye shall know the truth, and the truth shall make you free” (John 8:31–32), and the later charge that they are of their father the devil, a liar and the father of it (John 8:44). Speech and word are distinguished in the verse itself: λαλιά (*lalia*) as the uttered speech; λόγος (*logos*) as the word they cannot hear.

**What is explicit.** Failure to understand speech is traced to inability to hear the word. The inability is not presented as a missing lexicon. It is presented as a moral-spiritual refusal inside a dispute about truth, freedom, and fatherhood.

**What the passage warrants for this mode.** Understanding of uttered speech depends on hearing the word that the speech carries. A man may have the sounds and not have the hearing. Poor perception, at this depth, is not only unused eyes. It is unused hearing of the word. John 8:32 also warrants the counterpart already used as third member: truth known frees. The writing’s communication is not a prison of obscurity.

**What the passage does not warrant here.** That every grammatical difficulty is John 8:43. That the First Principles have been taught because “truth makes free” has been cited. That institutional aridity has been examined. The verse is mode-evidence: speech not understood because the word is not heard.

Thus:

> **John 8:43 warrants the first mode at the hearing of the word: speech can be in the air and the word not heard; understanding then fails because intake of the word failed.**

# 67.17 Mode II — Deficient Understanding

Deficient understanding is the second mode because a writing may be perceived as words and still not grasped as sense.

Understanding, in this argument, is the grasp of what the perceived writing communicates: the meaning of the words in their relations, the force of the statement, the thing asserted, commanded, promised, or asked. It is not a second inspiration. It is not authority over the text. Chapter 66 already established that understanding is reception, not rule. The present file examines the failure of that reception.

Deficient understanding is the operation Matthew 22:29 names as not knowing the scriptures. Possession is not the knowledge. The Sadducees handle Moses. They do not know what Moses communicates concerning the living God and therefore concerning the dead. Deficient understanding is also the operation Acts 8 names when a man reads Isaiah aloud and answers that he cannot understand except some man should guide him. The reading is real. The grasp is not yet real.

Deficient understanding may follow poor perception. It may also follow perception of words without comparison, without the rest of the subject, or without the speaker’s own later light on the same matter. Luke 24 records hearers who had the prophets and were slow of heart to believe what the prophets had spoken. The writings stood. The grasp had not occurred. He then, beginning at Moses and all the prophets, expounded unto them in all the scriptures the things concerning Himself. Exposition here is sense given toward writings that already communicated. It is not a second Graphē.

The reconstructible Introduction proposition that the Bible is self-interpreting in the sense that its so-called mysteries are explained by the writings themselves, and that passages generally have one simple meaning, belongs here as a claim to be tested, not as a charm. If the writings communicate, understanding is grasp of that communication, not invention of a second meaning. If a passage has a double meaning, the writings themselves make that fact clear. Deficient understanding includes treating a clear communication as if it had not been given, and treating a figurative communication as if no literal truth were intended.

Thus:

> **Deficient understanding is failure to grasp the sense of a writing that communicates. Words may be in hand. Knowledge of the scriptures is not thereby in hand.**

# 67.18 Biblical Record: Matthew 22:29 — Ye Do Err, Not Knowing the Scriptures

Matthew 22:23–33 records Sadducees, who say there is no resurrection, putting a case of seven brothers and one wife, and asking whose wife she shall be in the resurrection. Jesus answered:

> “Ye do err, not knowing the scriptures, nor the power of God.”

He then states that in the resurrection they neither marry nor are given in marriage, and, as touching the resurrection of the dead, appeals to what was spoken by God: “I am the God of Abraham, and the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob.” God is not the God of the dead, but of the living. Mark 12:24, 27 records the same rebuke and the same appeal, and adds, “ye therefore do greatly err.”

**Context.** The speaker is Jesus. The audience is a party that denies resurrection and uses Moses as a puzzle against it. The occasion is a public dispute. The cited writing is Exodus 3:6, in the bush, where God identifies Himself by the patriarchs. The surrounding argument is not a licence to invent a resurrection-doctrine from a mood. It is a reasoning from what is written: the identification “I am” of patriarchs who had died as to the body is unintelligible if they have perished as persons.

**What is explicit.** Error is named. The cause named is not knowing the scriptures, and not knowing the power of God. The scriptures they do not know are scriptures they handle. The correction is itself a use of what is written.

**What the passage warrants for this mode.** Deficient understanding is compatible with handling Moses. Possession of the writings is not knowledge of the writings. Error is the later act. The writings and the power of God are not the error. Chapter 66 already used this scene as exhibition that possessors can err. The present file uses it as the second mode’s primary warrant: the operation named is not-knowing, and the not-knowing is of scriptures already in hand.

**What the passage does not warrant here.** That every eschatological disagreement is this scene. That tradition as a system has been examined because Sadducees are a party. Party is the speaker. Mode is the not-knowing.

Thus:

> **Matthew 22:29 warrants deficient understanding as not knowing the scriptures while handling them. Error is named. The writings remain the measure and the correction.**

# 67.19 Biblical Record: Acts 8:30–35

Acts 8:27–35 records a man of Ethiopia, a eunuch of great authority, sitting in his chariot and reading Esaias the prophet. The Spirit said to Philip, Go near. Philip ran thither and heard him read, and said:

> “Understandest thou what thou readest?”

The eunuch said:

> “How can I, except some man should guide me?”

He had been reading Isaiah 53:7–8. He asked of whom the prophet spoke, of himself or of some other man. Philip opened his mouth, and began at the same scripture, and preached unto him Jesus.

**Context.** The reader is a man already possessing and reading the writing. The guide is not a second source of revelation beside the writing. He begins at the same scripture. The occasion is a reading of a prophetic passage whose subject the reader has not yet identified. The surrounding narrative includes the Spirit’s sending and, after the sense is given, baptism. Those later acts do not convert the scene into the First Principles or into a baptism-file. They locate the reading as a real reading that needed grasp.

**What is explicit.** Reading and understanding are distinguished. Guidance is requested as help toward the reading, not as replacement of the reading. The same scripture is the starting-point of the explanation. The question “of whom” is a question about the writing’s subject.

**What the passage warrants for this mode.** Deficient understanding can be honest. The eunuch is not named as a wrestler. He is named as a reader who does not yet grasp. The distinction between difficulty and wresting, already required, has a scene. Guidance that begins at the same scripture is interpretation in Chapter 66’s defined sense: accountable sense-giving toward a writing that remains the object. The scene also warrants the third member again: understanding can be given. The catalog of failure includes a failure that is not yet a crime, and that can be remedied by the writing’s own communication opened.

**What the passage does not warrant here.** That an office of guides is a second Graphē. That Movement X’s labour-files have been written because a guide appears. That every reader who asks for help is thereby *nēpios* as a finished diagnosis.

Thus:

> **Acts 8:30–35 warrants deficient understanding as reading without grasp, and warrants help as sense given from the same writing, not as a second writing.**

# 67.20 Biblical Record: Luke 24:25–27, 44–45

Luke 24:25–27 records the risen Christ saying to the two on the way:

And beginning at Moses and all the prophets, He expounded unto them in all the scriptures the things concerning Himself.

Then opened He their understanding, that they might understand the scriptures.

**Context.** The writings already stood. Moses, the prophets, and the psalms are named as the already-written field. The hearers are disciples who have the writings and the prior spoken words, and who are slow to believe what those writings had spoken. The operation He performs is exposition in the writings, and opening of understanding toward those writings.

**What is explicit.** Slowness of heart is named. Belief of all that the prophets have spoken is what was lacking. Exposition is in all the scriptures. Understanding is opened so that the scriptures might be understood. The writings are the object before and after the opening.

**What the passage warrants for this mode.** Deficient understanding may be slowness toward writings that already communicate the very thing not grasped. The deficiency is not the writings’ emptiness. The remedy is not a replacement writing. The remedy is understanding opened toward the same corpus. Chapter 66 already used this scene as two objects: writings that stand, understanding that may be opened. The present file uses it as mode-evidence: the failed operation is slowness to grasp what the prophets have spoken.

**What the passage does not warrant here.** That opening of understanding has been examined as a dedicated labour-stage. That “all the scriptures” has been rewritten as a second “all Scripture” file. Chapter 31 remains the “all” file. That every later exposition is thereby Christ. The scene warrants the mode and the possibility of grasp. It does not baptize later mouths.

Thus:

> **Luke 24 warrants deficient understanding as slowness toward writings that already speak, and warrants understanding as grasp of those same writings, not as their replacement.**

# 67.21 Mode III — Faulty Inference

Faulty inference is the third mode because a writing may be perceived and even partly understood, and a conclusion may still be drawn that the writing does not warrant.

Inference, in this argument, is the movement from premises to a conclusion. Chapters 5–6 already distinguished valid from invalid inference, deduction, induction, analogy, and the common failures: equivocation, non sequitur, false dichotomy, begging the question. Applied to Scripture, faulty inference is that movement performed so that a conclusion is attached to a text as if the text had given it, when the text has not given it.

Faulty inference is not the existence of inference. The writings themselves infer. Christ infers from “I am the God of Abraham” to the living. The Bereans search whether those things were so — a whether that is itself an inferential test. Faulty inference is the invalid step.

The typical forms in this file are few and sufficient.

**Wrong object.** A sentence about one thing is taken as a sentence about another. “Leaven” is taken as “bread.” “This temple” is taken as Herod’s stones when the speaker speaks of His body.

**Invalid step.** A genuine sentence is granted, and a command or doctrine is attached that does not follow. Psalm 91:11–12 is genuine. “Cast thyself down” does not follow as a duty.

**Over-extension.** A true particular is made a universal the text does not state, or a true universal is denied by a particular the text does not isolate as a cancellation.

**Under-extension.** A statement that governs a subject is restricted to a fragment that leaves the subject ungoverned.

**Speaker-confusion as inference.** A recorded lie is treated as a premise of truth because it is in the writing. Chapter 56 already required speaker-distinction as inspiration’s guarantee: inspiration records what was said; it does not baptize every speaker. Using Satan’s sentence as a divine command is a faulty inference from presence in the record to divine endorsement.

Thus:

> **Faulty inference is an invalid step from a writing, or from a fragment of a writing, to a conclusion the communication does not warrant. The text used may be genuine. The step is still a misreading.**

# 67.22 Biblical Record: Matthew 16:6–12

Matthew 16:5–12 records the same crossing-warning as Mark 8, with the conclusion stated. Jesus said, “Take heed and beware of the leaven of the Pharisees and of the Sadducees.” They reasoned among themselves, saying, “It is because we have taken no bread.” He rebukes their little faith and their failure to remember the five thousand and the four thousand, and asks how they do not understand that He spake not of bread. Then they understood how that He bade them not beware of the leaven of bread, but of the doctrine of the Pharisees and of the Sadducees.

**Context.** The speaker’s sentence is a warning about teaching. The disciples’ inference is about provisions. The surrounding argument includes prior miracles that already answered the provision-worry. The correction is not a new writing. It is the same sentence understood.

**What is explicit.** They reasoned. The reasoning is named as the wrong conclusion. Understanding later consists in grasping that the warning was about doctrine, not bread.

**What the passage warrants for this mode.** A genuine sentence can be in the ear, and the inference drawn from it can be false. “They reasoned among themselves” is not already sound judgment. Chapter 6 already required that reasoning be examined as reasoning. The present scene is that requirement applied to a sentence of Christ. Faulty inference here is not wresting as a completed corruption. It is a miss of the referent. The miss is still a misreading of the sentence they heard.

**What the passage does not warrant here.** That every figurative word has been catalogued. That doctrine as a system-file has been written because “doctrine of the Pharisees” appears. The word here is teaching as leaven, used as the correct referent. The system-file remains later.

Thus:

> **Matthew 16:6–12 warrants faulty inference as reasoning from a genuine sentence to the wrong object. Understanding is the sentence grasped. The false step is the misreading.**

# 67.23 Biblical Record: John 2:19–22

John 2:19–22 records Jesus answering the Jews who demanded a sign: “Destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise it up.” Then said the Jews, “Forty and six years was this temple in building, and wilt thou rear it up in three days?” But He spake of the temple of His body. When therefore He was risen from the dead, His disciples remembered that He had said this unto them; and they believed the scripture, and the word which Jesus had said.

**Context.** The speaker is Jesus, in the temple, after the cleansing. The audience takes “temple” as the building in which they stand. The narrator states the intended referent: His body. The later remembrance of disciples joins “the scripture” and “the word which Jesus had said.”

**What is explicit.** A genuine sentence is heard. A conclusion about Herod’s stones is drawn. That conclusion is not what He spake. The body is what He spake. Later belief includes both scripture and His word, remembered after the resurrection.

**What the passage warrants for this mode.** Faulty inference may be a wrong referent attached to a true sentence. The sentence is not emptied by being misunderstood. The disciples’ later remembrance is not a licence to treat all later readings as equally delayed. It is evidence that a sentence can stand, be misread as to referent, and later be grasped as what it was.

**What the passage does not warrant here.** That every temple-saying in Scripture is this saying. That figurative language has no literal truth: the literal truth here is His body raised. The figure is the word “temple” used of that body. The reconstructible Introduction rule — when language is figurative, get the literal truth conveyed — has a scene.

Thus:

> **John 2:19–22 warrants faulty inference as a wrong referent attached to a genuine sentence, and warrants the sentence’s standing through the miss. The literal truth intended is not the hearers’ building-inference.**

# 67.24 Biblical Record: Matthew 4:5–7 — A True Writing Used for a False Step

Matthew 4:5–7 records the devil taking Jesus into the holy city, setting Him on a pinnacle of the temple, and saying:

> “If thou be the Son of God, cast thyself down: for it is written, He shall give his angels charge concerning thee: and in their hands they shall bear thee up, lest at any time thou dash thy foot against a stone.”

Jesus said unto him, “It is written again, Thou shalt not tempt the Lord thy God.”

The cited writing is Psalm 91:11–12. The answering writing is Deuteronomy 6:16.

**Context.** The speaker of the citation is the tempter. The audience is the Son. The occasion is temptation after the forty days. The cited psalm promises angelic charge in the ways of trust, not a command to manufacture a fall in order to force a proof. The answering sentence forbids tempting the Lord. Luke 4:9–12 records the same exchange.

**What is explicit.** “It is written” can be in a later mouth that is not thereby the writing. A genuine psalm is cited. A command to cast Himself down is attached. The Son answers by another writing, not by denying that the psalm is written.

**What the passage warrants for this mode.** Faulty inference may be performed with a true citation. The invalid step is the movement from angelic charge to a duty to leap. Context failure is already neighbouring: the psalm is not a licence to tempt God. Improper handling is already neighbouring: the writing is used to urge what the writings forbid. The scene is placed under inference because the live operation exhibited is the false step from a cited sentence to a commanded act. The other modes are present as materials. The inference is the step that must be refused.

The scene also warrants the already-earned distinction of quotation from the writing. Chapter 66 established that quotation is not the Graphē. The present file uses that conclusion as mode-evidence: the tempter quotes and misreads. Citation is not correctness.

**What the passage does not warrant here.** That every later citation is satanic. That Psalm 91 has been emptied of promise. The promise stands. The step fails. That spiritual warfare has been written as a system. The mode is the false step.

Thus:

> **Matthew 4:5–7 warrants faulty inference from a genuine writing to an unwarranted act. “It is written” in a later mouth is not, by itself, a true use. Another writing judges the step.**

# 67.25 Mode IV — Context Failure

Context failure is the fourth mode because a sentence of a communicating writing has a location, and isolation treats it as if it had none.

**Context**, in this argument, is the writing’s own placement of a statement among the things that govern its sense: the speaker, the audience, the occasion, the surrounding argument, the wording and grammar, the literary form, the historical setting where the writing itself requires it, and the other scriptures that bear upon the same subject. The biblical-context protocol of the governing methodology is this list. It is not a later academic fashion imposed on a book that has no neighbours.

Context failure is the operation of refusing that placement. The typical form is the isolated sentence: a clause is lifted, used, and made to govern a subject its neighbours do not permit it to govern, or made to mean what its speaker does not say. Other forms include ignoring who is speaking, ignoring to whom it is spoken, ignoring the occasion, ignoring the argument in which the sentence is a step, and ignoring the rest of the corpus on the same matter.

Context failure is already a misreading when the isolated sentence is true as a sentence and false as used. Psalm 91:11–12 is true in the psalm. Isolated as a command to leap, it is a failed use. “Destroy this temple” is true as spoken of His body. Isolated as a claim about Herod’s stones, it is a failed use. “I am the God of Abraham” is true in Exodus 3. Isolated from the force of “I am” toward the patriarchs, it is a sentence the Sadducees can possess without knowing.

The reconstructible Introduction rule that all scriptures on a subject must be harmonized, and that a doctrine out of harmony with any one written statement is false, belongs here as a tested proposition about context in the wide sense: the corpus is a neighbour of the sentence. The law of two or three witnesses, stated in Deuteronomy 19:15 and used of testimony in Matthew 18:16 and 2 Corinthians 13:1, will be tested below as a related discipline: a later doctrine is not established by one isolated usefulness. Those propositions are not yet the labour-file of rightly dividing. They are the reason isolation is a mode of misreading.

Thus:

> **Context failure is refusal of the writing’s own placement of a sentence. Isolation is the typical form. A true clause, cut from its neighbours, becomes a false use.**

# 67.26 Biblical Record: Genesis 3:1–5 — The First Distortion of What Was Said

Genesis 2:16–17 records the command: of every tree of the garden the man may freely eat; of the tree of the knowledge of good and evil, he shall not eat; in the day he eats, he shall surely die. Genesis 3:1–5 records the serpent’s questions and claims:

> “Yea, hath God said, Ye shall not eat of every tree of the garden?”

The woman answers with a narrowed permission, an added “neither shall ye touch it,” and a softened “lest ye die.” The serpent answers, “Ye shall not surely die,” and attaches a purpose: God knows that in the day ye eat, your eyes shall be opened, and ye shall be as gods, knowing good and evil.

**Context.** The speaker of the command is God. The speaker of the question and the denial is the serpent. The audience is the woman, with the man. The occasion is the first recorded distortion of a given word. The surrounding argument of the book is not a parable of reading-theory. It is a historical account of a command, a distortion, and a fall. The present file uses the scene as the record’s first exhibition of a later mouth handling a given word by questioning, adding, softening, and reversing.

**What is explicit.** The command was given with a permission, a prohibition, and a penalty. The later handling questions whether God has said, alters the scope, adds a clause, weakens the penalty, then denies the penalty and assigns a rival motive to God.

**What the passage warrants for this mode.** Context failure begins when the given wording is no longer the measure of the later sentence about it. “Hath God said” is already a move to detach the later discussion from the given wording. Addition and subtraction are already mishandling of neighbours: “every tree” and “surely die” were neighbours of the prohibition. The scene also warrants improper handling and faulty inference as materials: the denial is a false conclusion about the command; the handling is a use of the field of God’s word to evacuate God’s word. It is placed under context because the first operation exhibited is the displacement of the given wording by a later version of the wording.

**What the passage does not warrant here.** That every later question about a text is the serpent’s question. Isaiah 1:18 remains an invitation to reason. Testing a sermon by the writings remains noble. The serpent’s question is not a search whether those things were so. It is a loosening of whether God has said what God has said. That later systems of temptation have been examined. The mode is the distortion of a given word.

Thus:

> **Genesis 3:1–5 warrants context failure as the first distortion of a given word: questioning the wording, adding, softening, reversing. The given command remains the measure of the later version.**

# 67.27 The Isolated Sentence and the Missing Neighbours

Isolation is the ordinary form of context failure in later use.

A sentence is isolated when it is made to do work its neighbours forbid, or to refrain from work its neighbours require. The work may be a doctrine, a permission, a threat, a promise, or a command. The isolation may be of a clause, a verse, a word, or a scene. The form does not matter. The missing neighbours do.

Neighbours include the immediate sentences. Psalm 91’s “in all thy ways” and the psalm’s character as trust, not as a dare, are neighbours of the angelic-charge sentences. Neighbours include the speaker. “Ye shall not surely die” is a neighbour of the command only as a contrary later mouth, not as a second divine sentence. Neighbours include the rest of the subject. A sentence about faith that is cut from the sentences about obedience, or a sentence about grace that is cut from the sentences about sin, is already a context failure even when each isolated clause is true as a clause.

The reconstructible Introduction proposition that the Bible is not a book of systematic discourses on any one subject, but that one must collect, from here and there, all God’s information through various writers in order to know the whole truth, belongs here as a tested claim about neighbours in the wide sense. If the proposition is sound, isolation of one writer’s sentence from the rest of the subject is a mode of missing the whole truth the writings give. The present file does not convert that proposition into a finished topical method. It uses it as warrant against isolation: a communicating corpus is not used as a communicating corpus when one useful clause is made to silence the rest.

Isolation often presents itself as reverence: “the verse says.” The verse may say. The use may still be a miss of what the writing says. “The verse says” is perception of a clause. Context is perception of the writing.

Thus:

> **Isolation is context failure in ordinary form. A true clause without its neighbours is already a false use when the neighbours govern the clause’s force.**

# 67.28 Speaker, Audience, Occasion, and Scope

Four particular neighbours must be named because their absence is a common form of the fourth mode.

**Speaker.** Inspiration records God, Christ, prophets, apostles, holy men, and also Satan, demons, and men speaking lies or errors. Chapter 56 already established that inspiration guarantees the accuracy of the record of what was said, not the truth of every recorded claim. To treat a recorded lie as a divine premise is context failure at the speaker. To treat a recorded human question as a revealed assertion is the same failure in a milder form.

**Audience.** A command given to a particular man, people, or office is not, by being written, already a command of the same force to every later reader without the writing’s own extension. Scope is part of sense. The present file does not write the later labour of dividing times and classes as a finished file. It names the failure: a sentence spoken to one audience, used as if audience were nothing, is already a miss of the writing’s placement.

**Occasion.** A sentence answering a trap, a grief, a request, or a controversy has that occasion as a neighbour. Matthew 22:29 is an answer to a resurrection-denying puzzle. To use “ye do err” as a charm against every opponent, without the scriptures and the power of God as the named cause, is already to isolate the rebuke from its occasion.

**Scope.** Universal statements and particular statements are not interchangeable. “All Scripture” is a scope already controlled in Chapter 31. A particular narrative is a particular narrative. Context failure includes making the particular do the work of the universal, and making the universal collapse into an anecdote.

The reconstructible Introduction rule that truth must be divided with regard for times, classes, and subjects is named here only as coherence with scope. The dedicated file of rightly dividing remains later. The present point is already full: ignoring speaker, audience, occasion, or scope is already a misreading.

Thus:

> **Speaker, audience, occasion, and scope are neighbours. Treating them as nothing is context failure. Naming them is not yet the labour-file of rightly dividing.**

# 67.29 Mode V — Improper Handling

Improper handling is the fifth mode because a writing may be perceived, partly understood, inferred from, and even placed among some neighbours, and still be used so as to evacuate what it communicates.

**Handling**, in this argument, is the use of the writing as writing: citing it, teaching it, applying it, arguing from it, translating its force into a later claim or a later life. Chapter 66 already established that such use is a later act. The present file examines the use that fails as use.

Improper handling includes, as the writings name it:

- **Wresting** — twisting the writing’s force so that the handler is destroyed by the handling (2 Peter 3:16).
- **Corrupting** — treating the word as merchandise or as material to be diluted (2 Corinthians 2:17).
- **Deceitful handling** — using the word as a cover for a hidden claim (2 Corinthians 4:2).
- **Making the word of none effect** — occupying the field of the word while laying the word aside (Mark 7:13).
- **Changing a communicated sense into a second sense the writing does not give** — spiritualizing a historical statement into a denial of the history, or literalizing a figure so as to miss the literal truth the figure conveys.
- **Using the writing to further a later interest** — the writing as instrument of the handler rather than as measure of the handler.

Improper handling is the completed form toward which the other modes tend. Poor perception supplies unused words. Deficient understanding supplies unused sense. Faulty inference supplies a false step. Context failure supplies an isolated clause. Improper handling is those materials used as if they were the writing’s force.

The reconstructible Introduction rule — take the Bible literally wherein it is at all possible; if symbolic, figurative, or typical language is used, then look for the literal truth it intends to convey; never change the literal meaning to a spiritual, mystical, symbolic, or figurative meaning unless the writing itself does so — belongs here as a tested discipline of handling. The rule is not a denial that figures exist. The writings contain parables, symbols, types, and figures. The rule is a refusal to treat “the writing says this” as “the writing means the opposite,” and a refusal to treat a figure as a licence to evacuate history. Novel interpretations supported by personal opinion alone are, in those same materials, to be laid aside. That refusal is handling-discipline, not a system-file.

Thus:

> **Improper handling is the writing used against its own communication — twisted, diluted, made a cover, laid aside while occupied, or exchanged for a second meaning the text does not give. The other modes are often its materials.**

# 67.33 Literal Sense, Figurative Language, and the Invented Second Meaning

A live form of improper handling, neighbouring context failure and faulty inference, is the exchange of a communicated sense for a second sense the writing does not give.

Two contrary abuses must be refused together.

**Spiritualizing against the communication.** A historical statement, a command, a promise, or a prophecy is treated as if the writing meant something else, usually something that leaves the stated thing unasserted. The reconstructible Introduction rule refuses this exchange except where the writing itself uses symbolic, figurative, or typical language. The rule is sound as handling-discipline if the writing is a communicating writing and not a book that says one thing and means another. The same materials state that generally the passages have one simple meaning, and that in the few which have a double meaning the fact is clear from the verses or from parallel passages. That claim is tested as coherence with a communicating corpus, not as a charm that no figure exists.

**Literalizing a figure so as to miss the literal truth the figure conveys.** Parables, symbols, types, and figures are in the writings. “Leaven” in Matthew 16 is a figure whose literal truth is doctrine. “Temple” in John 2:19 is a figure whose literal truth is His body. To insist on Herod’s stones is not fidelity to the letter. It is a miss of the communication. The Introduction rule’s second clause is this refusal: when the language cannot be taken literally, get the literal truth conveyed by the figurative language.

The two abuses share one operation: the handler, not the writing, becomes the giver of the sense. Novel interpretation supported by personal opinion alone is that operation named as a method. The present file refuses the method as improper handling. It does not write a complete hermeneutical manual. Movement X remains that labour.

A related form is the claim that the Bible is a book of remaining mysteries, so that a later decoder is required before any sense can be had. The reconstructible Introduction proposition that the Bible explains its so-called mysteries and is so self-interpreting that no mystery remains as an unintelligible residue must be tested, not shouted. What it warrants here is limited and full: the writing is not a sealed charm; later decoding is not a second Graphē; hardness of some things (2 Peter 3:16) is not a remaining unintelligible residue that baptizes every later invention. What it does not warrant here is that every reader already understands every sentence, or that Hebrews 5’s milk and meat have been examined.

Thus:

> **Improper handling includes exchanging the communicated sense for a second sense the writing does not give — whether by spiritualizing away what is said, or by missing the literal truth a figure conveys. Opinion is not the court. The writing is the court.**

# 67.34 Who Is Speaking: Inspiration Records Without Baptizing Every Speaker

Speaker-distinction, already a guarantee of inspiration in Chapter 56, is now a rule against a live mishandling.

The writings record the language of God, of Christ, of the Spirit, of angels, of prophets, of apostles, and also of Satan, of demons, and of men. Inspiration guarantees that what is recorded as said was said as recorded. It does not place all that is said on the same level of truth required for obedience. A recorded lie remains a lie. A recorded fallacy remains a fallacy. A recorded crime remains a crime. The record of those things is true as record. The things recorded are not thereby true as doctrine.

Misreading at this point is a faulty inference from presence in the Graphē to divine endorsement, and it is an improper handling of the record as if every sentence were a premise of the same kind. Genesis 3:4 is in the writing. “Ye shall not surely die” is not therefore a promise of God. Matthew 4:6 is in the writing. The tempter’s use of Psalm 91 is not therefore a command of God. Job’s friends speak many sentences. The end of the book judges their counsel. The Sadducees’ puzzle is in the writing. The puzzle is not the doctrine of the resurrection.

The rule is not cynicism about the record. It is fidelity to the record. A writing that accurately records a lie has told the truth about the lie. A reader who obeys the lie because it is printed has not become more biblical. He has misread the record’s speaker.

Thus:

> **Speaker-distinction is a handling-rule. Presence in the writing is not endorsement of every speaker. The record of a lie is true as record. The lie is not truth.**

# 67.35 Comparing Scripture with Scripture: The Two-or-Three Witness Rule

A further handling-discipline belongs to the fifth mode as the refusal of its ordinary material: the single useful clause.

Deuteronomy 19:15 requires that a matter be established at the mouth of two or three witnesses. Matthew 18:16 uses the same requirement of testimony. 2 Corinthians 13:1 uses it of a word established. The reconstructible Introduction proposition that this states a law of interpreting Scripture — every doctrine proved by at least two or three scriptures before it is accepted as binding — is a later methodological claim. It must be tested, not adopted as if 2 Corinthians 13:1 had already written a hermeneutics manual.

**What the claim means.** A later doctrine that hangs on one isolated usefulness, against the rest of the subject, has not been established as the writings’ communication of that subject.

**What the biblical sentences establish.** Testimony is not established by one witness in the judicial scenes those verses govern. A “word” in 2 Corinthians 13:1 is established in the mouth of two or three witnesses. The analogical step from judicial testimony to doctrinal proof is an inference. It is a sound inference so far as it refuses isolation: a communicating corpus on a subject is not mastered by one clause used against the neighbours. It is an unsound inference if it is treated as a ban on receiving a single clear sentence as a clear sentence. “I am the God of Abraham” is one citation in Matthew 22, and it is decisive as used with the rest of what the scriptures and the power of God require. The Son’s “It is written again” in Matthew 4 is the second writing judging the isolated use of the first.

**What follows for mishandling.** A doctrine out of harmony with any one written statement is already false as a claim to be the writings’ doctrine. “As it is written” remains a guide: any later claim that cannot sit with what is written is already a failed handling. The reconstructible Introduction note that “as it is written” is one of the safest guides to truth and fallacy coheres here: harmony with the written thing is the test; novelty against the written thing is the failure.

**What does not follow here.** That Movement X’s discipline of interpretation has been written. That a concordance-method has been imposed as a second Graphē. That two citations, stacked without sense, become knowledge. Quotation of two clauses can still be wresting. Number of citations is not yet understanding.

Thus:

> **Comparison is a refusal of isolation. A later doctrine that silences neighbours has already misread. One clear sentence remains a clear sentence. A stack of citations is not, by stacking, a true handling.**

# 67.36 “As It Is Written” as Guide, Not as a Ban on Sense

The reconstructible Introduction claim that Christ never once gave an interpretation of what was written, or left one to believe that any part of it needed interpretation, and that His application of what was written to certain events was sufficient as to the meaning, must be tested with the same care Chapter 66 already required.

**What can be received.** He treated what was written as already having meaning. He did not treat the writing as empty until a later interpreter filled it. He applied “it is written” as court. He did not grant that the writing says one thing and means another until a school supplies the second meaning. Those sentences cohere with John 10, with Matthew 4, with Matthew 22, and with Luke 24’s exposition in the scriptures.

**What cannot be received as a ban.** Nehemiah 8:8 records giving the sense. Luke 24 records expounding and opening understanding. Acts 8 records beginning at the same scripture and preaching Jesus. Chapter 66 already refused to treat 2 Peter 1:20 as a ban on stating sense. The present file refuses to treat “Christ applied rather than replaced” as “no later mouth may state sense.” Application is sense exhibited. Exposition is sense given. Both sit under the writing.

The guide, then, is this: “as it is written” tests later speech by the written thing. It does not forbid later speech that states what the written thing communicates. Misreading includes treating “as it is written” as a charm that makes any cited clause true as used, and treating “interpretation” as a crime so that the writing is left unused as communication.

Thus:

> **“As it is written” is a guide of later speech by the writing. It is not a baptism of every citation, and it is not a ban on stating the sense the writing communicates.**

# 67.42 Competing Claim: The Bible Is Inherently Obscure, Therefore Misreading Is the Text’s Fault

That exchange is the first abuse already named: spiritualizing against the communication.

The writings refuse the exchange as a method. Luke 24 treats Moses, the prophets, and the psalms as writings that already spoke of Christ’s sufferings and glory — not as a code that leaves the sufferings unasserted. Matthew 22 treats Exodus 3 as a statement whose force toward living patriarchs stands. Mark 7 treats “Honour thy father and thy mother” as a command that still commands. John 10 treats “I said, Ye are gods” as wording that still words. A method that would have those sentences mean something else that leaves the stated thing unasserted has already misread them.

Christ’s use of figures does not convert the corpus into a figure. He distinguishes parable and explanation (Matthew 13). He distinguishes “this temple” as body from the hearers’ building-inference (John 2). He reasons from the letter of Psalm 82 (Chapters 59–61, used not retried). Figure is a form the writings use. Figure is not a licence to evacuate.

The reduction often presents itself as depth. Depth that will not sit under what is written is not depth. It is a later invention using the writing as decoration. Analogical religiousism, already tested, is this reduction’s popular form.

Thus:

> **“Spiritualizing is the higher method” fails. Figures are granted. Evacuation of communicated fact, command, promise, or history is refused. Figure is a form, not a licence.**

# 67.45 Competing Claim: One Useful Verse Suffices

The reduction says:

The reduction fails.

A clear sentence remains a clear sentence. The reduction’s error is not that one sentence can communicate. Matthew 22’s citation is one sentence, decisive as used. The error is usefulness as the test, and isolation as the method. Usefulness is not correspondence. A clause can serve a later interest and fail of the writing. 2 Corinthians 2:17 already named that use. Matthew 4 already exhibited a useful citation in a later mouth.

The writings refuse usefulness as court. Isaiah 8:20 sends a people to the law and to the testimony: if they speak not according to this word, there is no light in them. The test is according to the word, not according to service of a later aim. Galatians 1:8–9 measures a later gospel by the deposit already received, not by the later gospel’s usefulness. Mark 7’s Corban was useful to a later device. It made the word of none effect.

Comparison, already tested, refuses the method without becoming a stack-of-citations charm. Neighbours govern force. A doctrine out of harmony with any one written statement is already false as a claim to be the writings’ doctrine. “As it is written” remains the guide.

Thus:

> **“One useful verse suffices” fails. A clear sentence can communicate. Usefulness is not the court. Isolation is already context failure. A later interest is already improper handling when it governs the clause.**

# 67.49 The Warranted Conclusion Stated with Full Force

The conclusion may now be stated without postponement and without inflation.

Scripture is misread when the later act of stating the Graphē’s sense fails to correspond to what that writing actually communicates. That failure is not a fate, not a mystery of a sealed book, and not a single fog called “error.” It is a set of inspectable operations.

**Poor perception** refuses or neglects the intake of what the writing presents: eyes that do not see, ears that do not hear, memory that does not serve, a word that is not heard.

**Deficient understanding** fails to grasp the sense of a writing that communicates: words in hand, the scriptures not known; a reading without grasp; slowness toward writings that already speak.

**Faulty inference** draws from a genuine sentence a conclusion the communication does not warrant: the wrong object, the wrong referent, an invalid step from a true citation.

**Context failure** refuses the writing’s own placement of a sentence among its neighbours: isolation; distortion of given wording; ignoring speaker, audience, occasion, or scope.

**Improper handling** uses the writing against its own communication: wresting, corrupting, deceitful use, making the word of none effect, exchanging the communicated sense for a second sense the writing does not give, or using the writing to further a later interest.

The writing remains the measure of those operations. Wresting does not unmake standing. Isolation does not rewrite neighbours. A sincere miss remains a miss. A useful clause remains a clause. A figure remains a figure that conveys a literal truth. A recorded lie remains a recorded lie.

A correct reading remains possible because the writing communicates. An incorrect reading remains incorrect because the writing communicates. Transfer remains invalid. Despair remains invalid. The catalog does not baptize the reader. The catalog does not abolish knowledge.

This continues Movement IX. It is not Chapter 66 retried. It is not Chapters 68–73. It is not Movement X. That is this chapter’s conclusion, stated here with full force.

Thus:

> **How Scripture is misread has been examined as five operations of a later act toward a standing, communicating writing. The catalog is in force. Later questions remain later because they are different questions.**
