## The Doctrine of the Graphē

# Chapter 71 — Accepted Heresies

> **Movement IX — When Authoritative Truth Is Mishandled**

## Central Objective

> **To examine, from the biblical record and from premises already in force, how erroneous doctrines become normalized or institutionalized, and why institutional acceptance does not transform error into truth, so that Movement IX’s sixth word is one inspectable verdict: an accepted heresy is an erroneous doctrine that a body has received as its ordinary teaching or installed in the forms by which it orders belief and life; the writings exhibit the operations of that reception; and the reception — by multitude, by fathers, by office, by chapel, by penalty, by age, by a sign that comes to pass, by a flattering name, or by the word “doctrine” itself — does not make the error conform to reality. The truth the writing has given remains the measure of the settled teaching, as it remained the measure of the turning. A remnant, a contrary word, and a book found in the house remain real refusals of the accepted state. The account keeps this settlement distinct from a second narrative of turning, from the loss of reasonability, from unskillfulness as a dedicated diagnosis, and from the labour-files. It refuses the reductions that treat consensus, antiquity, office, survival, or religious show as creators of truth; that treat the truth’s survival as the acceptance’s harmlessness; that treat every sect-name as an accepted heresy or every minority as fidelity; that treat Chapters 68 and 69 as already this file; and that treat this file as already Chapter 70, Chapters 72–73, or Movement X. It refuses both under-claim and over-claim.**

The governing movement is:

> **Turning aside already stated → question of accepted heresies → acceptance defined → normalization and institutionalization distinguished → biblical words collected → objects distinguished → premises already in force used, not retried → one clean account of sequence and limits → positive warrant from the non-transforming character of reception, from the writings’ exhibition of settlement, and from the real possibility of refusal → each exhibition examined with biblical record → operations of acceptance joined → non-transformation stated from the exhibitions → reconstructible Introduction and Aletheia propositions tested as coherence → competing reductions refused → inspectable inference → warranted conclusion → sequence to aridity.**

The chapter’s conclusion is affirmative:

> **An accepted heresy is an erroneous doctrine that has become the ordinary teaching of a body, or that has been installed in that body’s places, priesthood, calendar, official mouth, school, penalty, or suffered teaching office, so that the error is now what the body holds, teaches, commands, or will not depart from. The writings exhibit how that settlement happens: by installation that imitates an authorized feast; by a court that speaks with one mouth; by appeal to the king’s chapel; by a people who love to have it so; by a claim to possess the law while the word is rejected; by the manner of the fathers; by teaching the commandments of men as doctrines and teaching men to break the commandment; by the named doctrine of a school; by an agreement to expel confessors; by an assembly’s command; by words that travel under a sending the senders did not give; by the carrying-away of respected men into one public walk; by the following of many and the slander of the way of truth; by a church that holds a doctrine and suffers a teacher; by ordinances that have a show of wisdom; by prohibitions taught as the rule; by a letter that wears an apostolic name; by a common report the body glories in. Institutional acceptance does not transform error into truth. A multitude is forbidden as a measure. A sign that comes to pass does not license the other gods. A sin successors will not depart from remains a sin. A chapel does not make a cult the Lord’s house. Fathers who did not hearken are the ground of wrath, not of legitimacy. Vain worship is worship that teaches human commandments as doctrines. The way of truth remains the way of truth while many follow the heresies and speak evil of the way. Christ hates the doctrine the church suffers. The creature remains good while a doctrine forbids it. What a hostile body calls heresy may be the way that believes all that is written, and what a body calls the faith may be the error. Names do not settle the case. Truth remains conformity to reality. The Graphē remains the primary written authority. The Scripture is not broken by a kingdom’s continuance in a sin, by a council’s command, or by a church’s toleration. Refusal remains possible: the multitude may be disobeyed; the book found in the house judges the fathers’ way; one prophet may speak what the Lord says against four hundred; the rest in a church may not have the doctrine; God is to be obeyed rather than men. This continues Movement IX. It is not Chapter 70 retried. It is not Chapters 72–73. It is not Movement X. That is this chapter’s conclusion, stated here with full force.**

The remaining questions follow by sequence. The verdict is already in force as a warranted account of accepted heresies, not as postponed guesswork.

# 71.3 Define the Question: Accepted Heresies

**Accepted heresy**, in this chapter, is an erroneous doctrine that a body has received as its ordinary teaching, or has installed in the forms by which it orders what is believed, taught, and enforced, so that the error presents itself as the doctrine, the manner, the faith, or the rule.

The English phrase is not a biblical hapax to be imported as a new revelation. It is the locked assignment’s name for a settlement the writings already exhibit. “Heresy” in later controversy often means a chosen doctrine formally contrary to a defined rule of a communion, held with persistence. That later technical sense is not the premise of this chapter, and it is not silently installed as the meaning of every Greek line below. The argumentative force of the assignment is narrower and prior: an error in doctrine has become what a body treats as normal, or what a body has built into its public order, and the question is how that happens and why the happening does not make the error true.

The phrase is easily abused. In ordinary speech “accepted” may be heard as any teaching a Christian may hold without suspicion, so that every received truth is already on trial as a heresy. “Heresy” may be heard as a sneer at every party one does not belong to, so that the opponent’s name is already the verdict. “Institutional” may be heard as a sneer at every ordered body, so that a church, a school, or a household is guilty by existing. None of those senses is this chapter’s object.

Four elements belong to the definition.

First, **an erroneous doctrine**. The object is a teaching, a διδαχή (*didachē*, G1322), a commandment taught as doctrine, a required account of what saves, a prohibition taught as the faith, a sentence about God and his worship that does not conform to the reality the writing has given. A custom with no doctrinal claim is not yet this object. A moral disorder the body also fails to mourn can exhibit the same structure of normalization, and one exhibition below uses that structure. The assigned object remains the doctrine. Error has already been distinguished from truth. This chapter does not re-open that distinction. It asks what happens when the error is the thing a body now teaches.

Second, **received by a body as ordinary, or installed in the body’s order**. Normalization is the error’s becoming the usual teaching: the way of the people, the manner of the fathers, the path many follow, the doctrine held, the thing loved, the report no longer mourned. Institutionalization is the error’s installation in the forms of a public order: a place of worship, a priesthood, a calendar, an official mouth, a school’s doctrine, a penalty of expulsion, a command of an assembly, a teaching office the church suffers to continue. The two are modes of one acceptance. A doctrine can be normal in a people’s love before it has a chapel. A doctrine can be installed in a chapel while some in the assembly have not received it. The chapter does not require both modes in every case. It does require that the error have become the body’s teaching or the body’s rule, and not merely a motion in one hearer that Chapter 70 already judged.

Third, **the reception offered as though it settled truth**. The theological force of the file is not only that bodies adopt errors. It is that the adoption is then treated as the reason the doctrine should be believed: because the people went, because the prophets speak with one mouth, because this is the king’s chapel, because our fathers walked in it, because we did not depart from it, because the synagogue has agreed, because she calls herself a prophetess, because we are wise and the law is with us, because many follow. The assignment’s second clause is the refusal of that offer. Acceptance is a historical and social fact. It is not an ontological act.

Fourth, **not those other claims**. The definition excludes, as not this object: a retry of Chapter 70’s causes and mechanisms of turning; aridity as the loss of reasonability; *apeiros* as unskillfulness in the Word; Movement X’s labour-files; the First Principles as this file’s achievement; a history of post-apostolic parties as the premise; a polity of councils as the cure. Those exclusions are part of the definition. They are not a later apology.

Thus:

> **The question of this chapter is not what causes a turning, and not yet how reasonability is lost or how skill is gained. Those questions have been examined or remain later. The question is how an erroneous doctrine becomes the ordinary or official teaching of a body, and why that body’s acceptance does not make the error true.**

# 71.4 Normalized and Institutionalized

The assignment names two operations. They are distinguished here so that a chapel is not required before an error can be the ordinary doctrine, and so that a vague mood of “everyone thinks so” is not treated as an institution.

**Normalized.** An erroneous doctrine is normalized when it has become the unremarkable teaching of a body: what the people love to have, what the fathers did, what many follow, what is held as “the doctrine,” what is commonly reported and gloried in, what the prophet of this people says because this people will have him. The mark is ordinariness. The error no longer has to be brought in beside the faith as a stranger. It is the house’s voice. Chapter 70 reserved this thickening. “Many shall follow their pernicious ways” was read there as a following. The following, become the path one does not apologize for, is this mode.

**Institutionalized.** An erroneous doctrine is institutionalized when it has been set into the ordering forms of a body: altars and houses of high places; priests made from those who are not the priests of the writing; a feast in a month devised of a king’s heart; a court of prophets who answer with one mouth; a sanctuary called the king’s chapel; a seat from which burdens are bound; an agreement that confession will be punished by expulsion from the assembly; a command of the council; a man who casts out of the church those who would receive the brethren; a teacher the church suffers to teach. The mark is installation. The error has furniture, office, calendar, or sanction.

The relation between the modes is exhibited, not theorized in the abstract. Jeroboam’s calves are installed, and then the people go, and then the thing becomes a sin, and then later kings depart not from it. Installation and normalization confirm one another in that narrative. They are still distinguishable. The devising of the feast is the king’s act. The not-departing of successors is the continuance of a people and a dynasty in what was devised. Thyatira suffers a teacher, which is institutional permission, and some have the doctrine while the rest have not, which shows that institutional permission and universal inward consent are not the same fact. A doctrine can be the church’s tolerated teaching while a remnant has not received it. The remnant’s existence does not make the toleration unreal. The toleration’s existence does not make the remnant’s refusal a second doctrine of equal truth. The writing addresses both.

Thus:

> **Normalization is the error become ordinary teaching. Institutionalization is the error installed in a body’s ordering forms. Accepted heresy is the name of the settlement either mode, or both, produces. Neither mode is the transformation of the error into truth.**

# 71.5 The Biblical Words the Name Collects

The writings do not use one Greek word for every settled error. The chapter collects the words that bear on choosing, holding, teaching, and suffering a doctrine, and it keeps them distinct so that a lexicon is not mistaken for the argument.

**αἵρεσις (*hairesis*, G139).** From αἱρέω, to take or choose. In the Acts passages the word names a party: the sect of the Sadducees (Acts 5:17), the sect of the Pharisees (Acts 15:5; 26:5), the sect of the Nazarenes as Tertullus calls it (Acts 24:5), “this sect” as the Roman Jews have heard it spoken against (Acts 28:22), and “the way which they call heresy” (Acts 24:14). In those lines the word is a party-designation. It does not, by itself, decide that the party’s doctrine is false. Paul uses the opponents’ word and then states the actual relation: he worships the God of the fathers, believing all things written in the law and the prophets. In 1 Corinthians 11:19, Galatians 5:20, and 2 Peter 2:1 the word names a chosen line that divides. Galatians lists it among the works of the flesh, beside seditions. First Corinthians says there must be heresies among the church, that the approved may be made manifest. Second Peter speaks of αἱρέσεις ἀπωλείας, heresies of destruction, privily brought in, which deny the Master who bought them. Chapter 70 took the privy bringing-in as a mechanism of turning. This chapter takes the word’s force as a chosen teaching that can be the thing a body follows, calls a sect, or suffers as doctrine. The party-name and the destructive choice are not one lexeme-use. Collapsing them would make every sect-label an accepted heresy, which Acts 24:14 forbids, or would empty 2 Peter 2:1 of the doctrines that destroy.

**αἱρετικός (*hairetikos*, G141).** Titus 3:10 only in the New Testament. The factious man, the man who chooses a line of his own. After a first and a second admonition, he is to be rejected. The admonitions mark settlement. The choice has been addressed and has remained his. He is subverted, he sins, and he is self-condemned. The word names a person whose chosen line has become his standing position. It does not name a vote by which a church creates truth or creates falsehood.

**διδαχή (*didachē*, G1322) and the verbs of holding and permitting.** Matthew 16:12 identifies the leaven of the Pharisees and Sadducees as their doctrine. Revelation 2:14 speaks of those who hold (ἔχω) the doctrine of Balaam. Revelation 2:15 speaks of those who hold (κρατέω, G2902) the doctrine of the Nicolaitans. Revelation 2:24 speaks of the rest who have not this doctrine. The doctrine is a distinguishable teaching some have and some have not. Revelation 2:20 uses ἀφίημι: thou sufferest, thou permittest, that woman Jezebel to teach. Permission to teach is the institutional act. The doctrine held is the content. Christ’s “which thing I hate” is the verdict the permission does not revise.

**δόγμα (*dogma*, G1378) and δογματίζω (*dogmatizō*, G1379).** Colossians 2:20 asks why, if the readers have died with Christ from the rudiments of the world, they are subjected to ordinances — touch not, taste not, handle not — after the commandments and doctrines of men. The ordinances have a show of wisdom. The show is relevant because show is one way a human doctrine becomes acceptable as a rule. The verb of subjection names the body’s coming under the rule. It does not name the rule’s truth.

These words are one assignment because each can mark an erroneous teaching that a body chooses, holds, teaches, suffers, or submits to. They are not one lexeme. A sect-name used by an accuser, a fleshly party, a destructive teaching many follow, a factious man who will not be admonished off his line, a school’s doctrine, a doctrine held in a church, and a set of ordinances with a religious show are exhibitions of acceptance in different courts. Collapsing the words into a single technical term of later controversy would hide the exhibitions. Leaving them unjoined would hide the assignment.

Thus:

> **Accepted heresy, in this argument, is the erroneous doctrine the writings exhibit as chosen, held, taught, suffered, or submitted to by a body. *Hairesis* keeps its range: party-name, fleshly faction, destructive choice. *Hairetikos* names the settled chooser. *Didachē* names the teaching held or not held. *Dogma* names the ordinance a body comes under. The words stay distinct. The object they serve in this file is one.**

# 71.6 Distinguish Objects That Must Not Be Collapsed

The investigation fails at the first step if it treats different objects as one.

**This acceptance and Chapter 70’s turning.** This chapter examines settlement. Chapter 70 examined departure. A turning can thicken into a normal doctrine. The thickening is not the departure restated, and the departure is not unstated because the thickening has its own file. A person can turn and still meet a body that has not made the error its doctrine. A body can hold an erroneous doctrine as its ordinary teaching long after the first movers are dead, so that the present members do not experience themselves as turning at all. They experience themselves as faithful to the chapel, the fathers, the school, or the feast. That experience is the reason this file exists. To treat this chapter as Chapter 70 retried would hide the settlement inside another account of the leaving. To omit the settlement in order to protect the turning-file would leave the turning unjoined to the sixth word Movement IX assigns.

**Acceptance and the governance of meaning.** Chapter 69 examined what happens when a secondary source occupies the seat of sense. A governor can be the condition under which an error is called the sense of the writing. The condition does not make the accepted doctrine a reprint of the occupation. A doctrine can become the ordinary teaching of a people by a feast, a priesthood, a loved arrangement, and a following, and the question of this file is the doctrine’s reception, not a second description of the seat. A court can be tempted to govern and still teach what the writing gives. Governance is a standing premise. It is not the whole of acceptance, and acceptance is not the proof that Chapter 69 was incomplete.

**Acceptance and the rank of institutions.** Chapter 68 refused theology, philosophy, tradition, and institution the office of primary written authority by Christian identity, academic status, antiquity, or recognition. That rank is in force. This chapter does not re-argue it. The new object is the erroneous doctrine’s becoming the body’s teaching. An institution can be secondary and still teach the truth under the writing. An institution can be secondary and teach an error it has come to call the faith. The rank explains why the institution’s recognition cannot save the error. It does not itself narrate how the error became the teaching. Narrating that settlement is this file.

**The body’s reception of a truth and the body’s reception of an error.** A church can hold the doctrine it was taught. Acts 2:42 speaks of continuing stedfastly in the apostles’ doctrine. Second Thessalonians 2:15 commands holding the traditions taught by the apostle’s word or epistle. That holding is reception of a given truth. It does not create the truth, and it is not an accepted heresy. The hostile name “heresy” can be laid on that holding, as Acts 24:14 records. The name fails. Despair, already refused in Chapter 66, is refused again if someone infers from this chapter that every body is an accepted heresy by being a body. The object is the erroneous doctrine received, not the existence of reception.

**A question not closed by the writing and an accepted heresy.** Chapter 70 refused the reduction that every disagreement is tergiversation. The same refusal holds here. A question the writings have not closed is not, by the mere fact that a school answers it one way and another school another way, an accepted heresy of the school one dislikes. The error in this file is error relative to truth the writing has given: another god assigned the exodus, a human commandment taught as God’s, a condition of salvation the apostles did not command, a denial of the Master, a doctrine Christ names and hates, a prohibition of what God created to be received. Where the writing has not settled the question, this chapter does not appoint the louder school’s doctrine as heresy or as orthodoxy.

**Acceptance and the truth’s survival.** Men can receive an error as the faith of their house for generations. They cannot, by receiving it, make it conform to reality. The calves remain a sin while Israel walks in them and departs not. The way of truth remains the way of truth while it is evil spoken of. The Scripture is not broken by the reception. The reception succeeds as the settlement of a body’s teaching. It fails as an ontological correction of God.

**This chapter and later files.** Aridity, *apeiros*, scholarly labour, *spoudazō*, and the First Principles remain later. A body that holds an error may also be dry of judgment and unskillful in the word. Appearance is not those files written now. The intellectual failures Chapter 70 identified remain premises of how departure is permitted. They are not re-diagnosed here as the loss of reasonability. The next chapter will examine that loss. This chapter examines the settlement the loss does not have to be fully described in order for the settlement to be judged.

Thus:

> **Accepted heresy is an erroneous doctrine received as a body’s ordinary teaching or installed in its order. It is not the turning retried, not the displacement retried, not the rank-file retried, not every reception, not every open question, not the abolition of the truth, and not the later files under another name.**

The chapter may use, as already in force:

- Truth as conformity to reality, distinct from belief, popularity, institutional consensus, and sincerity as a determinant of truth (Chapters 1–3).
- Knowledge, evidence, reason, comparison, inference, and rational warrant as inspectable operations (Chapters 4–7).
- Knowing God by revelation rather than by speculation; human accounts about God as themselves to be tested (Chapters 13–14).

- Infallibility of the record as not infallibility of the interpreter, the church as church, or every later tradition (Chapter 57).

- Authority of Scripture as distinct from authority of interpretation; transfer and despair already refused; quotation is not the Graphē; stating sense is a real accountable act (Chapter 66).
- Misreading as five operations of a later act, the writing remaining the measure; a correct reading remains possible; an incorrect reading remains incorrect (Chapter 67). The modes may carry a doctrine into a form a body can then receive. They are not re-catalogued.
- Theology, philosophy, tradition, and institution as secondary systems that may serve and that do not become primary written authority by Christian identity, academic status, antiquity, or institutional recognition (Chapter 68).
- Governance of meaning by commentaries, traditions, institutions, teachers, systems, or experiences as occupation of the seat of sense and reversal of the test; the writing unbroken; service not the event (Chapter 69).
- Doctrinal turning aside as an inspectable movement from a given truth, with named causes, mechanisms, and intellectual failures that permit and do not excuse; the truth remaining the measure; continuance a real contrary (Chapter 70). “Many shall follow,” the little leaven, and the privy bringing-in are used now in the function Chapter 70 reserved: the following and the lump as acceptance, not as a second account of the departure’s causes.
- Biblical exhibitions already used as rank, governance, or turning — Matthew 15, Galatians, 2 Peter 2, Colossians 2, 1 Timothy 4 — used now where the new function is the doctrine’s settlement as teaching, rule, or tolerated office.

The chapter may not use, as if already finished:

- Chapters 72–73 as finished pathology-files; aridity as the loss of reasonability; *apeiros* as the dedicated diagnosis of unskillfulness.
- Movement X’s labour-files; *spoudazō* as a dedicated object; rightly dividing as a finished file; the discipline of biblical interpretation as this chapter’s achievement; readiness for the Six First Principles as this chapter’s achievement.
- The Six First Principles themselves as content established here, including the foundation-list of Hebrews 6:1–2 and any doctrine built from the leaflet’s counts of baptisms, sequences of resurrections, or map of the underworld.
- Unverifiable historical colour offered as a premise: a named post-apostolic heresy as the moment acceptance began, a reconstructed council as the inventor of truth-by-vote, a census of which later communions are the calves, or a particular modern school as the historical chapel.
- The particular doctrinal constructions of the Bible Introduction materials or of the Aletheia materials as if those constructions were the list of accepted heresies, or as if a warning against accepted heresies certified the warner’s next paragraphs.

The chapter may use neighbouring passages that exhibit normalization, institutionalization, or the failure of reception to transform error. It may not treat those passages as a substitute for the join of the preceding argument, and it may not treat Chapters 72–73 or Movement X as already performed.

Thus:

> **Premises already in force may be used as members of the acceptance. Premises that remain later may not be used as if finished. The settlement of erroneous doctrine, and the impotence of that settlement to create truth, are the present object.**

# 71.8 The Sequence of Levels: One Clean Scope Section

What this chapter does not settle is a matter of sequence, not of doubt about what it does settle.

The investigation proceeds by levels. Each level answers its question with full force. The next level remains next because it is a different question.

**Already in force.** Truth is conformity to reality. God is the ground of truth and cannot lie. Revelation has been given and authenticated. Scripture as writing is God-breathed. The record does not fail as truthful divine writing of what it actually communicates. The Scripture cannot be broken. The Graphē is the primary written authority. The authority of that writing has been distinguished from the authority claimed for a later statement of sense. Transfer fails. Despair fails. Scripture is misread in five inspectable modes, and the writing remains the measure of those modes. Theology, philosophy, tradition, and institution are secondary systems. They may serve. They do not become primary written authority by identity, academy, antiquity, or recognition. When secondary sources govern the meaning of Scripture, the seat of sense is occupied and the test is reversed. The writing is not broken by that occupation. Service is not that event. Doctrinal turning aside has causes and mechanisms the writings name. Intellectual failures permit departure and do not excuse it. The truth turned from remains the measure. Continuance is a real contrary. Recognition of the corpus is not creation of the corpus. Human accounts about God are accountable.

**This level.** Given those premises, erroneous doctrines become normalized and institutionalized by operations the writings exhibit, and institutional acceptance does not transform error into truth. The settled doctrine remains measurable by the writing. Refusal of the multitude, of the chapel, of the fathers’ manner, and of the suffered teacher remains a real duty where the doctrine does not speak according to the word. The warrant is full at this level. It is not postponed until Chapter 72 in order to keep the present acceptance weak, and it is not inflated into a dryness-file, an unskill-file, or a labour-file in order to make the acceptance sound larger than a settlement.

**Next levels, named once.** Aridity and the loss of reasonability belong to Chapter 72. *Apeiros*, unskillfulness in the Word, belongs to Chapter 73. Scholarly labour, perception as a dedicated stage-file, reasoning through the Word as a labour-file, quotation and knowledge as a dedicated file, *spoudazō*, exercise, maturity, rightly dividing, and readiness for the Six First Principles belong to Movement X. Those files remain later.

**Limits that belong to this level itself, not to later hesitation.** This acceptance is not a second statement of turning. This acceptance is not a claim that every body teaches error, or that reception of apostolic doctrine is a heresy. This acceptance is not a claim that every party-name is a destructive choice. This acceptance is not a history of later communions and not a manual of church courts. This acceptance is not a denial that a remnant can refuse the settled doctrine, or that a book found in the house can judge the fathers. This acceptance is not a pardon of the body that loves the lie, and it is not a decree that the truth has been defeated because the body is old. Using Chapters 57, 62–63, 65, 66, 67, 68, 69, and 70 is not rewriting them. A normalized doctrine is not a sneer at teaching. An institution is not a sneer at order. A feast like the authorized feast is not fidelity by resemblance. A name kept from the holy vocabulary is not the thing the vocabulary names. Those limits are part of what the acceptance-file is. They are not a stack of clauses designed to drain the positive conclusion.

One scope section is enough. Later sections will not re-plead these limits as though the conclusion were still tentative. Where a particular passage does not carry a further inference, that local limit will be stated at that passage. The local limit protects the exhibition. It does not weaken the level.

Thus:

> **The sequence is: truth, record, primary writing, distinguished interpretation, catalogued misreading, ranked secondary systems, governance, and turning already in force; accepted heresies at this level; aridity, unskillfulness, and the labour of the Word at later levels. The acceptance is warranted now.**

# 71.9 Define the Operations of Settlement

Chapter 70 defined a cause as a condition the writings attach to a departure, and a mechanism as an operation by which the departure is carried. Those definitions stay in that file. This chapter defines the operations by which an erroneous doctrine, once in motion, becomes the received teaching or the installed rule. The operations are read from the exhibitions that follow. They are named here so the exhibitions are not a pile of stories without a question.

**Installation.** The doctrine is given a place, a priesthood, a calendar, and a likeness to an authorized worship. The king takes counsel, makes the calves, sets them in Bethel and Dan, makes priests of those who are not of Levi, and ordains a feast like the feast in Judah, in the month he devised of his own heart.

**Unison.** The official speakers answer with one mouth. The messenger then urges the remaining speaker to conform his word to theirs. Consensus of the authorized mouth is offered as the safe word.

**Sacred location.** The place is cited as the reason a contrary word must stop. It is the king’s chapel and the king’s court. The land is said not to be able to bear the contrary words.

**Popular love.** The prophets prophesy falsely, the priests bear rule by their means, and the people love to have it so. Love of the arrangement is the people’s part in the settlement.

**Claimed possession.** The body says, We are wise, and the law of the Lord is with us, while the word of the Lord is rejected. The claim to hold the holy writing is part of the acceptance of a teaching that does not speak according to it.

**Generational manner.** The fathers did not hearken. Successors depart not from the sin. The nations do after the former manners unto this day. What has been done long enough is treated as the religion.

**Teaching as doctrine.** Commandments of men are taught for doctrines. A commandment is broken, and men are taught so. The breach has become curriculum.

**School-doctrine.** A recognized body has a διδαχή of its own. The doctrine of the Pharisees and of the Sadducees is a leaven to be guarded against precisely because it is their doctrine, not because it lacks a school.

**Agreement and penalty.** The assembly has already agreed. Confession of the truth is punished by expulsion. Love of the institution’s praise keeps the public doctrine in place even where some rulers believe otherwise in private.

**Command of the assembly.** The council straitly commands what may not be taught. The command is the house’s rule.

**False sending.** A requirement goes out as if the sending body had commanded it. The body says it gave no such commandment. The hearers’ reception of the requirement as the mother-body’s doctrine is how the error travels as an accepted rule.

**Social carrying-away.** Respected men, and then the rest, and then a companion, adopt one public walk contrary to the truth of the gospel. The company’s walk becomes the settled behavior that claims the company of the faithful.

**Following and slander.** Many follow the destructive ways. By reason of them the way of truth is evil spoken of. The heresy is the public path. The truth is the thing slandered.

**Holding and suffering.** A church has there those who hold a named doctrine. A church suffers a self-named prophetess to teach. Space to repent is given and refused. The doctrine is what some have. The rest are addressed as not having it.

**Show.** Ordinances of men have a show of wisdom, will-worship, and humility. A Levite in the house is taken as proof that the Lord will do good. Likeness to the true feast, and religious furniture, make the error look received.

**Standing prohibition.** The doctrine crystallizes into forbidding and commanding: forbid marriage, command abstinence from meats. The rule is the form in which the doctrine is taught as the ordinary requirement.

**Borrowed name.** A letter as from the apostles troubles the church. A woman calls herself a prophetess. Holders speak of depths. The way is called heresy by its enemies, and an error is called the law of the Lord by its friends. The name is an instrument of acceptance and an instrument of accusation. In neither use does the name decide.

These operations are the how. They are not a sociology imported from outside the writings. Each will be read from a text. Several texts exhibit more than one. The list is plural because the record is plural.

Thus:

> **Settlement proceeds by installation, unison, sacred location, popular love, claimed possession of the law, generational manner, teaching human commandments as doctrine, school-doctrine, penalty, assembly-command, false sending, social carrying-away, following and slander, holding and suffering, religious show, standing prohibition, and borrowed names. The operations make the error ordinary or official. They do not make it true.**

# 71.10 Positive Warrant: Acceptance Is Not an Ontological Act

The first warrant is the warrant already earned, now applied with full force to a settled doctrine.

Truth is conformity to reality. Belief does not create it. Popularity does not create it. Sincerity does not create it. Institutional consensus does not create it. Those sentences are Chapters 1–3, used and not retried. Their application here is the assigned conclusion’s ontological half: when a body accepts an erroneous doctrine, the acceptance is a fact about the body. It is not a fact about the reality the doctrine claims to state. The calves are called the gods who brought Israel up. The calling, the setting in Bethel and Dan, the feast, and the centuries of not-departing are real acts. They do not become the exodus. The exodus remains the Lord’s. A council’s command that the apostles not teach in the name of Jesus is a real command. It does not make the name unspeakable before God, and it does not make the silence true. A church’s permission that a woman teach seduction is a real permission. It does not convert the doctrine into something Christ does not hate.

The Scripture cannot be broken. The record does not fail as truthful divine writing of what it actually communicates. The Graphē is the primary written authority. Those sentences are Chapters 62–63, 57, and 65, used and not retried. Their application here is that a received doctrine is still a claimant before the writing. If the doctrine does not speak according to what is written, the body’s age, the body’s office, and the body’s vote do not repair the disharmony. Recognition did not create the corpus. Reception does not create the truth of a doctrine laid on the corpus. Chapter 66 refused the transfer of the writing’s authority to the interpreter’s statement of sense. The transfer fails again when the statement of sense has become the ordinary doctrine of a house. Ordinariness is not a new species of authority. It is the same claim wearing the clothes of settlement.

Isaiah 8:20, already used as a criterion and not re-argued as a new proof, still measures the settled teacher: if they speak not according to this word, it is because there is no light in them. The absence of light is not cured by the number of lamps the house has hung. Deuteronomy 12:32 forbids adding to the command and diminishing from it. An addition that has become the thing observed is still an addition. Observance is not transubstantiation of the addition into the command.

This warrant is positive. Acceptance is a real human act, describable, blamable, and reversible in the sense that a body can cease to teach the error. It is not a divine act of making. God is not compelled by the chapel. The writing is not revised by the feast that was devised of a heart. The investigator who treats a synod, a dynasty, a school, or a multitude as the creator of truth has changed the definition of truth. This chapter does not change it.

Thus:

> **Institutional acceptance is a fact about a body. It is not an act that conforms an error to reality. The truth remains what it was. The writing remains the measure of the doctrine the body has settled.**

# 71.11 Positive Warrant: The Settlement Is Inspectable Because the Writings Exhibit It

The second warrant is that the how is not a mystery behind the text.

The writings do not say, “Error sometimes becomes traditional, and the manner is hidden.” They say: the king took counsel and made two calves, and said it was too much to go up to Jerusalem, and set them in Bethel and Dan, and made priests who were not of Levi, and ordained a feast like Judah’s feast in a month devised of his own heart, and this thing became a sin, and the people went, and he returned not from his evil way, and Israel walked in the sins and departed not from them. They say: about four hundred prophets said, Go up and prosper, with one mouth. They say: prophesy not again any more at Beth-el, for it is the king’s chapel. They say: the prophets prophesy falsely, and the priests bear rule by their means, and my people love to have it so. They say: our fathers have not hearkened unto the words of this book. They say: in vain they do worship me, teaching for doctrines the commandments of men. They say: the Jews had agreed already, that if any man did confess that he was Christ, he should be put out of the synagogue. They say: certain went out from us, to whom we gave no such commandment. They say: many shall follow their pernicious ways; by reason of whom the way of truth shall be evil spoken of. They say: thou sufferest that woman Jezebel to teach; thou hast there them that hold the doctrine; which thing I hate.

Because the settlement is described, it can be recognized in a later body without a private revelation of motives. The test is public. Is an erroneous teaching now the ordinary doctrine? Has it been given place, office, calendar, penalty, or permission to teach? Do the holders call it the faith, the law, the chapel, the depth, the feast like the true feast? Those questions are the questions the texts themselves make askable. Forewarning is possible for the same reason Chapter 70 gave for the turning: the departure was describable in advance. The settlement is describable while it wears the name of faithfulness. A people who only possess the warning “do not turn” can still fail to see the turn once it has become the religion they were born into. The exhibitions of installation, love, chapel, fathers, and suffered teaching are the remedy for that blindness. They teach the reader to inspect the ordinary.

The warrant is not a claim that every later institution has been identified in these pages. The chapter does not name a post-apostolic party as the calf. The warrant is that the operations are readable, so the acceptance is not an opaque fate of “religion” and not a phenomenon only a historian of councils can see. Any body that holds a doctrine stands under the same test the writings apply to Bethel, to the four hundred, to the synagogue’s agreement, and to Thyatira.

Thus:

> **The normalization and institutionalization of erroneous doctrine are inspectable because the writings exhibit the operations. Settlement is not a fog that descends after the turning. It is a set of acts the texts describe: making, ordaining, speaking with one mouth, loving the arrangement, not departing, teaching as doctrine, agreeing to expel, suffering to teach.**

# 71.12 Positive Warrant: The Accepted State Can Be Refused

The third warrant is that acceptance is not fate, and the commands that refuse it are not rhetoric.

Exodus 23:2 commands Israel not to follow a multitude to do evil. The multitude is contemplated as a pressure. The command treats disobedience to the multitude as possible and obligatory. Deuteronomy 13 contemplates a prophet whose sign comes to pass and whose doctrine is other gods, and commands: thou shalt not hearken. The success of the credential is the hardest case. The command still binds. Micaiah, pressed to make his word like the word of the four hundred, says that what the Lord says to him, that will he speak. The pressure of unison is real. The contrary speech is real. Josiah, when the book of the law is read, tears his clothes because the fathers have not hearkened. The fathers’ manner is the accepted state. The book in the house is enough to judge it. The apostles, straitly commanded not to teach in the name, answer that they ought to obey God rather than men. The council’s acceptance of silence as the rule does not obligate the witnesses. Revelation 2 speaks to the rest in Thyatira, as many as have not this doctrine. The church’s suffering of Jezebel has not made the doctrine universal inside the church, and the Lord addresses the non-holders without telling them that the doctrine’s toleration has made it clean. Ephesus is credited with trying those who say they are apostles and are not, and with hating the deeds of the Nicolaitans, which Christ also hates. Hatred of the deeds is a real churchly act. It is the contrary of holding the doctrine.

Chapter 70 established continuance in the truth as a real contrary of turning. This chapter establishes refusal of a settled error as a real contrary of acceptance. The two contraries are kin and are not the same sentence. Continuance is remaining in what was learned. Refusal is declining to adopt, to teach, to suffer, or to call true what a body has already made ordinary. A man born into Jeroboam’s feast is not described as personally inventing the calves. He is described, if he walks in that way, as not departing from a sin already made. The duty that meets him is not only “do not turn,” as though the only danger were a fresh departure from a pure church. The duty is also “do not follow the multitude; do not hearken to the successful prophet; do not let the chapel silence the word; do not call the fathers’ neglect the covenant.” Those duties are intelligible because the truth has not been altered by the acceptance. If acceptance had transformed the error, refusal would be rebellion against a new truth. The writings treat refusal as obedience.

“Some” and “the rest” do in this file the work “some” did in Chapter 70. Not every ruler confesses the synagogue’s public doctrine in his heart. Not every member of Thyatira has the doctrine. Not every voice in Ahab’s court is the four hundred. The accepted state can be the public doctrine while persons inside the body have not consented. Their non-consent does not by itself remove the public doctrine. It does show that the public doctrine is not an irresistible substance. It can be refused in the same house where it is taught.

Thus:

> **The accepted state is not fate. A multitude may be disobeyed, a successful sign refused, a unanimous court contradicted, a fathers’ manner judged by the book, a council’s command answered by obedience to God, and a suffered doctrine not held. Refusal is possible because acceptance did not make the error true.**

# 71.13 What the Acceptance Is

The three warrants control the description.

Accepted heresy is the condition in which an erroneous doctrine is what a body ordinarily teaches or officially orders, and in which that ordinariness or that order is available to be mistaken for truth. The mistake is the theological danger. The condition is the historical and ecclesial fact. The chapter affirms both. Bodies do settle errors into feasts, schools, penalties, and pulpits. The settlement is visible. The visibility does not sanctify.

The acceptance is doctrinal because what is settled is an account of God, of his worship, of the way of salvation, of what is commanded, or of what the writing means, offered as the thing to be believed and kept. Jeroboam’s sentence is a doctrine of the exodus: these are the gods who brought thee up. The Judaean teachers’ sentence is a doctrine of salvation: except ye be circumcised after the manner of Moses, ye cannot be saved. The commandments of men are taught for doctrines. Jezebel teaches. The doctrine of Balaam is held. The peace-oracle is a doctrine of the Lord’s intention: ye shall have peace, no evil shall come. Where a passage exhibits a practice received and gloried in, the chapter uses it for the structure of normalization and does not pretend the practice was a proposition. Where the passage exhibits a teaching, the teaching is the object.

The acceptance is corporate. A private error that the holder still knows to be a departure, and that no body teaches, remains Chapter 70’s object if it is a turning, and is not yet this file’s object. The threshold is the body’s reception: the people went; the people love to have it so; the church has them there; the church suffers her to teach; the company dissembled likewise; many follow; the synagogue had agreed already. Corporate reception can coexist with private dissent, as John 12:42 shows. The public doctrine is still the accepted doctrine.

The acceptance often keeps a holy word. The feast is like the feast in Judah. The priest is a Levite. The chapel is called the king’s, in a land that still says Lord. The law of the Lord is claimed. The fear of the Lord is professed while the nations’ gods are served. She calls herself a prophetess. The letter is as from us. Chapter 70 established substitution that keeps the holy sentence. This chapter uses that premise in the settled form: the kept word is now the public name of the accepted error. Likeness, office, and vocabulary are the dignity in which the error sits. They are not the error’s conversion.

Thus:

> **Acceptance is the corporate reception of an erroneous doctrine as ordinary teaching or official order, often under a retained holy name, likeness, or office. The reception is real. The conversion of error into truth is not.**

# 71.14 What Does Not Constitute Accepted Heresy

The name is withheld where the definition’s elements are absent. The withholding is part of the doctrine’s shape.

**Reception of what the writing gives is not an accepted heresy.** Continuing in the apostles’ doctrine, holding what the apostle taught by word or epistle, believing all things written in the law and the prophets, is not guilty by being corporate. A body that confesses what the Graphē teaches is receiving a truth. The reception does not create the truth. It also does not become a heresy because another body calls it one.

**A party-name is not yet a verdict.** *Hairesis* in the mouth of Tertullus, or in the mouth of Roman Jews who have heard this sect spoken against, is an accusation’s label. Acts 24:14 answers the label by the writings. To treat every group one’s opponents call a sect as an accepted heresy is to hand the verdict to the accuser. To treat one’s own party-name as proof of innocence is the same error in the other direction.

**An open question is not yet this object.** Where the writing has not closed the matter, a school’s answer is a school’s answer. It may later be tested. It is not, by being taught in a school, already the calf. Chapter 67’s modes remain available for testing a reading. This chapter does not promote every disputed reading into an accepted heresy in order to sound severe.

**Service under the writing is not this object.** A tradition, a commentary, a school, or an office that speaks according to the word and does not govern in its place remains what Chapters 68 and 69 said it could be: a servant. Service can be corporate and old. Age and corporateness are not the heresy. The erroneous doctrine is the heresy.

**The first motion of a turning, while it is still a departure and not yet the body’s doctrine, is not this file’s whole object.** Chapter 70 owns that motion. This chapter begins where the error is taught, held, suffered, commanded, or not departed from as the body’s own.

**A true reform is not a new heresy by disturbing an accepted error.** Josiah’s tearing of clothes is not tergiversation and not a faction’s vanity. It is the book judging the fathers. Paul’s withstanding of Peter is not the founding of a sect against the truth of the gospel. It is the truth of the gospel judging a company’s dissimulation. The disturbance of a settled error is not normalized by this chapter into a suspicion of everyone who will not follow the multitude.

Thus:

> **Accepted heresy is not reception of the given word, not a mere party-label, not an open question, not service under the writing, not the turning-file reprinted, and not the act of judging a settled error by the book.**

# 71.15 Biblical Record: Exodus 23:2 — Thou Shalt Not Follow a Multitude

> “Thou shalt not follow a multitude to do evil; neither shalt thou speak in a cause to decline after many to wrest judgment.”

**Context.** The sentence stands in the book of the covenant, among commands that order Israel’s justice. The multitude and the many are named as pressures a judge and a person can be drawn to follow. The evil and the wresting of judgment are the acts the following would join.

**What is explicit.** A multitude can be followed into evil. Many can be the direction in which judgment is bent. The command forbids both. The existence of the multitude is assumed as a real social fact. The command does not treat that fact as a reason. It treats it as a temptation.

**What the passage warrants for this acceptance.** The smallest and clearest refusal of normalization is already in the covenant book. Number is not a moral agent that changes evil into good. When a doctrine or a judgment has become the many’s way, the many’s way remains testable, and the test is not a count of heads. The verse does not use the later word heresy. It does establish the principle the accepted-heresy file requires: following a multitude to do evil is still doing evil. Institutional and popular acceptance are species of following. They inherit the prohibition. A later exhibition in which many follow destructive ways is not a surprise against this command. It is the command’s case occurring in the life of a people. The wresting of judgment after many is also a warning against the court that bends because the room has bent. Unison, examined below in Ahab’s prophets, is already forbidden as a method of decision.

**What the passage does not warrant here.** That every assembly is a multitude to be distrusted. The verse forbids following a multitude to do evil, not the existence of a people. That the verse is a psychology of crowds sufficient to replace the exhibitions. It is the principle. The exhibitions show the principle inside worship, doctrine, and churches. That loneliness is itself proof of truth. The command does not say, “Be few, and you will be right.” It says, do not follow the many into evil. The few can also do evil. The measure is the evil, not the size.

Thus:

> **Exodus 23:2 forbids following a multitude to do evil and bending judgment after many. Number is a pressure. It is not a transformation. Accepted error remains error when it is the many’s way.**

# 71.16 Biblical Record: Deuteronomy 13:1–5 — The Sign That Comes to Pass

> “If there arise among you a prophet, or a dreamer of dreams, and giveth thee a sign or a wonder, And the sign or the wonder come to pass, whereof he spake unto thee, saying, Let us go after other gods, which thou hast not known, and let us serve them; Thou shalt not hearken unto the words of that prophet, or that dreamer of dreams: for the LORD your God proveth you, to know whether ye love the LORD your God with all your heart and with all your soul. Ye shall walk after the LORD your God, and fear him, and keep his commandments, and obey his voice, and ye shall serve him, and cleave unto him.”

**Context.** Moses charges Israel concerning prophets who will arise among them. The case is stated at its strongest: the sign or the wonder comes to pass. The doctrine attached to the successful sign is service of other gods, which they have not known. The required response is not to hearken. The stated purpose of the case is a proving, whether they love the Lord. The positive walk is after the Lord, in his commandments. The prophet is to be put to death because he has spoken to turn them away from the Lord who brought them out of Egypt.

**What is explicit.** A credential can succeed. The wonder can come to pass. The speech that rides on the wonder can be a call to other gods. Success of the sign does not create a duty to hearken. The duty is refusal. The measure is the Lord already known and the commandments already given. The other gods are gods “which thou hast not known.” Novelty of object is not innocence. The proving shows that the case is a test of love, not a riddle with no answer. The answer is stated: walk after the Lord and keep his commandments.

**What the passage warrants for this acceptance.** This is the hardest form of the claim that confirmation transforms a doctrine. If any public success could convert an erroneous teaching into truth, a sign that comes to pass would be that success. The text grants the sign and withholds the hearkening. Institutional acceptance often appeals to a softer confirmation than this: the chapel still stands, the feast is well attended, the school is old, the prophet is still in office, the people are at peace. Deuteronomy 13 cuts under those appeals. A greater confirmation than all of them is imagined, and it still does not license the other gods. The accepted doctrine — let us go after other gods — remains a turning-speech even after the wonder. Chapter 70’s account of turning is the premise of the speech’s evil. This chapter’s use is the non-transformation: the public proof attached to the speech does not alter the speech’s relation to the commandment. A body that hearkens has failed the proving. Its hearkening is not the new commandment.

**What the passage does not warrant here.** That every wonder is false. The text’s case is a wonder joined to another god. A sign that confirms the word already given is a different relation, and earlier movements of this book have used authentication without making wonders into rivals of the word. That the civil penalty is this chapter’s subject. The death of the prophet is the text’s judgment in Israel. This chapter does not write a criminal code. It uses the epistemic command: thou shalt not hearken. That the love of God as a full doctrine of the heart has been newly proved. The proving is explicit. The file’s use of it is the refusal to let a successful teacher redefine God.

Thus:

> **A sign that comes to pass does not transform a doctrine of other gods into truth. The strongest public confirmation still leaves the hearer under the commandments already given. Hearkening to the successful prophet is failure of the proving, not discovery of a new faith.**

# 71.17 Biblical Record: Judges 17–18 — Furniture, a Levite, and a Tribal Cult Beside Shiloh

> “And the man Micah had an house of gods, and made an ephod, and teraphim, and consecrated one of his sons, who became his priest. In those days there was no king in Israel, but every man did that which was right in his own eyes.”

> “Then said Micah, Now know I that the LORD will do me good, seeing I have a Levite to my priest.”

> “And the children of Dan set up the graven image: and Jonathan, the son of Gershom, the son of Manasseh, he and his sons were priests to the tribe of Dan until the day of the captivity of the land. And they set them up Micah’s graven image, which he made, all the time that the house of God was in Shiloh.”

**Context.** Judges 17–18 narrates Micah’s private shrine, his hiring of a Levite, the Danites’ seizure of the priest and the images, and the installation of the graven image for the tribe. The narrator states the condition of the days: no king, and every man doing what is right in his own eyes. The same narrator states the duration: until the captivity of the land, and all the time that the house of God was in Shiloh.

**What is explicit.** A house of gods, an ephod, teraphim, and a consecrated priest are made. The maker then treats the acquisition of a Levite as evidence that the Lord will do him good. The Danites set the graven image up as a tribal priesthood continuing for generations. The house of God in Shiloh stands during the same time. Two cultic orders run together. One of them is the graven image. Its duration does not move the narrator to call it the house of God. The house of God is named separately, at Shiloh.

**What the passages warrant for this acceptance.** The exhibition is institutionalization by furniture and by tribal adoption. A private invention becomes a priestly house and then a tribe’s lasting cult. The theological error inside the settlement is Micah’s inference: the presence of a Levite proves the Lord’s favor. Office is offered as the transformation. The text records the inference in Micah’s mouth and records the object as a graven image. The inference fails inside the narrative. The further institutional fact is continuance beside the true house. A people can have the house of God in the land and still maintain, for as long as until the captivity, a tribal image-cult with a priesthood. Longevity and parallel existence do not merge the image into Shiloh. Normalization is in the duration and in the tribe’s ownership. “Until the captivity” is the narrator’s way of saying the accepted cult was not a moment’s turning only. It was the tribe’s ordinary religion across a long time. Chapter 70’s turning is the premise of how such a cult can begin. The present warrant is the settled form: a tribe’s priests, a graven image, a pious reading of the Levite, and a time-span that runs while the true house still stands.

**What the passages do not warrant here.** That the absence of a king is the whole cause, so that the file becomes a treatise on monarchy. The narrator states the condition. The doctrinal settlement is the shrine and the inference and the tribal installation. That every Levite in service is Micah’s proof. The fallacy is the use of the Levite to certify a house of gods. A Levitical service under the writing is not this scene. That the captivity date must be reconstructed from outside the verse before the verse can function. The text’s own “until” is enough to show duration. This chapter does not build a chronology beyond the sentence.

Thus:

> **Judges 17–18 exhibits a private shrine become a tribal cult with priests, maintained until the captivity and all the time the house of God was in Shiloh. A Levite in the house does not transform a graven image into the Lord’s favor. Duration beside the true sanctuary does not merge the image into the sanctuary.**

# 71.18 Biblical Record: 1 Kings 12:26–33 and the Sin Departed Not From — Installation as a Kingdom’s Religion

> “Whereupon the king took counsel, and made two calves of gold, and said unto them, It is too much for you to go up to Jerusalem: behold thy gods, O Israel, which brought thee up out of the land of Egypt. And he set the one in Beth-el, and the other put he in Dan. And this thing became a sin: for the people went to worship before the one, even unto Dan. And he made an house of high places, and made priests of the lowest of the people, which were not of the sons of Levi. And Jeroboam ordained a feast in the eighth month, on the fifteenth day of the month, like unto the feast that is in Judah, and he offered upon the altar… even in the month which he had devised of his own heart; and ordained a feast unto the children of Israel.”

> “After this thing Jeroboam returned not from his evil way, but made again of the lowest of the people priests of the high places: whosoever would, he consecrated him, and he became one of the priests of the high places. And this thing became sin unto the house of Jeroboam.”

> “For the children of Israel walked in all the sins of Jeroboam which he did; they departed not from them.”

**Context.** The kingdom has been rent. Jeroboam, established over the ten tribes, reasons that if the people go up to sacrifice in the house of the Lord at Jerusalem, their heart will turn again to the house of David. He takes counsel and produces an alternative cult. The sentence he puts in the people’s mouth echoes the calf of Exodus 32, which Chapter 70 examined as a quick turning. The present narrative is the later king’s deliberate installation of that pattern as state worship, and the subsequent books’ record that Israel walked in it and did not depart from it. First Kings 13:33–34 adds that Jeroboam himself returned not, and consecrated whoever would. Second Kings 17:21–22 summarizes the national continuance: Jeroboam made Israel sin a great sin, and the children of Israel departed not from those sins.

**What is explicit.** The motive is political retention of the people. The product is two golden calves, placed. The saying assigns the exodus to them. The people go. “This thing became a sin.” The institutional parts are named: a house of high places, priests not of the sons of Levi, a feast ordained like the feast in Judah, on the fifteenth day of the eighth month, the month devised of his own heart. He returns not from the way. Whoever would, he consecrates. Later Israel walks in these sins and departs not. The verb of continuance is the not-turning from the sin. The object of the loyalty is the erroneous cult.

**What the passages warrant for this acceptance.** This is the central Old Testament exhibition of an erroneous doctrine institutionalized and then normalized. The doctrine is the sentence about the gods of the exodus. The institution is the set of forms: geography, priesthood, calendar, likeness. The likeness is the disguise of orthodoxy. The feast is “like unto” Judah’s feast, so that a worshipper can feel he has not left the pattern of Israel’s worship. The month is nonetheless one the king devised. בָּדָא (*bada*, H908), to devise or invent, is the text’s word for the origin of the month. Invention is not hidden. It is also not allowed to remain a private idea. It is ordained unto the children of Israel. Ordination is the institutional act. “Became a sin” is the settlement-word. A counsel and a manufacture become the people’s sin because the people go. The king makes Israel to sin. Agency is corporate and royal, not only individual. Then the not-departing of later generations is the normalization completed. What Chapter 70 called continuance in the truth is here inverted in object: a dynasty and a people continue in the sin. Steadfastness attached to the error is the accepted state. The echo of Exodus 32 does not make this passage a reprint of that chapter. There the turning was quick, out of a way just commanded. Here the substitution has become the religion a kingdom will not leave, with priests, a feast, and a century-spanning charge in the books of Kings. The political motive does not excuse the doctrine and does not need to be generalized into a theory that every heresy begins in fear of losing a throne. It shows, in this case, why a king would want the error to be the people’s ordinary worship. Once it is ordinary, the heart does not turn to Jerusalem. The acceptance serves the installer. The service does not make the calves the God who brought them up.

**What the passages do not warrant here.** That every alternative place of worship outside a single city is Jeroboam’s sin. The text’s charge is the calves, the non-Levitical priesthood he invented for them, the devised feast, and the assignment of the exodus to the works of his hands. That the later divided monarchy’s every king has been adjudicated by this chapter. The formula “he departed not from the sins of Jeroboam” is the text’s own summary, and it is used as that summary. A full reign-by-reign history is not this file. That the first calf’s causes have been reopened. Speed, Aaron’s concealment, and the refusal to wait were Chapter 70. The present object is the kingdom’s installed and unabandoned cult.

Thus:

> **Jeroboam’s calves are an erroneous doctrine of the exodus installed as a kingdom’s worship: places, non-Levitical priests, a feast like Judah’s feast, a month devised of the king’s heart, a people who went, a sin the house returned not from, and a nation that departed not. Likeness to the authorized feast does not transform the invention. Not-departing does not transform the sin.**

# 71.19 Biblical Record: 2 Kings 17:33–41 — Fearing the LORD and Serving Their Own Gods

> “They feared the LORD, and served their own gods, after the manner of the nations whom they carried away from thence. Unto this day they do after the former manners: they fear not the LORD, neither do they after their statutes, or after their ordinances, or after the law and commandment which the LORD commanded the children of Jacob, whom he named Israel.”

> “So these nations feared the LORD, and served their graven images, both their children, and their children’s children: as did their fathers, so do they unto this day.”

**Context.** After the removal of Israel, the king of Assyria places nations in the cities of Samaria. Lions come upon them. A priest of the land is sent back to teach them the manner of the God of the land. The narrator’s summary is the both-and of verses 33 and 41, and the “unto this day” of the former manners. Verse 34’s “they fear not the LORD” stands in the same passage as “they feared the LORD,” and the resolution the text itself supplies is that the fear which keeps the nations’ gods and does not do the law and commandment is not the fear the Lord commanded. This chapter does not smooth the sentences into a contradiction in order to dismiss them. It reads the settled practice the narrator describes twice: fear-language together with service of their own gods and graven images, generation after generation.

**What is explicit.** A manner is taught. The nations serve their own gods after the manner of the nations carried away. They do after the former manners unto this day. Children and children’s children do as their fathers did. The law and commandment the Lord commanded Israel are what they do not do. Graven images are served. The phrase “feared the LORD” is the narrator’s description of the same people.

**What the passage warrants for this acceptance.** The exhibition is syncretism as a generational institution. The holy name is in the public religion. The gods and the images are in the public religion. The fathers transmit it. “Unto this day” is normalization stated as duration. Teaching “the manner of the God of the land” does not, in the outcome the text records, produce a people who put away the images. It produces a people whose religion can be described with both clauses. The accepted doctrine is the compatibility of the two services. That compatibility is the error. It is institutionalized as manner, priestly instruction, and family succession. It is not transformed by the presence of the Lord’s name in the sentence. Chapter 70’s substitution kept a holy sentence and reassigned it. Here the holy fear-word is kept and the other service is not even reassigned away. Both stand, and the standing is the manner unto this day. A body can therefore call its settled mixture the fear of the Lord. The text’s other clause, that they do not do the law and commandment, is the measure that judges the mixture. Vocabulary of fear does not replace the commandment.

**What the passage does not warrant here.** A modern ethnography of the Samaritans beyond the verses. The “unto this day” functions inside the book’s own horizon. That every use of the title Lord in a mixed sentence is incurable, so that no speaker who has once mixed terms can afterward speak truly. The passage describes a people’s manner. It does not close repentance. Josiah’s generation, examined below, shows a house in which the book can still judge the fathers.

Thus:

> **Second Kings 17 exhibits a taught manner in which the Lord is feared in name and the nations’ gods and graven images are served in practice, and in which children do as their fathers did unto this day. Generational acceptance of the mixture does not make the mixture the commandment. The law remains the measure the manner does not keep.**

# 71.20 Biblical Record: 1 Kings 22 — Four Hundred with One Mouth

> “Then the king of Israel gathered the prophets together, about four hundred men, and said unto them, Shall I go against Ramoth-gilead to battle, or shall I forbear? And they said, Go up; for the Lord shall deliver it into the hand of the king.”

> “And the messenger that was gone to call Micaiah spake unto him, saying, Behold now, the words of the prophets declare good unto the king with one mouth: let thy word, I pray thee, be like the word of one of them, and speak that which is good. And Micaiah said, As the LORD liveth, what the LORD saith unto me, that will I speak.”

**Context.** Ahab, joined by Jehoshaphat, inquires before a campaign. The four hundred prophets answer in unison with prosperity. Jehoshaphat asks whether there is yet a prophet of the Lord besides. Ahab names Micaiah and hates him, because he does not prophesy good concerning Ahab but evil. The messenger coaches Micaiah to conform. Micaiah refuses the coaching and afterwards prophesies defeat. Zedekiah the son of Chenaanah makes horns of iron and claims the Lord has spoken victory by them. The narrative’s issue is Ahab’s death, in the face of the unanimous good word.

**What is explicit.** The number is about four hundred. The answer is one answer. The messenger identifies the unanimity and asks for likeness to it, and defines the likeness as speaking good. Micaiah binds himself to what the Lord says, not to the mouth-count. Ahab’s hatred is explained by the prophet’s refusal to say good. A symbolic act, the iron horns, accompanies the court prophecy. The court word fails. The solitary word accords with the death that follows.

**What the passage warrants for this acceptance.** Unison of the official prophets is an institutionalized doctrine for the length of this decision: the Lord will deliver Ramoth-gilead. The institution is the gathered prophetic body at the king’s inquiry. The pressure to accept is stated by the messenger in the form acceptance always prefers: let your word be like theirs; speak good. Good here means favorable to the king’s will, not according to reality. The accepted oracle is the pleasant one. Its acceptance by the court does not make the campaign safe. Micaiah’s sentence is the refusal Chapter 71.12 warranted. The exhibition also refuses a piety that would call the four hundred the church’s teaching office merely because they are many and agreed. Agreement is the instrument of the false word’s respectability. Zedekiah’s horns are the furniture of that respectability, a made sign accompanying a made assurance. Deuteronomy 13 has already shown that a sign’s impressiveness does not redeem a false speech. Here the horns do not redeem the unison. The chapter does not need the vision of the lying spirit, which the narrative also contains, in order to make the institutional point. The point stands on the public facts: one mouth, a demand to conform, a prophet who will say only what the Lord says, and an outcome that vindicates the single word against the accepted word. A doctrine can be the doctrine of the authorized gathering and still be the doctrine that gets the king killed.

**What the passage does not warrant here.** That every solitary speaker is Micaiah. The test is whether the word is the Lord’s, not whether the speaker is outnumbered. A lone teacher of calves is still a teacher of calves. That every agreeable word is false. Unison is condemned in this scene because the content is not what the Lord has said. Prophets who speak according to the word can agree without becoming Ahab’s four hundred. That the vision of the heavenly court must be developed into a doctrine of how God uses deception. The vision is in the chapter of Kings. This file’s use is the earthly institution of the unanimous mouth. A theology of that vision is not required for the warrant, and it is not begun here.

Thus:

> **About four hundred prophets institutionalize a favorable oracle with one mouth, and a messenger asks the remaining prophet to liken his word to theirs. Unison does not make the oracle true. What the Lord says remains the word, and the court’s accepted good word remains false.**

# 71.21 Biblical Record: Amos 7:10–17 — The King’s Chapel

> “Also Amaziah said unto Amos, O thou seer, go, flee thee away into the land of Judah, and there eat bread, and prophesy there: But prophesy not again any more at Beth-el: for it is the king’s chapel, and it is the king’s court.”

**Context.** Amos has prophesied against Israel. Amaziah, priest of Bethel, sends to Jeroboam and accuses Amos of conspiracy, saying the land is not able to bear all his words. He then orders Amos out of Bethel. The reason he gives is institutional: Bethel is the king’s chapel and the royal house. Amos answers that he was no prophet nor a prophet’s son, but a herdman whom the Lord took from the flock, and he announces judgment against Amaziah’s house and against Israel.

**What is explicit.** A priest of Bethel treats the prophet’s words as unbearable to the land. He forbids further prophecy at Bethel. The prohibition’s ground is the status of the place: the king’s sanctuary, the kingdom’s court. The alternative he offers is to earn bread by prophesying in Judah. The established cult’s priest speaks. The place he defends is the place of Jeroboam’s altar.

**What the passage warrants for this acceptance.** Jeroboam’s installation has, by Amos’s day, a priest who can cite the chapel as a reason the contrary word must stop. That citation is the voice of an accepted religion. The erroneous cult no longer argues only by the sentence “behold thy gods.” It argues by location and by royal ownership. Sacred geography and political chapel-status are offered as the transformation: this place cannot be the place where such words are spoken, because this place is the king’s. The offer fails. The status of Bethel is real as a royal fact and null as a doctrinal justification. The land’s inability to bear the words is the normalized ear. What Chapter 70 described as a demand for smooth things is here the priestly claim that the official sanctuary has a right not to hear. The present function is the chapel as accepted institution. Amos’s non-membership in the prophetic guild is relevant only as the text’s refusal to let guild-status be the measure. The Lord took him. The priest’s office at Bethel did not. Office at the accepted altar does not decide who speaks for God.

**What the passage does not warrant here.** That every royal or ordered place of worship is Bethel. The chapel in the verse is the chapel of the calf-cult, identified by the book’s own narrative. That Judah is pure because Amaziah tells Amos to go there. The dismissal is Amaziah’s. This chapter does not build a geography of innocent kingdoms. That Amos’s call has been made a manual for recognizing ministers. The verse distinguishes the Lord’s taking from the priest’s dismissal. It does not write the labour-file or the office-file of Movement X.

Thus:

> **Amaziah forbids the word at Bethel because Bethel is the king’s chapel and the king’s court. The accepted sanctuary cites its own status as the reason it need not hear. The status does not transform the cult, and it does not silence the word the Lord has given.**

# 71.22 Biblical Record: Jeremiah 5:30–31, 8:8–9, and 23:16–22 — Loved, Claimed, and Still Said

> “A wonderful and horrible thing is committed in the land; The prophets prophesy falsely, and the priests bear rule by their means; and my people love to have it so: and what will ye do in the end thereof?”

> “How do ye say, We are wise, and the law of the LORD is with us? Lo, certainly in vain made he it; the pen of the scribes is in vain. The wise men are ashamed, they are dismayed and taken: lo, they have rejected the word of the LORD; and what wisdom is in them?”

> “Thus saith the LORD of hosts, Hearken not unto the words of the prophets that prophesy unto you: they make you vain: they speak a vision of their own heart, and not out of the mouth of the LORD. They say still unto them that despise me, The LORD hath said, Ye shall have peace; and they say unto every one that walketh after the imagination of his own heart, No evil shall come upon you.”

**Context.** Jeremiah accuses a settled religious order in Judah. Prophets and priests are not absent. They are the structure. The people are not depicted as merely deceived in a corner. They love the arrangement. The wise claim possession of the law. The Lord commands the people not to hearken to the prophets who promise peace to despisers. “They say still” marks continuance of the oracle. Ezekiel 13:10, in the same prophetic accusation, has the people building a wall and the prophets daubing it with untempered mortar, saying peace when there is no peace. The wall is the people’s construction. The daub is the prophetic covering.

**What is explicit.** False prophecy and priestly rule by means of that prophecy are one arrangement. The people love it. The claim “the law of the LORD is with us” is in their mouths, and the rejection of the word of the Lord is the Lord’s account of them. The pen of the scribes is in vain. The prophets speak a vision of their own heart. They say still, Peace, and No evil shall come, and they attribute the saying to the Lord. The commanded response is: hearken not. If they had stood in the Lord’s counsel and caused the people to hear his words, they would have turned the people from their evil way.

**What the passages warrant for this acceptance.** Three operations of normalization appear together. Popular love: the horrible thing is maintained because the people love to have it so. Acceptance here is affection for the false order, not only ignorance of it. Claimed possession: wisdom and the law are asserted as present while the word is rejected. A body can hold the artifacts of the writing — the pen, the claim of the law — as the proof that its doctrine is the Lord’s, and the proof fails. Standing oracle: “they say still” is the settled message of peace to people who walk after their own heart. The doctrine has become what is regularly said in the Lord’s name. Ezekiel’s wall adds the institutional image of a public structure the prophets cosmetically seal. None of these operations transforms the false word. The end is asked as a threat, not as a happy continuance. The command to hearken not treats the popular, scribal, priestly, prophetic acceptance as a fact to be refused, not as a development of the law. The evil way is still the evil way while the peace-doctrine is the ordinary preaching. Chapter 70 located the demand for smooth things in Isaiah 30 as a cause of turning. The present use is the loved and standing character of that smoothness once priests rule by it and the people love it. The turning’s mechanism is not re-tried. The settlement is.

**What the passages do not warrant here.** That every scribe’s pen is vain, or that writing about the law is contemptible. The vanity is the pen’s failure in a specific charge: the word has been rejected while wisdom is claimed. A scribe who causes the people to hear the Lord’s words is the text’s own contrary, stated in Jeremiah 23:22. That the love of the people excuses the prophets, or that the prophets’ falsehood excuses the people’s love. Both are named. Love of a false order is culpable. It is the people’s mode of acceptance.

Thus:

> **Judah’s prophets, priests, scribes, and people can form one loved arrangement: false prophecy, rule by means of it, a claim that the law is with them, and a standing promise of peace in the Lord’s name. Love, possession of the pen, and repetition do not transform the vision of their own heart into the word of the Lord. The commanded act is not to hearken.**

# 71.23 Biblical Record: Micah 2:11 — He Shall Even Be the Prophet of This People

> “If a man walking in the spirit and falsehood do lie, saying, I will prophesy unto thee of wine and of strong drink; he shall even be the prophet of this people.”

**Context.** Micah has said that the people bid the true prophets not to prophesy, for shame will not overtake them. He then states the condition under which a man becomes their prophet. The content they will receive is wine and strong drink. The walking is in wind and falsehood. The lie is the method. The outcome is office: he shall be the prophet of this people.

**What is explicit.** A lying promise of drink qualifies a man, in this people’s eyes, to be their prophet. The office is conferred by the fit between the message and the people. “This people” is the appointing body.

**What the passage warrants for this acceptance.** The shortest account of normalization is an appointment. The prophet of this people is the prophet this people will have. The doctrine — prosperity of appetite, prophesied as if it were the word — becomes accepted by being the message that earns the title. Institutional recognition is here nothing more than the people’s reception, and the reception is governed by what they want said. The title “prophet” does not transform the lie. It is the lie’s public dignity. The verse also guards a later sentimentality that would treat “our prophet,” “our teacher,” “our church’s voice” as a credential. The credential is exactly what the verse assigns: he is theirs because he says what they drink. Belonging to the body is the mode of acceptance. It is not the mode of truth.

**What the passage does not warrant here.** That every promise of good is a promise of wine. The lie is specified. A true prophet can speak comfort the Lord has actually given, and Jeremiah 23 itself distinguishes the Lord’s word from the vision of the prophet’s own heart. That the verse has diagnosed aridity. The appetite is explicit. The loss of reasonability as a dedicated condition remains Chapter 72. The present use stops at the appointment: a people can make a liar their prophet, and the making is the acceptance.

Thus:

> **Micah 2:11 exhibits a people conferring the office of prophet on the man who lies in the direction of their appetite. The title is real as their title. It does not make the lie a word of the Lord.**

# 71.24 Biblical Record: 2 Kings 22:8–13 — Our Fathers Have Not Hearkened

> “And Hilkiah the high priest said unto Shaphan the scribe, I have found the book of the law in the house of the LORD… And it came to pass, when the king had heard the words of the book of the law, that he rent his clothes. And the king commanded… Go ye, enquire of the LORD for me, and for the people, and for all Judah, concerning the words of this book that is found: for great is the wrath of the LORD that is kindled against us, because our fathers have not hearkened unto the words of this book, to do according unto all that which is written concerning us.”

His stated reason is wrath kindled because the fathers have not hearkened to the words of this book, to do all that is written concerning them. Huldah’s answer confirms the wrath against the place and its inhabitants because they have forsaken the Lord, and she speaks peace to Josiah because his heart was tender and he humbled himself when he heard.

**What is explicit.** The book was in the house and had to be found. When it is read, the king judges the fathers by it. The fathers’ failure is not-hearkening unto the words of the book. The standard is “all that which is written.” Wrath is the consequence named, not legitimacy. The king’s tearing is the contrary of the fathers’ acceptance of another way. The book, not the continuity of the temple’s routine, is the measure.

**What the passage warrants for this acceptance.** Generational practice can be the ordinary religion of a people whose temple is still standing, while the book of the law is unread. The fathers are the name of that accepted way. Josiah does not say the fathers’ antiquity has matured their practice into the covenant. He says their not-hearkening is why wrath is kindled. Antiquity of practice is the problem the book reveals, not the solution. This is Chapter 68’s refusal of antiquity as primary authority, now exhibited as a narrative rather than restated as a rank. The new function is the accepted manner of the fathers judged in a moment by a book that was theirs and was not heeded. Institutional continuity of the house did not preserve hearkening. The high priest and the scribe are the ones who handle the finding. Their offices did not, until the reading, constitute obedience. Office without the book’s words is not the doctrine of the book. The chapter does not adopt any theory of how the book was composed, lost, or edited. Those theories are not the text. The text exhibits a found book, a father’s neglect, a king’s humiliation, and wrath attached to the neglect. That exhibition is sufficient. A body can inherit a settled worship and call the inheritance the faith of the fathers, and the book can still be the standard the fathers missed. Acceptance by the fathers does not transform the missed word into a kept word.

**What the passage does not warrant here.** A reconstruction of the documentary history of Deuteronomy. The argument does not need it and does not smuggle it. That every generation between Moses and Josiah is hereby condemned in detail. The king’s sentence is “our fathers.” It is used as his sentence under the book just read. That finding a neglected book is the whole of reformation, so that the labour of understanding is unnecessary. Josiah inquires, humbles himself, and the narrative goes on to do what is written. Movement X will examine labour. This section establishes only that the fathers’ accepted way is judgable by the book, and that the judgment calls the way failure rather than development.

Thus:

> **The book of the law, found in the house and read, judges the fathers’ not-hearkening. The temple’s continuity and the fathers’ manner had been the ordinary state. They do not transform neglect into covenant-keeping. Wrath attaches to the accepted failure. The written words remain the measure.**

# 71.25 Biblical Record: Isaiah 5:20 and Isaiah 29:13 — Renaming, and Fear Taught by Precept

> “Woe unto them that call evil good, and good evil; that put darkness for light, and light for darkness; that put bitter for sweet, and sweet for bitter!”

> “Wherefore the Lord said, Forasmuch as this people draw near me with their mouth, and with their lips do honour me, but have removed their heart far from me, and their fear toward me is taught by the precept of men.”

**Context.** Isaiah 5 pronounces woe on a series of inversions. The relevant inversion is linguistic and moral: evil is called good, and good evil. Isaiah 29 describes a people whose mouth honors the Lord while the heart is far, and whose fear toward the Lord is a taught fear. The teacher of that fear is the precept of men. Matthew 15 and Mark 7 will cite this saying as teaching for doctrines the commandments of men. The citation belongs to the Gospel exhibition below. The prophetic sentences are read here in their own force.

**What is explicit.** Calling is an act that renames. The woe falls on the callers. The reality of evil and good is assumed, because the charge is that the names have been swapped, not that evil and good have changed places in themselves. Isaiah 29 distinguishes mouth-honor from heart-distance, and identifies the fear as taught. The curriculum is human precept.

**What the passages warrant for this acceptance.** Normalization has a vocabulary. When a doctrine has been accepted, the body does not only practice it. It calls it good, light, and sweet. The woe treats that calling as guilt, not as a successful revision of the moral world. Institutional teaching appears in the second sentence as curriculum: fear of the Lord, the proper thing, is nevertheless taught by men’s precept, and the result is vain nearness. A body can have a catechism of “the fear of the Lord” whose content is human precept. The title of the catechism does not transform the precept into the Lord’s commandment. This is the prophetic form of the claim Chapter 71.10 stated. The names move. The things do not. Taught religion can be the ordinary religion and still be precept of men. The heart’s distance shows that public honor is not inward conformity, and the chapter does not need that distance to become a new doctrine of sincerity. Sincerity was already refused as a determinant of truth. The present use is the taught character of the public fear.

**What the passages do not warrant here.** That every severe moral name is Isaiah’s woe, so that a body which calls sin sin is guilty of harshness. The woe is on inversion, not on true naming. True naming is the contrary. That the precept of men as a full account of tradition has been reopened against Chapter 68. Tradition’s rank is in force. The new point is that a precept can be the curriculum by which a people are taught what their fear is, and the teaching does not make the precept divine.

Thus:

> **Isaiah 5:20 judges the calling of evil good. The calling does not move the evil. Isaiah 29:13 judges a fear of the Lord that is taught by the precept of men. Public honor and a religious curriculum do not transform human precept into the Lord’s word.**

# 71.26 Biblical Record: Matthew 15:1–9 — Teaching for Doctrines the Commandments of Men

> “Why do ye also transgress the commandment of God by your tradition? … Thus have ye made the commandment of God of none effect by your tradition. Ye hypocrites, well did Esaias prophesy of you, saying, This people draweth nigh unto me with their mouth, and honoureth me with their lips; but their heart is far from me. But in vain they do worship me, teaching for doctrines the commandments of men.”

**Context.** Scribes and Pharisees from Jerusalem ask why the disciples transgress the tradition of the elders by eating with unwashed hands. Jesus answers by the fifth commandment and by the tradition of corban, which he says makes the commandment of God of none effect. He calls the result vain worship, and he identifies the act as teaching for doctrines the commandments of men. Chapter 69 used this passage for tradition governing the meaning and force of the commandment. The present function is the teaching-act by which a human commandment has become doctrine, and the failure of that teaching-act to give the commandment divine force.

**What is explicit.** There is a tradition of the elders, public enough to be the basis of an accusation from Jerusalem. There is a commandment of God, quoted. The tradition is said to transgress and to void that commandment. The worship offered in that condition is vain. The doctrinal act is named: teaching (διδάσκοντες) commandments of men as doctrines (διδασκαλίας). The “ye say” of the corban practice is a corporate saying. The elders’ tradition is a received teaching, not a private scruple invented in the argument.

**What the passage warrants for this acceptance.** This is the sentence that most nearly states the assignment in Gospel language. An erroneous rule — a human commandment contrary to the word of God — has become what is taught as doctrine. Normalization is the tradition’s status as the elders’ tradition, old enough and public enough to accuse disciples. Institutionalization is the teaching office of scribes and Pharisees who bind that tradition as the standard of transgression. The phrase “teaching for doctrines” is the moment of acceptance as doctrine. What was a commandment of men is now offered in the place of doctrine. Christ’s verdict is that the worship is vain and the commandment of God is made of none effect in their practice. The verdict refuses the transformation. If teaching a human commandment as doctrine made it doctrine in truth, the worship would not be vain and the prophecy would not fit. The commandment of God remains the commandment. The tradition remains the tradition. Their public marriage in the teaching of the schools does not unite them. The corban case shows the settlement in operation: a vowed gift to God, a pious form, is the instrument by which father and mother are left without honor. Pious form is how the error sits inside a body that still speaks of God. Chapter 70’s retained vocabulary is the premise. The new fact is that the retained piety has a tradition-name, an accusing public, and a teaching office.

**What the passage does not warrant here.** That every tradition of practice is corban. The text identifies a tradition that sets aside a commandment. A practice that serves the commandment is not this accusation. That the washing of hands is the whole of the error. The washing is the occasion. The principle Jesus states is the teaching of men’s commandments as doctrines and the voiding of God’s commandment. That governance of meaning has been re-argued. The seat of sense was Chapter 69. The present object is the status of the human commandment once it is taught as the doctrine.

Thus:

> **Matthew 15 exhibits a received tradition, taught by recognized teachers, as doctrine. The teaching does not transform the commandments of men into the commandment of God. It makes worship vain and the commandment of none effect. The elders’ acceptance is the mode of the error’s respectability, not the mode of its truth.**

# 71.27 Biblical Record: Matthew 5:19 — Break One of These Least Commandments, and Teach Men So

> “Whosoever therefore shall break one of these least commandments, and shall teach men so, he shall be called the least in the kingdom of heaven: but whosoever shall do and teach them, the same shall be called great in the kingdom of heaven.”

**Context.** The Sermon on the Mount has just affirmed that Christ came not to destroy the law or the prophets but to fulfil, and that till heaven and earth pass, one jot or tittle shall not pass from the law. The next sentence concerns doing and teaching. Breaking is paired with teaching men so. Doing is paired with teaching the commandments.

**What is explicit.** A commandment can be broken and, in addition, taught. The teaching spreads the breach as instruction. The evaluation is not suspended because the commandment is called least. Teaching men so is culpable together with the breaking. The contrary is doing and teaching the commandments.

**What the passage warrants for this acceptance.** Institutionalization of error has a schoolroom form. It is not only a king’s feast. It is a teacher telling others that the breach is the way. “Teach men so” is how a personal transgression becomes a doctrine of permission. Once men are taught so, the breach is on the way to being the ordinary instruction. The verse also refuses a graduation in which small commandments may be publicly set aside because they are least. The acceptance of a diminished law, taught as the appropriate attitude toward small commands, is still a teaching God names. The doer-and-teacher of the commandments is the contrary, and that contrary shows that teaching as such is not the evil. Teaching is evil when what is taught is the breach. A body’s curriculum can therefore be judged by this pair without a sneer at the existence of teachers.

**What the passage does not warrant here.** A full doctrine of ranks in the kingdom beyond the verse’s own “least” and “great.” The names mark the weight of teaching the breach and of teaching the command. They are not developed here into an ecclesiology of rewards. That every pedagogical simplification is a breaking. The text’s object is breaking a commandment and teaching men so, not the labour of making a commandment understood. That labour remains Movement X.

Thus:

> **Matthew 5:19 joins breaking a commandment with teaching men so. The teaching is how a breach becomes instruction. Public instruction in the breach does not make the breach the commandment. Doing and teaching the commandments remain the contrary.**

# 71.28 Biblical Record: Matthew 16:6–12 — The Doctrine of the Pharisees and of the Sadducees

> “Then understood they how that he bade them not beware of the leaven of bread, but of the doctrine of the Pharisees and of the Sadducees.”

**Context.** Jesus tells the disciples to beware of the leaven of the Pharisees and of the Sadducees. They reason among themselves about bread. He recalls the feeding of the multitudes and asks how they do not understand that he did not speak of bread. The narrator states the understanding reached: the leaven is the doctrine of those two bodies. Chapter 70 used leaven in Galatians as a mechanism by which a little contrary spreads through a turning people. The present function is the identification of leaven with a school’s settled doctrine.

**What is explicit.** Two recognized bodies have a doctrine. That doctrine is leaven. Disciples are to beware of it. The misunderstanding that takes the warning as a warning about bread is corrected. The warning is doctrinal.

**What the passage warrants for this acceptance.** A school can possess a διδαχή as its public teaching. The Pharisees and the Sadducees are not private speculators in this sentence. They are bodies whose doctrine is identifiable enough to be named and avoided. Institutional existence and doctrinal identity are granted. What is not granted is safety. The very fact that it is their doctrine is the reason for the warning, once “doctrine” has been understood. Acceptance inside the school — this is what the Pharisees teach, this is what the Sadducees teach — does not transform the leaven into bread. A disciple who has received the school’s doctrine as the ordinary teaching of serious religion is the disciple who needs the warning. The warning is intelligible only if a received school-doctrine can be the error. It is also intelligible only if the disciple can refuse it. Beware is a real command. The schools’ age, their teachers, and their public recognition in Israel do not retire the command.

**What the passage does not warrant here.** A catalog of every Pharisaic and Sadducean tenet. The text identifies the leaven as their doctrine and does not itemize it in this paragraph. Other passages in the Gospels exhibit pieces of that doctrine, including the tradition of the elders and the denial of resurrection. This section does not compile the catalog. It establishes that a named school’s doctrine can be the leaven a disciple must refuse. That leaven as a spreading mechanism has been redefined. The spreading was Chapter 70’s use in Galatians. Here the figure is Christ’s own, and its gloss is doctrine. The new point is the school’s ownership of the doctrine, not the chemistry of the lump.

Thus:

> **The leaven to beware of is the doctrine of the Pharisees and of the Sadducees. A recognized school’s settled teaching can be the error. The school’s recognition does not transform the doctrine. The command to beware treats refusal as possible.**

# 71.29 Biblical Record: Matthew 23:2–4 — Moses’ Seat and the Burdens Bound

> “The scribes and the Pharisees sit in Moses’ seat: All therefore whatsoever they bid you observe, that observe and do; but do not ye after their works: for they say, and do not. For they bind heavy burdens and grievous to be borne, and lay them on men’s shoulders; but they themselves will not move them with one of their fingers.”

**Context.** Jesus speaks to the multitude and to the disciples about the scribes and Pharisees. He acknowledges a seat described as Moses’ seat, and he distinguishes what they bid from what they do. He then describes a binding of heavy burdens laid on men’s shoulders. The chapter continues with woes against shutting the kingdom, making proselytes, swearing by the gold of the temple, tithing herbs while omitting judgment, mercy, and faith, and building the tombs of the prophets. This section does not preach the chapter. It takes the seat and the burdens as the institutional form of accepted teaching.

**What is explicit.** There is a recognized seat. Sitting in it is conceded as a fact. A distinction is drawn between bidding and doing: they say, and do not. Burdens are bound and laid on shoulders. The binders do not move the burdens with a finger. Later woes treat certain teachings and omissions as culpable. The gold of the temple and the gift on the altar are valued above the temple and the altar that sanctify them, which is a taught inversion inside a religious institution.

**What the passage warrants for this acceptance.** A recognized seat can be the platform from which a body receives its ordinary instruction. Chapter 68 already refused the inference that institutional recognition creates primary authority. The present use is narrower. The seat explains how an erroneous burden becomes what the people are told to bear. Binding and laying on shoulders are institutional acts. The burden is then the accepted requirement. Christ’s exposure of the burdens, and of sayings that do not match doing, shows that the seat does not guarantee the burden’s truth. The sentence “all whatsoever they bid you observe, that observe and do” is often pressed into a theory that the seat sanctifies every bidding. The same paragraph forbids following their works, states that they say and do not, and goes on to condemn what they teach in the woes. This chapter does not build a magisterium out of the first clause and does not erase the first clause to win an argument. The coherent reading that belongs to this file is the reading the paragraph’s own distinctions require: where they bid what Moses bids, the bidding has Moses’ authority because it is Moses, not because the sitter creates it; where they bind burdens and teach inversions, the seat does not transform those burdens into Moses. The gold-and-temple saying is one such taught inversion inside the institution. Acceptance of that saying as scribal doctrine does not make the gold greater than the temple. Jesus tells the hearers which word is the inversion. The seat remains a seat. The writing of Moses remains the measure of what may be called Moses’ bidding.

**What the passage does not warrant here.** A finished polity of synagogue authority. The chapter is not a constitution. That every burden is evil because it is heavy. Christ’s own yoke is described elsewhere as a burden of another kind. The burdens in this accusation are the ones the binders will not touch, joined to teachings the woes condemn. That Chapter 68’s rank has been restated for its own sake. The rank is used. The new exhibition is the binding of a burden as the people’s accepted load.

Thus:

> **Sitting in Moses’ seat is a real institutional fact. It does not transform a bound burden or a taught inversion into the word of Moses. What is bidden as Moses is to be kept because it is Moses. What is bound beyond and against that word remains a burden the seat cannot sanctify.**

# 71.30 Biblical Record: John 9:22 and 12:42–43 — The Agreement Already Made

> “These words spake his parents, because they feared the Jews: for the Jews had agreed already, that if any man did confess that he was Christ, he should be put out of the synagogue.”

> “Nevertheless among the chief rulers also many believed on him; but because of the Pharisees they did not confess him, lest they should be put out of the synagogue: For they loved the praise of men more than the praise of God.”

**Context.** The parents of the man born blind refuse to say who opened his eyes, from fear of an agreement already in force. Later, many chief rulers believe and do not confess, for the same fear. The narrator gives the motive: love of the praise of men more than the praise of God. The penalty is expulsion from the synagogue.

**What is explicit.** An agreement has already been made. The content of the agreement is a sanction: confession that Jesus is the Christ brings exclusion from the assembly. The agreement produces public silence, including from parents who know the facts of the healing, and from rulers who believe. The praise sought is the praise of men, set over against the praise of God. The public doctrine enforced is non-confession.

**What the passages warrant for this acceptance.** Institutionalization by penalty is here fully visible. The erroneous public rule — he is not to be confessed as the Christ — does not need to be proved in each conversation. It has been agreed already. The agreement is the settlement. Expulsion is the instrument that keeps the settlement ordinary. People who know better, and rulers who believe, still leave the public doctrine in place because the institution’s praise and the institution’s threat govern the mouth. Private belief does not equal public acceptance. The accepted doctrine is the one the body will punish deviation from. That doctrine is false. Jesus is the Christ, and the Gospel is writing the signs that show it. The agreement does not make the non-confession true, and the fear does not make it faithful. Love of the institution’s praise is a cause of conformity. It is not a cleansing of the doctrine conformed to. A body can therefore hold, as its enforceable orthodoxy, a denial the writing contradicts, and can do so while some of its own rulers believe the opposite in secret. The secret belief does not rewrite the public rule. The public rule does not falsify the Christ.

**What the passages do not warrant here.** That every form of discipline or exclusion is this agreement. The text identifies a specific agreement against confessing Christ. A body that refuses a destructive teaching, as Titus 3:10 will require in another relation, is not thereby the synagogue of John 9. The object of the exclusion decides. Exclusion of the confession of Christ is the instrument of an accepted error. Exclusion of a factious man who is subverted is a different act, examined below, and is not the creation of truth by procedure either. That private belief is worthless in every sense. The narrator says they believed. The failure he marks is the failure to confess. This chapter uses that failure as evidence that public accepted doctrine and inward opinion can diverge. It does not write a full doctrine of confession beyond the verses.

Thus:

> **An agreement already made, with expulsion as its sanction, can be the accepted public doctrine of an assembly, even where parents know the work of Jesus and rulers believe. The agreement does not transform non-confession into truth. Love of the assembly’s praise keeps the public error in place. It does not justify it.**

# 71.31 Biblical Record: Acts 4:18–20 and 5:27–39 — The Council’s Command

> “And they called them, and commanded them not to speak at all nor teach in the name of Jesus. But Peter and John answered and said unto them, Whether it be right in the sight of God to hearken unto you more than unto God, judge ye. For we cannot but speak the things which we have seen and heard.”

> “Did not we straitly command you that ye should not teach in this name? … Then Peter and the other apostles answered and said, We ought to obey God rather than men.”

> “And now I say unto you, Refrain from these men, and let them alone: for if this counsel or this work be of men, it will come to nought: But if it be of God, ye cannot overthrow it; lest haply ye be found even to fight against God.”

**Context.** The council commands the apostles not to speak or teach in the name of Jesus. The apostles refuse by an appeal to what is right in the sight of God and to what they have seen and heard. Brought again, they state the duty: obey God rather than men. Gamaliel advises restraint, on the ground that a work of men will come to nought and a work of God cannot be overthrown. The council beats the apostles and again commands them not to speak, and the narrative continues with them teaching daily.

**What is explicit.** A commanding assembly forbids the teaching of the name. The prohibition is “straitly” given and then repeated. The apostles treat the command as real and as non-binding where it contradicts God. Their positive content is what they have seen and heard. Gamaliel’s criterion is survival and failure over time. The text records his speech. The apostles’ practice does not adopt it as their reason. They obey God rather than men before the outcome is known, and they continue when the command is repeated.

**What the passages warrant for this acceptance.** A council can institutionalize silence as the house’s rule. The rule is accepted by the council with threats and stripes. Acceptance at that level does not transform the silence into the will of God, and does not transform the name of Jesus into a name that must not be taught. The apostolic sentence is the non-transformation stated as duty. Hearkening unto the council more than unto God is the wrong order. The things seen and heard remain speakable. Gamaliel’s counsel is recorded, and inspiration’s record of a councillor’s advice is not yet the Lord’s adoption of the advice as the test of doctrine. A reconstructible caution from the Bible Introduction materials, tested below, is that a recorded speech is not thereby sanctioned. Gamaliel is the illustration inside this exhibition, not a later theory. His test would make longevity the transformer of error into truth or of truth into error. Jeroboam’s sin, already exhibited, came not to nought for many reigns and remained sin. The apostles’ work is opposed by the institution and remains the thing they must speak. Survival and suppression are institutional outcomes. They are not ontological verdicts. This chapter does not mock counsel of patience in every civic danger. It refuses Gamaliel’s principle as a doctrine of truth. A work’s continuance does not make it God’s, and a council’s command does not make it man’s duty when God has spoken otherwise.

**What the passages do not warrant here.** A general theory of civil disobedience beyond the case. The case is a command not to teach the name of Jesus, answered by witnesses of what they have seen and heard. That every council is this council. An assembly can gather to hear the word, as Acts 15 does in the next section, without being the author of truth. The difference is what the assembly binds, and whether what it binds is what God has said. That difference is not a polity written out. It is the test already in force: the writing and the things God has done, not the assembly’s will as such.

Thus:

> **The council institutionalizes a prohibition on teaching in the name of Jesus. The prohibition does not become God’s will by being straitly commanded. God is to be obeyed rather than men. A councillor’s hope that time will sort works of men from works of God is recorded without becoming the test. Continuance does not transform error, and suppression does not transform truth.**

# 71.32 Biblical Record: Acts 15:1, 5, 24 — Words That Went Out from Us

> “And certain men which came down from Judaea taught the brethren, and said, Except ye be circumcised after the manner of Moses, ye cannot be saved.”

> “But there rose up certain of the sect of the Pharisees which believed, saying, That it was needful to circumcise them, and to command them to keep the law of Moses.”

> “Forasmuch as we have heard, that certain which went out from us have troubled you with words, subverting your souls, saying, Ye must be circumcised, and keep the law: to whom we gave no such commandment.”

**Context.** Men from Judaea teach the Gentile brethren a condition of salvation. In Jerusalem, some of the sect of the Pharisees who have believed rise and state the same necessity. The gathered church and apostles consider the question. Peter speaks of the Gentiles’ reception of the Spirit and of a yoke the fathers could not bear. James appeals to the prophets’ words. The letter that follows denies that the troublers were commissioned. Chapter 70 noted Acts 15 only to refuse the collapse of every dispute into tergiversation, and it refused to write a polity of councils. This chapter keeps that refusal. The new function is the false institutional credential: a doctrine of salvation travels as if a sending body had commanded it, and is received in that costume.

**What is explicit.** A teaching makes circumcision after the manner of Moses necessary to be saved. The teachers include members of a *hairesis*, the Pharisees, who believe. The letter says the troublers went out “from us,” that they troubled the brethren and subverted souls, and that “we gave no such commandment.” The content they imposed is quoted: ye must be circumcised, and keep the law. The denial of the commandment is the sending body’s own denial.

**What the passage warrants for this acceptance.** An erroneous doctrine becomes the received requirement of churches at a distance when it wears the name of the mother-body. “From us” is the instrument of acceptance. The churches are troubled because the words arrive with institutional origin attached. The letter’s work, for this file, is not to found a theory that councils create doctrine. It is to strip a false sending. The doctrine “ye cannot be saved” except by that addition is not true because it came down from Judaea, and it is not true because Pharisees who believe rose up to command it. The manner of Moses, a holy name, is the retained vocabulary in which the addition travels. Salvation is the doctrinal stake. Adding a condition the apostles did not command is the error. The error had already gained enough public force to subvert souls and to require a public denial. That is normalization in progress: a requirement taught as needful, identified with a sect inside the believing company, and credited to a sending that did not occur. The gathered answer in the narrative appeals to God’s giving of the Spirit and to the words of the prophets. Those appeals are the measure. They are not exhibited here as a blank warrant for later assemblies to speak true whatever they say. An assembly that says “we gave no such commandment” is judging a doctrine by what was actually given. An assembly that offers its own vote as the creator of what was given has changed the test. This chapter does not describe Acts 15 as that second assembly.

**What the passage does not warrant here.** The conclusion that every Judaean teacher is a liar, or that the manner of Moses is contemptible. The text distinguishes a false necessity for salvation from the writings James says agree. That a letter from Jerusalem is, in every later century, the form of true doctrine. The letter functions because its denial matches the truth of the case: no such commandment was given. A letter that invented a commandment and called the invention apostolic would be the instrument 2 Thessalonians 2 warns against, examined below. That the sect of the Pharisees is, by the word *hairesis* alone, an accepted heresy in the sense of 2 Peter 2:1. The word here is the party-name, as Acts 24 will also use it. Some of that party teach a destructive addition. The party-name does not do the work of the judgment. The addition does.

Thus:

> **A condition of salvation can be received as a church’s requirement because it travels “from us,” when the us gave no such commandment. Institutional origin, a holy manner’s name, and the voice of believers from a known sect do not transform the addition into the gospel. The measure is the commandment actually given, and the work of God actually done.**

# 71.33 Biblical Record: Acts 24:14 — The Way Which They Call Heresy

> “But this I confess unto thee, that after the way which they call heresy, so worship I the God of my fathers, believing all things which are written in the law and in the prophets.”

**Context.** Paul answers Tertullus before Felix. The accusation has called him a ringleader of the sect of the Nazarenes. Paul confesses the way his accusers call *hairesis*, and he defines his worship by the God of the fathers and by belief of all things written in the law and the prophets. He adds that he has hope toward God, which they themselves also allow, that there shall be a resurrection of the dead.

**What is explicit.** Opponents name the way a heresy. Paul does not grant that the name describes his relation to the writings. He worships the ancestral God. He believes all that is written. The name and the believing are set in one sentence so that the name cannot be mistaken for the measure.

**What the passage warrants for this acceptance.** The word the assignment uses can be a weapon of institutional accusation. A body, or a city’s orators speaking for a body, can call the truth a heresy and can make that calling the public classification. The classification does not transform the way into error. The way’s relation to the law and the prophets is the relation that counts. This exhibition is the necessary guard on the whole chapter. Without it, “accepted heresy” collapses into “whatever the reigning body disapproves.” With it, the chapter’s test stays where Chapters 65 and 66 placed the test: the writing, not the accuser’s vocabulary. The converse is included in the same logic and is exhibited throughout the other sections: a body can call an error the faith, the law, the chapel, or the feast, and the calling does not transform the error into truth. Acts 24:14 states one direction. Jeroboam’s “behold thy gods” and the scribes’ “teaching for doctrines” state the other. Both directions are one principle. Names bestowed by institutions are events of acceptance or of accusation. They are not events of truth-making. Paul can confess the way and refuse the name’s verdict in the same breath. A disciple in a later body can refuse a flattering name with the same breath. The measure he states is available: all things which are written.

**What the passage does not warrant here.** That every accused minority believes all that is written. Paul states what he believes. The verse is not a romance of dissent. A minority that denies what is written is not protected by being called a sect. That the law and the prophets are believed by being mentioned. The verse sets belief of them as Paul’s actual claim, to be tested by the writings themselves, which earlier chapters have already received as the primary written authority. This section does not re-prove that authority. It uses it as the answer to a false institutional name.

Thus:

> **What a body calls heresy may be the way that believes all that is written. The institutional name does not decide. The same impotence of names afflicts the flattering title laid on an error. Written truth remains the measure in both directions.**

# 71.34 Biblical Record: 1 Corinthians 11:18–19 — Heresies Among You, That the Approved May Be Manifest

> “For first of all, when ye come together in the church, I hear that there be divisions among you; and I partly believe it. For there must be also heresies among you, that they which are approved may be made manifest among you.”

**Context.** Paul has heard of divisions when the Corinthian church comes together. He partly believes the report. He then says there must be also heresies among them, in order that the approved may be made manifest. The following verses condemn the supper’s disorder. The present use is the sentence about heresies, not a commentary on the supper.

**What is explicit.** Divisions (σχίσματα, *schismata*, G4978) are reported in the assembly. Heresies (αἱρέσεις) are said to be necessary in the sense the clause gives: δεῖ, there must be, with a purpose clause, ἵνα, that the approved (οἱ δόκιμοι) may become manifest. The heresies are “among you.” They are inside the church, not only in the street. The approved are distinguished from the mere fact of the parties. Manifestation is the purpose named. Approval is not assigned to the heresy.

**What the passage warrants for this acceptance.** A church can contain chosen party-lines as an actual condition of its gatherings. The lines are not hypothetical. Paul hears of them and accounts for their presence. Their presence does not make a party’s doctrine the church’s truth. The purpose clause runs the other way. The parties serve, in the apostle’s account, to make visible who is approved. Approval is a tested standing, not the prize of having formed a successful faction. A faction that becomes the ordinary voice of a meeting has achieved acceptance inside that meeting. It has not achieved the status of the approved. The δεῖ must be stated with the care the word requires. Paul is not commanding the church to manufacture heresies, and he is not blessing them as goods. He is saying that their occurrence has a revelatory effect the church should understand: the approved show up against them. To convert the δεῖ into “heresy is the will of God for the health of doctrine” would be to transform the evil the rest of the canon condemns. To convert it into “once heresies exist, their longevity proves they were the approved party” would be the institutional fallacy again. The approved are manifest by trial, not created by the party’s reception. What the standard of approval is, in the labour of handling the word, belongs to later files, especially the approved workman of Movement X. This section does not write that file. It establishes only that heresies inside a church do not become truth by being the church’s actual divisions, and that the approved are a different object from the faction.

**What the passage does not warrant here.** That division is desirable. Paul partly believes a bad report. The δεῖ explains a fact in relation to manifestation. It does not invite the fact. That every doctrinal difference in Corinth has been classified. The verse names heresies in connection with the divisions he has heard of. It does not inventory them. That “approved” has been defined as a technique. The word stands. The technique of approval in study is later.

Thus:

> **Heresies among a church are a real condition, and they make the approved manifest. They do not make the party’s doctrine true, and they do not confer approval on the party that succeeds in being the gathering’s voice. The must is not a blessing of the faction.**

# 71.35 Biblical Record: Galatians 2:11–14 — Carried Away with Their Dissimulation

> “But when Peter was come to Antioch, I withstood him to the face, because he was to be blamed. For before that certain came from James, he did eat with the Gentiles: but when they were come, he withdrew and separated himself, fearing them which were of the circumcision. And the other Jews dissembled likewise with him; insomuch that Barnabas also was carried away with their dissimulation. But when I saw that they walked not uprightly according to the truth of the gospel, I said unto Peter before them all, If thou, being a Jew, livest after the manner of Gentiles, and not as do the Jews, why compellest thou the Gentiles to live as do the Jews?”

**Context.** Paul narrates a public confrontation at Antioch. Peter had eaten with the Gentiles. When certain came from James, he withdrew, fearing those of the circumcision. The other Jews dissembled with him. Barnabas was carried away with the dissimulation. Paul names the walk as not upright according to the truth of the gospel, and he says the withdrawal compelled Gentiles to Judaize. Chapter 70 examined the Galatian removal unto another gospel. This scene is not that removal restated. It is a company’s settled public walk, produced by fear and imitation, contrary to the gospel’s truth, and already strong enough that a pillar and a companion are inside it.

**What is explicit.** The change is public withdrawal and separation. The motive is fear of a party. The spread is “likewise” and then “even Barnabas.” The moral name is dissimulation. The standard is the truth of the gospel. The effect on Gentiles is compulsion to live as Jews. Paul withstands Peter to the face, before them all. The withstanding treats the company’s walk as blamable and as resistible.

**What the passage warrants for this acceptance.** Social acceptance can settle a false requirement faster than a formal decree. No one in the paragraph is said to have written a canon. The walk itself becomes the doctrine: Gentiles are compelled to live as Jews. The compulsion is practical and public, which is how a doctrine of separation becomes ordinary without a new revelation. The carriers are not marginal. Peter, the other Jews, and Barnabas are the company’s weight. Weight is the instrument of normalization. Once the respected have withdrawn, the withdrawal looks like the church’s way. Paul’s sentence refuses the transformation. The truth of the gospel remains the standard of an upright walk, and the company’s dissimulation remains dissimulation while Peter is inside it. Fear of “them which were of the circumcision” shows a party functioning as an enforcing public, kin to the synagogue’s agreement and not identical with it. The accepted practice is what one does in order not to lose that public’s approval. Barnabas’s being carried away shows that a good companion can be swept into the accepted walk. His sweeping does not amend the gospel. The withstanding shows the contrary of fate. One voice, speaking the truth of the gospel before them all, can name a settled company-walk as blame. The chapter does not make Paul a model of temperament. It makes the text a model of judgment: the truth of the gospel judges the walk the company has accepted.

**What the passage does not warrant here.** That “certain from James” were James’s doctrine, or that James authorized the withdrawal. The text says Peter feared those of the circumcision when certain came from James. Acts 15’s letter, and Galatians 2’s earlier handshake, are not erased by this scene. A reconstruction of James’s mind beyond the verse is not offered. That table-fellowship as a complete doctrine has been written. The use is the company’s acceptance of a walk contrary to the gospel’s truth. That every public rebuke is this withstanding. The warrant is the standard he names, not a license for strife. Galatians 5 will list strife and heresies together as works of the flesh. The withstanding is justified by the truth of the gospel, not by the love of a scene.

Thus:

> **At Antioch a respected company withdraws, dissembles, and carries even Barnabas away, and the walk compels Gentiles to Judaize. The company’s acceptance does not make the walk the truth of the gospel. The truth of the gospel remains the standard, and a public withstanding can name the settled walk as blame.**

# 71.36 Biblical Record: Galatians 5:19–21 — Heresies Among the Works of the Flesh

> “Now the works of the flesh are manifest, which are these; Adultery, fornication, uncleanness, lasciviousness, Idolatry, witchcraft, hatred, variance, emulations, wrath, strife, seditions, heresies, Envyings, murders, drunkenness, revellings, and such like: of the which I tell you before, as I have also told you in time past, that they which do such things shall not inherit the kingdom of God.”

**Context.** Paul has contrasted the flesh and the Spirit. He lists works of the flesh. *Hairesis* stands after seditions (διχοστασίαι, *dichostasiai*, G1370) and before envyings. The list is said to be manifest. Those who do such things shall not inherit the kingdom. The warning is one he has given before.

**What is explicit.** Heresies are works of the flesh. They sit with party-splitting seditions. They are manifest, not occult. They are incompatible with inheriting the kingdom, in the company of the other works named. The “and such like” keeps the list from being a closed ritual formula while keeping the named works inside the judgment.

**What the passage warrants for this acceptance.** A chosen party-line is not a neutral social form that becomes good when a church grows used to it. It is classified with the flesh. Normalization is the danger the classification answers. A body can become so accustomed to its factions that faction feels like zeal. The text calls the work manifest and repeats a warning already given, which implies that familiarity must not dull the name. What a church has come to accept as its ordinary party-life can still be a work that excludes from the kingdom. The acceptance does not reclassify flesh as fruit. The verse does not say that every doctrinal conviction is a work of the flesh. It says heresies, in a list with seditions, hatred, and strife, are. The conviction that holds the truth of the gospel, exhibited in the withstanding at Antioch, is not in this list. The party that divides the church as a fleshly choosing is. A settled faction can be the accepted social shape of a body and remain flesh. Inheritance of the kingdom is not awarded for institutional success in forming the faction.

**What the passage does not warrant here.** That the fruits of the Spirit have been made the subject. They stand in the next verses as the contrary. This file does not expound them. That “heresies” here has been silently defined as every doctrine later controversies condemned. The word’s force in this list is the fleshly party-choice, beside seditions. Where a doctrine is also a destructive teaching, 2 Peter 2 supplies that further exhibition. The Galatians list supplies the moral classification of the choosing.

Thus:

> **Heresies are manifest works of the flesh, kin to seditions. A body’s habituation to its parties does not turn the work into fruit of the Spirit. Those who do such things do not inherit the kingdom. Familiarity is not transformation.**

# 71.37 Biblical Record: 1 Corinthians 5:1–2, 6 — Commonly Reported, and Gloried In

> “It is reported commonly that there is fornication among you, and such fornication as is not so much as named among the Gentiles, that one should have his father’s wife. And ye are puffed up, and have not rather mourned, that he that hath done this deed might be taken away from among you.”

> “Your glorying is not good. Know ye not that a little leaven leaveneth the whole lump?”

**Context.** Paul names a case of sexual sin public enough to be commonly reported, and more shameless than the Gentiles’ standard. The church’s response is puffing up rather than mourning. He commands removal, and he corrects their glorying. The leaven sentence warns that the lump is affected. Chapter 70 used the leaven sentence of Galatians 5 for the spread of a doctrinal contrary. This exhibition uses the Corinthian leaven for the structure of corporate normalization. The case is a deed. The structure is what the acceptance-file needs: a thing the writing condemns, known commonly, unmourned, and gloried in.

**What is explicit.** The report is common. The deed is named. The fitting response was mourning and removal. The actual response is inflation and glorying. Glorying is not good. A little leaven leavens the whole lump. The assembly is addressed as responsible for its public posture toward the deed.

**What the passage warrants for this acceptance.** Normalization is not only a process that happens to doctrines in the abstract. It is a corporate posture: what everyone knows, what no one mourns, what the body is proud of. A doctrine can sit in that same posture. When a teaching contrary to the gospel is the common report of a church and the church glories in its tolerance or in its sophistication, the teaching has become ordinary. The common report did not make the Corinthian deed lawful. Common teaching does not make an erroneous doctrine true. Glorying is an aggravation, not a sanctification. The leaven figure, in this function, is the lump’s reception of what should have been purged. Chapter 70’s mechanism of spread is the premise that a little contrary does not stay little. The present point is the body’s boast in the unpurged condition. A church can preach its acceptance as charity or as wisdom. Paul calls the glorying not good. The command to purge treats the accepted condition as reversible and as not authoritative. This section does not convert 1 Corinthians 5 into a definition of heresy. It exhibits the posture of acceptance, and it joins that posture to doctrines in the sections where the thing accepted is a teaching. The principle is one: public reception and corporate pride do not transform what God condemns.

**What the passage does not warrant here.** That every discipline case is this case, or that the chapter has written a code of expulsion. The warrant is the failure of common report and glorying as moral authorities. That leaven has received a second full exposition as turning. The Galatian leaven remains Chapter 70’s mechanism. The Corinthian use is the gloried lump.

Thus:

> **A deed commonly reported, unmourned, and gloried in has been received as compatible with the assembly. The reception and the glorying do not make it lawful. The same posture, when the thing received is a doctrine, does not make the doctrine true. Leaven in the lump is a condition to be purged, not a consensus to be obeyed.**

# 71.38 Biblical Record: Romans 16:17–18 — Contrary to the Doctrine Which Ye Have Learned

> “Now I beseech you, brethren, mark them which cause divisions and offences contrary to the doctrine which ye have learned; and avoid them. For they that are such serve not our Lord Jesus Christ, but their own belly; and by good words and fair speeches deceive the hearts of the simple.”

**Context.** Near the end of Romans, Paul warns the church to mark those who cause divisions and offences contrary to the doctrine the Romans have learned, and to avoid them. He characterizes such persons as serving their own belly, and as deceiving the simple by good words and fair speeches.

**What is explicit.** The standard is the doctrine already learned. Divisions and offences are condemned when they are contrary to that doctrine. Marking and avoiding are commanded. Fair speech is the method by which the simple are deceived. Service of the belly is distinguished from service of the Lord Jesus Christ.

**What the passage warrants for this acceptance.** A doctrine can be the learned doctrine, the public teaching a church has actually received from the apostolic word, and that learned doctrine is the measure of later divisions. The verse is the contrary of the claim that the latest institutional consensus defines the faith. The learned doctrine judges the dividers. It is not judged by them. Fair speeches are how an erroneous line becomes acceptable to the simple. The simple are not a slur beyond the text. They are the ones whose hearts are deceived by what sounds good. Acceptance among them can grow because the speech is fair, not because the speech matches the doctrine learned. Once the fair speech has become what many of the simple repeat, the erroneous line has been normalized in that circle. The normalization does not make it the doctrine learned. Avoidance is the commanded contrary. A church that instead receives the fair speech as its new ordinary teaching has inverted the verse. Belly-service explains a motive. It does not need to be proved in every later teacher before the doctrinal test applies. The test is contrast with the doctrine learned. Where the doctrine learned is the apostolic gospel the writing gives, a contrary teaching remains contrary after it has become a party’s watchword.

**What the passage does not warrant here.** That “doctrine which ye have learned” means whatever a later local custom taught, even against the gospel. The Romans’ learning, in this letter, is the apostle’s teaching. Chapter 68 already refused antiquity and local recognition as creators of primary authority. A custom learned against the writing is not protected by this verse. It is judged by it. That fair speech is always false. Fairness of manner is not the charge. Fairness in the service of a contrary doctrine, deceiving the simple, is the charge. That marking and avoiding have been developed into a complete discipline. The command is explicit. The procedure of a court is not this section’s addition.

Thus:

> **Divisions contrary to the doctrine already learned are to be marked and avoided. Fair speech can make a contrary line acceptable to the simple and, by that acceptance, ordinary. Ordinariness among the deceived does not make the line the doctrine learned. The learned apostolic doctrine remains the measure.**

# 71.39 Biblical Record: Colossians 2:20–23 — Ordinances with a Shew of Wisdom

> “Wherefore if ye be dead with Christ from the rudiments of the world, why, as though living in the world, are ye subject to ordinances, (Touch not; taste not; handle not; Which all are to perish with the using;) after the commandments and doctrines of men? Which things have indeed a shew of wisdom in will worship, and humility, and neglecting of the body; not in any honour to the satisfying of the flesh.”

**Context.** Paul has warned against a philosophy and vain deceit that spoil those who have received Christ, which Chapter 70 used as a carrying off. He now asks why those who died with Christ from the world’s rudiments are subjected to ordinances. The ordinances are quoted as prohibitions. Their origin is the commandments and doctrines of men. Their attractiveness is a show of wisdom, will-worship, humility, and severity to the body.

**What is explicit.** Subjection (δογματίζεσθε) is the body’s coming under decrees. The content is touch not, taste not, handle not. The decrees are commandments and doctrines of men. They perish with use. They have a show of wisdom. The show’s ingredients are named: self-made worship, humility, neglect of the body. The show is “indeed” a show. It is not denied as a rhetorical surface. It is denied as wisdom’s substance.

**What the passage warrants for this acceptance.** An erroneous rule becomes acceptable, and then becomes the rule one is subject to, by looking like wisdom and humility. The show is the instrument of normalization. Severity looks holy. Prohibition looks serious. A body that has adopted the prohibitions can point to its strictness as proof that the prohibitions are the doctrine of Christ. The text says the strictness is a show, and the origin is men. Institutionalization is the subjection: the decrees are not merely ideas admired; the church is asked why it is living under them. A doctrine of abstinence, once it is the community’s rule, has been accepted. The acceptance does not change the origin. Commandments of men remain commandments of men while the body is severe. Chapter 70’s beguiling speech got the hearer carried off. The present verses are the settled form of that capture: life under the ordinances, defended by the show. The perishability of the prohibited things is part of the argument that the decrees do not bind the dead-with-Christ. This chapter does not develop the union-with-Christ doctrine beyond the verse. It uses the verse’s refusal: subjection and show do not transform human doctrines into the rule of those who are Christ’s.

**What the passage does not warrant here.** That every abstinence is Colossians 2. The text identifies decrees after the commandments and doctrines of men, with the show described. An obedience the writing itself commands is not a show of will-worship by being costly. That the philosophy of verse 8 has been re-tried as the turning. The carrying off remains Chapter 70. The ordinances are the accepted rule.

Thus:

> **Human commandments become a body’s ordinances by a show of wisdom, humility, and severity. Subjection makes them the accepted rule. The show and the subjection do not transform the doctrines of men into the doctrine of Christ.**

# 71.40 Biblical Record: 1 Timothy 4:3–5 — Forbidding and Commanding as the Settled Rule

> “Forbidding to marry, and commanding to abstain from meats, which God hath created to be received with thanksgiving of them which believe and know the truth. For every creature of God is good, and nothing to be refused, if it be received with thanksgiving: For it is sanctified by the word of God and prayer.”

**Context.** First Timothy 4:1–2 has said that some shall depart from the faith, giving heed to seducing spirits and doctrines of devils, a departure Chapter 70 examined as a transfer of heed. Verses 3–5 state the content that the doctrines take as public rules: forbidding marriage, commanding abstinence from meats. The meats are then described as created to be received. Every creature of God is good. The word of God and prayer sanctify the reception. Timothy is to put the brethren in remembrance of these things.

**What is explicit.** The erroneous teaching issues in prohibitions and commands. Marriage is forbidden. Meats are refused by command. God created the meats to be received with thanksgiving by those who believe and know the truth. The creature is good. Nothing is to be refused on the doctrine’s ground, where it is received with thanksgiving. Sanctification is by the word of God and prayer, not by the prohibition.

**What the passage warrants for this acceptance.** A departure can crystallize into a standing rule taught as the stricter faith. The crystallization is the acceptance-form. Hearers do not only transfer heed in a moment. They receive a list of forbiddings and commandings as what the faithful do. That list is institutionalized wherever it is taught as the brethren’s requirement. The text refuses the transformation in the most concrete way. The creature’s goodness is not altered by the command to abstain. What God created to be received remains to be received. A doctrine that calls the refusal holiness has been accepted wherever the brethren treat marriage or meats as unclean by that doctrine’s authority. The acceptance does not revise creation and does not revise the word of God that sanctifies reception. “Know the truth” is set on the side of reception with thanksgiving, not on the side of the prohibition. The truth is not the property of the stricter-sounding rule. Remembrance is the contrary of letting the prohibition become the ordinary teaching. This section does not re-narrate the heed given to spirits. It takes that heed as the premise and examines the rules the doctrines have become.

**What the passage does not warrant here.** That the chapter has identified a particular later ascetic movement as the referent, beyond the description in the verses. The rules are explicit. A named historical order is not required and is not supplied. That every fast or every unmarried life is this prohibition. The text condemns a doctrine that forbids and commands as if creation were not good. It does not condemn a person who abstains in freedom and thanksgiving. The difference is the doctrine’s command, laid on the brethren as the faith.

Thus:

> **Doctrines that forbid marriage and command abstinence from meats can become the standing rules of a teaching. The rules do not transform the creature. Every creature of God remains good, sanctified by the word of God and prayer. Acceptance of the prohibition does not make the prohibition the truth.**

# 71.41 Biblical Record: 2 Peter 2:1–2 — Many Shall Follow

> “But there were false prophets also among the people, even as there shall be false teachers among you, who privily shall bring in damnable heresies, even denying the Lord that bought them, and bring upon themselves swift destruction. And many shall follow their pernicious ways; by reason of whom the way of truth shall be evil spoken of.”

**Context.** Peter has said that prophecy came not by the will of man. He then warns that as there were false prophets among the people, there will be false teachers among you. Chapter 70 took the privy bringing-in, the denial, and the forsaking of the right way as mechanisms of turning, and it reserved the following of many, and the question why a body’s acceptance does not make the heresy true, for this file.

**What is explicit.** False teachers will be among you. They will bring in heresies of destruction. The heresies include denying the Master who bought them. Swift destruction is theirs. Many will follow their licentious ways. Because of them, the way of truth will be evil spoken of. The way of truth remains named as the way of truth in the same sentence as the slander.

**What the passage warrants for this acceptance.** The many’s following is the normalization. A heresy privily brought in does not stay privy if many follow it. It becomes the public path. The public path then supplies the reason the true way is slandered. Acceptance has a further fruit beyond the error itself: the truth is spoken of as evil because the followers of the error are many and are identified, falsely, with the way. That slander is a common form of institutional judgment. The accepted heresy becomes the standard from which the original way looks like the deviation. Peter does not grant the inversion. He calls the heresies destructive, the followers followers of pernicious ways, and the slandered way the way of truth. The following is large. The name “truth” stays on the way that is evil spoken of, not on the way many have accepted. This is the assignment’s second clause in apostolic wording. Many shall follow does not transform damnable heresies into the faith. It explains how the faith comes to be maligned. Denial of the Master who bought them remains denial while it is popular. Swift destruction is not adjourned until the many become few. Popularity is not a stay of judgment and not a revision of the doctrine’s character.

**What the passage does not warrant here.** A reprint of the covert mechanism. Privy introduction was Chapter 70. The present object is the many and the slander. That “many” is a statistic to be applied as a headcount of later centuries. The word is the text’s. It establishes that largeness of following is compatible with destruction and with error. It does not authorize a census. That every popular teaching is a pernicious way. The verse describes the following of these teachers’ ways. A multitude that believes what is written is not guilty by being a multitude, as Acts 24:14 has guarded.

Thus:

> **Many will follow the destructive heresies, and because of those followers the way of truth will be evil spoken of. The following normalizes the heresy as the public path and makes the truth look like the deviation. It does not move the name “truth” onto the heresy. The way of truth remains the way of truth while it is slandered.**

# 71.42 Biblical Record: Titus 3:10–11 — The Heretick After Admonition

> “A man that is an heretick after the first and second admonition reject; Knowing that he that is such is subverted, and sinneth, being condemned of himself.”

**Context.** Titus is instructed about a man who is αἱρετικός. The action is rejection after a first and a second admonition. The knowledge that justifies the rejection is that such a man is subverted, sins, and is self-condemned.

**What is explicit.** The man is a factious chooser. Admonition is to be given, and given twice. The rejection follows the repeated admonition, not a first irritation. The state known is subversion (ἐξέστραπται, he has been turned inside out, perverted), ongoing sin, and self-condemnation (αὐτοκατάκριτος). The condemnation is of himself. It is not described as a guilt the church’s vote creates.

**What the passage warrants for this acceptance.** Settlement can be a person’s settled choice, and the church is told how to treat it once admonition has failed. The two admonitions are the evidence that the choice is no longer a momentary disagreement. It has remained after correction. That remaining is the accepted state in the man: he has accepted his own line as the line he will keep. The church’s rejection is a recognition of a subversion already true. It is not the act that makes his doctrine false. If he were false only because he was rejected, the self-condemnation would be unintelligible, and the admonitions would have had nothing to appeal to. The admonitions appeal to a standard already in place, the standard Titus has been told to hold in the letter. Patience before rejection is also not an act that makes the line true during the interval. Two warnings do not sanctify the third persistence. This guards a sentimental reading of institutional time: while a body is still “in dialogue,” the error is supposed to be unjudgable, and after a body has expelled someone, the expulsion is supposed to have created the error. The verse contradicts both. The man is subverted while he is being admonished, and he is self-condemned in the act of rejection. Procedure does not manufacture the doctrinal fact. It responds to it. The section does not write a handbook of trials. It uses the verse’s order: settled choice, admonition, rejection, self-condemnation already attaching to the chooser.

**What the passage does not warrant here.** That every person who asks a question is the heretick. The word is the factious man, known by a subversion that survives admonition. A hearer who is being taught is not this man by needing teaching. That the verse has defined heresy as disagreement with a later creed not stated in the letter. The content of sound doctrine in Titus is the letter’s own charge, under the writing. This chapter does not add a creed. That rejection is the church becoming infallible. Chapter 57 refused infallibility of the church as church. The verse does not confer it. It commands a response to a man who is already self-condemned by his subversion of the truth.

Thus:

> **The heretical man is a settled chooser, admonished twice, then rejected. His subversion and his self-condemnation are not created by the rejection, and they are not suspended by the patience of the admonitions. Institutional procedure neither transforms his error into truth during the wait nor creates its falsity by the vote.**

# 71.43 Biblical Record: 3 John 9–10 — Cast Out of the Church

> “I wrote unto the church: but Diotrephes, who loveth to have the preeminence among them, receiveth us not. Wherefore, if I come, I will remember his deeds which he doeth, prating against us with malicious words: and not content therewith, neither doth he himself receive the brethren, and forbiddeth them that would, and casteth them out of the church.”

**Context.** John commends Gaius for receiving brethren. He then names Diotrephes, who loves to have the preeminence, does not receive the apostle, speaks maliciously against them, forbids those who would receive the brethren, and casts them out of the church. Demetrius, in the next sentences, has a good report of all men, and of the truth itself.

**What is explicit.** Preeminence is the love named. Non-reception of the apostolic party is the doctrinal-practical stance. Malicious speech spreads it. The stance is enforced: those who would receive the brethren are forbidden, and are cast out. The casting is out of the church. The truth itself is able to give a good report, in the case of Demetrius. Membership after the purge is not said to be that report.

**What the passage warrants for this acceptance.** Institutional power can make an erroneous refusal the church’s actual boundary. Who is in and who is out becomes the accepted account of faithfulness. In this case the account is inverted. The apostle and the brethren are the ones not received, and the members who would receive them are the ones expelled. The accepted “church,” as Diotrephes polices it, is the body that has institutionalized the refusal. The expulsion does not make the refusal true, and it does not make the apostle false. Love of preeminence explains why a man would want the power to define the body. The explanation does not legitimize the definition. A church’s roll can be the instrument of an accepted error. Being on the roll is not the measure. The truth’s own report is the measure the letter names in Demetrius. Gaius’s faithfulness in receiving the brethren is the contrary practice, inside the same crisis. Refusal of Diotrephes’s boundary is possible and is commended. The accepted boundary is not fate.

**What the passage does not warrant here.** A complete doctrine of church membership. The exhibition is a particular casting out, against reception of the brethren, by a man who loves the first place. That every leader is Diotrephes. Preeminence and the casting are the marks in the text. An elder who receives the brethren and commends them to the word is not this portrait. That malicious speech as a turning-mechanism has been newly catalogued. Speech that draws disciples after the speaker was Chapter 70’s use of Acts 20. The present fact is the institutional result: expulsion as the enforcement of a received refusal.

Thus:

> **Diotrephes institutionalizes a refusal of the apostle and the brethren by forbidding reception and casting the willing out of the church. The purged roll does not transform the refusal into faithfulness. The truth itself remains able to give the good report. Preeminence can police a body. It cannot define the truth.**

# 71.44 Biblical Record: Revelation 2:6, 14–15, 20–24 — Holding a Doctrine, and Suffering a Teacher

> “But this thou hast, that thou hatest the deeds of the Nicolaitans, which I also hate.”

> “But I have a few things against thee, because thou hast there them that hold the doctrine of Balaam, who taught Balac to cast a stumblingblock before the children of Israel, to eat things sacrificed unto idols, and to commit fornication. So hast thou also them that hold the doctrine of the Nicolaitans, which thing I hate.”

> “Notwithstanding I have a few things against thee, because thou sufferest that woman Jezebel, which calleth herself a prophetess, to teach and to seduce my servants to commit fornication, and to eat things sacrificed unto idols. And I gave her space to repent of her fornication; and she repented not.”

> “But unto you I say, and unto the rest in Thyatira, as many as have not this doctrine, and which have not known the depths of Satan, as they speak; I will put upon you none other burden.”

**Context.** The letters to Ephesus, Pergamos, and Thyatira address churches in which a named teaching is hated, held, or suffered. Ephesus hates the Nicolaitans’ deeds, and Christ also hates them. Pergamos has there those who hold the doctrine of Balaam and those who hold the doctrine of the Nicolaitans, which thing Christ hates. Thyatira suffers Jezebel, who calls herself a prophetess, to teach and to seduce the servants. She was given space to repent and did not. The rest in Thyatira, who have not this doctrine and have not known the depths of Satan as they speak, are addressed separately. Balaam’s teaching is identified with the counsel that cast a stumblingblock before Israel: idol-food and fornication. Numbers 25 and 31:16 are the narrative that identification assumes. This chapter does not retell the whole narrative. It uses the letter’s identification of a doctrine held.

**What is explicit.** Deeds and doctrine are both named. A church can hate the deeds. A church can have the holders there. Holding is ἔχω and κρατέω: the doctrine is possessed and gripped. Christ’s hate is constant across the letters. Suffering (ἀφεῖς) is permission to teach. The woman calls herself a prophetess. The teaching seduces servants. Space to repent was given. Non-repentance is the fact. Some in Thyatira have not this doctrine. The holders’ own name for their knowledge is depths. Christ names those depths as Satan’s, “as they speak.”

**What the passages warrant for this acceptance.** These letters are the New Testament’s most direct exhibition of accepted heresy inside churches that are still addressed as churches. The doctrine of Balaam and the doctrine of the Nicolaitans are teachings with names, holders, and a place “there” in Pergamos. The church’s identity as a lampstand does not transform the doctrine. Christ hates it while the church harbors the holders. Thyatira’s act is specifically institutional: thou sufferest her to teach. Permission is the office granted. Self-naming as prophetess is the borrowed credential, the same class of claim as a letter as from the apostles or a chapel called the king’s. The credential does not make the teaching prophecy. Space given to repent is institutional and divine patience. It is not absolution, and it is not a transformation of the doctrine during the delay. She repented not. The settledness is refusal to leave the teaching after time was given. Time, which institutions often cite as maturation, is here the interval in which repentance did not occur. The doctrine remains what the holders have. “As they speak” is the important phrase for the flattering name. The accepted doctrine has an internal title, depths, a title of advanced knowledge. Christ’s title for the same knowledge is depths of Satan. The church’s toleration does not adjudicate between the titles. Christ does. The rest who have not this doctrine prove that a church can suffer a teacher without every member receiving the doctrine, and that the Lord addresses the non-receivers as faithful in that refusal. Their refusal is not schism against a truth. It is non-participation in a doctrine he hates. Ephesus shows the contrary institutional act at the level of moral judgment: hating the deeds Christ hates. A church is not condemned for refusing a doctrine. Pergamos is blamed for having the holders. Thyatira is blamed for suffering the teacher. Acceptance, not existence as a church, is the charge.

**What the passages do not warrant here.** A reconstructed sociology of the Nicolaitans beyond the deeds and doctrine the letters name. The text gives eating things sacrificed to idols, fornication, a doctrine held, and Christ’s hate. External legends are not premises. That “depths” has been mapped onto a later system of initiation. The phrase “as they speak” is enough. Their name for their doctrine is not Christ’s name for it. That the letters have been made a complete ecclesiology of lampstands. The use is the holding and the suffering. That patience toward Jezebel was approval. The text separates the space to repent from the non-repentance and from the hate.

Thus:

> **Churches can hate a doctrine’s deeds, harbor those who hold it, or suffer a self-named prophetess to teach it. Christ’s hate is not revised by the harbor or the permission. Space to repent does not transform the teaching. A title of depth, as they speak, does not transform it. The rest who have not this doctrine show that refusal inside the same church is possible and is addressed as fidelity.**

# 71.45 Biblical Record: 2 Thessalonians 2:2 and 2:10–15 — A Letter as from Us, and Believing a Lie

> “That ye be not soon shaken in mind, or be troubled, neither by spirit, nor by word, nor by letter as from us, as that the day of Christ is at hand.”

> “And with all deceivableness of unrighteousness in them that perish; because they received not the love of the truth, that they might be saved. And for this cause God shall send them strong delusion, that they should believe a lie: That they all might be damned who believed not the truth, but had pleasure in unrighteousness.”

> “Therefore, brethren, stand fast, and hold the traditions which ye have been taught, whether by word, or our epistle.”

**Context.** The Thessalonians are told not to be shaken by a spirit, a word, or a letter as from the apostles, to the effect that the day of Christ is at hand. The chapter goes on to speak of a falling away and of a man of sin. Those further figures are not this section’s subject. The acceptance-facts used here are the forged or pretended apostolic instrument, the believing of a lie by those who did not receive the love of the truth, and the command to hold what the apostles actually taught. Chapter 68 examined tradition as a secondary system and distinguished apostolic delivery from later tradition claiming primacy. That distinction is used, not retried. The traditions in verse 15 are what the Thessalonians were taught by the apostle’s word or epistle. They are not a blank name for whatever a later house calls tradition.

**What is explicit.** A letter can present itself as from the apostles and can trouble a church into a false account of the day. The readers are not to be shaken by that instrument. Those who perish are described as not having received the love of the truth. On that account a strong delusion is sent, that they should believe a lie. Pleasure in unrighteousness accompanies not believing the truth. The brethren are to stand fast and hold the traditions actually taught by apostolic word or epistle.

**What the passage warrants for this acceptance.** An erroneous account becomes the trouble of a church by wearing an institutional name. “As from us” is the same instrument as Acts 15’s false “from us,” now in documentary form. A body that receives the letter because it looks apostolic has accepted a doctrine of the day the apostle tells them not to receive. The look does not transform the letter into his letter. Believing a lie is then the settled inward side of acceptance. The lie believed does not become truth by being believed, even when the believing is of a powerful delusion. The cause named is moral and doctrinal together: they received not the love of the truth, and had pleasure in unrighteousness. This chapter does not develop the judicial sending of delusion into a full doctrine of reprobation, and it does not place aridity’s loss of judgment here under another name. The use is limited and firm. A lie can be the thing a people believe, and the believing is not a creative act. The contrary is holding what was actually taught. Apostolic delivery, already distinguished from a later system’s claim, is the holding that verse 15 commands. A church that instead holds the letter-as-from-us as its ordinary account has preferred the borrowed name to the teaching. The man of sin and the temple are not required for that conclusion, and they are not opened. To open them would be to write a different chapter. The acceptance-point stands on the verses quoted.

**What the passage does not warrant here.** A commentary on the whole of 2 Thessalonians 2. The falling away as a full eschatological file is not this assignment. That “traditions” here authorizes any subsequent institutional tradition as equal to the epistle. The clause defines the traditions as those taught by the apostle’s word or his epistle. Chapter 68’s refusal of later tradition as primary authority remains in force. That delusion as a psychological treatise has been written. The verse states a judicial consequence of refusing the love of the truth. The consequence includes believing a lie. The lie believed remains a lie.

Thus:

> **A letter as from the apostles can trouble a church into receiving a false account. The borrowed name does not make the letter apostolic. Believing a lie does not make the lie truth. The command is to hold what was actually taught by apostolic word and epistle. Reception of a counterfeit instrument is acceptance. It is not revelation.**

# 71.46 The Operations of Acceptance Joined

The exhibitions are one argument.

Installation gives an erroneous doctrine places, priests, and a calendar that imitates the true feast, and the people go, and the thing becomes a sin, and successors depart not from it. A tribal image receives a priesthood and runs for generations beside the house of God, while a Levite in the house is taken as proof of the Lord’s favor. Unison gives a king four hundred mouths and a messenger who begs the last prophet to speak like them. Sacred location gives Bethel’s priest a reason the word must stop: the king’s chapel. Popular love gives Judah an arrangement the people love, in which prophets prophesy falsely and priests rule by their means. Claimed possession gives the wise a sentence, We are wise and the law is with us, while the word is rejected and the pen is vain. A standing oracle says still, Peace, to people walking after their own heart. A people appoints as its prophet the man who prophesies wine. Generational manner gives the fathers’ not-hearkening, the children’s doing as the fathers, and the both-and of fearing the Lord and serving graven images unto this day. Teaching as doctrine gives the commandments of men the status of doctrine, and gives the breach of a commandment a schoolroom. School-doctrine gives the Pharisees and Sadducees a leaven that is their διδαχή. The seat gives a platform. The burden gives a load laid on shoulders. Agreement and penalty give the synagogue a rule already made, and give rulers a reason to withhold confession. The council’s command gives silence the force of an institution. False sending gives a salvation-requirement the costume of the mother-church. Social carrying-away gives Antioch a public walk in which even Barnabas is swept, contrary to the truth of the gospel. Following and slander give the destructive heresies a multitude, and give the way of truth a bad name. Holding and suffering give Pergamos its holders and Thyatira its prophetess. Show gives human ordinances the look of wisdom. Standing prohibition gives doctrines of demons the form of a rule about marriage and meats. Borrowed names give a letter the face of an apostle, a woman the title of prophetess, and a teaching the title of depth. Common report and glorying give a body a posture in which what God condemns is known and boasted in. Fleshly party-choice gives factions the false dignity of zeal. The factious man gives the church a chooser who has accepted his own line past admonition. The preeminent man gives the roll of the church an editor.

These are the how. They are operations of settlement. They presuppose Chapter 70’s turning wherever a truth was left, and they do not repeat that file’s causal inventory as if it had not been written. A body can inherit the settlement without having been the inventor. The son of a Jeroboam-king departs not from a sin he did not devise. The rest in Thyatira are not the ones who gave Jezebel the space. The chief rulers who believe are not the ones who first made the agreement. Inheritance of an accepted doctrine is still acceptance when the heir teaches it, enforces it, glories in it, or will not depart from it. Inheritance is not innocence. It is the ordinary form of normalization across time.

The operations often stack. Jeroboam stacks installation, likeness, popular going, and generational not-departing. Jeremiah’s Judah stacks love, priestly rule, claimed possession of the law, and a standing peace-oracle. Thyatira stacks permission to teach, self-naming, non-repentance after space given, a doctrine some hold, and a flattering title. The stack is how an error becomes obvious to a later reader of the narrative and invisible to the participant, who feels each layer as piety: the feast is like the true feast, the law is with us, she is a prophetess, the letter is from the apostles, the chapel is the king’s, the fathers did it, the many follow, the seat is Moses’. Piety of that stacked kind is the experience of accepted heresy. It is not the evidence of truth.

Thus:

> **Erroneous doctrines become normalized and institutionalized by operations the writings exhibit, often stacked, often inherited, often clothed in likeness, office, and holy names. The participant’s sense of piety is the settlement’s interior. It is not a second measure.**

# 71.47 Why Institutional Acceptance Does Not Transform Error into Truth

The how has been exhibited. The why is now gathered. It is not a new premise. It is the premises and the exhibitions speaking as one conclusion.

**Because truth is conformity to reality, and acceptance is a relation of persons to a doctrine.** A body’s receiving a sentence, teaching it, punishing its denial, or not departing from it for generations, changes the body. It adds a public fact. It does not change the reality the sentence claims to name. The exodus was not performed by calves because the calves were assigned it and the assignment was ordained as a feast. Jesus is the Christ while the synagogue agrees that confession will be punished. The creature is good while a doctrine forbids the meats. The way of truth is the way of truth while it is evil spoken of. If acceptance transformed error, these sentences would be false whenever the contrary institution was strong. The sentences stand. Therefore acceptance does not transform.

**Because the writing is not broken by the settlement.** The Scripture cannot be broken. A kingdom that departs not from Jeroboam’s sins does not break the commandment it is sinning against. A tradition that makes the commandment of none effect in the tradition’s courts does not make the commandment of none effect in reality. Jesus says they make it of none effect by their tradition, and he means their use and their teaching, not the commandment’s fall from the status of God’s word. The Graphē remains the primary written authority over the chapel that cites itself as the reason the Graphē’s messenger should leave. Bethel does not become the measure. Amos’s word is not unmade.

**Because the exhibitions contain the refusal inside the scene.** The multitude is forbidden as a guide into evil. The sign that comes to pass is denied a right to be hearkened to. The four hundred are contradicted by one prophet who will say what the Lord says, and the narrative’s death agrees with the one. The book found in the house condemns the fathers. The apostles obey God rather than the council. The letter from Jerusalem denies a commandment attributed to Jerusalem. Paul worships according to the way they call heresy, believing all that is written. Paul withstands a walk that is not upright according to the truth of the gospel. Christ hates the doctrine the church suffers, and he speaks peace to those who have not that doctrine. A transformation-theory cannot read these scenes. In each, the accepted doctrine is present and is judged false by a word that does not consult the acceptance.

**Because confirming credentials are explicitly denied transforming power.** Deuteronomy 13 is the strongest credential, a wonder that comes to pass, and the other gods remain unlawful. Micah’s Levite is a credential of office, and the image remains an image. The feast’s likeness to Judah’s feast is a credential of resemblance, and the month remains devised of a heart. The claim to possess the law is a credential of custody, and the rejected word remains rejected. Self-naming as prophetess is a credential of title, and the teaching remains seduction. A letter as from us is a credential of claimed apostolic origin, and the readers are told not to be shaken. If the lesser credentials of chapel, age, and title cannot do what a successful wonder cannot do, they cannot transform error into truth.

**Because procedure, patience, and expulsion do not occupy the office of truth-making either.** Two admonitions do not sanctify the heretick’s line. The rejection does not create his self-condemnation. Space given to Jezebel does not become approval. Diotrephes’s casting out does not become the truth’s report. The council’s stripes do not become the apostles’ duty. Institutional acts are real acts. They are the wrong kind of act to make a doctrine conform to reality. They can recognize a truth already given. They can enforce an error. They cannot mint truth.

The why is therefore not a mood of suspicion toward ordered life. Ordered life can hold the traditions taught by the apostle. Ordered life can hate the deeds Christ hates. Ordered life can find the book and do what is written. The why is a classification of acts. Reception of an error is a human and institutional act. Conformity to reality is not produced by that act. It is measured by the word of the God who does not lie, written in the Graphē, which a body’s vote does not edit.

Thus:

> **Institutional acceptance does not transform error into truth because truth is not a relation of acceptance, because the writing is not broken by the bodies that refuse to hear it, because the writings judge accepted doctrines inside the very scenes of their acceptance, because the strongest credentials are denied transforming power, and because procedure cannot mint what only conformity to reality can be. The accepted doctrine remains accountable to the Graphē.**

# 71.48 Reconstructing the Bible-Introduction Proposition: False Doctrines Are Recorded Without Being Sanctioned

A reconstructible proposition from the Bible Introduction materials states that the writings contain lies of Satan and of men, false doctrines, and many sins; that inspiration guarantees that these lies were told, these fallacies taught, and these crimes committed; and that inspiration does not make the lies and fallacies the truth or place God’s sanction on the crimes. Only what God says or teaches is truth he requires men to obey. The same materials distinguish the speaker: one must find out whether Satan, evil men, or God is speaking.

The proposition is not adopted as “the source says.” It is tested.

**What is claimed.** The presence of a false doctrine inside the inspired writing is evidence that the doctrine was taught, not evidence that the doctrine is true. Sanction is not identical with record.

**Premises already in force.** Chapter 57 established that the record does not fail as truthful divine writing of what it actually communicates, and that this non-failure is not the infallibility of every speech the record quotes. Chapters 62–63 established that the Scripture cannot be broken. The exhibitions of this chapter include speeches of Jeroboam, the four hundred, Amaziah, the troublers from Judaea, Diotrephes’s malice as reported, and Jezebel’s teaching as condemned. The record of those speeches is true record. The speeches are not thereby the Lord’s doctrine.

**Biblical correspondence.** The correspondence holds. The book of Kings truly records “behold thy gods, O Israel, which brought thee up,” and the same book says this thing became a sin. Jeremiah truly records the prophets’ “Ye shall have peace,” and the Lord says they speak a vision of their own heart. Revelation truly records that Jezebel teaches, and the Lord hates the doctrine and names the depths as they speak, not as he speaks. Gamaliel’s survival-test is truly recorded in Acts, and the apostles’ “We ought to obey God rather than men” is the sentence the narrative follows as duty. The Introduction’s distinction between record and sanction is the distinction these passages already practice.

**What follows.** The coherent remainder is received with full force for this chapter. A false doctrine’s being written down does not make it true. A false doctrine’s being accepted by a body does not make it true either. The two non-transformations are kin. If the holier event — being recorded in God-breathed writing — does not sanction the fallacy, the lesser event — being received by a king, a council, a school, or a church — does not sanction it. The investigator who treats institutional acceptance as stronger than inspiration’s mere record has inverted the order of authority this book has established. The body’s reception is not above the writing. It is one of the things the writing can record and judge.

**What does not follow, and is not adopted.** A theory of degrees of inspiration, which the same materials also sketch, as this chapter’s doctrine. Inspiration’s nature was the work of earlier movements. This chapter does not reopen it. The claim that every sentence spoken by a man inside Scripture is unsanctioned. The test the materials themselves state is the test the passages state: whether God is the one teaching the sentence. “Thou shalt not follow a multitude to do evil” is God’s command. “Go up; for the Lord shall deliver it” in the mouth of Ahab’s four hundred is not. The distinction is not a license to pick. It is the duty to read who is speaking and whether the writing judges the speech. That duty is the ordinary duty of reading, not a new governor placed over the Graphē.

Thus:

> **The record of a false doctrine guarantees that the fallacy was taught. It does not sanction the fallacy. Institutional acceptance, a weaker event than God-breathed record, does not sanction the fallacy either. Only what God teaches is the truth he requires to be obeyed.**

# 71.49 Reconstructing the Bible-Introduction Proposition: Disharmony with What Is Written, Applied to a Received Doctrine

A further reconstructible proposition from the same materials states that “as it is written” is one of the safest guides to get truth and to discover fallacy, and that any doctrine out of harmony with any written statement of Scripture is false. Chapters 69 and 70 already received the direction of this test and refused two distortions: a mechanical rule that a doctrine is true only when two or three verses are counted in its favor, and the isolation of a single clause from the corpus. The present test asks what the proposition contributes when the doctrine in question is no longer a fresh turning but the ordinary teaching of a house.

**What is claimed.** Disharmony with the writing discovers fallacy. The writing is the instrument. The claim, if it is to serve this chapter, must still discover fallacy when the doctrine has a chapel, a fathers’ manner, a council, or a school.

**Premises.** Isaiah 8:20: if they speak not according to this word, it is because there is no light in them. Josiah’s standard: all that which is written concerning us. Romans 16:17: contrary to the doctrine which ye have learned. Acts 24:14: believing all things which are written. Chapter 70: a turning is detected by disharmony with the truth the writing has given. Chapter 65: the Graphē is the primary written authority.

**Biblical correspondence.** The accepted doctrines exhibited in this chapter are detected by a given word, not by a lack of institutional support. The calves are exposed because the Lord, not the calves, brought Israel up, and because the priesthood and the feast were not what he commanded. The peace-oracle is exposed because it is a vision of the prophets’ own heart. Corban is exposed because it contradicts the commandment to honor father and mother. The added condition of circumcision for salvation is exposed because no such commandment was given and because the prophets’ words and the gift of the Spirit say otherwise. Jezebel is exposed because the Lord names the teaching as seduction and as a doctrine the rest do not have to know. Detection does not wait until the institution falls. Amos detects Bethel while Bethel is the king’s chapel. Micaiah detects the four hundred while they are speaking. The writing detects the accepted doctrine in the doctrine’s own house.

**What follows.** Disharmony with the Graphē is how an accepted heresy is known as heresy and not as development, maturation, or the sense of the church. A teaching that cannot stand with what is written has not become true by becoming ordinary. The detection does not depend on the teaching’s age, the teaching’s office, or the number of its followers. This is the criterion applied to settlement. Chapter 70 applied it to the movement of turning. The criterion is the same because the truth is the same. The object it now judges is the settled teaching.

**What does not follow, and is not adopted.** Isolation of one clause as a weapon, already refused. A margin’s count of two or three verses as the method of detection, already refused. The same materials’ further rules that require a plan of ages and a standing division of mankind into three classes before Scripture can be read are not adopted as the detector of accepted heresy. Those rules are a grid. A grid can itself become a received doctrine, taught as the way the writing must be taken. If it is received as the test, the test has been replaced by a secondary system, which Chapter 69 already forbade. The coherent remainder is the direction only: what is written discovers the fallacy of a doctrine, including a doctrine a body has long called the faith. The labour of holding the writings together remains Movement X. This chapter does not perform that labour by installing a shorter method.

Thus:

> **An accepted doctrine is still detected by disharmony with what is written. Age, office, and following do not supply the light the word withholds. Mechanical counting, isolation of clauses, and a dispensational grid are not adopted as the detector. The detector is the Graphē.**

# 71.50 Reconstructing the Bible-Introduction Proposition: Bias, Explaining-Away, and a Religious Background

A further reconstructible proposition lists, among those who find the writing hard, persons biased contrary to its true doctrines, and rebels who have listened to religious teachers who explain it away. The materials cite Matthew 15 and John 8 in that connection. Notes on Hebrews 5 add that men take a wrong attitude due to religious background. Chapters 69 and 70 received the corresponding cautions about bias and about teachers who void the word, and they refused to turn the whole list of classes into a single psychology. The present test asks what the proposition contributes to acceptance.

**What is claimed.** A prior commitment contrary to the doctrine, and a habit of listening to teachers who explain the writing away, and a religious background that trains the attitude, are conditions under which the writing is not grasped.

**What may be used now.** Matthew 15, the materials’ own citation, is this chapter’s exhibition of commandments of men taught as doctrines. The correspondence holds at that point. A tradition already accepted is precisely a bias contrary to the commandment, institutionalized as the elders’ doctrine, and taught by religious teachers. Explaining away is what the corban tradition does to the fifth commandment: the commandment is still recited in the environment, and the tradition explains how it need not be done. Religious background, in the limited sense of a doctrine a body has already made ordinary, is a real condition of not hearing the writing. Josiah’s fathers are the narrative form of that condition. The book was in the house. The background of not-hearkening was why the words, when read, came as wrath rather than as the familiar rule. An accepted heresy functions as a background. It makes the writing sound strange, as Hosea 8:12 says in one line that belongs here and not to a later file: “I have written to him the great things of my law, but they were counted as a strange thing,” while Ephraim multiplies altars to sin. The altars are ordinary. The written law is the strange thing. That inversion is what an accepted error does to perception. It does not, by making the law seem strange, make the altars the law.

**What may not be used as finished.** The ten classes as a complete causal theory of institutionalization. Laziness, worldliness, and other conditions on the list may be neighbours of a body’s life. They are not this chapter’s mechanisms, which have been read from the settlements themselves. Dullness of hearing as the loss of reasonability is Chapter 72. *Apeiros* and the babe are Chapter 73. The first principles as a foundation to be left are the book’s end. The claim that simply reading and believing, said in a way that denies hard texts and denies that Christ and the apostles expounded the writings, is all understanding requires, was already refused in Chapter 70 and is not revived. The present use stops at the coherence just stated: an accepted human doctrine can be the background and the explaining-away that teach a people to count the written law strange, and the strangeness does not transform the human doctrine into the law.

Thus:

> **Bias contrary to the commandment, teachers who explain the writing away, and a religious background of accepted precepts cohere with the settlements this chapter has exhibited. An ordinary error can make the written law seem strange. Strangeness to the heirs of that error is not a defect in the law and not a proof of the altars. Aridity, unskill, and the first principles are not written by this reception.**

# 71.51 Reconstructing the Aletheia Proposition: Ensuing in Damnable Accepted Heresies Called Doctrines

A reconstructible proposition from the project’s own doctrinal materials states that the absence of reasonability, understood as sound judgment, and the presence of unskillfulness are responsible for aridity and for tergiversation among persons and among age-old organizations ostensibly Christian; that this has ensued in damnable accepted heresies called doctrines; and that the lack of skill is blamed on failure to realize the scholarly impetus of the scripture and to labour with the diligence the writing requires. The same materials say that the design of God for persons, organizations, governments, and nations has its measure in conformity with the Graphē.

The proposition is not adopted as “the source says.” It is tested. Chapter 70 received it only where intellectual failure is joined to turning, and refused it as a finished sequence through aridity, accepted heresies, unskillfulness, and *spoudazō*. This chapter is the file in which accepted heresies are the assigned object. The test asks what part of the sentence the present evidence establishes.

**What is claimed.** Turning, among persons and among old organizations that claim the Christian name, issues in heresies that are damnable, that are accepted, and that are called doctrines. The organizations’ age and their Christian name are not, in the proposition, a shield. The measure for organizations as for persons is conformity with the writing. The root the proposition names is still unskill and the failure of diligence.

**Premises.** Chapter 70 established the turning, including among churches, households, and elders arising from within. Chapter 68 established that the Christian name, antiquity, and institutional recognition do not confer primary authority. Chapters 1–3 established that institutional consensus does not create truth. The exhibitions of this chapter show doctrines held, taught, suffered, and followed, under holy names, inside bodies that still speak of the Lord.

**Biblical correspondence.** The correspondence that holds is the correspondence between a body’s reception and the status “called doctrines.” Matthew 15 names the act: teaching for doctrines the commandments of men. Revelation 2 names “the doctrine of Balaam,” “the doctrine of the Nicolaitans,” and “this doctrine” which some have. The holders’ speech names depths. Jezebel calls herself a prophetess. Second Peter 2 names heresies of destruction which many follow. The phrase “called doctrines” is not a sneer added by the leaflet. It is a fair description of what the texts exhibit: the error receives the name and the office of doctrine. Age-old continuance is also exhibited, without the leaflet’s adjective being a statistic: until the captivity, unto this day, children and children’s children, the sins departed not from, the fathers who have not hearkened. An organization that claims the Lord’s name is not protected by the claim. Ephesus, Pergamos, and Thyatira are churches, and the hate, the holding, and the suffering are inside the letters to churches. “Ostensibly” is a just caution where a body says it is the Lord’s and teaches what he hates. Chapter 68 already warranted the caution: saying is not being. Conformity with the Graphē as the measure for a body, and not only for a private person, coheres with Amos at the chapel, with Josiah under the book, and with the way of truth remaining the way of truth while many follow something else.

**What follows.** The coherent remainder is received with full force at this level. Erroneous doctrines do become the accepted teachings of bodies, including bodies of long continuance that keep the holy name, and the name “doctrine” is part of the acceptance. That acceptance does not transform the error. Damnable, in the sense 2 Peter 2 gives, belongs to heresies of destruction. It is not a decorative intensifier, and it is not a license to attach the word to every teaching this chapter has not examined. The ensuing is real: a turning can issue in a held doctrine. Chapter 70 reserved that ensuing. This chapter has exhibited it. The exhibition does not require the leaflet’s order of causes as its proof. The writings supply the proof.

**What does not follow, and is not adopted.** The adverb “predominantly,” as a weighed finding that failed judgment and failed skill are the main causes and that love, fear, preeminence, political installation, and pleasure in unrighteousness are minor. Those causes stand in the texts just exhibited. The single ordered chain in which aridity is the first product, tergiversation the second, and accepted heresies the third is not adopted as a result this chapter has established. The book’s order is the locked order: turning, then accepted heresies, then aridity, then unskillfulness. The leaflet’s sentence arranges the titles differently. The arrangement is a map of concerns, not a finding that reorders the investigation. Aridity remains Chapter 72. *Apeiros* remains Chapter 73. *Spoudazō* remains Movement X. Using the ensuing as if those causes had been demonstrated would close later files inside a leaflet’s syntax. The identification of “damnable accepted heresies” with the leaflet’s own later constructions, or with any named modern party, is not adopted. The converse identification, in which those constructions are declared the cure because the leaflet named the disease, is not adopted either.

Thus:

> **The reconstructible sentence is received where it says that accepted heresies are called doctrines, that age and a Christian name do not shield an organization, and that the measure for a body is conformity with the Graphē. It is not adopted as a finished causal chain through aridity, unskillfulness, and the labour of diligence, and it is not adopted as a verdict on the leaflet’s own system. The writings have exhibited the ensuing. The later causes remain later.**

# 71.52 Reconstructing the Aletheia Proposition: Naming the Disease Does Not Certify the Doctrines That Follow

A second reconstructible feature of the same materials is that, having named damnable accepted heresies, they proceed to state what repentance, faith, baptisms, resurrection, and judgment are to mean, including counts of baptisms, sequences of resurrections and raptures, and a map of the underworld, and they attach those constructions to passages by citation. They offer the constructions as the first principles a hearer should receive.

The feature is not adopted as “the source says.” It is tested as an illustration of this chapter’s object. Chapter 69 refused the grid the seat of sense. Chapter 70 refused it the offices of cause-list and of cure for turning. The present question is whether a warning against accepted heresies certifies the doctrines that follow the warning, and whether those doctrines, if received because the document named the disease, would be an instance of the acceptance this chapter has defined.

**What is happening in the materials.** A true concern — that bodies call destructive errors doctrines — is stated. Then a system is spoken as the sense of the passages about the first principles. If that system is received as the orthodox replacement for accepted heresies because it denounced accepted heresies, the denunciation has been used as a credential. The credential is the same kind as the credentials already refused: a holy word retained, here the word about heresy and about the Graphē, and a grid assigned the dignity of the doctrine. The grid may contain sentences the writing teaches. Those sentences remain the writing’s where they correspond. The grid as a grid is still a teaching a body can accept. Acceptance of it as the faith, on the ground that the document warned against false faiths, is the operation of borrowed name and claimed possession. The document claims the writing. The claim does not transform every construction in the document into the writing.

**Premises.** Institutional acceptance does not transform error into truth. A borrowed apostolic or biblical face does not make a teaching apostolic. Chapter 70 established that a true warning and an unadopted grid can stand in one document. The present premise is acceptance, not turning. A doctrine becomes an accepted heresy only if it is erroneous and received as the ordinary teaching. This test does not first assume the grid is erroneous in each article. It refuses the path by which it would be installed as the standard that defines which heresies are heresies.

**What follows.** A document that warns against accepted heresies is itself capable of offering doctrines for acceptance, and the warning does not certify them. Each sentence meets the writing on its own. The true warning does not canonize the next paragraph. The false paragraph, where it is false, does not erase the true warning. This is the chapter applying its own conclusion to a source it has used. The application is required by honesty. A book that judged Bethel, the four hundred, the synagogue, and Thyatira, and then exempted its research materials from the same test, would be performing the acceptance it has just described: a teaching called doctrine because the source that attacked false doctrines said it. The First Principles are not taught or corrected here. The materials name them. The teaching of them is the book’s end, after the labour-movement. The reader is not left without a measure. The measure is the Graphē, which is why the warning about doctrines called doctrines could be received in the previous section at all.

**What does not follow here.** That every sentence in the materials has been adjudicated as an accepted heresy. This chapter does not write that commentary. It refuses the grid the office of replacement orthodoxy. That organizations are exempt from the measure because the measure is inconvenient for a source that speaks to organizations. The materials themselves extend the measure to organizations. The extension is what makes the self-application necessary.

Thus:

> **A warning against accepted heresies does not certify the doctrines printed after the warning. If those doctrines were received as the faith because the warning was true, the reception would be the acceptance-pattern this chapter has judged. True warning and unadopted grid can stand in one document. Each sentence meets the writing. The grid is not the list of heresies and not the orthodoxy that replaces them.**

# 71.53 Competing Claim: The Multitude, the Consensus, and the Following Transform the Error

> **What many follow, what the prophets say with one mouth, and what the people love to have so, becomes the true doctrine. The way of truth is the way of the many. A remnant’s protest is private opinion.**

The claim fails.

Exodus 23:2 forbids following a multitude to do evil and forbids bending judgment after many. The prohibition is unintelligible if the multitude transforms evil into good. First Kings 22 exhibits the one mouth at full strength, about four hundred, and the favorable consensus is false. Jeremiah 5:31 puts the people’s love in the horrible thing, not in the justification of it. Second Peter 2:2 puts the many on the side of the pernicious ways and keeps the name “way of truth” on the way they cause to be evil spoken of. Matthew 7:13–14, in a saying this chapter has not needed as a main exhibition and does not now turn into a sociology of numbers, says the gate is strait and the way narrow that leads to life, and few find it, while the way to destruction is broad and many enter it. The saying is not a proof that fewness is truth. It is a refusal of the inference that many-ness is truth. Acts 24:14 remains the guard against the opposite romance. The claim’s grain of use is only this: a multitude is a real pressure, and a teacher who ignores it will not understand why an error feels obvious. Pressure was never the disputed fact. Transforming power was. The texts deny the power.

Thus:

> **Consensus, love of the arrangement, and the following of many make a doctrine ordinary. They do not make it true. The way of truth can be the way that is evil spoken of. The multitude can be the way into evil.**

# 71.54 Competing Claim: The Fathers, the Age, and the Not-Departing Transform the Error

> **What our fathers practiced, what has stood until the captivity, what children and children’s children do unto this day, and what kings departed not from, has by age become the covenant. Antiquity is the final commentary.**

The claim fails.

Josiah’s words are the direct answer. Wrath is kindled because the fathers have not hearkened unto the words of the book, to do according unto all that which is written. The fathers are the accused, not the canon. Second Kings 17:41 records the children’s imitation of the fathers as the continuance of image-service, not as its cleansing. Judges 18 records duration until the captivity as the duration of the graven image, while the house of God is named separately at Shiloh. Second Kings 17:22 records that Israel departed not from Jeroboam’s sins, and the not-departing is the charge, not the absolution. Chapter 68 already refused antiquity as a creator of primary authority. The present failure is the specific form that refusal takes when antiquity is the accepted doctrine’s argument for itself. A long-held error is a long error. Steadfastness in it is persistence, not development into truth. The claim’s grain of use is that fathers really do transmit a manner, and a son can think the manner is the faith because he never saw its installation. Transmission explains the feeling of obviousness. It does not amend the book the fathers did not heed.

Thus:

> **Generational manner and dynastic not-departing are the forms of normalization across time. They do not transform the sin into the covenant. The book judges the fathers. Duration of an image is not identity with the house of God.**

# 71.55 Competing Claim: Office, Chapel, Seat, and Penalty Transform the Error

> **The king’s chapel cannot be false while it is the chapel. Moses’ seat sanctifies whatever is bound from it. The synagogue’s agreement, the council’s command, and the power to cast out of the church decide who is in the truth. Office is the doctrine’s proof.**

The claim fails.

Amaziah cites the chapel and the court, and the citation is the false religion’s self-defense, not a successful proof. The Micah of Judges 17 cites the Levite, and the graven image remains. Matthew 23 concedes a seat and condemns burdens and inversions taught from the recognized teachers. The seat does not do the work the claim wants. John 9’s agreement is real and the Christ remains the Christ. Acts 5’s command is strait and the apostles ought to obey God rather than men. Third John 10’s casting out is real and the truth itself still gives the good report. Office, place, and penalty are instruments by which an error is institutionalized. Instruments are what the claim mistakes for measures. Chapter 68 refused recognition as primary authority. This refusal is that rank applied to the moment of enforcement. A true servant can sit in a true office and speak according to the writing. The possibility of faithful office is why the claim is plausible. The exhibitions are why it is false as a rule. The office does not prove the doctrine. The doctrine is proved, or exposed, by the writing.

Thus:

> **Chapel, seat, agreement, command, and expulsion can install an erroneous doctrine as the body’s rule. They cannot transform it into the word of God. Faithful office speaks according to the writing. It does not replace the writing.**

# 71.56 Competing Claim: A Sign That Comes to Pass, or Survival in Time, Transforms the Error

> **If the wonder comes to pass, the prophet should be hearkened to. If the work survives, it is of God. If it is suppressed or dies, it was of men. Institutional outcomes are the verdict.**

The claim fails.

Deuteronomy 13 states the claim’s strongest case and denies it. The sign comes to pass, and the hearer shall not hearken, because the doctrine is other gods and the measure is the commandment. Jeroboam’s cult survives through the reigns the books of Kings summarize, and the survival is a great sin departed not from, not a late legitimization. The apostles are beaten and commanded to stop, and the suppression does not make their witness a work of men. Gamaliel’s counsel is the claim in a cautious voice. It is counsel in the narrative, not the Lord’s test. A work of God can be opposed for a time. A work of error can be the manner unto this day. Outcomes are not nothing. They are not the ontological act the claim describes. Swift destruction is promised to the false teachers in 2 Peter 2, and many follow them before that destruction. The interval of success is not an interval of truth.

Thus:

> **A wonder that comes to pass does not license a doctrine contrary to the commandment. Survival of a cult does not sanctify it. Suppression of the witnesses does not falsify them. Time is a theater of acceptance and of judgment. It is not the author of truth.**

# 71.57 Competing Claim: The Name Transforms the Doctrine

> **If it is called the law of the Lord, the feast of Israel, prophecy, depth, or the faith, it is what it is called. If it is called heresy, it is heresy. The institutional name is the judgment.**

The claim fails in both directions.

Jeremiah 8:8 quotes the name “the law of the LORD is with us” and answers with the rejection of the word. First Kings 12’s feast is like unto the feast in Judah and is devised of a heart. Revelation 2:20 records “which calleth herself a prophetess” as her claim, not as the Lord’s commission. Revelation 2:24 records “depths” as they speak, and the Lord calls them depths of Satan. Second Thessalonians 2:2 records a letter as from us, and the church is told not to be shaken by it. Acts 24:14 records “heresy” as their name for the way, and Paul believes all that is written. The name is an instrument. Accusers use it to settle a verdict without the writings. Holders use it to settle a verdict without the writings. The principle of the chapter is that instruments of acceptance are not measures of truth. Holy vocabulary is the most successful instrument because it feels like agreement with God. Chapter 70 called retained vocabulary a disguise of turning. This chapter calls it the public dignity of a settled error, and calls the hostile name a dignity of another kind, the dignity of an institution’s condemnation. Neither dignity is the Graphē.

Thus:

> **Flattering names do not transform error into the law, the feast, the prophecy, or the depth they claim to be. Hostile names do not transform the way that believes the writings into heresy. The name is part of the settlement or part of the accusation. The writing is the measure.**

# 71.58 Competing Claim: Acceptance Is Harmless Because the Truth Still Stands

The claim fails.

It uses this chapter’s own non-transformation as if non-transformation meant non-importance. The texts that keep the truth standing also state the weight. This thing became a sin, and it became sin unto the house, even to cut it off. Wrath is kindled because the fathers have not hearkened. The truth’s survival is the reason the acceptance can be judged. It is not the reason the acceptance can be shrugged. Chapter 70 refused the same move against the turning. Glorying in the unmourned thing is the Corinthian form of the shrug. Paul says the glorying is not good.

# 71.62 Reason Through the Evidence

The inference can now be exhibited.

**Premise 1.** Truth is conformity to reality and is not created by belief, popularity, institutional consensus, or sincerity. The Graphē is the primary written authority. The record does not fail as truthful divine writing of what it actually communicates. The Scripture cannot be broken. Recognition of the corpus is not creation of the corpus (Chapters 1–3, 51–53, 57, 62–63, and 65, used not retried).

**Premise 2.** The authority of that writing is distinct from the authority claimed for a later statement of sense. Transfer fails. Despair fails (Chapter 66, used not retried).

**Premise 3.** Scripture is misread in five inspectable modes. The writing remains the measure. A correct reading remains possible. An incorrect reading remains incorrect (Chapter 67, used not retried).

**Premise 4.** Theology, philosophy, tradition, and institution are secondary systems. They may serve. None becomes primary written authority by Christian identity, academic status, antiquity, or institutional recognition (Chapter 68, used not retried).

**Premise 5.** When secondary sources govern the meaning of Scripture, the seat of sense is occupied and the test is reversed. The writing is not broken. Service is not the event (Chapter 69, used not retried).

**Premise 6.** Doctrinal turning aside is an inspectable movement from a truth the Graphē has given, carried by named causes and mechanisms, permitted by intellectual failures that do not excuse it. The truth left remains the measure. Continuance is a real contrary (Chapter 70, used not retried).

A sign that comes to pass does not license other gods (Deuteronomy 13). A shrine with a Levite becomes a tribal cult until the captivity, beside Shiloh (Judges 17–18). **Premise 8.** Reconstructible Introduction and Aletheia propositions, tested rather than cited as authority, cohere where they distinguish record from sanction, where they detect fallacy by what is written even when a doctrine is the house’s doctrine, where they locate explaining-away and religious background in the work of accepted precepts, and where they say that accepted heresies are called doctrines inside bodies that claim the holy name and are still measured by the Graphē. They do not cohere, and are not adopted, where a verse-count or a dispensational grid becomes the detector, where a causal chain through aridity, *apeiros*, and *spoudazō* is treated as already finished, or where the warning against accepted heresies is used as the credential of the warner’s own system.

**Premise 9.** Competing reductions fail: multitude and consensus as transformation; fathers and age as transformation; office, chapel, seat, and penalty as transformation; successful signs and survival as transformation; names as transformation; harmlessness; sect-labels and minorities as automatic verdicts; Chapters 68 and 69 as already this file; this file as already Chapter 70 or as already Chapters 72–73 or Movement X.

> **The join is inspectable: primary writing + distinguished interpretation + catalogued misreading + ranked secondary systems + governance already exhibited + turning already exhibited + the writings’ own scenes of installation, unison, chapel, love, fathers, taught precepts, penalties, false sending, following, and suffered doctrine = a warranted account of how erroneous doctrines become normalized or institutionalized, and of why institutional acceptance does not transform error into truth, at this level and not beyond it.**

# 71.64 The Warranted Conclusion Stated with Full Force

The conclusion may now be stated without postponement and without inflation.

An accepted heresy is an erroneous doctrine that a body has received as its ordinary teaching or installed in the forms by which it orders belief, worship, and discipline.

**The how** is inspectable. Erroneous doctrines become normalized when they are the way a people go, the manner of fathers and children, the path many follow, the doctrine held, the report gloried in, the arrangement loved. They become institutionalized when they are given altars, priests, a devised calendar, a unanimous official mouth, a chapel, a seat and a burden, a school’s name, an agreement and a penalty, a council’s command, a false sending, a permission to teach, a roll enforced by expulsion, or a set of prohibitions taught as the rule. Inheritance, likeness to the true worship, custody of the law’s name, and titles of prophecy or depth are the ordinary clothes of the settlement. The clothes are not the transformation.

**The why** is equally inspectable. Institutional acceptance does not transform error into truth because truth is conformity to reality and acceptance is not that conformity; because the Scripture is not broken by the bodies that teach against it; because the writings judge these doctrines inside the scenes of their greatest acceptance; because a sign that comes to pass is still denied the power to license other gods; and because patience, procedure, survival, and expulsion do not mint truth. The Graphē remains the primary written authority over the settled doctrine. The church as church is not infallible. The chapel is not the writing. The fathers are not the book.

**The contrary** is real. Do not follow a multitude to do evil. Do not hearken to the successful false prophet. Speak what the Lord says rather than what the four hundred say. Hear the book against the fathers. Obey God rather than men. Believe all that is written rather than the accuser’s name. Withstand a walk that is not upright according to the truth of the gospel. Hate the deeds Christ hates. Do not hold the doctrine he hates. Do not be shaken by a letter as from the apostles. Hold what they actually taught. The rest who have not this doctrine are not a fiction. Refusal is obedience, because the acceptance did not create a new truth to which refusal would be unfaithful.

This continues Movement IX. It is not Chapter 70 retried. It is not Chapters 72–73. It is not Movement X. That is this chapter’s conclusion, stated here with full force.

Thus:

> **How erroneous doctrines become normalized or institutionalized has been examined, and why institutional acceptance does not transform error into truth has been shown. The acceptance is in force. Later questions remain later because they are different questions.**

# 71.65 Apply the Standard

The standard has been applied.

A doctrine that has become the ordinary teaching of a body is now to be asked not only, “What truth was left, and by what mechanism?” — Chapter 70’s question — but, “Has this erroneous teaching become what the body holds, ordains, loves, commands, or will not depart from, and is that reception being offered as if it were the truth?” Was a feast devised, and is likeness to the authorized feast being treated as identity? Do the official mouths agree, and is agreement being treated as the word of the Lord? Is the place cited as the reason the contrary word must stop? Do the people love the arrangement? Do the wise say the law is with them while the word is rejected? Is the fathers’ manner the argument? Are commandments of men being taught for doctrines? Is a school’s doctrine the leaven the disciples were told to beware? Is confession punished, and is the punishment being treated as discernment? Did a requirement travel under a sending the senders deny? Are many following, and is the way of truth the thing now evil spoken of? Does a church suffer a teacher and call the suffering patience or depth? Does a prohibition of what God created present itself as the stricter faith?

The questions are practical without becoming a tribunal’s handbook, a history of later parties, or a technique of interpretation. A people who confess that Scripture is primary may still depart not from a sin a founder installed. A church addressed as a church may still hold a doctrine Christ hates. A ruler may believe and still leave the public doctrine in place for love of the institution’s praise. A research document may warn against accepted heresies and still offer its own grid for acceptance under the warning’s name. A hard sentence of Scripture may still be hard without being a human ordinance, and a human ordinance may still be severe without being Scripture.

The standard also protects. Reception of the apostles’ doctrine is not an accepted heresy. A party-name in an accuser’s mouth is not a verdict. An open question is not a calf. A servant who speaks according to the writing is not a Nicolaitan by having an office. A remnant that does not have the doctrine is not faithless to the church by refusing what Christ hates. Josiah is not a faction by tearing his clothes at the book. Micaiah is not a heretic for declining to speak like the four hundred. The protection is the same fact as the judgment: the writing is the measure of both the faithful holding and the accepted error.

The standard is the Graphē. Accepted heresy is erroneous doctrine in the form of a body’s ordinary or official teaching. Institutional acceptance is not an ontological act. The openings Chapter 70 named do not, by being inherited in a feast or a school, move the standard.

Thus:

> **The standard has been applied: how erroneous doctrines become normalized or institutionalized, and why that acceptance does not transform error into truth, have been identified as Movement IX’s sixth word, not announced as a slogan, and not postponed as if the settlement were weak.**

# 71.66 From Accepted Heresies to Aridity

A settlement, now controlled as an inspectable reception that does not create truth, still leaves a further question.

The acceptance-file is in force. Erroneous doctrines become ordinary and official by the operations exhibited. Acceptance does not transform them. The writing remains the measure. Refusal remains possible. Those sentences are the cleared ground. They are not yet the dedicated examination of aridity in connection with deficient intellectual engagement, poor judgment, and failure to labour properly in the Word.

If the acceptance were not stated, the next question would still be how error becomes the house’s doctrine. The acceptance has been stated. If this chapter were already the account of aridity, the next file would be repetition. This chapter is not that account. The next question is therefore the dryness the locked assignment names: Aridity and the Loss of Reasonability.

A people can love to have the false arrangement, can count the great things of the law a strange thing, can be deceived by fair speeches, and can call the strange thing wisdom because the background has trained them. Those facts raise the question of judgment. They do not answer it. The leaflet placed aridity before the heresies in its own sentence. The book’s order is the order the turning and the acceptance have now earned: after a doctrine has become the ordinary teaching, the intellectual condition in which a body can no longer tell reasonability from the chapel’s voice is a further object. Chapter 72 must examine that condition. It must not steal this settlement as if the how of acceptance had not been stated. This file must not steal the dryness as if dullness, poor judgment, and the failure to labour had already been the chapter.

That is the proper open of the next chapter. The acceptance was never meant to be a slogan that finishes Movement IX, and it was never meant to deny that settled error has an intellectual interior. It was meant to be the naming of normalization and institutionalization, and of the non-transformation, sufficient to justify taking the next question, which is the question the movement itself forces once the doctrine is ordinary and the ordinary has not become true: what is the aridity, the loss of reasonability, that stands in connection with deficient intellectual engagement, poor judgment, and failure to labour properly in the Word?

The next file must not treat this acceptance as if the settlement had not been described. This file must not treat that aridity as if Chapter 72 were already written. Naming the neighbourhood is not performing the examination.

# What Has Been Established

This chapter has established:

1. **Accepted heresy, in this argument, is an erroneous doctrine that a body has received as its ordinary teaching or installed in the forms by which it orders belief, worship, and discipline.**
2. **Normalization is the error become the usual teaching. Institutionalization is the error set into place, office, calendar, official speech, penalty, or permission to teach. Either mode, or both, is the acceptance.**
3. ***Hairesis* ranges from party-name to fleshly faction to destructive choice. *Hairetikos* names the settled chooser. *Didachē* names the teaching held or not held. The words stay distinct. The assigned object is one.**
4. **Acceptance is not the turning retried, not governance retried, not the rank of institutions retried, not every corporate reception, and not every open question.**
5. **Reception of what the writing gives is not an accepted heresy. A hostile sect-name does not make it one.**
6. **Truth is conformity to reality. Acceptance is a fact about a body. It is not an act that conforms an error to reality.**
7. **The Scripture is not broken by a settled contrary doctrine. The Graphē remains the measure of the chapel, the council, the school, and the fathers.**
8. **The settlement is inspectable because the writings exhibit the operations. It is not a fog that follows turning.**
9. **Refusal is a real contrary. The accepted state is not fate.**
10. **Exodus 23:2 forbids following a multitude to do evil. Number does not transform.**
11. **Deuteronomy 13 denies transforming power to a sign that comes to pass, where the doctrine is other gods.**
12. **Judges 17–18 exhibits a tribal image-cult with priests, lasting until the captivity beside Shiloh. A Levite does not certify the shrine.**
13. **Jeroboam’s installation — calves, non-Levitical priests, a feast like Judah’s, a month devised of his heart — became a sin the people went to and later kings departed not from. Likeness and continuance do not transform the sin.**
14. **Second Kings 17 exhibits generational syncretism: the Lord’s name, the nations’ gods, the fathers’ manner, unto this day. The mixture is not the commandment.**
15. **The four hundred with one mouth do not make a favorable oracle true. Micaiah’s measure is what the Lord says.**
16. **The king’s chapel is cited to stop the word. The citation is the accepted cult’s self-defense, not its justification.**
17. **Judah loves a false prophetic-priestly order, claims the law while rejecting the word, and hears a standing peace-oracle. Love, pen, and repetition do not transform the vision of the prophets’ own heart.**
18. **A people can make the liar who prophesies wine into their prophet. The appointment does not make the lie the Lord’s word.**
19. **The book found in the house judges the fathers’ not-hearkening. Antiquity of practice is not the covenant.**
20. **Calling evil good does not move the evil. Fear of the Lord taught by the precept of men is still human precept.**
21. **Teaching the commandments of men for doctrines is vain worship. The elders’ acceptance does not make the tradition the commandment of God.**
22. **Breaking a commandment and teaching men so institutionalizes the breach as instruction.**
23. **The doctrine of the Pharisees and of the Sadducees is leaven to beware of. A school’s ownership of a doctrine does not make the doctrine safe.**
24. **Moses’ seat does not sanctify burdens and inversions bound from it. What is Moses is to be kept because it is Moses.**
25. **An agreement to expel confessors of Christ can be the enforceable public doctrine, even where some rulers believe. The agreement does not make non-confession true.**
26. **A council’s command not to teach in the name of Jesus does not become God’s will. God is to be obeyed rather than men. Survival in time is not the test.**
27. **A requirement can be received as a sending body’s doctrine when that body gave no such commandment. “From us” does not transform an added condition of salvation.**
28. **The way which they call heresy may be the way that believes all that is written. Institutional names do not decide, in either the hostile or the flattering direction.**
29. **Heresies among a church manifest the approved. They do not approve the faction. The “must” is not a blessing of party-making.**
30. **A respected company can be carried into a public walk contrary to the truth of the gospel. The company’s weight does not amend the gospel.**
31. **Heresies are works of the flesh. Habituation does not reclassify them as fruit.**
32. **What is commonly reported and gloried in is not thereby lawful. Corporate pride is an aggravation of acceptance, not a sanctification.**
33. **Fair speeches can make a line contrary to the doctrine learned seem acceptable to the simple. The learned apostolic doctrine remains the measure.**
34. **Ordinances of men become a body’s rule by a show of wisdom and humility. The show does not change their human origin.**
35. **Forbidding marriage and commanding abstinence from meats does not change the goodness of what God created to be received. A stricter-sounding rule is not thereby the truth.**
36. **Many follow destructive heresies, and the way of truth is evil spoken of. The following normalizes the error and slanders the truth. It does not move the name “truth.”**
37. **The heretical man, after two admonitions, is rejected as already subverted and self-condemned. Patience does not sanctify his line. Rejection does not create its falsity.**
38. **Casting out of the church can enforce a refusal of the apostle and the brethren. The purged roll is not the truth’s report.**
39. **A church can harbor holders of a doctrine Christ hates and can suffer a self-named prophetess to teach. His hate stands. Space to repent is not approval. The rest who have not this doctrine are addressed as such.**
40. **A letter as from the apostles can be the instrument of a false account. Believing a lie does not make the lie truth. The holding commanded is of what was actually taught.**
41. **The operations of settlement stack and are often inherited. Inherited piety is the interior feel of accepted heresy. It is not a second measure.**
42. **Record of a false doctrine in Scripture does not sanction it. Institutional acceptance, a weaker event, does not sanction it either.**
43. **Disharmony with what is written detects an accepted heresy in the house where it is ordinary. A verse-count and a dispensational grid are not adopted as the detector.**
44. **An accepted background can make the written law seem strange and can teach men to explain the commandment away. The strangeness does not transform the altars into the law.**
45. **The Aletheia sentence is received where accepted heresies are called doctrines, where age and a Christian name do not shield a body, and where the measure remains the Graphē. It is not adopted as a finished chain through aridity, *apeiros*, and *spoudazō*.**

52. **“Acceptance is harmless because the truth still stands” fails.**
53. **“Every sect-name is an accepted heresy” fails, and “every minority is faithful” fails.**
