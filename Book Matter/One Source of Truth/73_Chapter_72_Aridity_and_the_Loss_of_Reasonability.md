# ONE SOURCE OF TRUTH

## The Doctrine of the Graphē

# Chapter 72 — Aridity and the Loss of Reasonability

> **Movement IX — When Authoritative Truth Is Mishandled**

> **Central objective:** Examine aridity in connection with deficient intellectual engagement, poor judgment, and failure to labour properly in the Word, so that a settlement already shown in its operations is now met by the intellectual condition in which hearing, judgment, and labour no longer yield what the writing gives — and so that the dryness is refused the power to make the writing obscure, the accepted error true, or a correct reading impossible. This continues Movement IX. It uses Chapters 57, 62–63, 65, 66, 67, 68, 69, 70, and 71 as premises. It is not a second acceptance-file, not *apeiros*, and not Movement X or the First Principles written in advance.

## Chapter Assignment

Chapter 71 established Movement IX’s sixth word. An accepted heresy is an erroneous doctrine that a body has received as its ordinary teaching or installed in the forms by which it orders belief, worship, and discipline. The settlement is inspectable. Institutional acceptance does not transform error into truth. Truth remains conformity to reality. The Graphē remains the measure. Refusal of the multitude, the chapel, the fathers’ manner, and the suffered teacher remains a real contrary. The acceptance-file is in force.

Chapter 71 ended by naming the next necessary question, not a new curiosity:

> **What is aridity, and what is the loss of reasonability, now that erroneous doctrines have been shown becoming the ordinary and official teaching of a body without becoming true, and now that the next file’s first word is to be that dryness rather than a second acceptance-file, and rather than unskillfulness?**

That is this chapter’s assignment.

The locked Table of Contents states the work in one sentence:

> **Examine aridity in connection with deficient intellectual engagement, poor judgment, and failure to labour properly in the Word.**

The locked knowledge-flow places this examination after the settlement and before the dedicated examination of unskillfulness. Acceptance has been stated as acceptance. Stated acceptance still leaves the intellectual condition in which a person or a people can possess the writing, the time, the organs of hearing, and even a labour that looks like study, and still not yield the sound judgment the writing requires. A doctrine become ordinary is one object. A dryness in which engagement is deficient, judgment is poor, and the labour proper to the Word is not given, is a further object. The present file is that object, at the level the premises warrant, and not beyond it.

The present file does not re-narrate how erroneous doctrines become normalized or institutionalized. It cites Chapter 71 as established. It does not re-narrate the causes and mechanisms of departure. It cites Chapter 70 as established. It does not re-describe the occupation of the seat of sense. It cites Chapter 69 as established. It does not re-rank theology, philosophy, tradition, and institution. It cites Chapter 68 as established. It does not re-catalog how Scripture is misread. It cites Chapter 67 as established. It does not re-draw the distinction between the authority of the text and the authority of interpretation. It cites Chapter 66 as established. It does not re-prove that the Graphē is the primary written authority. It cites Chapter 65 as established. It does not re-answer why the Scripture cannot be broken, or why the record does not fail as truthful divine writing of what it actually communicates. It cites Chapters 62–63 and 57 as established. It does not write *apeiros* as a dedicated file. It does not write Movement X. It does not enter the First Principles. It examines aridity in its three assigned connections, without which Movement IX would possess a settlement and still lack the earned account of the dryness in which reasonability is lost.

A settlement, now distinguished from a mere turning and from a mere disagreement, still leaves a further question. If a body can teach an error as its doctrine without making the error true, what is the condition of hearing and judgment in which that doctrine can be lived as though it were the reasonable thing, and in which the writing, though given, does not yield in the hearer the consideration, the discrimination, and the labour it commands? If the dryness were already the settlement restated, the sixth word would be reprinted as the seventh. If the dryness were already the diagnosis of the unskillful, or already the method of diligence, the later files would be closed inside this one. If the examination stopped at the warning “do not be dull,” the connections would be a slogan. The present file is the aridity, at the level the writings and the locked assignment require, and not beyond it.

The remaining question of the book’s sequence follows. Sequence continues the investigation. It does not convert the present line into hesitation.

## Central Objective

> **To examine, from the biblical record and from premises already in force, aridity in connection with deficient intellectual engagement, poor judgment, and failure to labour properly in the Word, so that Movement IX’s seventh word is one inspectable verdict: aridity is the dryness in which the Word is given to be heard, judged, and laboured in, and the hearer does not yield the reasonability that such engagement requires; reasonability is sound judgment already defined, and its loss is the failure of that judgment toward the writing, not the disappearance of thought and not the obscurity of the Graphē; the writings exhibit the dryness as not-considering, dull hearing, a sealed book, a famine of hearing, feeding on ashes, a mind that does not pass the test, judgment omitted, an answer before hearing, a learning that never arrives, and a search that will not come; the three connections are the assignment’s own relations, read from the exhibitions, and not a single-cause system. The Word remains water, light, and measure. The Scripture is not broken by dull ears. A correct reading remains possible. Consideration, righteous judgment, and the labour the writing commands remain real contraries. The account keeps this dryness distinct from a second narrative of acceptance, from unskillfulness as a dedicated diagnosis, and from the labour-files. It refuses the reductions that blame the writing’s difficulty, that treat dryness as harmless because the truth still stands, that treat the loss as the abolition of a correct reading, that treat judicial hardening as an excuse or as the author’s injustice, that treat ever-learning as proof of engagement, that treat the chapel’s voice as reasonability once a doctrine is ordinary, and that treat this file as already Chapter 71, Chapter 73, or Movement X. > **Acceptance already stated → question of aridity → aridity and the loss of reasonability defined → the three connections distinguished → biblical words collected → objects distinguished → premises already in force used, not retried → one clean account of sequence and limits → positive warrant from the writing’s not being dry, from the writings’ exhibition of the dryness, and from the real possibility of the contrary → each exhibition examined with biblical record → the three connections joined → non-excuse and non-breakage stated from the exhibitions → reconstructible Introduction and Aletheia propositions tested as coherence → competing reductions refused → inspectable inference → warranted conclusion → sequence to *apeiros*.**

The chapter’s conclusion is affirmative:

> **Aridity is the dryness in which what the Graphē gives to be heard, judged, and laboured in does not yield, in the hearer, the sound judgment that engagement with that writing requires. Reasonability is that sound judgment: perception, distinction, inference, and conclusion accountable to grounds, exercised toward the writing. Its loss is not the silence of the mind. Men still reason, still learn, still claim wisdom, still search, and still tithe the small exactness. The loss is the failure of those acts to do the work the writing assigns them. The people do not know and do not consider, though the ox knows his owner. They are famished and dried up with thirst because they have no knowledge, and they are wise in their own eyes. Their heart is gross, their ears dull, their eyes closed, and the condition can be judicial after refusal without becoming the writing’s fault or the hearer’s excuse. A spirit of deep sleep is poured out, and the vision is as a sealed book: the learned cannot read it because it is sealed, and the unlearned cannot because he is not learned, while wisdom perishes. The idolater feeds on ashes and does not say that a lie is in his right hand, while the same prophet says that water is poured on the thirsty and floods on the dry ground. The fountain of living waters is forsaken, and the cisterns hewed cannot hold water. The man who trusts in man inhabits the parched places and does not see when good comes. Knowledge is rejected, and the people are destroyed for the lack. A famine is sent, not of bread, but of hearing the words of the Lord, and they run to and fro and do not find it, after they have commanded the prophets not to prophesy. A matter is answered before it is heard. Wisdom cries, and the simple love simplicity, and the reproof still says, Turn. The if-clauses of seeking knowledge as silver are the form of the labour, and the labour not given is the failure. Disciples reason about bread with eyes, ears, and a memory of the baskets, and do not understand. Men are asked why they do not, even of themselves, judge what is right, and are commanded to judge righteous judgment rather than appearance, and to judge what is said. They are slow of heart to believe all that the prophets have spoken. They search the scriptures and will not come to the one of whom the scriptures testify. Love is prayed up into knowledge and all judgment, that the excellent may be approved; the mind is to be renewed so that it may prove the will of God; and where God was not approved to be retained in knowledge, the mind given over is an unapproved mind. Judgment, mercy, and faith are omitted while mint is tithed. Some are ever learning and never able to come to the knowledge of the truth, and reprobate concerning the faith. Many things are hard to utter because the hearers have become dull of hearing, not because the things have been made mysteries. The same word that names the dullness commands, Be not slothful. The writing is not dry. Dullness does not break it. The loss does not transform an accepted error into truth, and it does not excuse the hearer who will not consider, judge, or labour. This continues Movement IX. It is not Chapter 71 retried. It is not Chapter 73. It is not Movement X. That is this chapter’s conclusion, stated here with full force.**

The remaining questions follow by sequence. The verdict is already in force as a warranted account of aridity and the loss of reasonability, not as postponed guesswork.

# 72.1 The Question Created by the Preceding Chapter

Movement IX is open. Its first word has been stated. The authority of the Graphē and the authority claimed for a later statement of sense are two objects. Transfer fails. Despair fails. Its second word has been stated. The later act fails in inspectable modes: poor perception, deficient understanding, faulty inference, context failure, and improper handling. The writing remains the measure. A correct reading remains possible. An incorrect reading remains incorrect. Its third word has been stated. Theology, philosophy, tradition, and institution are secondary systems. They may serve under the writing. None becomes the primary written authority merely by claiming Christian identity, academic status, antiquity, or institutional recognition. Its fourth word has been stated. When commentaries, traditions, institutions, teachers, systems, or experiences govern the meaning of Scripture instead of serving under it, the secondary source occupies the seat of sense and the test is reversed. The writing is not broken. Service is not the event. Its fifth word has been stated. Doctrinal turning aside is an inspectable movement from a truth the Graphē has given, carried by causes and mechanisms the writings name, permitted by intellectual failures that do not excuse it. The truth left remains the measure. Continuance is a real contrary. Its sixth word has been stated. An erroneous doctrine can become a body’s ordinary teaching or its installed rule, and the reception does not make the error true. Refusal remains possible. The distinction-file is in force. The catalog is in force. The rank is in force. The displacement-file is in force. The turning-file is in force. The acceptance-file is in force.

Chapter 71 established the settlement. That conclusion is a premise here. A stated acceptance still leaves a further question: what is the aridity, and what is the loss of reasonability, in connection with deficient intellectual engagement, poor judgment, and failure to labour properly in the Word?

A people can receive the acceptance-file as a piety — “we know that a chapel does not make an error true” — and still not consider, still answer a matter before they hear it, still omit judgment while they are exact about the small thing, still learn without coming to knowledge, still treat the book as sealed, still count their own prudence as wisdom. They can quote Chapter 71’s refusal of transformation and still be unable to tell the writing’s reason from the voice they have long called reasonable. The settlement has named the reception. The dryness of judgment is a further object.

The question is therefore not:

> **How do erroneous doctrines become normalized or institutionalized, and why does acceptance fail to transform them?**

That question has been answered.

The question is:

> **What is aridity, and what is the loss of reasonability, in connection with deficient intellectual engagement, poor judgment, and failure to labour properly in the Word?**

If Movement IX proceeded from the acceptance to unskillfulness without examining aridity, it would skip the locked step. If it treated this file as already Chapter 71, it would convert a dryness-file into a second acceptance-file. If it treated this file as already Chapter 73 or Movement X, it would close the movement’s later question, and the labour-movement, inside its seventh word. The present file is the dedicated examination of aridity and the loss of reasonability.

That is a different operation from a repeated warning that dullness is bad. It is the operation this chapter must perform.

# 72.3 Define the Question: Aridity and the Loss of Reasonability

**Aridity**, in this chapter, is the dryness in which the Word is given to be heard, judged, and laboured in, and the hearer does not yield the reasonability that such engagement requires.

**Loss of reasonability**, in this chapter, is the failure of sound judgment toward that Word. Reasonability is not a new faculty invented for this file. Chapter 6 already named sound judgment as the discipline accountable to grounds. The loss is the condition in which that discipline does not do its work: the mind still moves, and the movement does not perceive, distinguish, prove, or conclude as the writing requires.

The English words are not biblical hapaxes to be imported as a new revelation. They are the locked assignment’s names for a condition the writings already exhibit in the language of thirst, parched places, a sealed book, a famine of hearing, dullness of hearing, a heart that does not consider, ashes for food, and a mind that does not pass the test. “Aridity” in later devotion often means a feeling of spiritual dryness, a season in which prayer feels empty. That later psychological sense is not the premise of this chapter, and it is not silently installed as the meaning of every dry image below. The argumentative force of the assignment is narrower and prior: intellectual engagement is deficient, judgment is poor, and the labour proper to the Word is not given, and the question is what that dryness is and how the three stand together.

The phrase is easily abused. In ordinary speech “arid” may be heard as a sneer at a mind one finds uninteresting, so that every opponent is dry by disagreement. “Reasonability” may be heard as the manners of a class, so that whatever sounds measured is already sound judgment. “Loss” may be heard as the abolition of the intellect, so that the dry hearer is no longer a hearer who can be commanded. None of those senses is this chapter’s object.

Four elements belong to the definition.

First, **a dryness relative to a given Word**. The object is not the absence of all mental life, and not the climate of a land as meteorology. It is the condition in which what God has given to be known does not become, in the hearer, knowledge, consideration, judgment, and the fruit of labour. The ox knows. Israel does not consider. The book is in the hand. It is not read. The baskets have been counted. They are not understood. The prophets have spoken. The heart is slow to believe all. Dryness is measured against a gift, not against a mood.

Second, **the loss of sound judgment, not the loss of the power to produce sentences**. Reasonability fails when judgment no longer approves what ought to be approved and refuses what ought to be refused, according to what is written and what has been shown. Men profess themselves wise and become fools. They are wise in their own eyes. They omit judgment. They answer before they hear. They reason, and the reasoning is about bread when the sentence was about leaven. The loss is compatible with fluency. Fluency is not the disputed fact. Accountability to grounds is.

Third, **the three connections as the form of the examination**. Deficient intellectual engagement, poor judgment, and failure to labour properly in the Word are not three optional illustrations. They are the assignment. Engagement is the intake and the holding. Judgment is the discrimination and the proving. Labour is the seeking, searching, meditating, and preparing of the heart that the writings command toward the Word. Aridity stands in connection with all three. A chapter that described a feeling of dryness and never reached engagement, judgment, and labour would have changed the assignment. A chapter that wrote the positive method of labour, the lexical file of unskill, or the foundation-list would have changed it in the other direction.

Fourth, **not those other claims**. The definition excludes, as not this object: a retry of Chapter 71’s settlement; *apeiros* as unskillfulness in the Word; Movement X’s labour-files; the First Principles as this file’s achievement; a history of mystical dryness as the premise; a technique of study as the cure written in advance. Those exclusions are part of the definition. They are not a later apology.

Thus:

> **The question of this chapter is not how error becomes the house’s doctrine, and not yet how unskill is diagnosed or how diligence is exercised as a method. Those questions have been examined or remain later. The question is what the dryness is, and what the loss of reasonability is, in connection with deficient engagement, poor judgment, and failure to labour properly in the Word.**

# 72.4 The Three Connections Named by the Assignment

The assignment names three relations. They are distinguished here so that a dull ear is not treated as already the whole of poor judgment, and so that a failure to labour is not treated as a mere insult added after the argument.

**Deficient intellectual engagement.** Engagement is deficient when the word, the time, the eyes, the ears, or the memory are present, and the acts of considering, perceiving, understanding, remembering, and believing what was spoken are not. The mark is presence without yield. Isaiah’s people do not know and do not consider. The disciples have eyes and do not see, ears and do not hear, and do not remember the baskets. The Hebrews have become dull of hearing, and many things are hard to utter for that reason. The heart is slow to believe all that the prophets have spoken. Some are ever learning and never able to come to the knowledge of the truth. Engagement can be noisy and still deficient. The deficiency is the failure of the act, not the absence of a religious hour.

**Poor judgment.** Judgment is poor when the discriminating and proving act does not do what the writings assign it: to judge what is right, to judge righteous judgment rather than appearance, to approve the excellent, to prove the will of God, to hear a matter before answering it, to include judgment among the weightier matters rather than omit it while the small exactness is performed. The mark is a verdict that does not pass the test of the grounds. Wisdom in one’s own eyes is this failure wearing the name of prudence. A reprobate mind is this failure as a mind that does not approve. The chapel’s unanimity, already judged in Chapter 71 as a settlement that does not create truth, is not itself this definition. The new object is the judgment that can no longer tell the writing’s reason from a voice it has ceased to test.

**Failure to labour properly in the Word.** The labour is the seeking, searching, meditating, and heart-preparation the writings themselves command toward the book, the law, the testimonies, and the knowledge of God. The failure is the not-giving of that labour, or the giving of a labour that does not come to the knowledge the writing holds out: knowledge rejected; a book sealed and unread; a search that will not come to the one of whom the scriptures testify; fallow ground unbroken; silver-seeking described and not done. The mark is a Word not worked. The positive method of that work — scholarly impetus, the stages of perception and learning, *spoudazō*, exercise, rightly dividing — remains later. The failure is readable now because the commands and the refusals are already in the texts.

The relation among the three is exhibited, not theorized as a machine. Dull hearing is an engagement-failure, and it leaves judgment without the matter it would judge, and it is the opposite of the labour of hearing a matter through. Answering before hearing is a judgment-failure whose form is a refused labour of hearing. Ever-learning is an appearance of labour whose yield is the engagement-failure of never coming to knowledge. The three are distinguishable. They confirm one another in the same hearer. The chapter does not require that every exhibition contain all three in equal measure. It does require that the dryness be read in these connections, and not as a mood beside them.

Thus:

> **Deficient engagement is presence without the act of considering and holding. Poor judgment is a discrimination that does not prove what the grounds require. Failure to labour is the Word not sought as the writings command it to be sought. Aridity is the dryness in which these stand. None of the three is the obscurity of the Graphē, and none is yet the dedicated file of unskill or of diligence.**

# 72.5 The Biblical Words the Name Collects

The writings do not use one Hebrew or Greek word for every dry condition. The chapter collects the words that bear on dryness, dullness, proving, and the refusal of knowledge, and it keeps them distinct so that a lexicon is not mistaken for the argument.

**νωθρός (*nōthros*, G3576).** Dull, sluggish, slothful. Hebrews 5:11: νωθροὶ γεγόνατε ταῖς ἀκοαῖς, ye are become dull of hearing. Hebrews 6:12: ἵνα μὴ νωθροὶ γένησθε, that ye be not slothful. The perfect γεγόνατε marks a condition arrived at. Dullness of hearing is not the infant’s first ignorance described as a permanent kind. It is what these hearers have become. The same adjective, in the later sentence, is the thing the readers are commanded not to be. The word names slowness and sloth of the hearing, and then of the whole course. It does not, by itself, open the file of inexperience in the word of righteousness. That word is ἄπειρος (*apeiros*, G552), and it remains Chapter 73.

**δυσερμήνευτος (*dysermēneutos*, G1421).** Hard to interpret, hard to be uttered. Hebrews 5:11 joins it to the dullness by ἐπεὶ, since, because. The many things are hard to utter seeing ye are dull of hearing. The difficulty of the saying is grammatically consequent on the hearers’ condition. The adjective does not mean that the subject has been classified as an impenetrable mystery. It means that utterance toward these hearers is hard because of what their hearing has become.

**παχύνω (*pachynō*, G3975), βαρέως (*bareōs*, G917), καμμύω (*kammyō*, G2576).** Matthew 13:15, citing Isaiah 6:10: the heart is waxed gross, the ears hear heavily, the eyes they have closed. The organs are named. The failure is located in them. Isaiah 6:9–10’s Hebrew commission — hear ye indeed, but understand not; see ye indeed, but perceive not; make the heart fat, make the ears heavy, shut the eyes — is the same relation in the prophet’s sending. Grossness, heaviness, and closing are the vocabulary of engagement that does not yield understanding, conversion, and healing.

**πωρόω (*pōroō*, G4456).** To harden, to petrify. Mark 8:17: πεπωρωμένην ἔχετε τὴν καρδίαν ὑμῶν, have ye your heart yet hardened? The “yet” (οὔπω) places the hardness after what they have already seen. Hardness here is the heart’s condition in a reasoning that does not perceive. It is not a compliment to sincerity.

**βραδύς (*bradys*, G1021) and ἀνόητος (*anoētos*, G453).** Luke 24:25: O fools, and slow of heart to believe all that the prophets have spoken. Slowness is specified. Its object is believing all. The reproach is not that they believed nothing of Israel’s hope. It is that the heart was slow toward the whole of what was spoken. Partial credit is not the speed the sentence requires.

**ἀδόκιμος (*adokimos*, G96) and δοκιμάζω (*dokimazō*, G1381).** Romans 1:28: even as they did not approve (οὐκ ἐδοκίμασαν) to retain God in knowledge, God gave them over to an unapproved mind (ἀδόκιμον νοῦν). The judicial mind is named by the failure of the proving-verb. Philippians 1:10 and Romans 12:2 use the same verb for the act reasonability is: to approve the things that differ, to prove the will of God. Αἴσθησις (*aisthēsis*, G144) in Philippians 1:9 is the judgment, the perception, in which love is to abound. The loss and the standing of reasonability share a lexicon. Collapsing ἀδόκιμος into a mere insult would hide the join. Treating every hard case of discernment as a reprobate mind would empty the command to prove.

**דַּעַת (*daʿath*, H1847), בִּין (*bin*, H995), יָדַע (*yadaʿ*, H3045).** Knowledge, discernment, knowing. Isaiah 1:3: Israel doth not know, my people doth not consider. Isaiah 5:13: because they have no knowledge. Hosea 4:6: destroyed for lack of knowledge, because thou hast rejected knowledge. The lack is named. The rejection is named beside it. Lack is not, in Hosea’s sentence, an innocent vacancy. It is the fruit of a refusal.

**רָעָב (*raʿav*, H7458) and צָמָא (*tsamaʾ*, H6772).** Famine and thirst. Amos 8:11 specifies the famine: not of bread, nor a thirst for water, but of hearing the words of the Lord. Isaiah 5:13’s multitude is dried up with thirst. The dryness is a hearing-famine and a knowledge-thirst. It is not a metaphor the chapter imposes on a text about grain.

**The parched and the sealed.** Jeremiah 17:6’s heath (עַרְעָר, *ʿarʿar*, H6176) inhabits the parched places (חֲרֵרִים, *charerim*, H2788). Isaiah 44:3’s dry ground (יַבָּשָׁה, *yabbashah*, H3004) is what God floods. Isaiah 29:11’s book is sealed (חָתוּם, *chathum*, from חָתַם, *chatham*, H2856). The images stay distinct. A parched place is a habitation that does not see when good comes. A sealed book is a writing present and unread. Dry ground, in Isaiah 44:3, is the object of promised water, not the name of a people abandoned without address.

These words are one assignment because each can mark a hearer or a people in whom the given word does not yield knowledge, judgment, or a reading. They are not one lexeme. Dullness of hearing, grossness of heart, slowness to believe all, an unapproved mind, rejected knowledge, a famine of hearing, a parched habitation, and a sealed book are exhibitions in different courts. Collapsing them into a single technical term of later spirituality would hide the exhibitions. Leaving them unjoined would hide the assignment.

Thus:

> **Aridity, in this argument, is the dryness the writings exhibit as dull hearing, gross and hardened hearts, slowness to believe all, an unapproved mind, rejected knowledge, famine of hearing, parched habitation, and a book sealed against reading. The words stay distinct. The object they serve in this file is one. *Apeiros* is not borrowed in order to complete the list.**

# 72.6 Distinguish Objects That Must Not Be Collapsed

The investigation fails at the first step if it treats different objects as one.

**This dryness and Chapter 71’s acceptance.** This chapter examines the condition of engagement, judgment, and labour. Chapter 71 examined the settlement of an erroneous doctrine as a body’s ordinary or official teaching. A settlement can be the place where judgment dries into the chapel’s voice. The drying is not the settlement restated, and the settlement is not unstated because the drying has its own file. A body can hold an error as its doctrine and still contain a hearer who tears his clothes at the book. A disciple can be dull of hearing inside a warning that has not yet become a chapel’s creed. To treat this chapter as Chapter 71 retried would hide the dryness inside another account of reception. To omit the dryness in order to protect the acceptance-file would leave the acceptance unjoined to the seventh word Movement IX assigns.

**This dryness and Chapter 67’s modes.** Poor perception, deficient understanding, faulty inference, context failure, and improper handling are operations of a later act. They are in force. Dull hearing and unused memory were already exhibited there as operations. The present object is not a sixth mode added to the catalog, and not the catalog reprinted under a new title. The new object is the condition in which those operations, and the judgment and the labour with them, stand as a dryness: the hearer has become dull; the people are famished for lack of knowledge; the learning never arrives; the book is sealed; the mind given over does not pass the test. A mode is an act that fails. Aridity is the condition the assignment names when engagement, judgment, and labour fail together as a loss of reasonability. Using Mark 8 again is not repeating the mode-file. The new function is the reasoning that moves and does not yield.

**This dryness and Chapter 70’s intellectual failures.** Not-understanding what one affirms, not consenting to sound words, not enduring sound doctrine, unlearned instability, and failure of earnest heed permit departure. They remain premises. They are not re-diagnosed here as if the turning were still the question. Dullness of hearing was reserved there for this file. The reservation is now kept by being performed. A failure that permits a turning is a cause in that file. The same neighbourhood, read now as the dryness of reasonability, is this file. The cause is not stolen back. The condition is not denied.

**Dryness and the unskillfulness of the babe.** Hebrews 5:11 and Hebrews 5:13 stand in one paragraph. The paragraph does not make them one object of this chapter. Dull of hearing is the condition this assignment examines. Unskillful in the word of righteousness, the babe, the milk and the strong meat, and the senses exercised to discern, are the next chapter and the labour-movement. Appearance in the same paragraph is not arrival at those files. The time-clause of Hebrews 5:12 — for the time ye ought to be teachers — belongs to the engagement-connection, because time passed is part of what makes the dullness a loss rather than a beginning. The content of the first principles, and the diet that marks the unskillful, are not taught here.

**Thirst that seeks, and dryness that does not.** Psalm 63:1 speaks of a soul that thirsts for God in a dry and thirsty land, and of an early seeking. Isaiah 44:3 pours water on him that is thirsty. That thirst is desire toward God in a land where water is not the landscape. It is not the aridity this chapter defines. Aridity is the condition in which the hearer does not consider, does not judge, and does not labour, or labours without coming to the knowledge. A man can be in a dry land and not be the dry hearer. The landscape-word is not the diagnosis. The diagnosis is the relation to the Word.

**The dryness of the bones, and this dryness.** Ezekiel 37 names a valley of bones that are very dry, and a people who say, Our bones are dried, and our hope is lost. The word is spoken to the bones, and they live. That dryness is the death of a people in a vision of restoration. It is not, by the shared English word, the loss of reasonability. To allegorize the vision into a psychology of study would be an improper handling of a text whose own question is whether the bones can live, and whose own answer is the word the Lord commands to be prophesied. The chapter refuses the allegory. It keeps the distinction so that “dry” in Scripture is not automatically this assignment.

**The loss and the abolition of a correct reading.** Chapter 66 refused despair. Chapter 67 established that a correct reading remains possible and an incorrect reading remains incorrect. Dryness does not repeal those sentences. The hearer who has become dull is still addressed. The command “be not slothful” is still a command. The invitation “let us reason together” still stands in the chapter that says the people do not consider. The loss is grave. It is not the metaphysical destruction of the intellect, and it is not the rewriting of the Graphē into a closed book in principle. A sealed book in Isaiah 29 is a judgment on a people. It is not a property of the writing as writing.

**This chapter and later files.** *Apeiros*, scholarly labour, *spoudazō*, exercise, maturity, rightly dividing, and the First Principles remain later. A dull hearer may also be unskillful in the word. Appearance is not that file written now. The failure to labour is this chapter’s third connection. The positive account of what the labour is, as impetus, as diligence, as right division, and as readiness for the foundation, is not the failure. Naming the refusal does not write the method.

Thus:

> **Aridity is the dryness of engagement, judgment, and labour toward a given Word. It is not the acceptance retried, not the mode-catalog retried, not the turning’s causes retried, not *apeiros*, not thirst that seeks, not the vision of the dry bones, not the abolition of a correct reading, and not the later labour-files under another name.**

# 72.7 Premises That May Be Used, and Premises That May Not

The chapter may use, as already in force:

- Truth as conformity to reality, distinct from belief, popularity, institutional consensus, and sincerity as a determinant of truth (Chapters 1–3).
- Knowledge, evidence, reason, comparison, inference, rational warrant, and sound judgment as inspectable operations (Chapters 4–7). Reasonability is this sound judgment, used under the assignment’s name, not redefined.
- The call to reason as the Lord’s own call (Chapter 7), used now as the standing invitation that not-considering has not cancelled.
- Knowing God by revelation rather than by speculation; human accounts about God as themselves to be tested (Chapters 13–14).
- γραφή as writing that, in biblical usage, designates Holy Writ / Scripture (Chapters 20–22).
- Infallibility of the record as not infallibility of the interpreter, the church as church, or every later tradition (Chapter 57).
- The Scripture cannot be broken (Chapters 62–63), used now as the reason dull ears do not break the writing or make it a mystery in principle.
- The Graphē as the primary written authority (Chapter 65).
- Authority of Scripture as distinct from authority of interpretation; transfer and despair already refused; a correct reading remains possible (Chapters 66 and 67). The five modes may be the acts by which engagement fails. They are not re-catalogued. Mark 8:17–18 and Matthew 13:13–15 may be used again where the new function is the dryness of reasonability, not the first naming of poor perception.
- Theology, philosophy, tradition, and institution as secondary systems (Chapter 68).
- Governance of meaning as occupation of the seat of sense (Chapter 69). A governor can be the condition under which judgment ceases to test the writing. The condition is not a second description of the seat.
- Doctrinal turning aside as an inspectable movement, with intellectual failures that permit and do not excuse (Chapter 70). Dullness was reserved there for this file and is now the file.
- Accepted heresy as an erroneous doctrine received as ordinary teaching or official order, which reception does not transform error into truth (Chapter 71). The strange law, the loved arrangement, and the chapel’s voice are premises of the world in which judgment can dry. They are not re-narrated as the how of acceptance.
- Biblical exhibitions already used as modes, distinction, or settlement — Mark 8, John 5:39–40, Isaiah 29:13, Matthew 23 — used now where the new function is engagement, judgment, or labour in the dryness.

The chapter may not use, as if already finished:

- Chapter 73 as a finished pathology-file; *apeiros* as the dedicated diagnosis of unskillfulness; the babe, the milk, and the strong meat as this chapter’s doctrine.
- Movement X’s labour-files; the scholarly impetus as a dedicated object; *spoudazō* as a dedicated object; rightly dividing as a finished file; the discipline of biblical interpretation as this chapter’s achievement; readiness for the Six First Principles as this chapter’s achievement.
- The Six First Principles themselves as content established here, including the foundation-list of Hebrews 6:1–2 and any doctrine built from the leaflet’s counts of baptisms, sequences of resurrections, or map of the underworld.
- Unverifiable historical colour offered as a premise: a named age as the moment reasonability died, a census of which later schools are the sealed book, or a particular modern party as the historical famine.
- The particular doctrinal constructions of the Bible Introduction materials or of the Aletheia materials as if those constructions were the water poured on the dry, or as if a warning against dullness certified the warner’s next paragraphs.

The chapter may use neighbouring passages that exhibit deficient engagement, poor judgment, or failure to labour. It may not treat those passages as a substitute for the join of the preceding argument, and it may not treat Chapter 73 or Movement X as already performed.

Thus:

> **Premises already in force may be used as members of the dryness. Premises that remain later may not be used as if finished. The aridity, and the loss of reasonability in its three connections, are the present object.**

# 72.8 The Sequence of Levels: One Clean Scope Section

What this chapter does not settle is a matter of sequence, not of doubt about what it does settle.

The investigation proceeds by levels. Each level answers its question with full force. The next level remains next because it is a different question.

**Already in force.** Truth is conformity to reality. God is the ground of truth and cannot lie. Revelation has been given and authenticated. Scripture as writing is God-breathed. The record does not fail as truthful divine writing of what it actually communicates. The Scripture cannot be broken. The Graphē is the primary written authority. The authority of that writing has been distinguished from the authority claimed for a later statement of sense. Transfer fails. Despair fails. Scripture is misread in five inspectable modes, and the writing remains the measure of those modes. A correct reading remains possible. An incorrect reading remains incorrect. Theology, philosophy, tradition, and institution are secondary systems. They may serve. They do not become primary written authority by identity, academy, antiquity, or recognition. When secondary sources govern the meaning of Scripture, the seat of sense is occupied and the test is reversed. The writing is not broken by that occupation. Service is not that event. Doctrinal turning aside has causes and mechanisms the writings name. Intellectual failures permit departure and do not excuse it. The truth turned from remains the measure. Continuance is a real contrary. Erroneous doctrines become normalized and institutionalized by operations the writings exhibit, and institutional acceptance does not transform error into truth. Refusal remains possible. Sound judgment is an inspectable discipline. The Lord calls his people to reason. Recognition of the corpus is not creation of the corpus. Human accounts about God are accountable.

**This level.** Given those premises, aridity is the dryness in which engagement with the Word is deficient, judgment is poor, and the labour proper to the Word is not given, and the loss of reasonability is that dryness as a failure of sound judgment. The writings exhibit the condition. The Word remains the measure and remains intelligible as what it communicates. The hearer is not excused. Consideration, righteous judgment, and seeking remain real duties. The warrant is full at this level. It is not postponed until Chapter 73 in order to keep the present dryness weak, and it is not inflated into an unskill-file or a labour-file in order to make the dryness sound larger than the condition the assignment names.

**Next levels, named once.** *Apeiros*, unskillfulness in the Word, belongs to Chapter 73, which closes Movement IX. Scholarly labour, perception as a dedicated stage-file, reasoning through the Word as a labour-file, quotation and knowledge as a dedicated file, *spoudazō*, exercise, maturity, rightly dividing, and readiness for the Six First Principles belong to Movement X. Those files remain later.

**Limits that belong to this level itself, not to later hesitation.** This dryness is not a second statement of acceptance. This dryness is not a claim that every hearer is dull, or that thirst which seeks God is unbelief. This dryness is not a claim that the writing is obscure in principle, or that a correct reading has become impossible. This dryness is not a claim that judicial hardening is a fiction, and it is not a claim that judicial hardening cancels the earlier refusal or the remaining command. This dryness is not a single-cause theory in which only laziness, or only the intellect, or only the heart, operates. This dryness is not a history of later spiritualities of desolation, and it is not a manual of study. Using Chapters 57, 62–63, 65, 66, 67, 68, 69, 70, and 71 is not rewriting them. A dull ear is not a sneer at the unlearned. A poor judgment is not a sneer at the existence of courts. A failure to labour is not a sneer at the simplicity of the gospel, and it is not a decree that industriousness is the whole obedience. Those limits are part of what the dryness-file is. They are not a stack of clauses designed to drain the positive conclusion.

One scope section is enough. Later sections will not re-plead these limits as though the conclusion were still tentative. Where a particular passage does not carry a further inference, that local limit will be stated at that passage. The local limit protects the exhibition. It does not weaken the level.

Thus:

> **The sequence is: truth, record, primary writing, distinguished interpretation, catalogued misreading, ranked secondary systems, governance, turning, and acceptance already in force; aridity and the loss of reasonability at this level; unskillfulness, and then the labour of the Word, at later levels. The dryness is warranted now.**

# 72.9 Define “Deficient Intellectual Engagement” at This Stage

**Deficient intellectual engagement**, in this argument, is the failure of the acts by which a hearer takes in, considers, remembers, and holds what the writing or the speaking has given, when the gift is in reach.

The phrase is the assignment’s, and it must not be allowed to drift into a measure of hours spent in a room. A man can be ever learning and never come to the knowledge of the truth. The hours are not the engagement. The coming to knowledge is the yield the text itself uses as the test. A man can have the baskets in memory as numbers he can recite when asked, and still not understand the sentence those baskets were meant to make intelligible. Recitation of the count is not yet the engagement the question “how is it that ye do not understand” requires.

The deficiency is intellectual because it is a failure of knowing, hearing, seeing, remembering, and considering. It is never, in the paragraphs where it appears, a world sealed off from the heart and the will. The heart is gross. The heart is hardened. The heart is slow. The people love simplicity. They reject knowledge. The identification keeps the join. It refuses the split that would call the mind failed and the heart sincere, or the heart failed and the mind exempt.

“Deficient” means short of the act the text requires, not low on a scale the chapter invents. Isaiah requires considering. Mark requires perceiving, remembering, and understanding. Luke requires believing all that the prophets have spoken. Hebrews requires a hearing that is not dull, in hearers who by reason of the time ought to be teachers. Where those acts are absent, engagement is deficient. Where a later file will ask how skill in the word is formed, this file asks what the absence already is.

Thus:

> **Deficient intellectual engagement is the Word in reach and the act of considering, perceiving, remembering, and holding not given, or given without coming to knowledge. The deficiency is culpable where the texts blame it. It is not a count of religious hours, and it is not yet the diagnosis of the unskillful.**

---

# 72.10 Define “Poor Judgment” at This Stage

**Poor judgment**, in this argument, is sound judgment failing at the point where a discrimination, an approval, or a verdict is required and is not performed according to the grounds.

Chapter 6 already distinguished sound judgment from personal preference. Preference does not establish truth. Dislike does not falsify a conclusion. Upbringing and institutional membership do not settle a doctrine. Sound judgment asks for grounds, for what follows, for the alternative considered, and for why a conclusion should be accepted. Poor judgment is the failure of that asking. It answers before it hears. It judges by appearance. It omits judgment while performing a smaller exactness. It calls itself wise. It does not approve having God in knowledge. It cannot, or will not, judge of itself what is right.

The failure is not the same object as a false doctrine’s becoming official. A poor judgment can be the interior of an accepted error: the people who love the arrangement no longer perform the discrimination that would separate the vision of the heart from the word of the Lord. It can also be the condition of disciples who are not, in that hour, installing a feast. The definition covers both without collapsing them into Chapter 71. The test is whether the discriminating act failed. The test is not whether a chapel has yet been built.

Proportionality belongs to the definition because Chapter 6 already required it. A judgment that treats a sealed oracle and a clear command as the same kind of difficulty has failed proportion. A judgment that treats every hard sentence as a wresting, or every clear command as already interpreted by one’s school, has failed proportion in the other direction. The chapter does not write the method by which proportion is trained. It identifies the failure the texts name.

Thus:

> **Poor judgment is the failure of discrimination, approval, and verdict at the point the writings require them. It can wear the name of wisdom. It can be fluent. It is poor because it does not prove according to the grounds. It is not preference honestly held, and it is not yet a treatise on how discernment is trained.**

---

# 72.11 Define “Failure to Labour Properly in the Word” at This Stage

**Failure to labour properly in the Word**, in this argument, is the not-giving, or the mis-giving, of the seeking, searching, meditating, and heart-preparation that the writings command toward the Word.

“Properly” is required by the assignment and is fixed by the texts, not by a later technique. Joshua 1:8 commands that the book not depart out of the mouth, and that meditation be day and night, so that observance may follow according to all that is written. Ezra 7:10 records a heart prepared to seek the law, to do it, and to teach it. Proverbs 2 makes the finding of the knowledge of God consequent on receiving words, inclining the ear, applying the heart, crying after knowledge, and seeking it as silver. John 5:39–40 places a search beside a refusal to come. Acts 17:11 places a daily search beside the question whether those things are so. Proper labour is labour that seeks the Word in order to hear it, do it, and come to what it testifies. A search that uses the writings as a possession in which one thinks one has life, and will not come to the one they testify of, is not proper labour. It is labour in the form, and failure in the end the text names.

The failure is readable without the lexicon of σπουδάζω (*spoudazō*, G4704). That verb will have its chapter. Using it now as the finished name of proper labour would close Chapter 78 inside this file. The writings already command the labour in other sentences. Those sentences are sufficient for the failure to be identified. They are not sufficient for the positive scholarly movement to be written.

“In the Word” keeps the object. Labour in a tradition, a controversy, or a system, if it does not seek the writing, is not the labour this clause names. Labour that seeks the writing in order to disprove it, or in order to explain a commandment away, fails propriety even when the hours are long. The Introduction materials will be tested on this point. They are not the definition.

Thus:

> **Failure to labour properly in the Word is the refusal or the evasion of the seeking the writings command: mouth, meditation, prepared heart, cry, search, and coming to what the writing testifies. The failure is this chapter’s third connection. The method of diligence, right division, and the approved workman remains later.**

# 72.12 Positive Warrant: The Writing Is Not Dry

The first warrant is the warrant already earned, now applied with full force to a dull hearer.

The record does not fail as truthful divine writing of what it actually communicates. The Scripture cannot be broken. The Graphē is the primary written authority. Those sentences are Chapters 57, 62–63, and 65, used and not retried. Their application here is that aridity is a condition of hearers and of peoples, not a property of the writing. The things hard to utter are hard to utter because the hearers have become dull of hearing. The famine is a famine of hearing the words, not a famine inside the words as if the words had no content. The book that is as a sealed book is still a book. The seal is the condition of the reading, described as judgment. The book’s existence as communication is what makes the unreadness a loss rather than an emptiness.

Chapter 66 refused despair, and refused the transfer of the writing’s authority to the interpreter. Both refusals bind here. If dullness transferred unintelligibility into the writing, the interpreter’s failure would have become the text’s nature, which is the despair already refused, wearing the clothes of humility. If dullness were cured by replacing the writing with a livelier voice, the transfer already refused would have returned as a pastoral necessity. The writing remains what it is while the hearer is dry. Isaiah 1:18 still says, Come now, and let us reason together, in the chapter that says the people do not consider. The invitation is not withdrawn by the indictment. The indictment is not softened by the invitation. Both are the text.

Isaiah 44:3 is the warrant in the image the assignment’s word suggests. Water is poured on the thirsty. Floods are poured on the dry ground. The dry ground is the recipient. It is not the source. The source pours. A theology that calls the Bible the dry thing has inverted the sentence. The dry thing is the ground. The Word is the water, in the figure the prophet uses for what God gives. The figure is not a license to allegorize every well in Scripture. It is a refusal to locate the dryness in the giver’s speech as speech.

This warrant is positive. The Graphē remains able to make wise unto salvation through faith in Christ Jesus, as 2 Timothy 3:15 already established and this chapter does not re-prove. Dryness does not repeal ability that belongs to the writing. The investigator who treats obscurity as the Bible’s characteristic has changed the doctrine of the record. This chapter does not change it.

Thus:

> **The writing is not dry. Aridity is the condition of the hearer and of the people toward a Word that still communicates. Dullness does not break the Scripture, and it does not transfer the hearer’s failure into the text’s nature.**

---

# 72.13 Positive Warrant: The Dryness Is Inspectable Because the Writings Exhibit It

The second warrant is that the condition is not a fog behind the text.

The writings do not say, “Some minds are uninteresting, and the cause is hidden.” They say: the ox knoweth his owner, and Israel doth not know, my people doth not consider. They say: my people are gone into captivity because they have no knowledge, and their multitude is dried up with thirst. They say: hear ye indeed, but understand not; make the heart of this people fat. They say: the vision of all is become as the words of a book that is sealed, and the learned saith, I cannot, for it is sealed, and he that is not learned saith, I am not learned. They say: he feedeth on ashes; a deceived heart hath turned him aside, that he cannot say, Is there not a lie in my right hand? They say: they have forsaken the fountain of living waters, and hewed them out cisterns, broken cisterns, that can hold no water. They say: he shall inhabit the parched places and shall not see when good cometh. They say: my people are destroyed for lack of knowledge, because thou hast rejected knowledge. They say: I will send a famine, not of bread, but of hearing the words of the Lord, and they shall run to and fro to seek the word of the Lord, and shall not find it. They say: he that answereth a matter before he heareth it, it is folly and shame unto him. They say: why reason ye? perceive ye not yet? do ye not remember? They say: why even of yourselves judge ye not what is right? They say: O fools, and slow of heart to believe all. They say: ye will not come to me. They say: they did not approve to retain God in knowledge; God gave them over to an unapproved mind. They say: ye have omitted the weightier matters, judgment, mercy, and faith. They say: ever learning, and never able to come to the knowledge of the truth. They say: ye are dull of hearing.

Because the dryness is described, it can be recognized without a private revelation of a soul’s feelings. The test is public and textual. Does the hearer consider what is in front of him? Does he judge by appearance, or omit judgment, or answer before hearing? Does he seek the word and refuse the prophets who speak it? Does the learning arrive at the knowledge of the truth? Has the hearing become dull in those who by time ought to be teachers? Those questions are the questions the texts themselves make askable. A people who only possess the warning “do not accept an error” can still fail to see that their judgment no longer labours on the writing that would test the error. The exhibitions of not-considering, dullness, sealing, famine, ashes, and ever-learning are the remedy for that blindness. They teach the reader to inspect the condition of the mind, and not only the furniture of the doctrine.

The warrant is not a claim that every later feeling of barrenness has been identified in these pages. The chapter does not name a modern school as the sealed book. The warrant is that the operations are readable, so the aridity is not an opaque fate of “spiritual dryness” and not a phenomenon only a director of souls can see. Any hearer of the Word stands under the same test the writings apply to Israel’s not-considering, to the disciples’ unused memory, and to the dullness of those who have had time.

Thus:

> **The dryness is inspectable because the writings exhibit it. Aridity is not a fog that descends after acceptance. It is a set of conditions the texts describe: not knowing, not considering, dull hearing, a sealed book, ashes for food, rejected knowledge, a famine of hearing, judgment omitted, learning that does not arrive.**

---

# 72.14 Positive Warrant: The Contrary Is Real, So the Loss Is Not Fate

The third warrant is that the loss of reasonability is not fate, and the commands that refuse it are not rhetoric.

Isaiah 1:3’s people, who do not consider, are the people of Isaiah 1:18, who are called to reason. The call treats reasoning as possible after the indictment of not-considering. Proverbs 1:23 says, Turn you at my reproof: I will pour out my spirit unto you, I will make known my words unto you. The simplicity loved by the simple is real. The turn at reproof is addressed as real. Hebrews 6:12 commands, Be not slothful, with the adjective Hebrews 5:11 has already used for dullness. A condition arrived at is still a condition the readers are forbidden to occupy. Luke 12:57 asks why they do not, even of themselves, judge what is right. The “even of yourselves” treats the judgment as within the reach of the persons addressed. John 7:24 commands righteous judgment. First Corinthians 10:15 says, I speak as to wise men; judge ye what I say. The apostle assigns the act. He does not reserve it for a class whose dryness has been cured in advance.

Chapter 71 established refusal of a settled error as a real contrary of acceptance. This chapter establishes consideration, righteous judgment, and seeking as real contraries of aridity. The two contraries are kin and are not the same sentence. Refusal declines to call an accepted error true. Consideration and judgment are the intellectual acts by which a hearer can tell the writing from the voice that has become familiar. Seeking is the labour those acts require. If dryness had abolished the acts, the commands would be mockery. The writings treat the commands as obedience. A correct reading remains possible because the writing still communicates and the hearer is still addressed as one who can consider, judge, and turn.

Judicial sentences do not cancel the contrary. Isaiah 6 sends a prophet to a people whose heart will be made fat. Amos 8 sends a famine of hearing. Those sendings are judgments, examined below, and they follow refusals the same books record. They are not the doctrine that from the beginning no one could have heard. Deuteronomy 29:29, used here only as the limit that secret things belong to the Lord and revealed things to the people and to their children that they may do the words, keeps the revealed word as the people’s possession for doing. The secret is not the excuse for not doing the revealed. The famine and the fat heart are not the rewriting of revelation into secrecy.

Thus:

> **The loss is not fate. A people who do not consider are still called to reason. The simple are still told to turn at reproof. The dull are still told not to be slothful. Hearers are still told to judge what is right and to judge what is said. The contrary is possible because the writing has not become dry and the hearer has not ceased to be an addressed hearer.**

---

# 72.15 What the Aridity Is

The three warrants control the description.

Aridity is the condition in which a person or a people stands before a given Word and does not yield the reasonability the Word requires: they do not consider, they do not judge according to the grounds, and they do not labour properly in what is written. The condition is intellectual because the failures are failures of knowing, hearing, discriminating, and seeking. The condition is moral because the same paragraphs bind those failures to refusal, love of simplicity, rejected knowledge, and a heart removed. The chapter affirms both. The split that would choose one and delete the other is a later reduction. It is not the texts.

The aridity is a loss, and not only a lack, where the texts mark a becoming or a prior gift. Ye are become dull of hearing. They knew God and did not approve to retain him in knowledge. The disciples have already seen the feedings. The hearers have had time in which they ought to be teachers. A lack that never had a word in reach is not yet this loss. The nations of Romans 1 are not described as men to whom no knowledge of God was ever present. They are described as men who, when they knew, did not glorify, and were given over. Israel is not described as a people without a law. They are described as a people who rejected knowledge and forgot the law of their God. The loss presupposes a gift. That presupposition is what makes the dryness culpable rather than tragic in the sense of a fate without address.

The aridity often keeps the forms of intellectual life. There is reasoning. There is learning. There is a learned man to whom the book can be delivered. There is a tithing exact enough to include mint, anise, and cummin. There is a claim to be wise, and a claim that the law is with us, already judged in Chapter 71 as a claimed possession that does not transform rejection of the word. The present use of the kept form is the dryness: the form continues, and the yield of reasonability does not. Wisdom in their own eyes is the form of judgment without the judgment. Ever learning is the form of labour without the arrival. The sealed book delivered to the learned is the form of reading without the reading.

The aridity can be judicial without ceasing to be the hearer’s condition. The Lord pours out a spirit of deep sleep. He gives over to an unapproved mind. He sends a famine of hearing. The judicial act is his answer to a prior not-knowing, not-retaining, not-hearing, commanding the prophets to be silent. The judgment hardens and withdraws. It does not invent the first refusal, and it does not make the first refusal innocent. Nor does it make God the author of the lie the deceived heart will not detect. He shuts eyes in the idol-passage after the idolater has burned part of the tree and worshipped the residue. The sequence in the text is the material of the later section. The definition here only refuses two collapses: that judicial language is a metaphor for nothing, and that judicial language is a pardon.

Thus:

> **Aridity is the loss of reasonability toward a Word that was in reach: engagement deficient, judgment poor, labour not properly given, often under the surviving forms of reasoning, learning, and exactness, and sometimes under a judgment of God that follows refusal. The condition is real. The writing’s dryness is not.**

# 72.16 What Does Not Constitute the Aridity

The name is withheld where the definition’s elements are absent. The withholding is part of the doctrine’s shape.

**Thirst that seeks God is not this aridity.** The soul that thirsts in a dry land and seeks early is the opposite of the hearer who does not consider. Dryness of circumstance is not dryness of hearing. Isaiah’s thirsty one is the one on whom water is poured.

**The vision of dry bones is not this aridity.** Ezekiel 37’s dryness is death and restoration under a commanded word. It is not the loss of judgment. The shared image of dryness does not authorize the transfer.

**A hard sentence is not itself aridity.** Second Peter 3:16, already used in Chapter 70, says some things are hard to be understood, and then names a different act: the unlearned and unstable wrest them. Difficulty is a feature of some texts. Wresting is an act. Dullness is a condition of hearers. The three are not one. Chapter 67’s refusal to make hardness into the writing’s fault remains in force. This chapter adds that hardness of utterance in Hebrews 5:11 is explicitly tied to the hearers. That tie does not convert every difficult paragraph into a dull audience, and it does not convert a dull audience into proof that no paragraph is difficult.

**A beginning is not yet a loss.** The hearer who has just received the word, and who is not yet a teacher, is not described by γεγόνατε, ye are become dull, in the sense of a time that ought already to have produced teachers. Chapter 73 will handle the babe. This chapter does not call the first day a famine.

**Sound judgment that is slow because the grounds are still being gathered is not poor judgment.** Proverbs 18:13 condemns the answer before hearing. It does not condemn the delay that hearing requires. Luke 12:57 condemns the failure to judge what is right. It does not consecrate haste. Reasonability includes the labour of hearing the matter through. The failure is the verdict without the hearing, not the hearing that has not yet closed.

**Faithful labour that has not yet finished a large book is not the failure.** The Introduction materials themselves note that the Bible is a large book and that mastery takes time. That remark, tested later, coheres with this withholding. Time required by the size of the book is not sloth. Sloth is the dullness that, after time, still needs the beginning and still does not hear. The withholding protects the student. It does not protect the hearer who will not seek.

**Service under the writing is not this object.** A teacher who speaks according to the word, a scribe who causes the people to hear the Lord’s words, a Berean who searches whether those things are so, is not arid by being intellectual. Chapter 71 protected reception of the given word from the name of heresy. This chapter protects true engagement from the name of dryness. The protection is the same fact as the judgment: the writing is the measure of both the hearing and the dullness.

Thus:

> **Aridity is not thirst that seeks, not the dry bones, not the mere hardness of a sentence, not a beginning, not the patience of hearing a matter through, not unfinished labour at a large book, and not service that actually hears the Word.**

# 72.17 Biblical Record: Isaiah 1:3 — The Ox Knows; My People Doth Not Consider

> “The ox knoweth his owner, and the ass his master’s crib: but Israel doth not know, my people doth not consider.”

**Context.** The opening indictment of Isaiah addresses heaven and earth. The Lord has nourished and brought up children, and they have rebelled. The animal comparison follows at once. The owner and the crib are known by beasts that have no covenant. Israel does not know. The people do not consider (לֹא הִתְבּוֹנָן, from בִּין, *bin*, H995). The same chapter will call them to reason together, to wash, and to cease to do evil. The call is later in the chapter. The indictment is first.

**What is explicit.** Knowing and considering are the acts denied. The denial is not that Israel lacks a master or a crib. The nourishing is the premise of the rebellion. The ox and the ass are the measure of a knowledge beneath the knowledge required of a people. The failure is stated as fact about the people, in the Lord’s voice.

**What the passage warrants for this aridity.** The simplest exhibition of deficient engagement is a failure to consider what is possessed. Owner and provision are in the relation. The people do not perform the act of attention those relations require. Reasonability, as sound judgment, cannot begin where consideration does not occur. Chapter 6 already placed perception before interpretation. The present use is not a reprint of that order. It is the prophetic naming of a people whose condition is the absence of the first act. The dryness is the not-considering. It stands in a chapter that still reasons with them, so the not-considering is not the end of the Lord’s address. Engagement is deficient. The invitation to reason remains. That pair is the positive warrant inside the indictment.

**What the passage does not warrant here.** That animals are the moral superiors of Israel in a general zoology. The comparison is the prophet’s instrument for a specific failure. That Isaiah 1:18 has been re-opened as a new chapter on the nature of reason. Chapter 7 remains the file of the invitation. This section uses the invitation only as the contrary that stands in the same oracle. That the not-considering is already *apeiros*. The verse does not use that word, and it does not describe inexperience in the word of righteousness as a technical condition of babes.

Thus:

> **Isaiah 1:3 exhibits a people who do not know and do not consider, measured against an ox that knows his owner. The deficiency is engagement with what they have been given. The same chapter’s call to reason shows that the not-considering has not closed the address.**

# 72.18 Biblical Record: Isaiah 5:13 and 5:21 — Famished for Lack of Knowledge, Wise in Their Own Eyes

> “Therefore my people are gone into captivity, because they have no knowledge: and their honourable men are famished, and their multitude dried up with thirst.”

> “Woe unto them that are wise in their own eyes, and prudent in their own sight!”

**Context.** Isaiah 5’s woes address those who join house to house, who rise early to follow drink, who call evil good and good evil, who are wise in their own eyes, and who justify the wicked for reward. Verse 13 states the therefore of captivity: no knowledge, honourable men famished, the multitude dried up with thirst (צִחֵה צָמָא). Verse 20, already used in Chapter 71 as the renaming that does not transform evil, sits between the thirst and the woe on self-referred wisdom. The present function is not the social renaming. It is the knowledge-famine and the judgment that takes itself as the measure.

**What is explicit.** Captivity is joined to absence of knowledge by “because.” Famishing and thirst are the images of that absence in the honourable and in the multitude. A separate woe falls on those who are wise in their own eyes and prudent in their own sight. The wisdom is real as their self-description. The woe treats that self-description as the offense.

**What the passages warrant for this aridity.** Dryness is the text’s own image for a people without knowledge. The multitude is dried up with thirst. The assignment’s word has a prophetic counterpart that does not depend on a later spiritual vocabulary. The thirst is of knowledge, in a verse that has just been prepared by woes on those who do not regard the work of the Lord. Poor judgment is the companion woe. Wisdom in one’s own eyes is reasonability collapsed into self-reference. The grounds are no longer the Lord’s work. The grounds are the eye’s opinion of its own prudence. Chapter 6 distinguished sound judgment from preference and from inherited opinion. Isaiah 5:21 is that distinction as a woe. The honourable are not exempt. Famine of knowledge reaches the men whose office would suggest they know. Honour does not hydrate the multitude. The drying is the condition. It is also, in the therefore, the path of captivity. Gravity is part of what the dryness is.

**What the passages do not warrant here.** That every captivity in Scripture is caused by ignorance in the same way. The because belongs to this indictment. That verse 20’s renaming has been re-tried as acceptance. The renaming remains Chapter 71’s exhibition. The thirst and the self-wise woe are the present ones. That the woe condemns all prudence. Prudence in one’s own sight is the object. Prudence that fears the Lord is another sentence, and the same book knows it.

Thus:

> **Isaiah 5:13 names the dryness as a people famished and parched because they have no knowledge. Isaiah 5:21 names the loss of reasonability as wisdom in one’s own eyes. Honour does not cure the thirst. Self-reference is not sound judgment.**

# 72.19 Biblical Record: Isaiah 6:9–10 and Matthew 13:13–15 — Gross Heart, Dull Ears

> “And he said, Go, and tell this people, Hear ye indeed, but understand not; and see ye indeed, but perceive not. Make the heart of this people fat, and make their ears heavy, and shut their eyes; lest they see with their eyes, and hear with their ears, and understand with their heart, and convert, and be healed.”

> “Therefore speak I to them in parables: because they seeing see not; and hearing they hear not, neither do they understand. And in them is fulfilled the prophecy of Esaias, which saith, By hearing ye shall hear, and shall not understand; and seeing ye shall see, and shall not perceive: For this people’s heart is waxed gross, and their ears are dull of hearing, and their eyes they have closed; lest at any time they should see with their eyes, and hear with their ears, and should understand with their heart, and should be converted, and I should heal them.”

**Context.** Isaiah 6 follows the woes and the prophet’s unclean lips. The sending is judicial. The people will hear and not understand. The heart is made fat, the ears heavy, the eyes shut, lest they convert and be healed. The commission is not the first word spoken to this people. Isaiah 1–5 have already indicted not-considering, thirst for lack of knowledge, and the refusal of the Lord’s work. Matthew 13 cites the commission as fulfilled in the hearers of the parables. Jesus speaks in parables because seeing they see not. He also says, Who hath ears to hear, let him hear, and he explains the sowing to those who ask. Chapter 67 used the citation as the mode of poor perception. The present function is the condition as a dryness that can be judicial, and the organs as the place of the loss.

**What is explicit.** Hearing and seeing occur at one level and do not yield understanding and perception. The heart, ears, and eyes are the organs. The purpose-clause names what the closing refuses: seeing, hearing, understanding, conversion, healing. In Matthew, the people have closed their eyes. The gross heart and the dull ears are the condition. The parable-form is tied to that condition. Ears that hear are still addressed in the same chapter.

**What the passages warrant for this aridity.** Deficient engagement is here a hearing that is real as sound and null as understanding. The loss of reasonability is the failure of the heart to understand, which is the organ of the discrimination the healing would follow. The judicial making-fat is a real act of God in the commission. It stands after indictment, not before any word was given. The lest-clause shows what the condition costs: conversion and healing are the goods the closed organs do not receive. Cost is not the same as a doctrine that God does not will those goods. The same Gospel that cites the commission shows the Lord explaining, inviting ears to hear, and grieving over those who will not. The judicial word and the inviting word are both in the record. Collapsing them into a single decree that the hearer was never addressed would delete Isaiah 1:18 and Matthew 13:9. Collapsing them into a single reassurance that the fat heart is only a figure of speech would delete the commission. Aridity can be a judgment. The judgment does not make the earlier not-considering innocent, and it does not make the writing unintelligible to the one who hears.

**What the passages do not warrant here.** A finished doctrine of election, reprobation, or the divine decrees. Those questions are not this assignment. The text warrants a judicial hardening after refusal, and a condition of dull organs. It does not, in this chapter, authorize a system in which the command to consider is a pretence. That a parable is never explained. The chapter records explanation to disciples who ask. The asking is a form of engagement. The not-understanding of the multitude is the deficient form. That Chapter 67’s mode has been replaced. The mode remains. The condition and the judicial weight are the present addition.

Thus:

> **Isaiah 6 and Matthew 13 exhibit hearing without understanding and eyes closed, a gross heart and dull ears, in a judgment that follows prior refusal and that costs conversion and healing. The condition is real. The invitation to ears that hear is also real. Judicial dullness does not make the first refusal innocent, and it does not make the Word a closed book in principle.**

# 72.20 Biblical Record: Isaiah 29:9–14 — Deep Sleep and the Sealed Book

> “Stay yourselves, and wonder; cry ye out, and cry: they are drunken, but not with wine; they stagger, but not with strong drink. For the LORD hath poured out upon you the spirit of deep sleep, and hath closed your eyes: the prophets and your rulers, the seers hath he covered. And the vision of all is become unto you as the words of a book that is sealed, which men deliver to one that is learned, saying, Read this, I pray thee: and he saith, I cannot; for it is sealed: And the book is delivered to him that is not learned, saying, Read this, I pray thee: and he saith, I am not learned… Therefore, behold, I will proceed to do a marvellous work among this people, even a marvellous work and a wonder: for the wisdom of their wise men shall perish, and the understanding of their prudent men shall be hid.”

**Context.** Isaiah 29 addresses Ariel. The people are commanded to wonder and to stagger, drunken not with wine. The Lord pours out a spirit of deep sleep (תַּרְדֵּמָה, *tardemah*, H8639) and closes eyes, covering prophets and seers. The vision becomes as a sealed book. Two refusals of reading are staged: the learned man cannot, because it is sealed; the unlearned man cannot, because he is not learned. Verse 13, already used in Chapter 71 for fear taught by the precept of men, stands between the sealed book and the perishing of the wise men’s wisdom. The present function is the book unread and the wisdom that perishes. The precept-teaching remains the acceptance-file’s exhibition, cited as the neighbour in the same oracle, not re-argued.

**What is explicit.** Deep sleep and closed eyes are poured and performed by the Lord. The vision is compared to a sealed book. Both the learned and the unlearned decline to read, each with a reason that sounds sufficient. The wisdom of the wise shall perish, and the understanding of the prudent shall be hid. The marvel God works includes that perishing.

**What the passage warrants for this aridity.** The sealed book is the strongest exhibition of a writing present and unyielded. The book is delivered. The sentence “Read this, I pray thee” is spoken. The reading does not occur. Failure to labour properly in the Word is here the failure to read what is put in the hand. The two excuses divide the field so that no class remains who reads. Learning cites the seal. Unlearning cites the lack of learning. Each reason protects the closed book. Reasonability is lost when the reasons for not reading are accepted as the end of the matter. The deep sleep is judicial, in the same family as the fat heart. It covers prophets and rulers, so office is not the exemption. The perishing of wise men’s wisdom is the loss of reasonability stated as a wonder God performs against a wisdom that has been the people’s boast. Chapter 71 showed that a precept of men can be taught as the fear of the Lord. This section shows the intellectual interior of that oracle: the book of the vision is as if sealed, and the wisdom that should have read it perishes. The interior is not a second account of how the precept became the doctrine. It is the dryness in which the book is not read.

**What the passage does not warrant here.** That learning is contemptible, or that the unlearned are justified by their unlearning. Both refusals are staged. Neither reads. That every difficult text is sealed in this sense. The seal here is the condition of this people under judgment, not a rule for interpretation. That the labour of opening a book has been written as a method. The exhibition is the unreadness. Movement X remains the place of the positive labour. That verse 13 has been re-tried. The fear taught by human precept remains settled as acceptance. The sealed book is the new object.

Thus:

> **Isaiah 29 exhibits a vision become as a sealed book, a deep sleep poured on prophets and rulers, and a wisdom that perishes. The learned and the unlearned both decline to read. The excuses are the loss of reasonability in the form of reasons. The book was delivered. The reading did not occur.**

# 72.21 Biblical Record: Isaiah 44:3 and 44:18–20 — Water on the Dry Ground, and Feeding on Ashes

> “For I will pour water upon him that is thirsty, and floods upon the dry ground: I will pour my spirit upon thy seed, and my blessing upon thine offspring.”

> “They have not known nor understood: for he hath shut their eyes, that they cannot see; and their hearts, that they cannot understand. And none considereth in his heart, neither is there knowledge nor understanding to say, I have burned part of it in the fire; yea, also I have baked bread upon the coals thereof; I have roasted flesh, and eaten it: and shall I make the residue thereof an abomination? shall I fall down to the stock of a tree? He feedeth on ashes: a deceived heart hath turned him aside, that he cannot deliver his soul, nor say, Is there not a lie in my right hand?”

**Context.** Isaiah 44 promises pouring on the thirsty and on the dry ground, and then, in the same chapter’s idol-satire, describes the man who burns part of a tree for heat and bread and worships the residue. The idolater does not know or understand. Eyes are shut. Hearts cannot understand. None considers in his heart enough to state the contradiction in his own hands. He feeds on ashes. A deceived heart has turned him aside. He does not say that a lie is in his right hand. Chapter 70 reserved the dedicated dryness when it handled a deceived heart as a cause of turning. The present function is the food of ashes and the failure of the sentence the hands themselves would teach, set beside the promise of water.

**What is explicit.** Water and floods are poured by the Lord on the thirsty and on the dry ground. In the satire, not-knowing and not-understanding are stated. The shutting of eyes and hearts is stated. The content of the missing consideration is concrete: the same wood cannot, without absurdity, be both fuel and god, and the man does not say so. Ashes are his food. The lie is in his right hand, and the saying of that fact is what he cannot, or does not, perform.

**What the passages warrant for this aridity.** The chapter holds the two poles the definition requires. Dry ground is the recipient of water, not the name of the Word. The idolater’s dryness is a failure of consideration whose material is in his hands. Reasonability would say the sentence the prophet says for him: Is there not a lie in my right hand? The loss is the inability, in the deceived heart, to pronounce the discrimination the evidence requires. Feeding on ashes is the yield. Ash is what remains when the fire has had the wood. It does not nourish. A judgment that worships the residue of its own craft eats what cannot feed it. Psalm 115:4–8, in the same register, says that the idols have eyes and see not, ears and hear not, and that those who make them are like unto them, so is every one that trusteth in them. The likeness is the sensory loss passing from the object trusted to the truster. The present use of that likeness is the dryness of the organs of judgment. It is not a new proof that idolatry is a turning. The turning has been established. The likeness of blind eyes is the condition.

**What the passages do not warrant here.** That the shutting of eyes is the whole doctrine of how God relates to the nations, or a cancellation of the pouring on the thirsty. Both sentences are in Isaiah 44. The satire describes the idolater who has already made the god from the tree. The promise describes the Lord pouring on the thirsty. The sequence inside the satire includes the man’s own act with the wood before the shutting is named. That every craftsman is an idolater. The case is the worship of the residue. That the ashes have been made a technique of spiritual diagnosis for every barren feeling. The food is specified. It is the food of the man who will not say that the lie is in his hand.

Thus:

> **Isaiah 44 sets water poured on the dry ground beside a man who feeds on ashes and cannot say that a lie is in his right hand. The Word’s giver is not the dry thing. The loss of reasonability is the failure to pronounce the discrimination one’s own hands make obvious. Trust in what cannot see produces a likeness that does not see.**

# 72.22 Biblical Record: Jeremiah 2:13 — The Fountain Forsaken, and Cisterns That Hold No Water

> “For my people have committed two evils; they have forsaken me the fountain of living waters, and hewed them out cisterns, broken cisterns, that can hold no water.”

**Context.** Jeremiah 2 asks what iniquity the fathers found in the Lord, that they are gone far from him. The two evils are stated as a pair. The fountain of living waters is forsaken. Cisterns are hewed, and they are broken, and they hold no water. The labour of hewing is real. The yield is not.

**What is explicit.** Two evils, not one. Forsaking the fountain is the first. Hewing broken cisterns is the second. The cisterns cannot hold water. The people did the hewing. The water is not there to be held by what they made.

**What the passage warrants for this aridity.** The image joins failure to labour properly with a labour that is intense and useless. Hewing cisterns is work. It is not the labour the fountain required, which was remaining at the living water. Proper labour in the Word is not identical with industriousness toward a substitute. The broken cistern is the arid product: effort, container, and no water. Deficient engagement with the fountain is the first evil. The second evil is the judgment that a hewed cistern can replace it. Poor judgment is the choice of the container that cannot hold what the people thirst for. The verse does not use the word reasonability. It exhibits the structure the definition names. Chapter 69 already refused the dream and the tradition as governors of meaning. The present use is not that refusal reprinted. It is the dryness of a labour that leaves the living water. A secondary source can be the cistern. The cistern’s emptiness is this chapter’s point. The seat it occupies was the earlier chapter’s point.

A writing-out of what the fountain has given, in order to hold it and do it, is Ezra’s labour, not this hewing. That the verse has identified which later systems are the cisterns. The structure is warranted. The census is not. That thirst itself is condemned. The condemnation is the forsaking of the water, not the need of water.

Thus:

> **Jeremiah 2:13 exhibits two evils: the fountain of living waters forsaken, and a labour of hewing cisterns that hold no water. Industriousness toward a substitute is not labour properly in the Word. The dryness is a full container with nothing in it.**

He inhabits the parched places (חֲרֵרִים, *charerim*, H2788), a salt land not inhabited. The present function uses Psalm 1 as the labour-clause the Jeremiah tree presupposes in the wider writing, without turning meditation into a method-file.

It is kept from ceasing to yield.

It is a planting by waters, which Psalm 1 joins to meditation in the law. The join is warranted as a relation the writings themselves draw between a fruitful tree and a word delighted in and meditated. It is not a warrant to write the practice of meditation as Chapter 75 or Chapter 78. The year of drought does not make the planted tree arid in this chapter’s sense. Circumstantial drought is survived by the tree whose roots are at the water. Aridity in the cursed man is the habitation and the not-seeing. The distinction protects the definition from the reduction that every hard season is the loss of reasonability.

Means under the Lord’s word are not the heath. That fruitfulness is a technique. The fruit is the tree’s yield because of where it is planted. The planting is trust, and, with Psalm 1, delight and meditation. The technique-file remains later. That the salt land is a geography to be identified with a later nation. The comparison is moral and intellectual. It is not a map.

Thus:

> **Jeremiah 17 exhibits the man who trusts in flesh as a heath in the parched places, who does not see when good comes, and the man who trusts in the Lord as a tree that still yields in drought. Not-seeing the good that comes is the dryness. Circumstantial drought is not that dryness. Meditation in the law is the neighbouring labour, not yet the labour-file.**

# 72.24 Biblical Record: Hosea 4:6 — Destroyed for Lack of Knowledge, Because Knowledge Was Rejected

> “My people are destroyed for lack of knowledge: because thou hast rejected knowledge, I will also reject thee, that thou shalt be no priest to me: seeing thou hast forgotten the law of thy God, I will also forget thy children.”

**Context.** Hosea 4 opens a controversy. There is no truth, nor mercy, nor knowledge of God in the land. Swearing, lying, killing, stealing, and adultery break out, and blood touches blood. The people are destroyed for lack of knowledge (דַּעַת, *daʿath*, H1847). The because-clause addresses the priest: thou hast rejected knowledge. Forgetting the law of God is set beside the rejection. Hosea 10:12–13, in the same book, commands, Sow to yourselves in righteousness, reap in mercy; break up your fallow ground: for it is time to seek the Lord, and then says, Ye have plowed wickedness, ye have reaped iniquity. The fallow ground is unworked ground. The present function of Hosea 4:6 is the rejection of knowledge. The fallow ground is the labour-image in the same prophet, used here as the command to seek and the fact of a plowing that is not that seeking.

**What is explicit.** Destruction is joined to lack of knowledge. The lack is immediately explained: knowledge was rejected. The priest is the one rejected from being priest. The law was forgotten. Children are forgotten in the consequence. Fallow ground is to be broken because it is time to seek the Lord. The plowing that occurred was a plowing of wickedness.

Deficient engagement, at the level of the priest whose lips should keep knowledge, is a refusal. Chapter 69 already used Malachi 2:7–8 for the priest under the test and the priest who departed out of the way. The present use is not that governance-file. It is the dryness as destruction by a lack the priest produced by rejecting the knowledge he was to hold. Failure to labour is the forgetting of the law and, in Hosea 10, the unbroken fallow ground. The pair prevents the reduction that all effort is obedience, and the reduction that the people perished because no knowledge had been offered. The law was there to be forgotten. Forgetting is a loss, not an original absence.

**What the passage does not warrant here.** That every destruction is ignorance. The because belongs to this controversy. That the priest’s rejection excuses the people’s lack, or the people’s lack excuses the priest. The people are destroyed. The priest is addressed as the rejecter. Both are in the indictment. Hosea 4:1 has already said there is no knowledge of God in the land. That fallow ground has been made an agricultural programme. The image is the prophet’s command to seek. The method of seeking is not written out as a curriculum.

Thus:

> **Hosea 4:6 exhibits destruction for lack of knowledge, and names the lack as rejection and as forgetting of the law. The priest’s office does not survive the rejection. Fallow ground and the plowing of wickedness exhibit a labour not given and a labour given to the wrong end. The knowledge was there to be rejected.**

# 72.25 Biblical Record: Amos 8:11–13 — A Famine of Hearing the Words of the Lord

> “Behold, the days come, saith the Lord GOD, that I will send a famine in the land, not a famine of bread, nor a thirst for water, but of hearing the words of the LORD: And they shall wander from sea to sea, and from the north even to the east, they shall run to and fro to seek the word of the LORD, and shall not find it. In that day shall the fair virgins and young men faint for thirst.”

**Context.** Amos has been told, in the chapel-scene Chapter 71 already judged, to prophesy not again at Bethel. Earlier, Amos 2:12 records that the people commanded the prophets, saying, Prophesy not. Chapter 8 announces an end, songs turned to howling, and then this famine. The Lord sends it. It is specified by negation: not bread, not thirst for water, but hearing the words of the Lord. The seeking that follows is extensive. They run from sea to sea and from north to east. They do not find. The young faint for thirst.

**What is explicit.** The famine is of hearing. It is sent. The search for the word is real and wide and unsuccessful. Thirst brings the fair and the young to fainting. The specification “not a famine of bread, nor a thirst for water” forbids the reduction of the oracle to economics.

**What the passage warrants for this aridity.** This is the assignment’s image in the form of a judicial sentence. Aridity is a famine of hearing the words. The words are the food that is not found. The not-finding follows a history, in the same book, of silencing the prophets. Failure to labour properly, in the form of “Prophesy not,” is answered by a day in which the word is sought and is not found. The sequence matters. The famine is not the original condition of a people to whom no prophet was sent. It is the condition sent after the prophets were refused. Poor judgment is the earlier command to the prophets, which treated the word as the thing the land could not bear, and it is the later running that seeks a word without the repentance the book has required. Running to and fro is not yet the labour of hearing. It is the agitation of a famine. Deficient engagement is the hearing that is absent in the famine’s own definition. Chapter 71 used Amaziah’s citation of the chapel as the accepted cult’s self-defense. This section does not re-try Bethel. It takes the famine as the intellectual and judicial consequence that stands in the book after the silencing. The young are not spared by being young. Thirst of this kind faints them.

**What the passage does not warrant here.** That God is unwilling that any should hear, as a general maxim detached from the refusal. The sending is a judgment in a book that has pleaded with Israel to seek the Lord and live. That every season in which a student cannot find a text is this famine. The oracle is a declared judicial day, not a description of ordinary study. That the search’s failure proves the word was never clear. The word was clear enough to be commanded into silence. Clarity is the premise of the prohibition they uttered. That the chapel-file has been reopened. The famine is the new object.

Thus:

> **Amos 8 exhibits a famine of hearing the words of the Lord, sent after prophets have been told not to prophesy. The people run to seek the word and do not find it. The search of famine is not the labour of hearing. The dryness is judicial and is not the original absence of a word.**

# 72.26 Biblical Record: Proverbs 1:20–23 and Proverbs 18:13 — Wisdom Cries, and the Answer Before Hearing

> “Wisdom crieth without; she uttereth her voice in the streets… How long, ye simple ones, will ye love simplicity? and the scorners delight in their scorning, and fools hate knowledge? Turn you at my reproof: behold, I will pour out my spirit unto you, I will make known my words unto you.”

> “He that answereth a matter before he heareth it, it is folly and shame unto him.”

**Context.** Proverbs 1 personifies wisdom as a public crier. The simple love simplicity. Scorners delight in scorning. Fools hate knowledge. The cry includes a turn at reproof, and a promise of spirit poured out and words made known. The sequel, in verses 24–31, says that because the call was refused, wisdom will laugh at calamity, and they will eat the fruit of their own way. Proverbs 18:13 is a sentence on the order of hearing and answering. Folly and shame attach to the inverted order.

**What is explicit.** Wisdom’s voice is public. The simple, the scorner, and the fool are addressed as lovers of the wrong object. The turn is commanded. The making-known of words is promised at the reproof. Refusal has a fruit. Answering before hearing is named as folly and shame.

**What the passages warrant for this aridity.** Deficient engagement is love of simplicity when wisdom is in the street. The voice is not distant. The love is the condition that keeps the simple simple. Poor judgment is the answer before hearing. Reasonability’s order, already fixed in Chapter 6 as perception before verdict, is here a proverb: the matter must be heard, and then answered. The loss is the verdict that precedes the hearing. That loss is shame, not a style of decisiveness. The promise “I will make known my words” is the contrary of the sealed book. Words can be made known. The condition of the making-known, in this text, is the turn at reproof. Failure to labour is the refusal to turn when the cry is already public. The labour is not yet a curriculum. It is the turn toward a voice that is already speaking. The fruit of one’s own way, in the sequel, is the yield of the refusal. A way loved against knowledge bears a fruit. The fruit is not wisdom’s cruelty as a first act. It is the consequence the refusal stored.

**What the passages do not warrant here.** That every simple person is a scorner. The verse distinguishes simple, scorner, and fool, and addresses them together without making them synonyms. The simple one who turns is the text’s own hope. That delay in judgment is always wisdom. The proverb condemns the answer that has not heard. It does not bless the refusal to answer once the matter has been heard. Luke 12:57 will forbid that other failure. That the pouring out of spirit has been made a doctrine of religious experience governing the text. Chapter 69 refused experience as a court of meaning. The promise here is wisdom’s making-known of her words at the turn. It is not a second canon.

Thus:

> **Proverbs 1 exhibits wisdom crying in public, simplicity loved, and a turn at reproof at which words are made known. Proverbs 18:13 exhibits poor judgment as the answer before the hearing. The voice was in the street. The loss is the love of simplicity and the verdict that will not wait to hear.**

# 72.27 Biblical Record: Proverbs 2:1–6 — The Labour the Writing Describes

> “My son, if thou wilt receive my words, and hide my commandments with thee; so that thou incline thine ear unto wisdom, and apply thine heart to understanding; yea, if thou criest after knowledge, and liftest up thy voice for understanding; if thou seekest her as silver, and searchest for her as for hid treasures; then shalt thou understand the fear of the LORD, and find the knowledge of God. For the LORD giveth wisdom: out of his mouth cometh knowledge and understanding.”

**Context.** The sentence is conditional. Receiving, hiding, inclining the ear, applying the heart, crying, lifting the voice, seeking as silver, and searching as for hid treasures are the if-clauses. Understanding the fear of the Lord and finding the knowledge of God are the then-clause. The giver is the Lord. The source is his mouth. The searching does not replace the gift. It is the path on which the gift is found.

**What is explicit.** A set of acts is required in the if. The acts are intellectual and bodily in their figures: ear, heart, voice, seeking, searching. The object found is the knowledge of God. The Lord gives what the son seeks. The mouth of the Lord is the origin of knowledge and understanding.

**What the passage warrants for this aridity.** Failure to labour properly in the Word is the failure to do what these if-clauses name. The writing itself describes the labour. The description is sufficient to identify the failure. It is not a manual that must wait for Movement X before it can be read as a command. Proper labour receives words, applies the heart, cries, and searches as for treasure. Deficient engagement is the ear not inclined and the heart not applied. Poor judgment is corrected in the then-clause by understanding the fear of the Lord, which is a finding, not a self-wisdom. The Lord gives wisdom. The search is not a works-system in which the son manufactures what the mouth of the Lord does not speak. The two are in the same paragraph. A dryness that will not cry after knowledge cannot cite the gift as an excuse for not crying. A labour that cites itself as the source cannot cite this paragraph either. The gift and the search are joint. Chapter 74 will examine the scholarly impetus as a dedicated claim that the writing demands serious intellectual labour. The present section establishes only the failure’s shape: where the if is refused, the then is not the hearer’s possession. The impetus-file is not written by the quotation.

**What the passage does not warrant here.** That every student who has not yet found is under the curse of the fool. The proverb states the path. It does not date the finding. That *spoudazō* has been lexically established because seeking and searching appear. The Greek verb of 2 Timothy 2:15 is not this Hebrew sentence. The labour is real in Proverbs without that later word being spent. That the mouth of the Lord has been separated from the writing. Earlier chapters established the writing as the primary written form of what he has given. This section does not reopen that establishment. It uses the mouth as the proverb’s own source-clause.

Thus:

> **Proverbs 2 exhibits the labour as receiving, inclining, applying, crying, and searching as for silver, and the finding as the knowledge of God which the Lord gives. The failure to labour is the if refused. The search does not replace the gift. The gift does not cancel the search. The positive method-files remain later.**

# 72.28 Biblical Record: Mark 8:17–21 — Reasoning That Does Not Yield

> “Why reason ye, because ye have no bread? perceive ye not yet, neither understand? have ye your heart yet hardened? Having eyes, see ye not? and having ears, hear ye not? and do ye not remember? When I brake the five loaves among five thousand, how many baskets full of fragments took ye up? They say unto him, Twelve… And when the seven among four thousand, how many baskets full of fragments took ye up? And they said, Seven. And he said unto them, How is it that ye do not understand?”

He names perception, understanding, hardness, eyes, ears, and memory. They can report the numbers of the baskets. He asks how it is that they do not understand. Chapter 67 used the questions as the mode of poor perception: intake refused, memory unused, a sentence in the ear and not received as what it is. The mode remains in force. The present function is the dryness of the reasoning itself. They are reasoning. The reasoning does not yield understanding. Reasonability is lost in an act that still deserves the name of reason as a motion of the mind.

**What is explicit.** He asks why they reason. He denies, in question form, that they yet perceive and understand. He asks whether the heart is yet hardened. Eyes, ears, and memory are named as unused. The numbers are available to them when asked. Understanding is still absent after the numbers are spoken. The “yet” and the “how is it” treat the condition as unaccountable given what they have seen.

**What the passage warrants for this aridity.** The loss of reasonability is not the absence of inference. They inferred from the word “leaven” to the absence of bread. The inference was a motion. It was a poor judgment because it did not use the grounds already in memory, and because it did not perceive the sentence actually spoken. Deficient engagement is the unused eye, ear, and memory in men who are in the middle of a discussion. The discussion is not engagement of the right object. Failure to labour, at the scale of this moment, is the failure to bring the remembered baskets to bear on the present sentence. The labour of memory is small and decisive. They perform it when he asks the count. They did not perform it as judgment. The scene warrants the connection of the three without a theory. Reasoning occurred. It was poor. The materials for a right judgment were already theirs. Hardness of heart is named in the same questions, so the intellectual failure is not a pure mistake of arithmetic. The “yet” refuses the excuse that they had not had enough evidence. Two feedings are the evidence. Aridity here is the condition of disciples, not of a chapel installing a calf. The definition must be wide enough to include them. If dryness were only the interior of accepted heresy, this scene would not belong. It belongs.

**What the passage does not warrant here.** That the mode-catalog of Chapter 67 has been repeated as this chapter’s structure. One scene is used for a further object, the unfruitful reasoning. The other modes are not re-listed. That the disciples are *apeiros* by this hour. The questions do not use that word. The babe-file remains later. That every inference from a metaphor to ordinary life is the bread-mistake. The mistake is specified: they took a warning about leaven as a bakery problem after they had seen bread multiplied. Proportion belongs to the judgment they did not exercise.

Thus:

> **Mark 8 exhibits reasoning that moves and does not yield. Eyes, ears, and a memory able to count the baskets are present. Understanding is not. The loss of reasonability is a real inference aimed at the wrong object, in men who had the grounds. The dryness is not reserved for outsiders.**

# 72.29 Biblical Record: Luke 12:57, John 7:24, and 1 Corinthians 10:15 — The Duty to Judge

> “Yea, and why even of yourselves judge ye not what is right?”

> “Judge not according to the appearance, but judge righteous judgment.”

> “I speak as to wise men; judge ye what I say.”

**Context.** Luke 12:54–57 rebukes the multitudes who can discern the face of the sky and the earth and do not discern this time, and then asks why they do not, even of themselves, judge what is right. John 7:24 follows a controversy about a work done on the sabbath. The command distinguishes appearance (ὄψις, *opsis*, G3799) from righteous judgment (δικαία κρίσις). First Corinthians 10:15, after the wilderness examples written for admonition, assigns the hearers the act of judging what the apostle says. He calls them wise men in the act of assigning it. The wisdom is the capacity he addresses, not a compliment that cancels the command.

**What is explicit.** Judging what is right is an act they can be asked why they do not perform, even of themselves. Appearance is a forbidden measure. Righteous judgment is the commanded measure. The Corinthians are told to judge the apostolic sentence. Discernment of weather is used as a foil for discernment of the time. The foil proves that discrimination is already in their power in a lesser field.

**What the passages warrant for this aridity.** Poor judgment is a dereliction, not only a misfortune. The question “why even of yourselves” locates the act in the persons. They are not waiting for a chapel to tell them what is right before any judgment is possible. Chapter 71 established that a chapel’s voice does not transform error. The present sentences establish the positive duty the chapel cannot replace: judge what is right, judge righteous judgment, judge what is said. The loss of reasonability is the non-performance of that duty, often by a judgment according to appearance, which is a real judgment of the wrong kind. Weather-wisdom beside time-blindness is the structure of Isaiah’s self-wise woe in a smaller key: competence in a chosen field, dryness in the field that matters. The apostolic “judge ye” refuses the piety that would call it humble not to judge a teaching. Humility toward the writing is the willingness to test a sentence by what is written. Refusal of the testing is not humility. It is the dryness. The standard of the testing is not supplied by this chapter as a new method. The standard remains the Graphē, already established. The duty to judge is what this chapter adds as the contrary of poor judgment.

**What the passages do not warrant here.** That private judgment is a second canon. “Of yourselves” means the act is theirs to perform. It does not mean the grounds are whatever they prefer. Chapter 6 already refused that reduction. John 7:24’s “righteous” refuses it again. That every appearance is false. The command forbids appearance as the measure of this judgment. It does not deny that some appearances correspond. That 1 Corinthians 10 has been turned into a treatise on typology. The examples are the apostle’s own use. The present force is the command to judge what he says.

Thus:

> **Luke 12:57, John 7:24, and 1 Corinthians 10:15 assign judgment as a duty: what is right, righteous judgment rather than appearance, and what the apostle says. Weather-wisdom does not excuse time-blindness. The loss of reasonability is the non-performance of a judgment the hearers are treated as able to give. The duty does not make preference a canon.**

# 72.30 Biblical Record: Luke 24:25 — Slow of Heart to Believe All

> “Then he said unto them, O fools, and slow of heart to believe all that the prophets have spoken: Ought not Christ to have suffered these things, and to enter into his glory? And beginning at Moses and all the prophets, he expounded unto them in all the scriptures the things concerning himself.”

**Context.** On the road to Emmaus, two disciples recount the crucifixion and the reports of the resurrection, and they say they had trusted that he was the one who should have redeemed Israel. He calls them fools (ἀνόητοι, *anoētoi*, G453) and slow of heart (βραδεῖς τῇ καρδίᾳ, *bradeis tē kardia*) to believe all that the prophets have spoken. He then expounds, beginning at Moses and all the prophets, in all the scriptures, the things concerning himself. Later their eyes are opened in the breaking of the bread, and they say that their heart burned while he opened the scriptures. Chapter 66 used the slowness as a distinction: writings already stand; hearers may be slow; understanding may be opened toward those writings; the opening does not replace the writings. That distinction remains. The present function is the slowness as deficient engagement, specified as failure to believe all.

**What is explicit.** The reproach names folly and slowness of heart. The object of the believing is all that the prophets have spoken. The “ought not” states a necessity they should have judged from those writings. The exposition that follows is his act in all the scriptures. The slowness is prior to the exposition and is not treated as a sufficient condition of permanent blindness. The heart burns when the scriptures are opened. The opening is his work toward writings that already spoke.

**What the passage warrants for this aridity.** Deficient engagement is slowness to believe all. They believed some things: that he was a prophet mighty in deed and word, that he was delivered, that the women reported a vision. The slowness is toward the whole. Reasonability toward prophetic writing includes the all. A judgment that holds the glorious parts and will not believe the suffering has failed to judge the writings as they stand. Poor judgment is that selection, reproached as folly. The dryness is compatible with religious sadness and with a true report of the events. They are not cheerful despisers. They are slow. The exposition does not confirm the slowness as the writings’ fault. He expounds what the prophets have spoken. The words were there. The slowness was the heart’s. Failure to labour, in this scene, is not a refused concordance. It is a hope that did not hold together all that was written. The labour of holding the writings together as a positive discipline remains Movement X. The scene establishes that not holding the all is already a named slowness. It does not write the discipline by which the all is held.

**What the passage does not warrant here.** That every partial understanding is the Emmaus reproach. The reproach is specific: the suffering and the glory were both in the prophets, and the heart was slow to believe the conjunction. That the opening of the scriptures by the risen Christ has been reduced to a general theory of illumination that replaces the words. The opening is toward the scriptures. The heart burns while he opens them. The words are not bypassed. That Chapter 66’s distinction has been re-argued. The new point is the all, and the slowness as aridity of engagement.

Thus:

> **Luke 24:25 exhibits slowness of heart to believe all that the prophets have spoken. Partial belief, a true sadness, and a correct report of the cross do not cure the slowness. He expounds the scriptures that already spoke. The opening does not accuse the writings. It addresses the slow heart.**

# 72.31 Biblical Record: John 5:39–40 — Search, and Ye Will Not Come

> “Search the scriptures; for in them ye think ye have eternal life: and they are they which testify of me. And ye will not come to me, that ye might have life.”

**Context.** In controversy with those who will not receive him, Jesus appeals to the witness of the Father, of the works, and of the writings of Moses. He tells them to search the scriptures (ἐραυνᾶτε, *eraunate*, G2045). In them they think they have eternal life. The scriptures testify of him. They will not come to him that they might have life. Chapter 66 used the verses for the distinction between searching and the writings: testimony can stand while the searchers refuse; use may fail; standing does not fail with the use. The distinction remains. The present function is the search as a labour that does not yield the life the searchers locate in the possession of the texts, which is arid labour.

**What is explicit.** A search is commanded, or, on the indicative reading, a search is acknowledged as their practice. The thought that life is in the scriptures as they handle them is their thought. The testimony of the scriptures is his sentence about the scriptures. The will-not-come is their act. Life is joined to the coming, not to the thought that one already has it in the search.

**What the passage warrants for this aridity.** Failure to labour properly in the Word is not only the absence of search. It is a search that will not come to the one of whom the writings testify. The form of labour is present. The end the text names is refused. That is the dryness of industriousness. Deficient engagement is the will not to come, while the mind is engaged enough to think it has life. Poor judgment is the mislocation of life in the possession and the search, rather than in the one the search, properly done, would reach because the scriptures testify of him. Chapter 71 showed that a body can claim the law is with it while the word is rejected. The present scene is the personal and controversial form of a kindred dryness: the writings are in hand, the opinion of one’s own searching is high, and the coming is refused. The testimony stands. The aridity does not break it. A correct coming remains what the verse holds out: that ye might have life. Despair is refused by the purpose-clause. The searchers’ refusal is not the writings’ silence.

**What the passage does not warrant here.** That study is suspect. The verse honours the scriptures as testimony and commands or acknowledges search. The failure is the not-coming, not the searching as such. That the Christological identification has been re-proved as a new file. Earlier chapters established the testimony. This section uses it as the end proper labour comes to. That every research labour which has not yet stated a conclusion is this refusal. The refusal is a will not to come. Unfinished work is a different condition, already withheld in the definition.

Thus:

> **John 5:39–40 exhibits a search in which the searchers think they have life, and a will not to come to the one of whom the scriptures testify. The labour’s form can be present and the labour improper. The testimony stands. The not-coming is the dryness, not the silence of the writings.**

# 72.32 Biblical Record: Acts 17:11 and Ezra 7:10 — The Contrary Labour, Named and Not Methodized

> “For Ezra had prepared his heart to seek the law of the LORD, and to do it, and to teach in Israel statutes and judgments.”

The nobility is comparative: more noble than those in Thessalonica, who in the preceding verses receive the word in a tumult of rejection. Ezra 7:10 gives the ground of Ezra’s commission in a prepared heart. The preparation has three ends stated in order: seek the law, do it, and teach statutes and judgments in Israel. Chapter 65 used the Berean search as the written measure in use. The present function is the contrary of the failure to labour, stated at the size of two sentences, without a curriculum.

**What is explicit.** Readiness and daily search and the question “whether those things were so” are the Berean acts. The word is received, and the reception is tested by the scriptures, not substituted for them. Ezra’s heart is prepared. Seeking, doing, and teaching are one sequence. The law of the Lord is the object of the seeking.

**What the passages warrant for this aridity.** The contrary of deficient engagement is readiness of mind. The contrary of poor judgment is the question whether the preached things are so, which is a judgment suspended until the scriptures are searched, not a judgment refused in principle. The contrary of the failure to labour is daily search, and a heart prepared to seek, to do, and to teach. Proper labour includes doing. A search that never intends observance is not Ezra’s sequence. The passages warrant that the contrary is concrete and already practiced in the record. They do not warrant a claim that this chapter has now written how to search, how to prepare the heart, or how to teach. The nobility and the preparation are exhibited so that the dryness is not the only human possibility the writings know. Chapter 71’s refusal of the accepted error, and this chapter’s command to judge, have a narrative shape here: a mind ready, a scripture searched, a law sought in order to be done.

That Ezra’s teaching-office is the cure of dullness written as a school. The verse records the order of his heart. The scholarly impetus remains Chapter 74. That the Bereans have been made a faction against all preaching. They received the word with readiness. The search tested the word. It did not despise the word. Reception and testing are one nobility in the sentence.

Thus:

> **Acts 17:11 and Ezra 7:10 exhibit the contrary labour: readiness, daily search whether things are so, a heart prepared to seek the law, to do it, and to teach it. The contrary is real and concrete. The method of that labour is not this chapter’s achievement.**

# 72.33 Biblical Record: Romans 1:21–22 and 1:28 — An Unapproved Mind

> “Because that, when they knew God, they glorified him not as God, neither were thankful; but became vain in their imaginations, and their foolish heart was darkened. Professing themselves to be wise, they became fools.”

> “And even as they did not like to retain God in their knowledge, God gave them over to a reprobate mind, to do those things which are not convenient.”

**Context.** Romans 1 argues that what may be known of God is manifest, that his invisible attributes are clearly seen from the creation, and that men are without excuse. Knowing God, they did not glorify him or give thanks. They became vain in their reasonings (ἐματαιώθησαν ἐν τοῖς διαλογισμοῖς). The foolish heart was darkened. Professing to be wise, they became fools, and they exchanged the glory of God for images. Verse 28 uses the proving-verb: οὐκ ἐδοκίμασαν τὸν θεὸν ἔχειν ἐν ἐπιγνώσει, they did not approve to have God in knowledge. God gave them over to an unapproved mind (ἀδόκιμον νοῦν, *adokimon noun*). The mind is named by the failure of δοκιμάζω (*dokimazō*, G1381). Three times the chapter says God gave them up or over. The present function is the mind that does not pass the test, after a knowledge that was not retained.

**What is explicit.** Knowledge of God preceded the vanity. The failure is not-glorifying and not-thanking. Imaginations become vain. The heart is darkened. The profession of wisdom is the form under which they become fools. Not approving to retain God in knowledge is the ground of the giving-over. The result is a mind called unapproved, issued in acts that are not fitting. Excuse is denied.

**What the passage warrants for this aridity.** The loss of reasonability has a lexicon. The act sound judgment performs, in Philippians 1:10 and Romans 12:2, is δοκιμάζειν. The mind that is lost, in this sentence, is the mind that is ἀδόκιμος, and the loss follows a refusal to δοκιμάζειν God as the one to be retained in knowledge. Poor judgment is that refusal. Deficient engagement is the knowledge not retained, the heart darkened, the reasonings become empty. The profession of wisdom is the kept form of intellectual life, already met in Isaiah 5:21, now in a passage that traces it to the exchange of the glory of God for a likeness. Failure to labour, at this scale, is the failure to retain and to thank, which is the opposite of the cry after knowledge. The giving-over is judicial. It answers the not-approving. It does not precede the knowledge. “When they knew God” forbids the account in which the unapproved mind is the original and only human equipment, so that the commands of the same letter to prove the will of God would be addressed to no one. Without excuse forbids the account in which the judgment is God’s injustice. The chapter does not, by this section, write a complete doctrine of original sin or of the Gentile conscience. It establishes the loss as a darkening after knowledge, a folly under the name of wisdom, and a mind given over as unapproved.

**What the passage does not warrant here.** That every reasoning is vain. The vanity is the reasoning of those who, knowing, did not glorify. Chapter 7’s call to reason remains. Romans 12’s renewing of the mind remains the contrary inside the same letter, and the next section will place it. That the giving-over is a license to disobey, because the mind is now unapproved. The inconsequent acts are the fruit of the giving-over, not a permission. The indictment treats them as the shame of the condition. That images have been re-tried as the calf of Chapter 70. The exchange is the context. The unapproved mind is the object.

Thus:

> **Romans 1 exhibits a knowledge of God not retained, reasonings become vain, wisdom professed and folly produced, and a giving-over to an unapproved mind because they did not approve to have God in knowledge. The loss of reasonability is lexical as well as moral. It follows knowledge. It does not erase the knowledge as if it had never been, and it does not excuse.**

# 72.34 Biblical Record: Romans 12:2 and Philippians 1:9–10 — The Proving That Reasonability Is

> “And be not conformed to this world: but be ye transformed by the renewing of your mind, that ye may prove what is that good, and acceptable, and perfect, will of God.”

> “And this I pray, that your love may abound yet more and more in knowledge and in all judgment; that ye may approve things that are excellent; that ye be sincere and without offence till the day of Christ.”

**Context.** Romans 12, after the mercies of God, commands presentation of the body and nonconformity to this world. The transformation is by the renewing of the mind (νοῦς, *nous*, G3563). The purpose is to prove (δοκιμάζειν) the will of God as good, acceptable, and perfect. Philippians 1 prays that love abound in knowledge (ἐπίγνωσις, *epignōsis*, G1922) and in all judgment (αἴσθησις, *aisthēsis*, G144), in order that the readers may approve (δοκιμάζειν) the things that differ, the things that are excellent. Sincerity and unoffendingness are joined to that approval. The prayer treats the present love as capable of more knowledge and more judgment. It does not treat the readers as men given over.

**What is explicit.** The mind is to be renewed. The proving of the will of God is the end of the renewal. Conformity to the world is the contrary of the transformation. Love is to abound in knowledge and in all judgment. The approval of the excellent is the end of that abounding. Both passages use the proving-verb. Both place the proving after a renewal or an abounding, not after a resignation of the mind.

**What the passages warrant for this aridity.** Reasonability, as this chapter uses the name, is this proving. It is not a temperament. It is the mind renewed so that it can prove the will of God, and the love abounding in knowledge and judgment so that it can approve what is excellent. The loss described in Romans 1 is the ruin of this act. The command and the prayer are the contrary, and they are addressed to churches. The contrary is therefore not reserved for a sinless class. Poor judgment is what these sentences forbid: conformity that does not prove, love that does not abound into discrimination. Deficient engagement is a mind not renewed, a love that does not increase in knowledge. The passages do not describe the labour of renewal as a course of study. They state the end. Movement X will have the labour by which the mind is exercised in the Word. The present warrant is that reasonability has a positive shape already in the writings, so that “loss” is not a vague insult. One can say what was lost: the proving. One can say what is commanded instead: the renewing, the abounding, the approval of what is excellent.

**What the passages do not warrant here.** That the renewing is a technique this chapter now teaches. The command is explicit. The means of the scholarly path are later. That love without knowledge is the prayer’s ideal, or knowledge without love. The prayer joins them. A judgment that abounds in information and does not love is not this αἴσθησις under this love. A love that refuses judgment is not this prayer either. That Romans 1’s giving-over and Romans 12’s renewing have been harmonized into a system of the decrees. They stand in one letter as indictment and as command. The command is intelligible because the mind can be renewed. The indictment is true because the mind was given over. This chapter does not write the larger soteriology that holds both. It uses both as the loss and the contrary.

Thus:

> **Romans 12:2 and Philippians 1:9–10 exhibit reasonability as the proving of the will of God and the approval of what is excellent, by a mind renewed and a love abounding in knowledge and judgment. The loss is the loss of that proving. The command and the prayer are the contrary, addressed to real churches, and not yet the labour-method.**

# 72.35 Biblical Record: Matthew 23:23 — Judgment Omitted

> “Woe unto you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites! for ye pay tithe of mint and anise and cummin, and have omitted the weightier matters of the law, judgment, mercy, and faith: these ought ye to have done, and not to leave the other undone.”

**Context.** Matthew 23’s woes address scribes and Pharisees who sit in Moses’ seat, bind burdens, and, in the verses Chapter 69 already used, shut the kingdom and strain at a gnat while swallowing a camel. Verse 23 specifies a labour that is exact and an omission that is weighty. They tithe garden herbs. They omit (ἀφίημι, *aphiēmi*, G863) judgment, mercy, and faith (τὴν κρίσιν καὶ τὸ ἔλεος καὶ τὴν πίστιν). The weightier matters ought to have been done. The herbs ought not to have been left undone. Chapter 69’s function was the guide who decides which clauses bind. The present function is the omission of judgment as poor judgment in the form of a misplaced exactness.

**What is explicit.** Tithing of mint, anise, and cummin is practiced. Judgment, mercy, and faith are omitted. Both the weightier and the lighter ought to be done. The omission is the ground of the woe, together with the hypocrisy the chapter names. Judgment is listed first among the weightier matters.

**What the passage warrants for this aridity.** Poor judgment is here the omission of judgment. The phrase is almost tautological, and the tautology is the text’s force: the matter called judgment is the matter left out by men whose whole posture is scrupulous judging of herbs. Reasonability fails by a substitution of small discriminations for the discrimination the law calls weighty. Failure to labour is not idleness. The tithe of mint is labour. It is not labour properly ordered toward the Word’s weight. Deficient engagement is the engagement with the lesser clause and the non-engagement with judgment, mercy, and faith. The verse refuses two reductions. It refuses the reduction that care about small commandments is the whole of obedience, and it refuses the reduction that weightier matters license the neglect of the small. “These ought ye to have done, and not to leave the other undone” is proportion. Chapter 6 required proportionality in sound judgment. Matthew 23:23 is that requirement as a woe. Aridity can look like precision. The dryness is the missing weight.

**What the passage does not warrant here.** That the seat of Moses has been re-argued. Chapter 71 used the seat and the burdens. The omitted judgment is the new object. That mercy and faith are dissolved into a single mood called judgment. The three are named. Judgment is not a word that eats the other two. That the herbs are contemptible. The Lord says they ought not to be left undone. The woe is the omission of the weightier, not the presence of the lighter.

Thus:

> **Matthew 23:23 exhibits a precise labour, the tithe of mint, and an omitted judgment. Poor judgment can be the leaving-out of judgment by men who discriminate the small thing. Proportion requires the weightier and does not cancel the lighter. Exactness is not reasonability.**

# 72.36 Biblical Record: 2 Timothy 3:7–8 — Ever Learning, and Never Able

> “Ever learning, and never able to come to the knowledge of the truth. Now as Jannes and Jambres withstood Moses, so do these also resist the truth: men of corrupt minds, reprobate concerning the faith.”

**Context.** Second Timothy 3 describes perilous times and men of listed vices, having a form of godliness and denying the power. Among them are those who lead captive silly women laden with sins, led away with divers lusts. The women’s intellectual life is then stated: ever learning (πάντοτε μανθάνοντα, *pantote manthanonta*), and never able to come to the knowledge of the truth (εἰς ἐπίγνωσιν ἀληθείας ἐλθεῖν). The men who capture that condition resist the truth, are corrupt in mind, and are reprobate concerning the faith (ἀδόκιμοι περὶ τὴν πίστιν), the same unapproved word Romans 1:28 used for the mind. Chapter 70 used verses 13–14 for deceiving and being deceived, and for the contrary of continuance in the things learned. The present function is verse 7’s learning that does not arrive, and verse 8’s unapproved standing toward the faith.

**What is explicit.** Learning is continual. Arrival at the knowledge of the truth never occurs. Ability is denied in the “never able,” in a context of lusts and of being led. The resisters of the truth are corrupt in mind and unapproved concerning the faith. The form of godliness is in the paragraph. The power is denied.

**What the passage warrants for this aridity.** Ever learning is the exhibition that activity is not engagement’s yield. The assignment’s first connection can be present as an appetite for teachers and lessons, and the knowledge of the truth can still be the place they do not come. Deficient intellectual engagement is defined by the end, not by the motion. Failure to labour properly is a labour of learning aimed so that it never arrives. The divers lusts are in the sentence as the leading-away. The chapter does not delete them in order to keep the failure purely academic. Poor judgment is the corrupt mind and the unapproved relation to the faith. The form of godliness is the kept religious shape, kindred to the kept form of wisdom in Romans 1 and of exactness in Matthew 23. Aridity wears a form. The form can be study. Study that cannot come to the knowledge of the truth is the dryness. The “never able,” read with the lusts and the resistance, is not a metaphysical sentence that these persons were created unable and then blamed. It describes the condition reached under the lead of lusts and the capture of resisters. Ability in the sense of Chapter 66’s possible correct reading is not repealed for the churches Timothy is told to continue in. The “never able” belongs to this company. Timothy is commanded, in the next verses, to continue in the things he has learned and been assured of, knowing of whom he has learned them, and that the holy scriptures are able to make wise unto salvation. The scripture’s ability and this company’s inability stand in one chapter. The dryness is not the scripture’s inability.

**What the passage does not warrant here.** That women as women are the subject. The silly women are the example of a captivity. The ever-learning is the intellectual mark of that captivity, and the men who produce it are reprobate concerning the faith. The mark can be stated without a doctrine about a sex. That all advanced learning is this verse. The verse condemns learning that never comes to the knowledge of the truth, in a company that resists the truth. It does not condemn the scriptures’ own power to make wise. That continuance in what was learned has been re-tried as Chapter 70’s contrary. Continuance remains that file’s contrary. The ever-learning that does not arrive is the new object, and it is distinct from continuance: continuance holds what was learned from a known teacher and from the scriptures; ever-learning moves without arriving.

Thus:

> **Second Timothy 3:7–8 exhibits learning that never comes to the knowledge of the truth, and minds corrupt and unapproved concerning the faith. The form of study can be the dryness. The same chapter says the holy scriptures are able to make wise unto salvation. The company’s inability is not the writing’s inability.**

# 72.37 Biblical Record: Hebrews 5:11 — Dull of Hearing

> “Of whom we have many things to say, and hard to be uttered, seeing ye are dull of hearing. For when for the time ye ought to be teachers, ye have need that one teach you again which be the first principles of the oracles of God; and are become such as have need of milk, and not of strong meat.”

**Context.** The writer has entered the priesthood of Christ after the order of Melchisedec and stops. Of whom (περὶ οὗ) there is much to say, and it is hard to utter (δυσερμήνευτος, *dysermēneutos*, G1421), since (ἐπεί) you have become dull of hearing (νωθροὶ γεγόνατε ταῖς ἀκοαῖς). Verse 12 adds the time: by reason of the time you ought to be teachers, and you need someone to teach you again the first principles of the oracles of God (τὰ στοιχεῖα τῆς ἀρχῆς τῶν λογίων τοῦ θεοῦ). You have become such as have need of milk, not of solid food. Verses 13–14 will name the unskillful, the babe, the mature, and the senses exercised to discern. Those verses are not this section’s exposition. Chapter 70 reserved the dullness as this chapter’s object and reserved ἄπειρος for Chapter 73. The reservation is kept. Verse 12 is used here only for the time-clause and the “become,” which fix dullness as a loss after time. The content of the first principles, the meaning of milk and meat, and the lexical file of unskillfulness are not opened.

**What is explicit.** Many things are to be said. They are hard to utter. The reason given is the hearers’ dullness. The dullness is a having-become. The time is long enough that they ought to be teachers. What they need is to be taught again. The need is a having-become. Hearing is the organ named in verse 11. The oracles of God are the object of the elements they need again.

**What the passage warrants for this aridity.** Dullness of hearing is the chapter’s central biblical name for the condition. Deficient intellectual engagement is hearing that has become sluggish. The difficulty of the discourse is located in the hearers by the conjunction. A reconstructible note in the Bible Introduction materials says the same thing in its own words: the many things are hard to make clear not because they are mysteries but because the hearers are slow to grasp. That claim is tested in a later section. The text already places the because. Poor judgment is implied in the inability to bear the discourse a teacher of their standing in time should bear, but the verse’s own word is hearing, and the chapter does not inflate hearing into the whole of the unskill-file. Failure to labour is the gap between the time and the ought. Time passed is not labour done. They ought, by the time, to be teachers. The ought is a judgment on engagement over time. It is not a contempt for new believers, who have not had the time. Loss, not mere lack, is the perfect tense. Ye are become dull. Ye are become such as have need. Something that time should have produced has not been produced, and something that hearing should not be has been become. The writing of the many things is not cancelled. The writer has many things to say. He says that saying them is hard. He then, in the following chapter of the epistle, still writes. Dullness makes utterance hard. It does not make the writer silent, and it does not make the subject a mystery by nature.

**What the passage does not warrant here.** That Hebrews 5:13–14 has been expounded. The unskillful babe, the word of righteousness, the full age, the senses exercised, and the discerning of good and evil remain Chapter 73 and the maturity-files. Citing the neighbourhood is not performing it. That the first principles have been listed and taught. The phrase names what they need to be taught again. The list in Hebrews 6:1–2 is the book’s end, after the labour-movement. This section does not lay the foundation and does not go on from it. That “hard to be uttered” means the Christian faith cannot be spoken plainly. The hardness is relational, toward these hearers, for the reason the clause gives. That every congregation of long standing is dull. The sentence addresses these hearers. It teaches the church to examine time against teaching-capacity. It does not pass the verdict on every later church by anticipation.

Thus:

> **Hebrews 5:11 exhibits hearers who have become dull of hearing, so that many things are hard to utter. The time in which they ought to be teachers is the measure of the loss. Dullness is a condition arrived at, not a mystery in the subject. The unskillful, the milk and the meat, and the first principles as a foundation are not this section’s doctrine.**

# 72.38 Biblical Record: Hebrews 6:12 — Be Not Slothful

> “And we desire that every one of you do shew the same diligence to the full assurance of hope unto the end: that ye be not slothful, but followers of them who through faith and patience inherit the promises.”

**Context.** After the warning that the land which drinks the rain and bears thorns is rejected, the writer says he is persuaded of better things concerning the hearers, things that accompany salvation. God is not unrighteous to forget their work and labour of love. The desire is that every one of them show the same diligence (σπουδή, *spoudē*, G4710) unto the full assurance of hope unto the end, that they be not slothful (νωθροί, *nōthroi*, G3576). The contrary of sloth is imitation of those who through faith and patience inherit the promises. The noun σπουδή is kin to the verb σπουδάζω, which Chapter 78 will develop. The present function is only the adjective already used for dullness, now in the negative command. The foundation-list of Hebrews 6:1–2 is not quoted and not taught.

**What is explicit.** Diligence is desired. The end is full assurance of hope. Slothfulness is forbidden. The pattern is the faith and patience of inheritors. The persuasion of better things, and the memory of work and love already shown, sit beside the desire. The hearers are not described as men with nothing to continue.

**What the passage warrants for this aridity.** The same word that names the dullness of hearing names the sloth the writer forbids. The loss is not a fate the epistle shrugs at. It is a condition the readers are told not to occupy. The contrary is real: diligence of hope, faith, patience, imitation. Because σπουδή appears, a careless reading would open Chapter 78 here. The care of this section is the opposite. The noun shows that the contrary of νωθρός is already a diligence the epistle can name. The lexical range of the verb, the labour of study in 2 Timothy 2:15, and the approved workman are not thereby written. Neighbourhood is stated by refusing the theft. The full assurance of hope is the end toward which diligence runs. Aridity, as dull hearing and sloth, is the threat to that running. The persuasion of better things forbids the teacher’s despair about these hearers even after Hebrews 5:11. Dullness has been charged. Better things are still persuaded. The pair is the chapter’s refusal of despair, inside the epistle that knows the danger.

**What the passage does not warrant here.** That Hebrews 6:1–2 has been opened. The repentance, the faith, the baptisms, the laying on of hands, the resurrection, and the eternal judgment are not this file’s content. The desire of verse 11 does not require the list to be rehearsed in order to be intelligible. That σπουδάζω has been defined. One noun in a command is not the chapter. That work and labour of love are the whole of proper labour in the Word. The labour remembered is ministry toward the saints. The labour in the Word has been examined in other sections. This verse adds the prohibition of sloth as the direct contrary of the dullness-word.

Thus:

> **Hebrews 6:12 forbids the sloth named by the same adjective as dull hearing, and sets diligence, faith, and patience as the contrary. The command treats the loss as refusible. The foundation-list is not opened. The verb of diligence is not spent.**

# 72.39 The Three Connections Joined

The exhibitions can now be joined under the assignment’s three names. The join is a reading of the texts, not a system laid on them.

**Deficient intellectual engagement** is the ox’s knowledge absent from the people; the heart that does not consider; the hearing that hears and does not understand; the eyes closed; the book delivered and not read; the good that comes and is not seen; the simplicity loved while wisdom cries in the street; the baskets remembered as numbers and not as understanding; the slowness to believe all; the knowledge of God not retained; the learning that never arrives; the hearing that has become dull after time enough to teach. The Word, the time, the organs, or the memory are in reach. The act of taking in and holding is not, or is so sluggish that the discourse cannot be uttered toward it.

**Poor judgment** is wisdom in one’s own eyes; the failure to say that a lie is in the right hand; the answer before the hearing; the judgment according to appearance; the omission of judgment while mint is tithed; the inference from leaven to bakery; the not-approving to retain God in knowledge; the unapproved mind; the wisdom of the wise that perishes; the reasons for not reading that sound sufficient and leave the book sealed. Discrimination occurs, in several of these scenes, and it discriminates the wrong object, or it does not occur where the grounds are public.

**Failure to labour properly in the Word** is the law forgotten; the knowledge rejected; the fallow ground unbroken and the wickedness plowed; the prophets commanded not to prophesy, and then a word sought in famine and not found; the if-clauses of Proverbs 2 refused; the cisterns hewed and the fountain forsaken; the search that will not come; the sealed book’s unreadness; the time in which they ought to be teachers and are not. The contrary labour is also exhibited, so the failure is not the only pattern: daily search whether things are so; a heart prepared to seek, to do, and to teach; meditation joined to the tree that yields; the command to turn at reproof.

The three confirm one another without becoming one word. Dull hearing leaves judgment without a heard matter and is itself the refusal of the labour of hearing. Answering before hearing is a judgment-failure whose mechanism is a refused labour. Ever-learning is a labour-form whose yield is an engagement that does not arrive. Omitted judgment is a poor judgment performed by means of a small labour. The join is the dryness. A hearer can be described truly by any one of the three. The assignment’s claim is that aridity stands in connection with them, not that every verse must be forced to say all three.

The judicial acts sit inside the join and do not replace it. The fat heart, the deep sleep, the famine of hearing, and the unapproved mind are God’s giving-over and God’s sending, after not-considering, after not-reading, after silencing the prophets, after not retaining God in knowledge. The judgment intensifies the dryness. It does not create the first refusal, and it does not relocate the dryness into the writing. The commands to reason, to turn, to judge, to seek, and not to be slothful are spoken in the same canon as the judgments. The investigator who keeps only the commands will not understand Amos 8. The investigator who keeps only the judgments will not understand Isaiah 1:18 or Hebrews 6:12. The writings keep both.

Thus:

> **Deficient engagement, poor judgment, and failure to labour properly in the Word are the three connections of one dryness. They confirm one another and remain distinguishable. Judicial hardening follows refusal and does not replace the refusal or the command. The Word remains the water, the book, and the measure.**

# 72.40 Why the Loss Does Not Break the Writing or Excuse the Hearer

The exhibitions have already refused two comforts. They are gathered here so that the refusal is one verdict.

The loss does not break the writing because the writing is what makes the loss visible. If the prophets had not spoken, slowness to believe all would have no measure. If the book were not a book, the seal would not be a judgment. If the scriptures did not testify, the not-coming would not be a refusal of a testimony. Chapters 57 and 62–63 established the non-failure of the record and the unbreakableness of the Scripture. Dull ears are not a new kind of breakage. They are a kind of hearer. The many things hard to utter are still many things the writer has to say. The holy scriptures remain able to make wise unto salvation in the chapter that describes a company never able to come to the knowledge of the truth. The ability and the inability have different subjects. Confusing the subjects is the transfer of the hearer’s condition to the text, which Chapter 66 already refused as despair.

The loss does not excuse the hearer because the texts that name it also blame it. Israel doth not consider, in the voice of indictment. Knowledge is rejected, not merely missed. The simple love simplicity. They are without excuse. Ye will not come. The heart is slow, and the slowness is folly in the speaker’s mouth. Ye are become dull, by a time in which ye ought to be teachers. The ought is a charge. Judicial giving-over answers a prior not-approving. It is not the discovery that the prior act was compelled and therefore innocent. Hosea’s priest is rejected from being priest because he rejected knowledge. The consequence confirms the blame. It does not absorb it.

The loss does not transform an accepted error into truth. Chapter 71 established the non-transformation. This chapter applies it to the interior. A people dried up with thirst for lack of knowledge are not, by the thirst, made accurate in what they still affirm. A sealed book does not ratify the precept of men taught in the same oracle. A mind given over to what is not fitting does not, by the giving-over, make the unfit fitting. Dryness can make an error feel like the only thing a reasonable person could think. The feeling is the loss. It is not a new ground. The Graphē remains the measure of the doctrine the dry mind calls reasonable. Josiah can still tear his clothes. The Berean can still search. The contrary acts are the proof that dryness is not an ontological editor of the truth.

Thus:

> **The loss of reasonability does not break the writing, does not excuse the hearer, and does not transform error into truth. The writing remains the measure by which the dullness is known as dullness. The ought, the rejection, the love of simplicity, and the will not to come are blame. The blame is as inspectable as the dryness.**

# 72.41 Reconstructing the Bible-Introduction Proposition: Dullness Is in the Hearers

A reconstructible proposition, in notes on Hebrews 5:11–14, states that the many things hard to utter are hard because the hearers are dull, not because the things are mysteries; that dullness (νωθρός, *nōthros*, G3576) is slowness and sloth, the word used also for slothful in Hebrews 6:12; that the hearers have been professed Christians long enough that they ought to be teachers and instead, because of laziness and dullness, need the first principles again; and that the unskilful are the inexperienced, with two characteristics of babes: dull of hearing and inexperienced in the Word. The same notes say the trouble is with men who take a wrong attitude due to religious background, citing the gross heart and dull ears of Matthew 13 and Acts 28.

The proposition is not adopted as “the source says.” It is tested. Chapters 70 and 71 received only the neighbourhood and reserved the dedicated account. This chapter is that account for dullness. The test asks what part of the note the present evidence establishes.

**What is claimed.** The difficulty of utterance is a fact about the hearers’ slowness. Time in the way is not growth. Religious background can fix a wrong attitude. Two marks distinguish the babe: dull hearing and inexperience.

**Premises.** Hebrews 5:11’s ἐπεὶ clause has been read. The perfect γεγόνατε has been read. The time-clause of verse 12 has been used as the measure of loss and not as the opening of the foundation-list. Chapter 71 established that an accepted background can make the written law seem strange. Chapter 67 established that a correct reading remains possible.

**Biblical correspondence.** The correspondence that holds is the location of the hardness. The text says the things are hard to utter seeing ye are dull of hearing. The materials’ “not because they are mysteries” coheres with that clause and with the wider warrant that the writing is not dry. The identification of νωθρός in 5:11 and 6:12 coheres with the Greek. The time-clause coheres with “for the time ye ought to be teachers.” Laziness, as a gloss of sloth, coheres with the adjective’s range and with the failure to labour, provided it is not made the only cause in a book that also names rejected knowledge, loved simplicity, lusts, and judicial sleep. Religious background, as a condition of dull hearing, coheres where Chapter 71 has already shown an ordinary human doctrine teaching men to count the law strange. The strange law is a background. It can dull the hearing of the great things written. It does not, by dulling them, make them small.

**What follows.** The coherent remainder is received with full force at this level. Dullness is in the hearers. It is slowness and sloth. It is a condition after time. It makes utterance hard without turning the subject into a mystery. Background can train the dullness. The blame of laziness is fair where the texts blame a not-hearing and a not-seeking. It is not a complete psychology.

**What does not follow, and is not adopted.** The two characteristics of babes as this chapter’s finished diagnosis. Dull of hearing is this chapter. Inexperienced in the Word is Chapter 73. The materials join them because the paragraph joins them. The join is a map of neighbouring files. It is not a finding that *apeiros* has been expounded. The first principles as doctrines to be listed, the milk-metaphor’s history among profane writers, and the claim that the hearers have not grown at all as a universal verdict on every long-standing church are not adopted. The text warrants the charge against the hearers it addresses. The materials’ intensification is not a second canon.

Thus:

> **The note on Hebrews 5:11 is received where it locates the hardness in the hearers’ dullness, identifies the word as slowness and sloth, and treats time as an insufficient substitute for hearing. It is not adopted as the file of *apeiros*, as the teaching of the first principles, or as a complete cause-list. Dullness is in the hearers. The subject is not thereby a mystery.**

# 72.42 Reconstructing the Bible-Introduction Proposition: Seven Reasons, Tested Against Mark 8

A further reconstructible proposition, in notes on Mark 8:17–18, lists seven reasons the Bible is not understood: reasoning, implying doubt and unbelief; lack of perception, implying dullness; ignorance, implying lack of interest and study; hardness of heart; dull eyes, as willful blindness; dull ears, as willful rejection; dull memory, as willful forgetfulness. The materials say all these reasons are willful, that people do not have to be in them, and that the disciples had no excuse because they had seen the feedings.

The proposition is not adopted as “the source says.” It is tested.

**What is claimed.** Non-understanding has causes in the hearer that match the questions Jesus asks in Mark 8, and those causes are willful. The disciples’ evidence was already sufficient.

**Premises.** Mark 8:17–21 has been read in this chapter as unfruitful reasoning. Chapter 6 distinguished sound reasoning from undisciplined reasoning. Chapter 7 established the call to reason. Chapter 67 established poor perception as a mode and did not treat it as the whole of aridity.

**Biblical correspondence.** The correspondence that holds is the scene. Jesus asks why they reason, whether they yet perceive and understand, whether the heart is yet hardened, whether eyes, ears, and memory are unused. The materials’ list is a fair analytic restatement of those questions. The “yet,” and the rehearsal of the baskets, cohere with “no excuse” in the limited sense the text itself presses: they had seen, and they could count. Willfulness, if it means the failure is blameworthy and not a defect in the evidence, coheres with the reproach. If it means a theory that every dullness is a pure act of spite with no judicial, habitual, or lust-led form, it does not cohere with Isaiah 6, Amos 8, Romans 1, and 2 Timothy 3, which this chapter has had to keep.

**What follows.** The coherent remainder is received. Unfruitful reasoning, unused perception, unused memory, and hardness are reasons understanding fails, and they are reasons in the hearer. The disciples illustrate that evidence can be public and the yield absent. People are addressed as persons who can perceive and remember. The command-character of the questions is the proof.

**What does not follow, and is not adopted.** The gloss that “reasoning” as such implies doubt and unbelief. The text condemns this reasoning, the inference to the missing bread. It does not condemn the proving Romans 12 commands or the reasoning Isaiah 1:18 invites. A list that makes reason the first cause of not understanding would set the materials against the writings and against Chapters 6 and 7. The gloss is refused. The question Jesus asks is kept. The claim that the seven are a complete account of aridity is not adopted. They illuminate one scene. The famine, the sealed book, the rejected knowledge, and the ever-learning are other scenes. The claim that “people do not have to” erases judicial giving-over is not adopted. They do not have to refuse. After the refusal, a judgment may be sent. The judgment is not the proof that the refusal was necessary.

Thus:

> **The seven reasons cohere where they restate Mark 8: unfruitful reasoning, dull perception, unused memory, hardness, and a blame that the feedings make intelligible. They do not cohere where reasoning as such is called unbelief, or where one scene is made the whole of aridity, or where willfulness is used to deny a later judicial sleep. The hearer’s failure is real. The call to reason remains.**

# 72.43 Reconstructing the Bible-Introduction Proposition: Laziness, Ignorance, and the Theory That the Book Is Hard

A further reconstructible proposition lists ten classes who find the Bible hard to understand: the ignorant of its contents; the too lazy to read it; the biased contrary to its doctrines; rebels who have listened to teachers who explain it away; the uninterested, under a theory that it is hard to understand, which the materials call a satanic theory; the worldly whom it condemns; unstable souls who wrest it; deceivers who use it for their interests; those whom Satan blinds; those who refuse to be converted. Companion propositions say the Bible is simple because it is revelation, because it repeats its truths, because it is written in simple terms, because God is able to speak clearly, and because simply reading and believing is all that understanding requires. A further sentence says the only difficulty is that the book is large and mastery takes time. Rules are added: a doctrine must be proved by two or three scriptures, and the reader must keep a plan of ages and three classes of mankind.

The proposition is not adopted as “the source says.” It is tested. Chapters 69 and 70 already refused the mechanical witness-count, the denial that Christ expounded the writings, and the use of the whole list as a single psychology. The present test asks what the classes contribute to aridity.

**What is claimed.** Hardness is often the reader’s ignorance, laziness, or a theory that the book is hard, and that theory discourages the attempt. Other classes, already tested in earlier chapters, reappear.

**What may be used now.** Ignorance of contents coheres with deficient engagement and with Isaiah’s not-considering and the sealed book’s unreadness. A book not read is hard in the way an unread book is hard. Laziness coheres with νωθρός as sloth and with the failure to labour. The theory that the book is hard, used as a reason not to try, coheres with the learned man’s “I cannot, for it is sealed” and with the competing claim, refused below, that aridity is the Bible’s nature. A theory of hardness can be a cause of the dryness. It is a poor judgment about the writing, and it produces a failure to labour. The remark that the book is large and that time is required coheres with the withholding already stated: unfinished labour at a large book is not sloth. The materials are right to separate size from impossibility, and wrong where another sentence of theirs says simply reading and believing is all understanding requires in a way that denies exposition. Luke 24 records an exposition. Nehemiah 8 records giving the sense. This chapter does not revive the denial Chapter 70 already refused.

**What may not be used as finished.** The ten classes as a complete causal theory. Bias, explaining-away, wresting, and worldliness have been received in earlier files at the size those files required. They are not re-adjudicated as if this chapter were the catalog of all hardness. The two-or-three-verse rule, the plan of ages, and the standing division of mankind into three classes are not adopted as the remedy for dullness. A grid is not water poured on the dry ground. If it is received as the thing that makes the Bible simple, it has become the cistern of Jeremiah 2:13. The percentage that divides the Bible into eighty parts plain and twenty parts prophecy is not a finding. It is a rhetoric of simplicity. Simplicity, in the sense this chapter affirms, is that the writing communicates and is not a mystery by nature. It is not a statistic.

Thus:

> **Ignorance of contents, laziness in reading, and the theory that the book is hard cohere with deficient engagement, failure to labour, and a poor judgment that excuses the unreadness. The ten classes are not a complete psychology. The witness-count, the dispensational grid, and the claim that no exposition is needed are not adopted as the cure of dryness. Size of the book is not impossibility. Sloth is not the same as unfinished work.**

# 72.44 Reconstructing the Aletheia Proposition: Reasonability, Aridity, and the Sentence’s Order

A reconstructible proposition from the project’s own doctrinal materials states that the absence of reasonability, understood in a parenthesis as sound judgment, and the presence of unskillfulness, glossed from Hebrews 5:13 as *apeiros*, inexperienced and unskillful, are responsible for aridity and for tergiversation among persons and among age-old organizations ostensibly Christian; that this has ensued in damnable accepted heresies called doctrines; and that the lack of skill is blamed on failure to realize the scholarly impetus of the scripture and to labour with the diligence the writing requires, the labour being glossed by σπουδάζω and 2 Timothy 2:15. The same materials say that two characteristics of babes are dullness of hearing and unskillfulness in the Word.

The proposition is not adopted as “the source says.” It is tested. Chapters 68, 70, and 71 received portions of the sentence and refused it as a finished sequence. This chapter is the file in which aridity and the loss of reasonability are the assigned objects. The test asks what part of the sentence the present evidence establishes.

**What is claimed.** Failed sound judgment, together with unskill, produces dryness, turning, and then accepted heresies. The root blamed is failure to recognize the writing’s demand for labour and failure to give that labour. Dull hearing and unskill are the two marks of the babe.

**Premises.** Sound judgment is already defined in Chapters 6 and 7. Its loss has been exhibited in this chapter as the failure of proving, considering, and discriminating. Dull hearing has been exhibited from Hebrews 5:11. Turning and accepted heresies have been established in Chapters 70 and 71 on their own evidence, not as consequences deduced from this sentence. The scholarly impetus and *spoudazō* have not been established. *Apeiros* has not been established.

**Biblical correspondence.** The correspondence that holds is the parenthesis and the dryness. Reasonability, taken as sound judgment, is a true name for the proving Romans 12 and Philippians 1 command and for the judgment Matthew 23 says was omitted. The absence of that judgment stands in real connection with the dryness the prophets call thirst, parched places, famine of hearing, and ashes, and with the dullness Hebrews calls νωθρός. Persons and organizations are both in the exhibitions: disciples reason and do not understand; priests reject knowledge; a people are famished; a church’s hearers have become dull. “Ostensibly” remains the caution Chapter 68 already warranted. The correspondence that does not hold, as a finding of this chapter, is the single ordered chain in which absence of reasonability and presence of unskill are the joint cause, aridity the first product, tergiversation the second, and accepted heresies the third, with *spoudazō* as the proved root. The book’s order is the locked order. Turning has been examined. Acceptance has been examined. Aridity is examined now. Unskillfulness is next. Labour is a movement after that. The leaflet’s syntax arranges the titles differently. The arrangement is a map of concerns the investigation has refused to let reorder the knowledge-flow. Dull of hearing is established. Unskilled in the Word is not established by being printed beside it.

**What follows.** The coherent remainder is received with full force at this level. The loss of sound judgment is the loss of reasonability. It stands in connection with aridity, and aridity stands in connection with deficient engagement, poor judgment, and failure to labour in the Word. The failure to labour is blamed in the writings themselves, in the forms this chapter has exhibited, without the Greek verb of 2 Timothy 2:15 being the proof. Organizations are not exempt. The ensuing of heresies from dryness is not required as the proof of dryness, because the dryness has its own exhibitions, including exhibitions in disciples who are not in that hour founding a heresy. Where a settled error is the air a people breathes, the dryness is the condition in which they no longer tell the writing’s reason from the chapel’s voice. That relation is real. It is a connection. It is not a license to rewrite Chapters 70 and 71 as aftereffects of a leaflet’s adverb.

**What does not follow, and is not adopted.** The adverb “predominantly,” as a weighed finding. The writings are plural in their causes. Loved simplicity, rejected knowledge, lusts, the arm of flesh, and judicial giving-over are not minor by a statistic the texts do not give. The blame-line that treats the scholarly impetus and *spoudazō* as already demonstrated is not adopted. Those words will have their chapters. Using them now as the finished root would close Movement X inside a leaflet’s sentence. The identification of the babe’s two marks as one finished doctrine is not adopted. This chapter has written the first mark. Chapter 73 is the second.

Thus:

> **The reconstructible sentence is received where reasonability means sound judgment, where the loss of that judgment stands with aridity, and where failure to labour in the Word is a real connection the writings already blame. It is not adopted as a finished order in which unskill, turning, accepted heresies, and the verb of diligence have been established by the sentence’s syntax. Dull hearing is this chapter. Unskillfulness remains next. The book’s order remains the locked order.**

# 72.45 Reconstructing the Aletheia Proposition: Naming the Dryness Does Not Pour the Water

A second reconstructible feature of the same materials is that, having named aridity and the loss of reasonability, they proceed to state what repentance, faith, baptisms, resurrection, and judgment are to mean, including counts of baptisms, sequences of resurrections and raptures, and a map of the underworld, and they attach those constructions to the passages by citation. They offer the constructions as the first principles a dull hearer should receive in order to leave the milk.

The feature is not adopted as “the source says.” It is tested as an illustration of this chapter’s object. Chapters 69, 70, and 71 refused the grid the offices of governor, of cure for turning, and of replacement orthodoxy. The present question is whether a warning against dryness certifies the cup that is then handed to the dry, and whether that cup, if received because the document named the dryness, would be water or a cistern.

**What is happening in the materials.** A true concern — that hearing becomes dull and judgment fails — is stated. Then a system is spoken as the sense of the passages about the first principles. If that system is received as the floods on the dry ground because it diagnosed the dry ground, the diagnosis has been used as a credential. The credential is the kind Jeremiah 2 condemns when the fountain is forsaken for a hewed container, and the kind Isaiah 29 stages when a reason for not reading is accepted as sufficient. The grid may contain sentences the writing teaches. Those sentences remain the writing’s where they correspond. The grid as a grid is still a teaching a dry mind can accept without judging, which is the poor judgment this chapter has defined. Acceptance of it as the cure, on the ground that the document warned against dullness, is a failure to judge what is said. First Corinthians 10:15 still binds the reader of a leaflet. Judge ye what I say. The saying includes the warning, which can be true, and the next construction, which must meet the writing on its own.

**Premises.** The writing is not dry. A secondary system does not become water by naming thirst. Chapter 71 established that a warning against accepted heresies does not certify the doctrines printed after the warning. The present premise is the dryness, not the acceptance. A doctrine becomes the water only if it is the Word poured, not if it is a system that has talked about pouring.

**What follows.** A document that warns against aridity is itself capable of offering a substitute for the labour of judging the Word, and the warning does not make the substitute floods on the dry ground. Each sentence meets the writing. The true warning does not hydrate the next paragraph. The false paragraph, where it is false, does not erase the true warning. This is the chapter applying its own conclusion to a source it has used. The application is required by honesty. A book that judged the sealed book, the broken cistern, and the answer before hearing, and then exempted its research materials from the duty to judge, would be performing the dryness it has just described: a reason for receiving a container, accepted before the container is heard against the fountain. The First Principles are not taught or corrected here. The cure’s positive form, as scholarly labour and as diligence, is Movement X. The reader is not left without water. The water is the Graphē, which is why the warning about dull hearing could be received in the previous section at all.

**What does not follow here.** That every sentence in the materials has been adjudicated as ashes. This chapter does not write that commentary. It refuses the grid the office of the pouring. That the duty to judge is a license to dismiss a warning one has not tested. The warning about sound judgment was tested and received in part. The constructions were not received as the cure. The difference is the method this whole book has required.

Thus:

> **A warning against aridity does not pour water. If the doctrines printed after the warning were received as the cure because the warning was true, the reception would be the answer before the hearing, and the container instead of the fountain. True warning and unadopted grid can stand in one document. Each sentence meets the writing. The grid is not the floods on the dry ground.**

# 72.46 Competing Claim: The Writing Is Dry

> **The Bible is the obscure thing. Hardness is its nature. Aridity is what a reasonable person feels in front of a book that will not speak plainly. The cure is a livelier interpreter, a thinner canon, or a decision that the plain parts alone bind.**

The claim fails.

Hebrews 5:11 locates the hardness of utterance in the hearers’ dullness. Isaiah 29 delivers a book and records two excuses for not reading it. Amos 8’s famine is of hearing, after the word was clear enough to be forbidden. Proverbs 1 puts wisdom in the street. Isaiah 44 pours water on the dry ground and does not call the water dry. Second Timothy 3:15 keeps the scriptures able to make wise in the chapter that describes a learning which never arrives. Chapter 57’s non-failing record and Chapters 62–63’s unbreakable Scripture are the premises the claim would have to repeal. It does not repeal them by describing the experience of a dull hearer. The experience is real. It is a fact about the hearer. The livelier interpreter, if received as the voice that replaces the hard book, is the transfer Chapter 66 refused and the cistern Jeremiah 2 condemned. The thinner canon, if received as the part one finds easy, is the slowness to believe all that Luke 24 reproaches. The plain parts do bind. They do not bind as a permission to leave the rest sealed. Difficulty of some sentences, already distinguished from dullness and from wresting, remains a real feature. It is not the nature of the whole as a closed mystery.

Thus:

> **The writing is not dry. Hardness of utterance toward the dull is a fact about the hearing. A substitute voice, a reduced canon, and a theory of mystery relocate the dryness into the wrong subject.**

# 72.47 Competing Claim: The Dryness Is Harmless Because the Truth Still Stands

> **If the Scripture cannot be broken, dullness does not matter. The truth remains true whether the people consider or not. Aridity is a private discomfort beside an unchanged authority.**

The claim fails.

The unbreakableness of the Scripture is the reason the dryness can be named as dryness. It is not the reason the dryness is light. Isaiah 5:13 joins the absence of knowledge to captivity, famine, and thirst. Hosea 4:6 joins the lack to destruction and to the priest’s rejection. Amos 8 joins the famine of hearing to fainting. Romans 1 joins the unapproved mind to acts that are not fitting. Hebrews 5:11 joins dullness to a discourse that cannot be uttered and to hearers who are not the teachers the time required. Second Timothy 3:7 joins ever-learning to never coming to the knowledge of the truth, in a paragraph about a form of godliness that denies the power. These are not discomforts. They are losses of the yield the Word was given to produce in hearers: knowledge, judgment, fruit, hope’s diligence, coming to Christ. Chapter 71 refused the kindred claim that acceptance is harmless because the truth still stands. The present claim is that claim moved inward. A truth that remains true while a people cannot consider it is a truth they do not have as knowledge. The objective standing of the writing was never the same object as the hearer’s possession of its sense. Chapter 66 distinguished those objects in order to refuse both transfer and despair. The distinction does not console the dull. It accuses the dullness of being a real failure toward a writing that really stands.

Thus:

> **The truth’s standing does not make the dryness harmless. Captivity, destruction, famine of hearing, an unapproved mind, and a discourse that cannot be uttered are the texts’ own weight. Standing is the measure of the loss, not the cancellation of the loss.**

# 72.48 Competing Claim: The Loss Abolishes a Correct Reading

> **If hearers have become dull, if God has shut eyes and given men over to an unapproved mind, then a correct reading is no longer possible. The commands to consider and to judge are courtesies. Despair is the honest theology of aridity.**

The claim fails.

It is the despair Chapter 66 refused, now using this chapter’s own exhibitions as the proof. The exhibitions do not teach it. Isaiah 1 calls the non-considering people to reason. Proverbs 1 tells the lovers of simplicity to turn and promises that words will be made known. Luke 24 reproaches the slow and then expounds the scriptures. Hebrews 6 charges dull hearers not to be slothful and is persuaded of better things. Romans 12 commands the renewing of the mind unto proving, in the letter that has described the giving-over. John 5 holds out life in the coming. The judicial sentences are real, and they are not universal in the sense the claim needs. The deep sleep is poured on a people in an oracle. The famine is sent in a declared day after a silencing. The unapproved mind is the mind of those who did not approve to retain God in knowledge. Timothy is not told that the scriptures have lost the ability to make wise. The disciples are asked how it is they do not understand, which is unintelligible if understanding had been metaphysically closed. A correct reading remains possible. An incorrect reading remains incorrect. Dryness makes the correct reading harder to utter toward the dull. It does not make the incorrect reading correct, and it does not make the correct one unavailable to the hearer who turns, searches, and judges.

Thus:

> **The loss does not abolish a correct reading. The same writings that name dullness, sleep, famine, and an unapproved mind also command reason, turning, expounding, diligence, and proving. Despair is not the honest reading of those commands. It is the deletion of them.**

# 72.49 Competing Claim: Judicial Hardening Makes the Commands Unfair

> **If the Lord makes the heart fat, pours out deep sleep, sends a famine of hearing, and gives men over to an unapproved mind, then the commands to consider, to judge, and to seek are unjust. The dryness is his act alone. Blame is impiety.**

The claim fails.

The judicial acts are in the texts, and the sequence is in the texts. Isaiah 6 follows Isaiah 1–5. The people have already not considered, have already been dried up for lack of knowledge, have already been called and have not answered, before the heart is made fat. Amos 8 follows the command “Prophesy not.” Romans 1’s giving-over follows “when they knew God” and the refusal to approve retaining him in knowledge. Isaiah 29’s sleep falls where the vision is already, in the people’s handling, a book they will not read, and where fear is taught by the precept of men. The judgment is an answer. An answer is not the first cause of the refusal it answers. Nor is the judgment a fiction. The claim’s grain of truth is that God really does give over, really does send the famine, really does close eyes. The grain does not reach the conclusion that the earlier call was a mockery. Deuteronomy 29:29 keeps the revealed things as the people’s possession, that they may do the words. The secret things are the Lord’s. The revealed command is not a secret. Isaiah 44:18’s shutting is in a satire that has already shown the man making a god from the residue of a tree. The absurdity was available to consideration before the sentence “he hath shut their eyes.” Blame of the not-considering is the prophet’s own speech. Calling that blame impiety corrects the prophet. This chapter does not write a theodicy beyond what these sequences show. The sequences are enough to refuse both the erasure of judgment and the erasure of command.

Thus:

> **Judicial hardening is real and follows refusal. It does not make the refusal innocent, and it does not make the commands unjust. The revealed word remains the people’s possession for doing. The judgment is an answer, not a pretence, and not the sole cause.**

# 72.50 Competing Claim: Activity Is Engagement

> **Ever learning, searching the scriptures, tithing the mint, and hewing cisterns are engagement. A mind that is busy cannot be arid. The charge of dryness is an insult against the industrious.**

The claim fails.

Second Timothy 3:7 is the writing’s own separation of ever learning from coming to the knowledge of the truth. John 5:39–40 is the separation of search from coming to the one the scriptures testify of. Matthew 23:23 is the separation of the tithe of mint from the judgment that was omitted. Jeremiah 2:13 is the separation of hewing from holding water. Mark 8 is the separation of reasoning, and even of correctly counting baskets, from understanding. Activity is real in each case. The yield the text requires is absent. Deficient engagement was defined by that absence, not by an empty calendar. The claim protects the form and loses the end. Proper labour is receiving the words, inclining the ear, seeking as silver, searching whether things are so, preparing the heart to do the law. Busy refusal is still refusal. The insult, where it is an insult, is the text’s: folly and shame, woe, two evils, never able. This chapter does not add a sneer. It reads the separations the writings already draw. The protection of unfinished honest labour, already stated, remains. The Berean is not the captive of ever-learning. The difference is arrival at the question whether things are so, and readiness to receive the word, against a learning that never comes to the knowledge of the truth.

Thus:

> **Activity is not engagement’s yield. Learning, search, exactness, and hewing can be the form of the dryness when they do not come to knowledge, to Christ, to judgment, or to water. Honest unfinished labour is a different object. Busy refusal is the object the texts blame.**

# 72.51 Competing Claim: The Chapel’s Voice Is Reasonability

> **Once a doctrine is the ordinary teaching of a body, reasonability is agreement with that teaching. To test it by the writing is the dryness. The sound mind is the mind that no longer feels the chapel’s word as a question.**

The claim fails.

It is Chapter 71’s failed claim that acceptance transforms error, translated into a definition of judgment. If the translation succeeded, this chapter would be a second acceptance-file, and the loss of reasonability would be renamed fidelity. The texts forbid the renaming. Wisdom in one’s own eyes is a woe, not a description of maturity. The four hundred with one mouth, already judged, are not made reasonable by the unison. The sealed book is not read by the agreement to leave it sealed. Luke 12:57 asks why they do not judge what is right, even of themselves, which is unintelligible if judgment’s only healthy form is the repetition of the settlement. First Corinthians 10:15 assigns the judging of what is said to the hearers, including what is said by an authority they rightly receive. Reception does not cancel the assignment. The condition this chapter has described, in which a people cannot tell the writing’s reason from the chapel’s voice, is the loss. The claim calls that condition reasonability. The inversion is the poor judgment at full strength: the name of the virtue is given to the vice. Chapter 71 established that the settlement does not create truth. This chapter establishes that the settlement does not create sound judgment either. A mind conformed to an untested ordinary is the mind Romans 12 tells not to be conformed to this world, but to be renewed so that it may prove the will of God.

Thus:

> **Agreement with an accepted doctrine is not reasonability. Reasonability proves, judges, and hears the matter through, under the writing. The loss is precisely the condition in which the chapel’s voice can no longer be told from the writing’s reason. Naming that condition health does not make it health.**

# 72.52 Competing Claim: Only the Heart, or Only the Intellect

> **Aridity is a moral problem only. Judgment and study are distractions from the heart. Or: aridity is an intellectual problem only. Lust, refusal, and judicial giving-over are a different subject from the one this chapter was assigned.**

The claim fails in both directions.

The texts will not divide. Isaiah’s people do not consider, and they have rebelled, and they are called to reason. Mark’s disciples do not perceive, and the heart is hardened. Romans 1’s reasonings become vain, and the heart is darkened, and God is not glorified. Second Timothy 3’s ever-learning is led by divers lusts. Hosea’s lack of knowledge is a rejection. Proverbs joins the ear, the heart, the cry, and the fear of the Lord in one conditional sentence. A moral-only account deletes the assignment’s words: intellectual engagement, judgment, labour in the Word. An intellect-only account deletes the heart, the lust, the rejection, and the giving-over that the same exhibitions contain. Chapter 70 already refused this split for the causes of turning. The refusal holds for the dryness. The intellectual acts are real acts. They are never, in these paragraphs, a clean chamber adjacent to obedience. Sound judgment is an intellectual discipline with a moral direction, because it is accountable to the God who speaks and to the reality his speech declares.

Thus:

> **The dryness is intellectual and moral together. Engagement, judgment, and labour are real intellectual failures. Rebellion, hardness, lust, rejection, and giving-over are in the same texts. Neither half is the whole.**

# 72.53 Competing Claim: This Chapter as Already Chapter 71, or Already Chapter 73 or Movement X

> **Once acceptance has been named, the dryness has been explained. The strange law, the sealed book, and the dull ear are simply what an accepted heresy feels like. Or: once dullness has been named, *apeiros* has been written, *spoudazō* has been commanded, and the first principles have been reached. The later chapters are surplus. Hebrews 5:11 is already 5:13. Proverbs 2 is already 2 Timothy 2:15. The time-clause is already the foundation-list.**

The claim fails.

The locked TOC distinguishes the assignments in distinct sentences. Chapter 71 examines how erroneous doctrines become normalized or institutionalized, and why acceptance does not transform error into truth. This chapter examines aridity in connection with deficient intellectual engagement, poor judgment, and failure to labour properly in the Word. Chapter 73 develops *apeiros*, unskillfulness in the Word. Movement X examines scholarly labour, *spoudazō*, rightly dividing, and readiness for the First Principles. Distinction of assignments is not a delay of nerve. It is the knowledge-flow.

Mark 8 belongs to this file because the disciples’ unfruitful reasoning is not an accepted heresy. If dryness were only the feeling of a settlement, the scene would have no place, and the place it has is plain. Hebrews 5:11 has been expounded as dull hearing and as a loss after time. Hebrews 5:13 has not been expounded as unskillfulness in the word of righteousness. The milk, the meat, and the exercised senses have not been made the doctrine of maturity. Proverbs 2 has been used as the shape of a labour whose refusal is dryness. The verb σπουδάζω, the approved workman, and the right division of the word have not been opened. Hebrews 6:12 has been used as the prohibition of the dullness-adjective. Hebrews 6:1–2 has not been taught. The leaflet’s sentence, which runs the titles together, was tested and not adopted as a reorder of the book. Naming a neighbourhood is not performing the next file. Performing this file is not reprinting the last one.

Thus:

> **This chapter is not already Chapter 71, and it is not already Chapter 73 or Movement X. The dryness of engagement, judgment, and labour is the object. Later questions remain later.**

# 72.54 Reason Through the Evidence

The inference can now be exhibited.

**Premise 1.** Truth is conformity to reality and is not created by belief, popularity, institutional consensus, or sincerity. The Graphē is the primary written authority. The record does not fail as truthful divine writing of what it actually communicates. The Scripture cannot be broken. Sound judgment is an inspectable discipline accountable to grounds. The Lord calls his people to reason (Chapters 1–7, 57, 62–63, and 65, used not retried).

**Premise 2.** The authority of that writing is distinct from the authority claimed for a later statement of sense. Transfer fails. Despair fails. A correct reading remains possible. An incorrect reading remains incorrect (Chapters 66 and 67, used not retried).

**Premise 3.** Scripture is misread in five inspectable modes. The writing remains the measure of those modes (Chapter 67, used not retried). The modes are not the present catalog. They are the reason a further condition can be named without being a sixth mode.

**Premise 4.** Theology, philosophy, tradition, and institution are secondary systems. When they govern meaning, the seat of sense is occupied and the test is reversed. The writing is not broken (Chapters 68 and 69, used not retried).

**Premise 5.** Doctrinal turning aside is an inspectable movement permitted by intellectual failures that do not excuse it. Erroneous doctrines become ordinary and official by operations the writings exhibit, and the reception does not transform error into truth. Refusal remains possible (Chapters 70 and 71, used not retried).

**Premise 6.** The writings themselves exhibit the dryness. A people do not know and do not consider, and are still called to reason (Isaiah 1). They are famished and dried up with thirst for lack of knowledge, and wise in their own eyes (Isaiah 5). Hearts are made fat and ears dull, after prior indictment, and ears that hear are still invited (Isaiah 6; Matthew 13). A book is delivered and not read, under a poured-out sleep, and the wisdom of the wise perishes (Isaiah 29). Water is poured on the dry ground, and the idolater feeds on ashes and does not say that a lie is in his hand (Isaiah 44; Psalm 115). The fountain is forsaken for cisterns that hold no water (Jeremiah 2). The man who trusts in flesh inhabits the parched places and does not see when good comes; the tree by the waters still yields in drought (Jeremiah 17; Psalm 1). Knowledge is rejected and the law forgotten; fallow ground is left unbroken (Hosea 4; Hosea 10). A famine of hearing is sent after prophets are silenced, and a wide search does not find the word (Amos 2; Amos 8). Wisdom cries in the street, and the answer before hearing is folly (Proverbs 1; Proverbs 18). The knowledge of God is found on the path of receiving, crying, and searching, and the Lord gives what is sought (Proverbs 2). Disciples reason, count the baskets, and do not understand (Mark 8). Hearers are commanded to judge what is right, to judge righteous judgment, and to judge what is said (Luke 12; John 7; 1 Corinthians 10). Hearts are slow to believe all that the prophets have spoken, and the scriptures are then expounded (Luke 24). Searchers will not come to the one of whom the scriptures testify (John 5). Readiness and daily search, and a heart prepared to seek, to do, and to teach, are real contraries (Acts 17; Ezra 7). Knowing God, men do not approve to retain him in knowledge, and are given over to an unapproved mind; the mind is also commanded to be renewed so that it may prove the will of God, and love is prayed up into knowledge and all judgment (Romans 1; Romans 12; Philippians 1). Judgment is omitted while mint is tithed (Matthew 23). Some are ever learning and never able to come to the knowledge of the truth, while the scriptures are able to make wise unto salvation (2 Timothy 3). Hearers have become dull of hearing, so that many things are hard to utter, after a time in which they ought to be teachers; the same adjective is then forbidden: be not slothful (Hebrews 5:11–12; Hebrews 6:12).

**Premise 7.** Reconstructible Introduction and Aletheia propositions, tested rather than cited as authority, cohere where they locate dullness in the hearers rather than in the subject as a mystery, where they restate Mark 8’s unused organs and memory without condemning reasoning as such, where they join ignorance, laziness, and the theory of hardness to unreadness, and where they take reasonability as sound judgment standing in connection with aridity and with failure to labour. They do not cohere, and are not adopted, where a seven-item or ten-item list becomes the whole psychology, where simply reading is said to cancel exposition, where a verse-count or a dispensational grid is installed as the water, where unskillfulness, *spoudazō*, and the leaflet’s causal order are treated as already finished, or where the warning against dryness is used as the credential of the warner’s system.

**Premise 8.** Competing reductions fail: the writing as the dry thing; harmlessness; despair of a correct reading; judicial hardening as excuse or as injustice; activity as proof of engagement; the chapel’s voice as reasonability; the heart-only and the intellect-only splits; this file as already Chapter 71 or as already Chapter 73 or Movement X.

**Inference.** Therefore aridity is the dryness in which the Word given to be heard, judged, and laboured in does not yield, in the hearer, the sound judgment that engagement requires. The loss of reasonability is that failure of judgment, not the disappearance of thought and not the obscurity of the Graphē. The three connections are exhibited. The writing is not broken by the loss. The hearer is not excused. The contrary is real. The investigator is warranted, with full force, in receiving this as Movement IX’s seventh word.

The inference is not a leap from “some do not understand” to “no one can understand.” The commands and the contraries forbid that leap. The inference is not a leap from “dryness is grave” to “unskillfulness and the method of diligence have been written.” Sequence forbids that leap. The inference is not a leap from “a source warned against dullness” to “the source’s system is the water.” Testing forbids that leap. The inference is not a leap from “acceptance can dull a people” to “aridity is only acceptance under another name.” Mark 8 and Hebrews 5:11 forbid that leap.

Thus:

> **The join is inspectable: primary writing + distinguished interpretation + possible correct reading + catalogued misreading + ranked secondary systems + governance + turning + acceptance already exhibited + the writings’ own scenes of not-considering, thirst, dull organs, sealed book, ashes, broken cisterns, parched places, rejected knowledge, famine of hearing, answer before hearing, unfruitful reasoning, omitted judgment, ever-learning, and dullness after time = a warranted account of aridity and the loss of reasonability, in connection with deficient engagement, poor judgment, and failure to labour properly in the Word, at this level and not beyond it.**

# 72.55 Explicit Versus Inferred

What is explicit in the writings used here includes: the ox’s knowledge set against Israel’s not-knowing and not-considering, in a chapter that also says, Come now, and let us reason together (Isaiah 1:3, 18); captivity because the people have no knowledge, the multitude dried up with thirst, and woe on those wise in their own eyes (Isaiah 5:13, 21); hearing without understanding, a fat heart, heavy ears, shut eyes, lest they convert and be healed, cited where a people seeing see not (Isaiah 6:9–10; Matthew 13:13–15); a spirit of deep sleep, a vision as a sealed book, the learned and the unlearned both declining to read, and the wisdom of the wise perishing (Isaiah 29:9–14); water poured on the thirsty and on the dry ground, and a man who feeds on ashes and does not say that a lie is in his right hand (Isaiah 44:3, 18–20); makers of idols become like them, eyes that see not (Psalm 115:4–8); two evils, the fountain forsaken and broken cisterns that hold no water (Jeremiah 2:13); the heath in the parched places who does not see when good comes, and the tree by the waters that does not cease from fruit in drought (Jeremiah 17:5–8); # 72.56 The Warranted Conclusion Stated with Full Force

The conclusion may now be stated without postponement and without inflation.

Aridity is the dryness in which the Word is given to be heard, judged, and laboured in, and the hearer does not yield the reasonability that such engagement requires.

**Reasonability** is sound judgment: the discipline that perceives, distinguishes, infers, and proves according to grounds. Its loss is not the silence of the mind. It is the failure of that discipline toward the writing. Men still reason, learn, tithe, hew, and call themselves wise. The yield is ashes, a sealed book, a counted basket and no understanding, a mint-tithe and an omitted judgment, a learning that never comes to the knowledge of the truth.

**The three connections** are inspectable. Engagement is deficient when the word, the time, the eyes, the ears, or the memory are present and the act of considering and holding is not. Judgment is poor when the discrimination does not prove what the grounds require, when it answers before hearing, when it judges by appearance, when it omits judgment, when it is wise in its own eyes, when it will not say that the lie is in the hand. Labour in the Word fails when the seeking the writings command is not given, or is given toward a cistern, a form of godliness, or a search that will not come. The three confirm one another and are not one word.

**The writing** is not dry. Hardness of utterance is located in the hearers. The Scripture is not broken by dull ears. A correct reading remains possible. An incorrect reading remains incorrect. The truth’s survival does not make the dryness harmless, and the dryness does not make an accepted error true.

**The hearer** is not excused. Not-considering, rejected knowledge, loved simplicity, the will not to come, and the ought of the time are charges. Judicial fatness, sleep, famine, and giving-over follow refusal. They are real. They are not the innocence of the first act, and they are not the injustice of the command. Be not slothful. Judge ye what is right. Turn at the reproof. Search whether those things are so.

This continues Movement IX. It is not Chapter 71 retried. It is not Chapter 73. It is not Movement X. That is this chapter’s conclusion, stated here with full force.

Thus:

> **Aridity in connection with deficient intellectual engagement, poor judgment, and failure to labour properly in the Word has been examined, and the loss of reasonability has been shown as the failure of sound judgment toward a Word that remains clear, binding, and able to be heard. The dryness is in force. Later questions remain later because they are different questions.**

# 72.57 Apply the Standard

The standard has been applied.

A hearer, a priest, a school, or a people is now to be asked not only, “Has an erroneous teaching become what the body holds?” — Chapter 71’s question — but, “Is the hearing dull, the judgment unproving, the labour improper, toward a Word that is in reach?” Do they consider? Do they believe all that the prophets have spoken, or only the part that fits the hope they already had? Do they answer before they hear? Do they omit judgment while they are exact about the small clause? Do they learn without coming to the knowledge of the truth? Do they search and not come? Do they cite the seal, or the lack of learning, or the theory that the book is hard, as the reason the book stays shut? Has the time been long enough that they ought to be teachers, and are they still dull? Is the chapel’s voice the thing they can no longer tell from the writing’s reason? Is the busyness a hewing of cisterns?

The questions are practical without becoming a director’s manual, a history of mystical desolation, or a technique of study. A people who confess that Scripture is primary may still be dried up with thirst for lack of knowledge. A disciple who has seen the works may still reason and not understand. A learned man may still say, I cannot, for it is sealed. A research document may warn against dullness and still offer its grid as the water. A hard sentence may still be hard without the hearer being dull, and a dull hearer may still be dull in front of a sentence that is plain.

The standard also protects. Thirst that seeks God is not aridity. The patience that hears a matter through before answering is not poor judgment. Unfinished labour at a large book is not sloth. The tree that keeps its fruit in a year of drought is not the heath in the parched places. A beginning is not yet a loss. Service that receives the word with readiness and searches whether things are so is not the dryness it resists. The protection is the same fact as the judgment: the writing is the measure of both the hearing and the dullness.

The standard is the Graphē. Aridity is the loss of reasonability in the three connections the assignment names. The openings Chapter 71 named do not, by being intellectually untested, move the standard. The dryness does not move it either.

Thus:

> **The standard has been applied: aridity in connection with deficient engagement, poor judgment, and failure to labour properly in the Word has been identified as Movement IX’s seventh word, not announced as a slogan, and not postponed as if the dryness were weak.**

# 72.58 From Aridity to Unskillfulness

A dryness, now controlled as an inspectable loss of sound judgment that does not make the writing obscure and does not excuse the hearer, still leaves a further question.

The aridity-file is in force. Engagement, judgment, and labour fail in the ways exhibited. The Word remains water and measure. The contrary remains possible. Those sentences are the cleared ground. They are not yet the dedicated examination of ἄπειρος (*apeiros*), unskillfulness in the Word.

If the dryness were not stated, the next question would still be what the loss of reasonability is. The dryness has been stated. If this chapter were already the account of unskillfulness, the next file would be repetition. This chapter is not that account. The next question is therefore the unskillfulness the locked assignment names, which closes Movement IX: Ἄπειρος — Unskillfulness in the Word.

Hebrews 5:13 stands in the paragraph this chapter has used and has not expounded. The dull of hearing have been examined. The one who uses milk, unskillful in the word of righteousness, for he is a babe, has not been examined. The relationship between lack of skill, immaturity, and inability to handle the Word appropriately is the next file’s own sentence. The leaflet joined the two marks. The join has been named as neighbourhood. Chapter 73 must develop the second mark. It must not steal this dryness as if dull hearing had not been stated. This file must not steal the unskillfulness as if *apeiros* had already been the chapter.

That is the proper open of the next chapter. The dryness was never meant to be a slogan that finishes Movement IX, and it was never meant to deny that the dull hearer is also, in the same paragraph, described as unskillful. It was meant to be the naming of aridity and the loss of reasonability, in the three connections assigned, sufficient to justify taking the next question, which is the question the movement itself forces once hearing has been shown dull and the dullness has not been allowed to swallow the neighbouring word: what is *apeiros*, unskillfulness in the Word, and how do lack of skill, immaturity, and inability to handle the Word stand together?

The next file must not treat this dryness as if the loss of reasonability had not been described. This file must not treat that unskillfulness as if Chapter 73 were already written. Naming the neighbourhood is not performing the examination.
