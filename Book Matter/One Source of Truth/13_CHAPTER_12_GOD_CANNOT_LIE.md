# ONE SOURCE OF TRUTH
## The Doctrine of the Graphē

# MOVEMENT II — GOD, TRUTH, AND REVELATION

# Chapter 12 — God Cannot Lie

> **Central question:** Why is it impossible for God to lie, and what does that impossibility establish about the trustworthiness of genuine divine communication?

Chapter 11 established three closely related features of God's nature:

> **God is true.**

> **God is faithful.**

> **God is immutable.**

Those conclusions were necessary, but they did not yet answer the strongest question raised by the biblical record.

The question is not merely:

> **Does God tell the truth?**

Nor merely:

> **Has God proved faithful?**

Nor even:

> **Does God remain consistent?**

The stronger biblical claim is:

> **“It was impossible for God to lie.”**  
> — Hebrews 6:18

That statement requires careful treatment.

If it merely meant that God has made a moral decision never to lie, it would tell us something important about His conduct. But Hebrews does not use merely behavioral language. It speaks of **impossibility**.

The question therefore becomes:

> **Why is lying impossible for God?**

The answer cannot simply be:

> “Because the Bible says God cannot lie.”

That would repeat the proposition without explaining it.

Nor can the answer be:

> “Because God chooses not to lie.”

That reduces impossibility to preference.

The investigation must go deeper:

> **What is God such that falsehood is incompatible with Him?**

That question belongs exactly here in the architecture of the book. The preceding chapters established truth, the ground of truth, and the truthfulness, faithfulness, and immutability of God. This chapter now draws those strands together and examines the impossibility of divine falsehood before the book turns to revelation.

---

# 1. “God Cannot Lie” Is Stronger Than “God Does Not Lie”

The first distinction is linguistic and logical.

There is a significant difference between:

> **God does not lie**

and:

> **God cannot lie.**

A statement that someone does not lie may describe a pattern of conduct.

A statement that someone cannot lie concerns a limitation—or, in this case, an incompatibility—within the nature of the subject.

Hebrews 6:18 says:

> **“it was impossible for God to lie.”**

Titus 1:2 likewise speaks of:

> **“God, that cannot lie.”**

The biblical language therefore does not merely establish an excellent record of divine honesty.

It establishes something more fundamental:

> **Falsehood is incompatible with God.**

That distinction matters enormously for the epistemological argument.

If God merely happened never to lie, then the possibility of divine falsehood would remain conceptually open. A person could ask:

> “What prevents God from saying something false tomorrow?”

But if falsehood is impossible for God because it is incompatible with His nature, then the question changes.

The issue is no longer:

> “Has God chosen to be truthful?”

It becomes:

> **“Can the God who is truth cease to be truthful?”**

The biblical answer is no.

---

# 2. What Would a Lie Actually Require?

To understand the force of the biblical claim, we should first understand what a lie is.

A lie is not simply an inaccurate sentence.

An inaccurate statement may result from:

- ignorance;
- mistaken perception;
- defective memory;
- confusion;
- inadequate information;
- faulty inference;
- or deception.

A lie involves something more specific: the communication of falsehood as though it were true, with the relevant intention of deceiving or misrepresenting reality.

That distinction matters because God is not presented as a finite knower who discovers reality gradually and may then misreport what He has discovered.

The biblical conception presents God as:

- the Creator;
- the source of existence;
- the knower of reality;
- true;
- faithful;
- holy;
- unchanging;
- and incapable of falsehood.

Therefore the question is not:

> “Could God make a factual mistake?”

as though God were a finite observer.

The biblical question is deeper:

> **Could falsehood proceed from the One whose nature is truth?**

The answer is no.

---

# 3. Truth Is Not Something God Merely Consults

This point follows from Chapter 10.

If truth were something external to God—a standard existing above Him to which He must conform—then God would not be the ultimate ground of truth.

He would be another knower standing under a higher standard.

But the investigation has already distinguished:

> **truth as reality and correspondence**

from:

> **human recognition of truth.**

God does not become truthful because human beings verify Him.

Human verification discovers what is true.

Likewise, God does not become truthful because He conforms to an external human standard.

The biblical conception presents God Himself as true.

Christ says:

> **“I am ... the truth.”**  
> — John 14:6

Paul writes:

> **“as God is true...”**  
> — 2 Corinthians 1:18

The biblical record therefore does not merely portray God as a being who possesses correct information.

It presents truth as belonging to His identity.

This is why the statement that God cannot lie has ontological significance.

Falsehood is not merely something God dislikes.

It is incompatible with who He is.

---

# 4. “God Is Not a Man, That He Should Lie”

Numbers 23:19 gives one of the clearest biblical contrasts:

> **“God is not a man, that he should lie; neither the son of man, that he should repent...”**

The verse does more than condemn lying.

It contrasts God with human beings.

Human beings are finite.

They can:

- lack knowledge;
- misperceive reality;
- forget;
- contradict themselves;
- change their minds;
- break promises;
- deceive;
- and deliberately misrepresent what they know.

The verse refuses to transfer these limitations to God.

It then immediately connects God's truthfulness with the reliability of His Word:

> **“hath he said, and shall he not do it? or hath he spoken, and shall he not make it good?”**

Notice the structure.

God:

> **says**

and therefore:

> **does.**

God:

> **speaks**

and therefore:

> **fulfills.**

The passage joins speech, truthfulness, faithfulness, and action.

This is exactly why Chapter 11 established faithfulness before Chapter 12.

The God who speaks is not a source whose statements must be treated as provisional guesses.

His Word corresponds to His nature and His action.

---

# 5. Titus 1:2 — “God, That Cannot Lie”

Titus 1:2 is unusually direct:

> **“In hope of eternal life, which God, that cannot lie, promised before the world began.”**

Three ideas appear together:

1. God;
2. His inability to lie;
3. His promise.

The order is significant.

The promise is trustworthy because the One who makes it **cannot lie**.

The text does not say:

> “God promised, and we hope He keeps His promise.”

The credibility of the promise is grounded in the character of the Promiser.

This gives us an important epistemological principle:

> **The reliability of a communication is inseparable from the reliability of its source.**

But this principle must still be applied carefully.

Titus 1:2 does not mean:

> “Any statement anyone attributes to God must be true.”

It means:

> **What genuinely proceeds from God cannot be false because God cannot lie.**

That distinction will become indispensable when the book later examines the authentication of claimed revelation.

---

# 6. Hebrews 6:17–18 — The Strongest Formulation

Hebrews 6:17–18 brings the argument together:

> **“Wherein God, willing more abundantly to shew unto the heirs of promise the immutability of his counsel, confirmed it by an oath:  
> That by two immutable things, in which it was impossible for God to lie, we might have a strong consolation...”**

The passage does not present God's inability to lie in isolation.

It places it within a structure involving:

- God's purpose;
- His counsel;
- immutability;
- promise;
- oath;
- and the impossibility of falsehood.

The writer is therefore showing why the recipients can possess strong confidence.

The confidence does not rest upon human optimism.

It rests upon the character of God.

The logic can be represented as:

> **God's counsel is immutable**
>
> ↓
>
> **God confirms His promise**
>
> ↓
>
> **the confirming realities are immutable**
>
> ↓
>
> **it is impossible for God to lie**
>
> ↓
>
> **therefore the promise provides strong ground for confidence.**

The important point is that the writer does not say merely:

> “God probably will not lie.”

The impossibility of lying is presented as a foundation for confidence.

---

# 7. Why Is It Impossible?

We can now answer the central question.

> **Why is it impossible for God to lie?**

Because, in the biblical conception, falsehood is incompatible with God's nature.

God is not merely a powerful person who happens to possess moral preferences.

He is presented as true.

Christ says:

> **“I am ... the truth.”**

The biblical record says:

> **“God is true.”**

And Hebrews says:

> **“it was impossible for God to lie.”**

These statements belong together.

The reasoning is:

1. A lie is falsehood presented deceptively as truth.
2. God is true.
3. God does not merely possess truth; truth is attributed to His very nature.
4. Falsehood is therefore incompatible with that nature.
5. Consequently, God cannot lie.

This is not an argument from God's statistical history of honesty.

It is an argument from His nature.

---

# 8. Does “Cannot” Mean God Lacks Power?

A serious question arises here.

If God is omnipotent, what can it mean to say that He **cannot** do something?

The answer requires a distinction between power and contradiction.

To say:

> **God cannot lie**

does not mean that God encounters an external force stronger than Himself.

It does not mean that some independent authority prevents Him from lying.

It means that lying is incompatible with what God is.

The distinction is similar to the difference between:

> **having power**

and:

> **being able to make a contradiction true.**

The existence of divine power does not turn contradictions into realities.

If a proposition is intrinsically contradictory, omnipotence does not make the contradiction coherent.

Likewise, if falsehood is incompatible with God's nature, His inability to lie is not a weakness.

It is a consequence of His perfection.

God's inability to lie is therefore not evidence against omnipotence.

It is evidence concerning the kind of being God is.

---

# 9. “Cannot Lie” Does Not Mean “Cannot Speak”

Another distinction is equally important.

God's inability to lie does not mean God is incapable of communication.

Quite the opposite.

The biblical record presents God as a speaking God.

He:

- speaks;
- commands;
- reveals;
- promises;
- warns;
- testifies;
- sends;
- teaches;
- and communicates.

The question is not whether God can speak.

The question is whether falsehood can genuinely proceed from Him.

The biblical answer is:

> **No.**

This becomes essential when we later examine the ways the biblical record says God communicated:

- by His audible voice;
- through angels;
- through prophets;
- through Christ;
- through apostles;
- through visions;
- through dreams;
- through revelation;
- and through inspired Scripture.

The impossibility of falsehood establishes the **source-side reliability** of genuine divine communication.

It does not by itself authenticate every alleged communication.

---

# 10. Truthfulness and the Possibility of Divine Revelation

The argument has now reached an important bridge.

If God cannot lie, then genuine divine revelation cannot be false.

That conclusion follows only conditionally:

> **If God genuinely revealed it, then it is true.**

This is different from:

> **If someone says God revealed it, then it is true.**

The first follows from God's nature.

The second does not.

This distinction is indispensable.

Suppose a person writes:

> “God told me X.”

There are now at least two questions:

### Question 1

> **Can God communicate X truthfully?**

If God genuinely communicates X, then yes, because God cannot lie.

### Question 2

> **Did God actually communicate X to this person?**

That is an authentication question.

The first question concerns God's nature.

The second concerns evidence.

The two must not be conflated.

---

# 11. God's Inability to Lie Does Not Authenticate Human Claims

This is where many arguments become circular.

Consider:

> “God cannot lie.”

Therefore:

> “This document is true.”

That inference is invalid unless the missing premise has been established:

> **“This document genuinely comes from God.”**

The proper structure is:

> **God cannot lie.**

+

> **This communication genuinely comes from God.**

Therefore:

> **This genuine divine communication is truthful.**

The second premise still requires authentication.

This is precisely why the book does not jump from Chapter 12 directly to:

> “Therefore the Bible is infallible.”

That conclusion belongs later, after the investigation of:

- what the Bible is;
- what kind of record it is;
- how revelation was given;
- witnesses;
- Christ;
- apostles;
- inspiration;
- canon;
- historical testimony;
- and cumulative authentication.

The architecture is deliberately preventing circular reasoning.

---

# 12. God's Truthfulness and the Reliability of His Word

The preceding chapters have prepared a second important conclusion.

If God's genuine communication is truthful, then God's Word cannot be treated as though it were merely one fallible human opinion among others.

The issue is not that human beings automatically understand everything God communicates.

That is a different question.

There is a crucial distinction between:

> **truthfulness of the source**

and:

> **accuracy of the receiver's interpretation.**

God may communicate truthfully while a human being misunderstands what God has communicated.

Therefore:

> **Divine truthfulness does not imply human interpretive infallibility.**

This distinction will become essential later when the book addresses Scripture and interpretation.

The authority of the text must not be confused with the authority of every interpretation of the text.

---

# 13. God's Truthfulness Does Not Eliminate the Need for Human Reason

The same distinction applies to reasoning.

If God cannot lie, it does not follow that every human conclusion about God's Word is automatically true.

A person can possess an infallible text and reason fallibly about it.

They can:

- quote the wrong passage;
- misunderstand context;
- confuse two meanings of a word;
- draw an invalid inference;
- ignore a qualification;
- mistake metaphor for literal description;
- or force a conclusion the text does not establish.

Therefore the reader still needs:

> **knowledge, evidence, reasoning, judgment, skill, and disciplined interpretation.**

This is not a concession to human authority over Scripture.

It is recognition of human fallibility.

The text can be authoritative while the reader remains responsible for understanding it correctly.

---

# 14. God's Truthfulness and God's Faithfulness Are Not Identical

Chapter 11 distinguished these attributes, and the distinction remains important here.

### Truthfulness

Concerns the incompatibility of falsehood with God's nature.

### Faithfulness

Concerns God's dependable adherence to His Word, covenant, and promises.

A truthful statement and a faithful promise are related but not identical.

For example, someone might tell the truth about what exists while failing to keep a promise.

The biblical conception of God does not leave that gap.

God is:

> **true**

and:

> **faithful.**

Therefore His communication is not merely accurate in content; His commitments are dependable in execution.

This becomes important when the biblical record later presents fulfilled prophecy, historical action, covenant fulfillment, and the correspondence between God's Word and events.

---

# 15. God's Truthfulness and Immutability Are Also Related

Immutability adds another layer.

Suppose a being were truthful today but could fundamentally become false tomorrow.

Its previous truthfulness would not provide permanent epistemic security.

But the biblical conception of God does not permit that instability.

Hebrews 6:17 speaks of:

> **“the immutability of his counsel.”**

James 1:17 speaks of:

> **“no variableness, neither shadow of turning.”**

Therefore:

> **God's inability to lie is not an isolated momentary characteristic.**

It belongs to the stable character of God.

The God who is true does not later become false.

The God who is faithful does not later become faithless.

The God whose counsel is immutable does not reverse Himself through a corruption of character.

This is why truthfulness, faithfulness, and immutability form a coherent foundation for trustworthy revelation.

---

# 16. 2 Timothy 2:13 — “He Cannot Deny Himself”

Paul writes:

> **“if we believe not, yet he abideth faithful: he cannot deny himself.”**

This statement adds another dimension.

The issue is not simply:

> “God has a rule against lying.”

The statement:

> **“he cannot deny himself”**

points again toward the relationship between God's actions and His identity.

God remains faithful because He cannot become contrary to Himself.

This gives us a useful conceptual formulation:

> **God cannot truthfully become false to His own nature.**

His truthfulness is not an external costume.

It belongs to Him.

Therefore falsehood is not merely an unacceptable behavior that He happens to avoid.

It is contrary to His identity.

---

# 17. 1 John 1:5 — No Darkness in Him

John gives another important description:

> **“God is light, and in him is no darkness at all.”**

The statement is not merely poetic decoration.

It contributes to the biblical picture of God's moral and epistemic purity.

If:

> **God is light**

and:

> **“in him is no darkness at all,”**

then deliberate falsehood cannot be treated as something naturally compatible with Him.

Again, the argument is cumulative.

We are not building the doctrine from one isolated phrase.

The biblical record converges:

> **God is true.**

> **God cannot lie.**

> **God is faithful.**

> **God is immutable.**

> **God cannot deny Himself.**

> **In Him is no darkness.**

These are not interchangeable statements, but together they illuminate why Hebrews can speak of the impossibility of God lying.

---

# 18. God Is Not the Author of Falsehood

If God cannot lie, then falsehood cannot be attributed to God as though it originated in His nature.

This creates an important distinction between:

> **God's Word**

and:

> **human claims made in God's name.**

A prophet may falsely claim divine authorization.

A teacher may misinterpret Scripture.

An institution may elevate its doctrine above Scripture.

A commentator may make an erroneous statement about God.

A person may sincerely believe that God has spoken when God has not spoken.

None of these possibilities contradicts God's truthfulness.

They demonstrate why authentication is necessary.

The truthful source does not make every alleged messenger truthful.

---

# 19. The Difference Between Divine Truth and Human Sincerity

The manuscript must be especially careful here.

A person's sincerity is not the foundation of divine truth.

A person can sincerely believe something false.

But that does not mean sincerity itself is worthless.

The relevant distinction is:

> **A sincere person may be honestly reporting what he believes.**

Yet:

> **what he believes may still fail to correspond to reality.**

Therefore the question concerning revelation is not:

> “Was the messenger sincere?”

alone.

It is:

> **Was the messenger genuinely commissioned by God, and is the testimony reliable?**

Sincerity can be relevant evidence concerning a person's state of mind.

It is not sufficient evidence that the message came from God.

This distinction protects the book from both extremes:

- treating sincerity as proof;
- or treating sincerity as though it has no evidential relevance at all.

---

# 20. Divine Truthfulness Does Not Mean Every Sentence Recorded in Scripture Is a Divine Utterance

This distinction must also be preserved for the chapters ahead.

The statement:

> **“God cannot lie”**

does not mean:

> “Every human speaker recorded in Scripture always speaks truth.”

The Bible records:

- truthful speech;
- false speech;
- questions;
- accusations;
- lies;
- misunderstandings;
- satanic speech;
- human arguments;
- historical testimony;
- divine speech;
- prophetic speech;
- and inspired narrative.

The inspiration of Scripture concerns the truthfulness and authority of the **written record**, not the proposition that every character's statement is itself a divine declaration.

This distinction will matter greatly when the book later establishes what “all Scripture” means.

The record can truthfully report:

> **“Someone lied.”**

without making the lie itself a true proposition.

The truthfulness of the record concerns the truth of what the record asserts as record.

This is why the doctrine of inspiration must be developed carefully rather than reduced to a slogan.

---

# 21. What Does “God Cannot Lie” Establish About Revelation?

We can now state the conclusion precisely.

It establishes a conditional but powerful principle:

> **If God genuinely reveals something, the revelation is truthful because falsehood is incompatible with God.**

This conclusion does not yet establish:

> **that every claimed revelation is genuine.**

That requires evidence.

But it establishes why the authenticity of genuine revelation matters so much.

If a message can be demonstrated to come from God, the investigator is no longer dealing with an ordinary fallible human source.

The source's nature changes the epistemic status of the communication.

Thus:

> **God's inability to lie is the source-side premise for the trustworthiness of genuine divine revelation.**

The authentication of the revelation is the communication-side problem.

The two must remain distinct.

---

# 22. Why This Matters for the Bible

The future argument concerning the Bible now has a necessary foundation.

Eventually the book will ask:

> **Why should Scripture be regarded as divine revelation?**

That question cannot be answered merely by saying:

> “Because God cannot lie.”

God's inability to lie establishes what follows **if God has spoken**.

It does not by itself identify which documents contain His speech.

The later argument therefore has to establish:

1. that God exists;
2. what God is like;
3. that God is true and cannot lie;
4. that God communicates/reveals;
5. how revelation was given;
6. who witnessed or received it;
7. how it was recorded;
8. what inspiration means;
9. what constitutes Scripture;
10. how the canonical corpus is recognized;
11. what evidence authenticates the record.

Only then can the conclusion concerning the written record be responsibly drawn.

This is why the book's architecture is sequential.

---

# 23. Why the Bible's Own Claim Still Matters

The anti-circularity rule does not mean that biblical self-testimony should be ignored.

Quite the opposite.

Once the investigation reaches the biblical writings, what they claim about their own origin becomes important evidence.

The Bible's internal testimony concerning:

- God speaking;
- prophetic communication;
- apostolic witness;
- inspiration;
- Scripture;
- Christ;
- revelation;

must be examined.

But the logical status of those claims must remain clear.

A document's statement:

> “I am from God”

is evidence of what the document claims.

It is not, by itself, sufficient external authentication of the claim.

The proper method is cumulative:

> **internal claim**
>
> +
>
> **historical testimony**
>
> +
>
> **witness**
>
> +
>
> **Christ's testimony**
>
> +
>
> **prophecy**
>
> +
>
> **miracles**
>
> +
>
> **historical correspondence**
>
> +
>
> **unity and continuity**
>
> +
>
> **transmission and preservation**
>
> +
>
> **canonical recognition**
>
> +
>
> **rational evaluation**
>
> → **cumulative authentication.**

The Bible's self-testimony is therefore not discarded.

It is placed in its proper evidential position.

---

# 24. What God Cannot Lie About

There is another subtle point.

If God communicates a proposition as a proposition, its truthfulness is secured by God.

But this does not mean that every statement involving God must be interpreted as a universal proposition detached from context.

For example, Scripture can record:

> “The fool hath said in his heart, There is no God.”

The inspired record is truthfully reporting what the fool said.

The truth of the record is not the same thing as the truth of the fool's worldview.

Therefore the reader must distinguish:

> **what the text records**

from:

> **what the recorded speaker asserts**

and from:

> **what the inspired author is asserting by recording it.**

This is not a weakness in Scripture.

It is evidence that the text must be read intelligently.

The inspired record can truthfully report false statements without becoming false.

That distinction will become important later when interpreting Scripture.

---

# 25. The Epistemological Consequence

The epistemological consequence can now be stated with precision.

If God is true and cannot lie, then:

> **genuine divine communication is not merely probable because of God's good reputation.**

Its truthfulness follows from the nature of its source.

But human beings still face a separate epistemological task:

> **How do we know that a particular communication genuinely comes from God?**

That requires:

- evidence;
- testimony;
- historical investigation;
- rational inference;
- authentication;
- and eventually examination of the written record.

Therefore the doctrine of divine inability to lie does two things simultaneously.

### It strengthens the conclusion concerning genuine revelation.

If God spoke, God did not deceive.

### It preserves the need for authentication.

We still have to establish that God actually spoke.

This is precisely the balance the book requires.

---

# 26. The Argument in Formal Form

The core reasoning can be stated compactly.

### Premise 1

Truth is objective correspondence with reality, not merely human agreement.

### Premise 2

God is the ultimate ground of truth and is Himself true.

### Premise 3

Falsehood is incompatible with the nature of a God who is truth, faithful, immutable, and incapable of denying Himself.

### Premise 4

Therefore God cannot lie.

### Premise 5

A genuine revelation from God is communication that genuinely proceeds from God.

### Conclusion

> **A genuine divine revelation cannot be false because the God from whom it genuinely proceeds cannot lie.**

Then the necessary qualification:

### Additional premise required for application

> **This particular alleged revelation genuinely proceeds from God.**

Only after that premise is established can the conclusion be applied to the particular document or message.

This is the precise point at which authentication becomes indispensable.

---

# 27. What This Chapter Has Established

We can now state the conclusions without overstating them.

### 1. “God cannot lie” is stronger than “God does not lie.”

The biblical language concerns impossibility, not merely behavioral consistency.

### 2. The impossibility is grounded in God's nature.

Falsehood is incompatible with the God who is true.

### 3. God is not merely a powerful finite knower.

The biblical conception does not attribute ordinary human epistemic limitations to God.

### 4. God's truthfulness is connected to His faithfulness.

What God says and what God does are presented as coherent.

### 5. God's truthfulness is connected to His immutability.

He does not become false after being true.

### 6. God's inability to lie is not a limitation imposed by something greater than God.

It is the consequence of divine perfection and the incompatibility of falsehood with His nature.

### 7. Genuine divine communication is therefore truthful.

If God genuinely communicates, the communication cannot be false because God cannot lie.

### 8. This does not authenticate every claim made in God's name.

The authenticity of a claimed revelation remains an evidential question.

### 9. Divine truthfulness does not make human interpretation infallible.

The text can be infallible while the interpreter remains fallible.

### 10. The next question is therefore unavoidable.

If God cannot lie and genuine revelation is truthful:

> **How has God actually revealed Himself, and how did His revelation become the written record we call Scripture?**

That is the question the next movement must answer.

---

# 28. The Necessary Boundary Before Revelation

The book has now established a critical source-side premise:

> **God cannot lie.**

But it has not yet established the Bible.

It has not yet established:

- which writings constitute Scripture;
- how revelation was communicated;
- how witnesses functioned;
- how revelation was recorded;
- what inspiration guarantees;
- how canon is recognized;
- or how the biblical record is authenticated.

Those questions remain.

The next movement must therefore begin with the actual nature of the Bible rather than assuming the conclusion.

The reader must now be prepared to ask:

> **What is this writing?**

> **What kind of record is it?**

> **How was it given?**

> **Who witnessed what it records?**

> **How was divine communication preserved in written form?**

> **What does “all Scripture” mean?**

Only through that progression can the investigation move from:

> **God cannot lie**

to:

> **the authentic written record of God's revelation is trustworthy.**

---

# Chapter 12 — Conclusion

The deepest significance of:

> **“it was impossible for God to lie”**

is not simply that God is morally superior to human beings.

It is that **falsehood is incompatible with the nature of God**.

God does not need an external truth standard to become truthful.

He does not need human verification to become faithful.

He does not become true because an institution declares Him true.

He is presented as true.

Christ declares:

> **“I am ... the truth.”**

The biblical record says:

> **“God is true.”**

It says:

> **“God, that cannot lie.”**

It says:

> **“it was impossible for God to lie.”**

It says:

> **“he cannot deny himself.”**

And it says:

> **“in him is no darkness at all.”**

These statements converge on a single fundamental reality:

> **God cannot contradict His own truthful nature by becoming a source of falsehood.**

That is why genuine divine revelation can be trusted.

But the word **genuine** must remain in the argument.

God's inability to lie does not make every human claimant truthful.

It does not authenticate every document.

It does not make every interpretation correct.

It does not allow the investigator to skip evidence.

Rather, it establishes the foundation upon which the authentication of divine revelation becomes meaningful:

> **If God has spoken, God has not lied.**

The next task is therefore not to assume that He has spoken through a particular document.

The next task is to investigate:

> **How did God speak?**

> **How was that communication received?**

> **Who witnessed it?**

> **How was it recorded?**

> **What is Scripture?**

> **What is the Graphē?**

> **And what evidence establishes that the written record before us genuinely carries divine authority?**

The investigation now moves from the **truthfulness of the Source** to the **authentication of the revelation and written record**.

That movement must be made without circularity, without philosophical shortcuts, and without surrendering reason.

- **John 14:6** — Christ: “I am ... the truth.”
- **2 Corinthians 1:18** — “God is true.”
- **John 1:1–3** — the Word, God, and creation.
- **James 1:17** — no variableness or shadow of turning.

## Argumentative chain

> **God is true**
>
> ↓
>
> **God is faithful**
>
> ↓
>
> **God is immutable**
>
> ↓
>
> **God cannot deny Himself**
>
> ↓
>
> **God cannot lie**
>
> ↓
>
> **Genuine divine revelation is truthful**
>
> ↓
>
> **The claimed revelation must still be authenticated**
>
> ↓
>
> **The written record must then be examined**

## Guardrail

> **Do not confuse “God cannot lie” with “every claim made in God's name is true.”**

The first concerns God's nature.

The second requires authentication.
