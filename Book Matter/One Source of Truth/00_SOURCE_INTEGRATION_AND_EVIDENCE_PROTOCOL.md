# ONE SOURCE OF TRUTH
## The Doctrine of the Graphē

# 00 — SOURCE INTEGRATION AND EVIDENCE PROTOCOL

> **Status:** Master control document for source use, evidence integration, argument development, and manuscript auditing.
>
> **Function:** This protocol supplements—not replaces—the locked Table of Contents, the book architecture, the front matter, and the chapter-by-chapter manuscript. It governs how additional source material is examined and incorporated without derailing the established knowledge flow.

---

# I. PURPOSE OF THIS PROTOCOL

*One Source of Truth — The Doctrine of the Graphē* is not to become a compilation of everything that has ever been written about truth, God, the Bible, Scripture, revelation, inspiration, theology, philosophy, or apologetics.

Nor is it to become a sequence of quotations followed by conclusions.

The governing task is more demanding:

> **Take the reader through a coherent investigation in which definitions, evidence, biblical record, reasoning, distinctions, and inferential steps accumulate until major conclusions are warranted.**

The locked TOC establishes the **architectural destination**.

The sources provide **material for the investigation**.

This protocol governs the relationship between them.

### Master rule

> **The TOC determines where an argument belongs; source material determines how accurately, completely, and evidentially that argument can be developed.**

A newly supplied source, quotation, lexical observation, historical claim, biblical passage, commentary, dictionary entry, or analytical insight does not automatically create a new chapter or alter the trajectory of the book.

It must first be **examined, classified, tested, and mapped**.

---

# II. THE MANUSCRIPT'S FOUR GOVERNING QUESTIONS

Every major argument must remain intelligible in relation to four distinct questions:

### 1. Ontological

> **What is true? What is real?**

### 2. Epistemological

> **How can truth be known?**

### 3. Evidential

> **What warrants concluding that something is true?**

### 4. Hermeneutical

> **Once an authoritative truthful text has been established, how is it correctly understood?**

These questions may interact, but they must not be casually collapsed into one another.

---

# III. SOURCE HIERARCHY

The manuscript must distinguish the **thing under investigation** from writings **about the thing under investigation**.

## A. The Graphē / Holy Writ

The biblical writings are the central object of the investigation and, once their authority has been established through the book's cumulative argument, the primary written authority being investigated.

The manuscript must therefore distinguish:

> **what Scripture says**

from:

> **what someone says Scripture says.**

## B. Biblical textual witnesses

These include the actual biblical passages used in the investigation. They must be handled in context rather than reduced to isolated proof texts.

## C. Historical and testimonial evidence

This includes eyewitness testimony, apostolic testimony, prophetic testimony, recorded events, historical persons, places, declarations, actions, manifestations, fulfilled predictions, manuscript evidence, and external corroboration.

These are evidential materials and must be treated according to what they can actually establish.

## D. Lexical sources

Greek and Hebrew lexicons, concordances, dictionaries, and lexical studies may establish word forms, transliteration, grammatical information, lexical ranges, historical usage, and semantic relationships. They do not automatically establish the theological conclusion drawn from a word.

## E. Historical sources

Historical sources may provide evidence concerning people, events, dates, documents, transmission, cultural setting, manuscript history, and external corroboration. Their claims must be distinguished from biblical claims.

## F. Commentaries and reference works

Works such as Bible dictionaries, commentaries, study Bibles, theological works, and scholarly discussions are **secondary witnesses and analytical resources**.

They may illuminate a question, provide an argument, preserve historical observations, identify relevant passages, expose alternative interpretations, or supply useful terminology.

But they do not become primary authority merely because they are old, famous, scholarly, widely cited, or written by a theologian.

## G. Philosophical and theological arguments

These may be useful for analysis and comparison. They must not be allowed to replace the biblical record when the question is:

> **What does Scripture actually establish?**

A philosophical argument may be strong or weak. A theological formulation may be accurate or inaccurate. Either way, the manuscript must examine the argument itself.

---

# IV. THE SOURCE-INTERROGATION RULE

Never incorporate a source by writing merely:

> “The source says…”

That formulation is permitted only when identifying a source's position is itself relevant.

The normal intellectual operation is:

> **What is being claimed?**
>
> ↓
>
> **What does the claim mean?**
>
> ↓
>
> **What premises support it?**
>
> ↓
>
> **What evidence is offered?**
>
> ↓
>
> **Are those premises established?**
>
> ↓
>
> **Does the inference follow?**
>
> ↓
>
> **Does the biblical record support it?**
>
> ↓
>
> **Does the source go beyond the evidence?**
>
> ↓
>
> **What conclusion is actually warranted?**

The source should therefore become **material for reasoning**, not a substitute for reasoning.

---

# V. SOURCE CLAIMS MUST BE CLASSIFIED

Every significant source statement should be classified before incorporation.

### A. Direct biblical assertion
A claim explicitly made by the biblical text.

### B. Biblical inference
A conclusion drawn by reasoning across one or more biblical passages. It must be presented as an inference unless Scripture itself states the conclusion explicitly.

### C. Historical claim
A claim about what happened, who existed, what was recorded, or what evidence survives. It requires historical evaluation.

### D. Lexical claim
A claim concerning the meaning or usage of a Hebrew or Greek term. It requires lexical evaluation and contextual control.

### E. Philosophical claim
A claim about reality, causation, logic, knowledge, morality, existence, or inference. It must be examined as an argument.

### F. Theological formulation
A synthesized doctrinal proposition. It must not be treated as though the label itself proves the proposition.

### G. Interpretive claim
A proposed meaning of a biblical passage. It must be distinguished from the biblical wording itself.

### H. Evidential conclusion
A conclusion reached from multiple pieces of evidence. The inferential pathway must be shown.

---

# VI. THE EVIDENCE PROTOCOL

The manuscript must never use the word **evidence** as decoration.

When evidence is presented, ask:

1. **Evidence of what?**
2. **What proposition is it supposed to support?**
3. **What makes it relevant to that proposition?**
4. **Is the evidence direct or indirect?**
5. **Is it independent or dependent upon the same source?**
6. **How reliable is the testimony or record?**
7. **What alternative explanations exist?**
8. **Does the evidence establish the conclusion, make it probable, or merely make it possible?**
9. **What additional premise is required to move from evidence to conclusion?**
10. **Has that premise itself been established?**

> **Evidence does not become evidence for a conclusion merely because it is related to the subject.**

It must have a demonstrable evidential relationship to the proposition.

---

# VII. RATIONAL WARRANT

The manuscript must distinguish:

> **claim → evidence → inference → conclusion**

For example, the claim that a particular document is divine revelation is not established merely because the document says it is divine.

The investigation must ask who produced it, what it claims about its origin, what historical circumstances surround its production, who witnessed the events it records, what evidence supports its testimony, what relationship it has to previously established revelation, whether its claims are internally coherent, whether there are independent confirmations, and what Christ and the apostles establish concerning the relevant Scriptures.

The reader must be able to see **why the conclusion follows**.

---

# VIII. ANTI-CIRCULARITY PROTOCOL

The manuscript must never silently assume:

> **The Bible is true because the Bible says it is true.**

Nor:

> **The Bible is God's Word because the Bible says it is God's Word; therefore what it says about itself proves that it is God's Word.**

The manuscript must distinguish:

### Internal claim
What the Bible claims about itself.

### External corroboration
Evidence that bears upon whether the biblical claims correspond to reality.

### Historical authentication
Evidence concerning origin, witnesses, transmission, and recognition.

### Cumulative argument
The convergence of multiple lines of evidence.

The biblical self-testimony is therefore **evidence within the cumulative case**, not a license to bypass authentication.

---

# IX. THE GOD → REVELATION → SCRIPTURE CHAIN

The manuscript must preserve the following architecture:

> **God exists**
>
> ↓
>
> **God's nature is established**
>
> ↓
>
> **God is true, faithful, immutable, and incapable of falsehood**
>
> ↓
>
> **Therefore genuine divine communication is trustworthy**
>
> ↓
>
> **God has communicated / revealed**
>
> ↓
>
> **The claimed revelation must be authenticated**
>
> ↓
>
> **The modes by which God gave revelation must be examined**
>
> ↓
>
> **The witnesses, prophets, Christ, apostles, visions, dreams, manifestations, and written records must be examined**
>
> ↓
>
> **Inspiration must be examined**
>
> ↓
>
> **“All Scripture” must be examined**
>
> ↓
>
> **The biblical corpus and canon must be examined**
>
> ↓
>
> **The cumulative evidential case must be evaluated**
>
> ↓
>
> **The authority and infallibility of the written record can then be argued**

This chain must not be compressed into:

> **“God wrote the Bible, therefore the Bible is true.”**

The intervening argument matters.

# X. “HOW THE BIBLE WAS GIVEN” — MANDATORY SOURCE-INTEGRATION AREA

The subject **How the Bible Was Given** must receive full treatment within the existing **MOVEMENT IV — HOW THE WRITTEN RECORD WAS GIVEN**.

It is not to be omitted merely because the book has already discussed God, revelation, or the Bible generally.

## 23. God Speaking: The Audible Voice

The supplied examples include:

- Exodus 19:19
- Deuteronomy 5
- Matthew 3:16–17
- John 12:28

## 24. Angels and Prophetic Mediation

Relevant passages include:

- Acts 7:38
- Hebrews 2:2
- Acts 3:21
- Hebrews 1:1

The treatment must distinguish angelic mediation from prophetic mediation.

## 25. Prophets

The prophetic mode of receiving and transmitting divine communication must be examined in its own right.

## 26. Jesus Christ

Christ must not be treated merely as one messenger among many.

Relevant passages include:

- Hebrews 1:1
- Revelation 1

The chapter must investigate Christ as the decisive manifestation and communication of God.

## 27. Apostles

Relevant passages include:

- Acts 1:2
- Ephesians 4

Apostolic commission, authority, testimony, and relationship to Christ must be examined.

## 28. Visions and Dreams

Relevant passages include:

- Isaiah 6
- Daniel 2
- Daniel 7–8
- Ezekiel 1
- Matthew 1:20
- Matthew 2:12

The manuscript must distinguish the **mode of receiving communication** from the later question of **recording and inspiration**.

## 29. Revelation

The lexical and biblical material concerning Greek **ἀποκάλυψις (*apokalypsis*, G602)** must be integrated where appropriate.

The supplied material emphasizes:

> **unveiling — the removal of a covering so that what was hidden is made known.**

The manuscript should examine the biblical uses of revelation rather than merely reproduce a dictionary definition.

The supplied examples include revelation concerning gospel light, judgment, previously hidden truths, coming glory, Christ's second coming, and manifestation.

The precise sense must always be determined by context.

## 30. Inspiration — 2 Timothy 3:15–17

The manuscript must examine:

> **“All scripture is given by inspiration of God...”**

with particular attention to the scope and force of:

> **“All scripture.”**

## 31. “All Scripture”

This is a mandatory safeguard.

The argument must not quietly weaken:

> **ALL**

into “some Scripture,” “the parts that are doctrinal,” “the passages directly spoken by God,” or another narrower proposition.

## 32. From Divine Communication to Written Testimony

This chapter must synthesize the preceding modes into:

> **divine communication → reception → witness/mediation → recording → inspired written testimony.**

The treatment must not imply that every mode of divine communication is identical to the completed written corpus.

---

# XI. WITNESS MUST NOT BE REDUCED TO EYEWITNESS

The supplied material concerning **Revelation 1:5** must be integrated into the evidential architecture.

The text identifies Jesus Christ as:

> **“the faithful witness”**

This is conceptually significant.

The manuscript must distinguish:

### Eyewitness
A person who personally saw or heard an event.

### Witness
A broader category involving testimony concerning truth.

### Faithful Witness
Christ's unique status as the truthful and faithful witness.

This prevents an impoverished conception of biblical testimony in which “witness” means only someone physically present.

The book should also examine how the historical manifestation of Christ, His crucifixion, His resurrection, His works, His words, the testimony of those who encountered Him, and the subsequent historical consequences function within the cumulative evidential argument.

**Historical consequence is not identical to eyewitness testimony**, but it may constitute a separate evidential consideration.

That distinction must remain explicit.

---

# XII. CHRIST AS WITNESS

The manuscript must integrate three related but distinct propositions:

> **Christ is the faithful witness.**

> **Others bear witness concerning Christ.**

> **The historical world bears evidence concerning the consequences and continuity of the events being claimed.**

These must not be conflated.

The evidential structure can therefore include:

> **Christ's own testimony**
>
> +
>
> **apostolic testimony**
>
> +
>
> **eyewitness testimony**
>
> +
>
> **prophetic testimony**
>
> +
>
> **historical manifestations**
>
> +
>
> **fulfilled prophecy**
>
> +
>
> **documentary evidence**
>
> +
>
> **cumulative corroboration**

The manuscript must then ask what each line independently establishes and what becomes established through convergence.

---

# XIII. REVELATION, INSPIRATION, RECORDING, AND CANON MUST REMAIN DISTINCT

### Revelation
What God makes known.

### Inspiration
The divine operation concerning the production of Scripture.

### Recording
The historical act by which communicated material is written down.

### Canon
The question of which writings constitute the recognized authoritative scriptural corpus.

These are related but not synonymous.

A person can receive revelation without the revelation itself being the completed written text.

A historical event can be witnessed without every witness becoming an inspired biblical writer.

A writing can claim divine authority without automatically being canonical.

The manuscript must preserve these distinctions throughout.

---

# XIV. LEXICAL ACCURACY PROTOCOL

Whenever Hebrew or Greek terminology is introduced, provide, where relevant:

1. Original script.
2. Transliteration.
3. Strong's number where the source uses it.
4. Basic lexical meaning.
5. Grammatical form where relevant.
6. Range of biblical usage.
7. Contextual meaning in the passage.
8. Distinction between lexical meaning and theological interpretation.

For example:

> **γραφή (*graphē*, G1124)**

must not be reduced to “the Bible.” Its basic lexical field concerns **writing / a written thing**, with biblical usage extending to **Holy Writ / Scripture**.

Likewise:

> **ἀποκάλυψις (*apokalypsis*, G602)**

must not be reduced to the title of the book of Revelation.

The same discipline applies to:

- *spoudazō*;
- *apeiros*;
- and every other technical term introduced.

---

# XV. BIBLICAL CONTEXT PROTOCOL

A verse is never sufficient merely because it contains a useful phrase.

Before using a major passage, ask:

- Who is speaking?
- To whom?
- Under what circumstances?
- What question or problem is being addressed?
- What precedes the statement?
- What follows it?
- What is the writer's argument?
- What does the passage explicitly establish?
- What is being inferred?

### Example: John 10:34–36

The manuscript must not simply quote:

> **“the scripture cannot be broken.”**

It must examine the surrounding argument, Christ's citation of Psalm 82:6:

> **“I said, Ye are gods”**

and why that written statement functions as decisive material in His argument.

The reader must be shown the reasoning.

---

# XVI. CHRIST'S “FAITHFUL WITNESS” MUST NOT BE AN AFTERTHOUGHT

Revelation 1:5 belongs naturally within the Christological/evidential movement.

It should be cross-connected to:

- Revelation 1;
- John 14:6;
- Mark 12:14;
- 2 Peter 1:16;
- apostolic testimony;
- Christ's resurrection;
- Christ's confirmation of Scripture;
- Christ's use of written Scripture;
- and John 10:34–36.

This does **not** mean repeating the same argument in every chapter.

The first occurrence should establish the concept; later chapters should develop only what the new context requires.

---

# XVII. SOURCE MATERIAL MUST STRENGTHEN, NOT DERAIL

When a new source is supplied, classify its function.

### 1. Strengthen
Provide additional evidence for an argument already in the TOC.

### 2. Clarify
Define a term or distinction previously unclear.

### 3. Correct
Expose an error in an earlier draft.

### 4. Complete
Supply an omitted argument required by the chapter's central question.

### 5. Qualify
Show that a conclusion is too broad and needs narrowing.

### 6. Cross-link
Provide material belonging in a later chapter but flag it now.

### 7. Challenge
Expose a competing argument the manuscript must answer.

### 8. Generate curiosity
Reveal a deeper field of study that the present work can introduce without exhausting it.

A source should not be included simply because it is interesting.

---

# XVIII. THE “WHY?” RULE

Whenever the manuscript makes a major assertion, ask:

> **Why?**

If the answer is another assertion, ask:

> **Why is that true?**

Continue until the relevant evidential or logical foundation has been exposed.

For example:

> **Scripture is authoritative.**

Why?

> Because it is God's Word.

Why?

> How has God's authorship been established?

Why should the claim that it is God's Word be believed?

What authenticates the revelation?

What establishes inspiration?

What establishes the canonical corpus?

What establishes the reliability of the written record?

Only after those questions have been handled can the conclusion be responsibly stated.

---

# XIX. PREMATURE-CONCLUSION PROTOCOL

A chapter must not establish a conclusion whose premises belong to later chapters.

Do not establish:

> **“The Bible is infallible”**

before the necessary arguments concerning God, divine truthfulness, revelation, authentication, inspiration, canon, and the written record have been developed.

Likewise, do not use:

> **“The Bible is God's Word”**

as a premise while supposedly proving that the Bible is God's Word.

The chapter architecture exists partly to prevent this.

---

# XX. DUPLICATION PROTOCOL

When new material resembles an argument already covered, determine whether it is:

1. the same argument;
2. a stronger form of the argument;
3. a new evidential line;
4. a new application;
5. a necessary cross-reference.

If it is the same argument, strengthen the original chapter rather than duplicating it.

If it is genuinely new evidence, integrate it where its evidential function belongs.

# XXII. MANDATORY PRE-CHAPTER SOURCE AUDIT

Before writing **every chapter**, review:

1. The complete locked TOC.
2. The complete architecture/guidance document.
3. The front matter and introduction.
4. This Source Integration & Evidence Protocol.
5. Every preceding chapter relevant to the argument.
6. Relevant source material already supplied.
7. Relevant new source material supplied since the previous chapter.

Then ask:

### Structural audit

- What exact chapter am I writing?
- What movement does it belong to?
- What question does it answer?
- What question does it deliberately leave for later?
- What chapter precedes it?
- What chapter follows it?

### Source audit

- What source arguments belong here?
- What source arguments have already been used?
- What important source material remains unused?
- What new material changes, strengthens, or corrects the argument?

### Reasoning audit

- Are the premises explicit?
- Are the inferences valid?
- Have I skipped an intermediate premise?
- Have I confused possibility with probability?
- Have I confused probability with certainty?
- Have I asserted what must be demonstrated?

### Biblical audit

- Have I examined the actual passage?
- Have I supplied sufficient context?
- Have I checked lexical claims?
- Have I distinguished quotation from interpretation?
- Have I accidentally converted commentary into Scripture?

### Terminology audit

- Are technical terms defined?
- Are Greek/Hebrew forms accurate?
- Is transliteration accurate?
- Is contextual meaning distinguished from lexical range?

### Anti-error audit

Check specifically for:

- omitted source arguments;
- duplicated arguments;
- premature conclusions;
- circular reasoning;
- undefined terminology;
- unsupported assertions;
- misplaced topics;
- weak transitions;
- biblical context failures;
- lexical inaccuracies;
- historical inaccuracies;
- evidential overstatement;
- philosophical assumptions;
- theological assumptions;
- conflation of Scripture with interpretation;
- reduction of truth to belief;
- reduction of knowledge to quotation;
- reduction of Scripture to proof texts;
- unanswered questions;
- confusion between God's Word and human commentary.

---

# XXIII. POST-CHAPTER ACCEPTANCE GATE

A chapter is not accepted merely because it is polished.

It must pass five gates.

## Gate 1 — Coverage

Did the chapter actually address everything the TOC assigns to it?

## Gate 2 — Argument

Can the reader reconstruct the reasoning from premises to conclusion?

## Gate 3 — Evidence

Are major factual and historical claims adequately grounded?

## Gate 4 — Biblical fidelity

Does the exposition accurately represent the relevant biblical texts and contexts?

## Gate 5 — Architectural integrity

Does the chapter advance the book without stealing arguments from later chapters or repeating earlier ones?

A chapter that fails any gate must be revised before the next chapter is written.

---

# XXIV. THE MASTER FORMULA

The entire book should operate according to this formula:

> **Define the question.**
>
> **Define the relevant terms.**
>
> **Establish the facts or premises.**
>
> **Present the relevant biblical record.**
>
> **Present relevant evidence.**
>
> **Examine competing claims where necessary.**
>
> **Reason through the evidence.**
>
> **Distinguish what is explicit from what is inferred.**
>
> **Draw only the conclusion warranted by the established premises.**
>
> **Identify what remains unresolved.**
>
> **Move naturally to the next question.**

And the governing authorial maxim remains:

> **Do not merely tell the reader what to conclude.**
>
> **Give the reader the definitions, evidence, biblical record, reasoning, distinctions, and inferential steps necessary to see why the conclusion follows.**

And:

> **Do not make the reader choose between reason and Scripture.**
>
> **Teach the reader to reason through Scripture.**

---

# XXV. COMPLETENESS STANDARD

The manuscript is not complete because it contains many facts.

It is complete when:

> **the reader can follow the argument;**

> **the major terms are understood;**

> **the relevant evidence has been examined;**

> **the biblical record has been properly handled;**

> **alternative explanations have been considered where necessary;**

> **the inferential steps are visible;**

> **the conclusions do not outrun the premises;**

> **and the questions raised by the investigation have been answered or deliberately carried forward to the precise place where they will be answered.**

That is the difference between an information-rich manuscript and a rigorous intellectual work.

---

# XXVI. FINAL RULE FOR ADDITIONAL MATERIAL

Additional source material is governed by one final principle:

> **Do not ask, “Where can I insert this?”**
>
> **Ask, “What does this material establish, what question does it answer, what question does it raise, what evidence does it provide, what premise does it strengthen, and where in the existing argument does that function properly belong?”**

If it belongs in the current chapter, integrate it.

If it strengthens a later chapter, record it for that chapter.

If it corrects an earlier chapter, revise the earlier chapter before proceeding.

If it duplicates an existing argument, consolidate.

If it introduces an unsupported assertion, investigate rather than adopt it.

If it exposes a weakness in the book's reasoning, repair the reasoning.

If it opens a deeper field that the book cannot exhaust, use it to establish the point and, where appropriate, direct the reader toward deeper study.

Additional sources may **strengthen the spine**.

They must not **replace the spine**.
