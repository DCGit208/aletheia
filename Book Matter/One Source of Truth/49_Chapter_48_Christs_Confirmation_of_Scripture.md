# 48.1 The Question Created by the Preceding Chapter

Movement VI has located a Person, identified Him as the Truth, examined His public claims and works as an authentication axis, taken His death and resurrection as the central historical claims of the career's end, and examined the seers of His majesty and of His raising as positioned witnesses. It has not yet examined His treatment of the writings already in force.

Chapter 43 established existence, publicity, chronology, Jewish setting, crucifixion under Pilate, and the failure of myth and coreless legend. Chapter 44 established identity with the Truth and decisive revelation of the Father. Chapter 45 established the claims-and-works conjunction as a central authentication axis. Chapter 46 established crucifixion as the public end of that ministry, burial as a belonging fact, and resurrection as the early, bodily, public, appearance-claim of the same testimony. Chapter 47 established eyewitness testimony as positioned knowledge of what was seen and heard. Those conclusions are premises here.

A Person can be located, identified, authenticated in the ministry, examined at the hinge of death and raising, and attested by seers, and a further question still remains: how did this Person treat the Scriptures He received? Identity that never stands under prior writings is not the identity the Gospels present. A raised Lord who never opens Moses and the prophets is not the Lord Luke 24 presents. The writings that have now been used as authentic testimony do not stop at “we were eyewitnesses of His majesty.” They record this man answering temptation with what is written, rebuking error as ignorance of the Scriptures, reading a prophet in the synagogue of His upbringing, claiming not to destroy the Law or the Prophets but to fulfill them, and, after the raising, beginning at Moses and all the prophets and expounding in all the Scriptures the things concerning Himself. Those are further claims about the same career. They are the claims for which a historical baseline, a revelatory identification, an authentication axis, a hinge, and a file of seers now exist.

The question is therefore not:

> **Did a man named Jesus occupy Galilee and Judea under Tiberius, is He presented as the Truth, do His claims and works function as an authentication axis, are death and resurrection the central historical claims of the career's end, and are there positioned witnesses of what was seen and heard?**

Those questions have been answered.

The question is:

> **How did this Person treat the Scriptures He received, quoted, interpreted, and appealed to as authoritative, and what does that treatment warrant now that the object of the investigation is this Person's confirmation of those writings?**

If citation stood alone as ornament, Movement VI would be the investigation of a preacher's stock of phrases. If later church use of the Old Testament stood alone, Movement VI would be the investigation of Christian midrash after the fact. If this Person treats the received writings as binding written word, Movement VI is the investigation of the Truth's own confirmation of the Scriptures already in force.

That is a different operation from piety. It is the operation this chapter must perform.

# 48.3 Define the Question: Confirmation as Authoritative Treatment of Received Writings

**Confirmation**, in this chapter, is Christ's treatment of already-written Scripture as binding written word.

The object is not a later ecclesiastical certificate. It is not a list of books. It is not a metaphysical verdict that every sentence of a later closed corpus is incapable of error. It is this Person's practice, in the authentic testimony, of receiving, quoting, interpreting, and appealing to the writings already in force as authoritative.

Four acts belong to the definition.

First, **reception**. He enters a people already possessed of writings. He is a Jew of Galilee and Judea under the Second Temple, as Chapter 43 established. Those writings are read in synagogues. They are the Law, the Prophets, and, in His own later naming, the Psalms. He does not invent the corpus He uses. He receives it.

Second, **quotation**. He cites what is written as written. The ordinary formula is **γέγραπται (*gegraptai*)**: it stands written. The citation is not an allusion that might as well have been a proverb. It is an appeal to a text.

Third, **interpretation**. He reads, explains, applies, and, after the raising, opens the writings concerning Himself. Interpretation is not the abolition of the text. It is the act of a mind upon a text treated as having force. The hermeneutical discipline of later Movements IX–X is not this chapter's assignment. The fact that He interprets, and that He interprets the writings as concerning Himself and as binding upon His hearers, belongs here.

Fourth, **appeal as authoritative**. He does not offer the writings as optional colour. He uses them as the court of appeal in temptation, in controversy, in the synagogue, in the passion, and in the post-resurrection instruction. “Have ye not read” is not a literary compliment. It is a charge that the written text was available and decisive.

**Central authentication line**, in this chapter, names the function of that confirmation inside Movement VI. The locked TOC assigns this chapter Christ's treatment of the Scriptures He received, quoted, interpreted, and appealed to as authoritative. The assignment is therefore not a free selection of pious Old Testament scenes. It is the examination of what the record places as this Person's own relation to the writings already in force.

The question of this chapter joins those definitions:

> **Does the authentic testimony present this Person as receiving, quoting, interpreting, and appealing to the Scriptures already written as binding written word, such that that confirmation functions as a central authentication line of Movement VI?**

Several elements belong to the question as this chapter defines it.

First, **the body He names**. Law, Prophets, and Psalms are His own grouping of the received writings, not a later scholar's convenience.

Second, **the formula of force**. “It is written,” “have ye not read,” “this scripture,” “the scriptures,” and “that the scriptures might be fulfilled” are the record's own names for the operation.

Third, **representative passages in context**. Temptation, synagogue reading, controversy over reading, the claim not to destroy but to fulfill, the Scriptures as witnesses concerning Him, the passion's necessity as written, and the Emmaus and upper-room openings are examined as arguments, not as a slogan list.

Fourth, **examinability**. These are claims inside a warranted written testimony about a historical Person. They can be defined, distinguished, compared with reductions, and reasoned through. They are not exempt from investigation because they are theological. They are not reduced to later church policy because they are Jewish.

Thus:

> **The question of Christ's confirmation of Scripture is the question of His authoritative treatment of the writings He received, under the writings already warranted as testimony, with Law, Prophets, and Psalms as the named body, and with “it is written” as a formula of force rather than of decoration.**

# 48.4 Distinguish Seven Objects That Must Not Be Collapsed

The investigation fails at the first step if it treats seven different objects as one.

## 48.4.1 Historical existence, identity, claims-and-works, the hinge, and the seers

These are the objects of Chapters 43–47. They include a real person, identity with the Truth, the public authentication axis of claims and works, death-and-resurrection as the central historical claims of the career's end, and eyewitness testimony as positioned knowledge of what was seen and heard. Those conclusions are premises here. This chapter does not re-try them. It examines this Person's treatment of the writings so established.

## 48.4.2 Confirmation of received Scripture as authoritative written word

This is the object of the present chapter. It includes: the definition of confirmation as reception, quotation, interpretation, and authoritative appeal; Law, Prophets, and Psalms as the body He names; the language of what stands written; temptation, reading, synagogue, fulfillment, controversy, passion, and the opened writings after the raising; the failure of specified reductions; and the warrant for treating this confirmation as a central authentication line of Movement VI.

## 48.4.3 Optional illustration

A teacher may quote a famous sentence because it is memorable. Illustration borrows light. Confirmation treats the text as binding. Collapsing the two converts “it is written” into décor. The record's use of the formula in conflict, in rebuke, and in the necessity of the passion is not décor.

## 48.4.4 Canon-list construction

Canon, in this book's architecture, is the question of which writings constitute the recognized authoritative scriptural corpus. That question is Movement VII. A man may confirm the writings He uses without issuing a catalogue of titles, without adjudicating disputed edges, and without performing the later church's recognition-history. Confirmation of received Scripture is not a list. Movement VII remains the list's proper file.

## 48.4.5 Infallibility as a finished verdict

Infallibility, in this book's architecture, is the conclusion concerning the truthfulness and reliability of the inspired record, built from premises concerning God, revelation, inspiration, authentication, and Scripture. That conclusion is Movement VII's later work, especially Chapters 56–57. Treating the writings as authoritative is not yet the metaphysical verdict that the later closed corpus is an infallible record. The present chapter may not steal that verdict by renaming confirmation as infallibility.

## 48.4.6 Movement VIII's finished Graphē-doctrine

John 10:34–36, “the scripture cannot be broken,” Christ's reasoning from the written wording of Psalm 82:6, John 17:17 as “thy word is truth,” and the conclusion that the Graphē is the primary written authority are the locked assignments of Chapters 59–65. He does appeal to what is written in John 10. That appeal is noted here as an instance of the same practice. The dedicated argument of why Scripture cannot be broken, and the Graphē-gate that follows, remain Movement VIII. Confirmation of received Scripture is the Movement VI line. The later verdict is the later verdict.

# 48.6 Confirmation Defined: Receive, Quote, Interpret, Appeal

The four acts named in the definition must be stated as operations, not as synonyms for piety.

**To receive** the Scriptures, in this argument, is to stand inside a people for whom those writings already function as Holy Writ, and to take them as given rather than as optional background. Reception is historical before it is homiletical. A Galilean Jew of the late Second Temple inherits Moses, the prophets, and the psalms as public writings. He is presented as hearing them, reading them, and treating them as the writings of that people. Reception is not passivity. It is the first act of confirmation: the writings are not a foreign library He happens to find useful.

**To quote** is to cite a writing as writing. The perfect **γέγραπται (*gegraptai*)** is the ordinary Synoptic form: it has been written, and the writing remains. Quotation in this sense is not the later scholar's footnote. It is speech that treats a prior sentence as present force. When He answers, “It is written,” the cited sentence is being used as the rule of the present conflict.

**To interpret** is to say what the writing means in the situation before Him. He reads Isaiah in the synagogue and claims the day of fulfillment. He asks what David means by calling the Messiah Lord. He begins at Moses and the prophets and expounds the things concerning Himself. Interpretation can be true or false. This chapter does not close the later hermeneutical discipline. It establishes that He interprets, and that He interprets writings He treats as having the right to bind.

**To appeal as authoritative** is to treat the writing as the court before which a dispute is to be settled, a temptation is to be answered, a charge is to be made, or a necessity is to be named. Authority here is not yet the Movement VIII thesis that the Graphē is the primary written authority by which all later claims are tested. It is the more precise and already sufficient claim that, in His practice, the received writings function as binding written word.

The four acts are one confirmation. Reception without quotation would be atmosphere. Quotation without appeal would be ornament. Appeal without interpretation would be talisman. Interpretation without reception would be invention. The record presents the four together.

# 48.7 Illustration Is Not Confirmation

A sentence may be quoted because it is beautiful, familiar, or useful as a comparison. Illustration does not treat the quoted sentence as the rule of the case. Confirmation does.

The distinction is not an accusation against all metaphor. Jesus speaks in parables. He uses comparisons. Those forms are not this chapter's object. The object is the class of acts in which He names what is written as written and uses it as decisive.

Three marks separate illustration from confirmation in the passages this chapter examines.

First, **the formula names writing**. “It is written,” “have ye not read,” “this scripture,” “the law,” “Moses,” “the prophets,” “the psalms,” “that which was spoken unto you by God” — these are designations of a prior written or God-spoken-and-written word, not of a free image.

Second, **the use is adjudicative**. In temptation the cited word is the refusal of the proposed act. In controversy the cited word is the exposure of error. In the passion the cited word is the necessity: thus it must be. Illustration does not ordinarily carry necessity.

Third, **the hearers are held responsible for the text**. “Have ye not read” assumes that reading was the available act and that failure to read, or failure to regard what was read, is the error. A mere illustration does not generate that charge.

The reduction that treats all citation as illustration therefore fails at the formula, at the adjudicative use, and at the charge. Confirmation is the right name for the operation the record exhibits.

---

# 48.8 Confirmation Distinguished from Canon-List Construction

A list of books is one kind of intellectual act. Confirmation of writings in use is another.

Jesus quotes Moses, Isaiah, the Psalms, Daniel, Hosea, and other writings of the received Jewish Scriptures. He names “the law of Moses, and the prophets, and the psalms” as the body of things written concerning Himself (Luke 24:44). That naming is a grouping of received writings. It is not a canon-synod. It does not enumerate Esther. It does not adjudicate the later Christian disputes about the edges of the corpus. It does not, by silence, automatically exclude every writing He does not happen to cite in the recorded scenes.

Two errors must be refused together.

The first error is **inflation**. Inflation says: He quoted these books; therefore He issued the later Protestant or Jewish list; therefore Movement VII is already finished. Quotation is not a synod. Grouping Law, Prophets, and Psalms is a real confirmation of a received body. It is not the later recognition-history.

The second error is **evacuation**. Evacuation says: because He did not print a catalogue, He confirmed nothing. The absence of a later list-form is not the absence of authoritative treatment. A man who lives by what is written, reads the prophet in the synagogue, and opens Moses and the prophets after His raising has confirmed writings. He has not thereby performed Movement VII.

The Bible Introduction's observation that the Jewish Scriptures were divided as Law, Prophets, and Writings, and that Christ referred to this division as the Law, the Prophets, and the Psalms, is a historical correspondence worth stating as correspondence. The correspondence supports reception of a known body. It does not license this chapter to settle which later titles belong at the edges. Movement VII remains the file of corpus and recognition.

# 48.9 Confirmation Distinguished from Infallibility Verdict

Infallibility, as this book will later argue it, concerns what follows from God's truthfulness, from genuine revelation, from inspiration, and from authentication, for the truthfulness of the inspired written record. That argument has unfinished premises. God as the ground of truth, and the impossibility of God's lying, have been established in Movement II. Inspiration has been examined as a doctrine of production. Authentication is still accumulating. Canon has not been examined. The Graphē-gate has not been drawn.

Confirmation is an act of this Person toward writings He received. The act has evidential force because of who He has already been shown to be. It does not, by itself, pronounce the later verdict that the completed corpus is an infallible record.

The distinction protects both conclusions. If confirmation is quietly renamed infallibility, Movement VII becomes a repetition. If confirmation is refused any force until infallibility is pronounced, Movement VI is emptied of the line the TOC assigns it. The writings He treats as binding written word are confirmed by the Truth as binding written word. That is already a central authentication line. The later verdict will ask why those writings, as Graphē, cannot be broken. Different question. Later chapter.

# 48.10 Confirmation Distinguished from Movement VIII's Finished Graphē-Doctrine

John 10:34–36 records a controversy in which Jesus answers an accusation of blasphemy by citing what is written in the Law: “I said, Ye are gods.” He then says that the scripture cannot be broken, and reasons from the written wording to His own consecration and sending. That scene is an instance of the practice this chapter examines: He appeals to what is written as written, and He treats the written wording as having force that His argument may not evade.

The scene is not this chapter's dedicated object.

The locked TOC assigns John 10:34–36, Psalm 82:6, Christ's reasoning from what is written, “the scripture cannot be broken,” the why of that statement, John 17:17, and the Graphē as primary written authority to Chapters 59–65. Those chapters exist because the argument of John 10 is an argument, not a slogan, and because the why of “cannot be broken” must rest on what Scripture is, not merely on the fact that Jesus quoted it.

This chapter therefore records the practice and reserves the verdict. He confirms received Scripture by treating the written wording as decisive in controversy. Movement VIII will ask why that wording cannot be broken, and what follows for the Graphē as primary written authority. The reservation is sequence. It is not a suspicion that John 10 is weak. It is the refusal to steal the later why.

John 17:17 — “Sanctify them through thy truth: thy word is truth” — is likewise reserved. The present chapter's object is His treatment of the received writings as authoritative written word. The later chapter's object is the relation of God's Word to truth as Graphē-doctrine. The sentences are neighbours. They are not the same assignment.

# 48.11 Confirmation Distinguished from Apostolic Witness and Apostolic Authority

The seers have been examined. The college has not.

Chapter 47 established positioned knowledge of what was seen and heard. It distinguished apostolic eyewitness from apostolic office as a finished doctrine. That distinction remains. This chapter's object is not the office. It is the Lord's own relation to the writings.

Luke 24 joins the two neighbourhoods without collapsing them. The raised Lord opens the Scriptures. He also says, “Ye are witnesses of these things” (Luke 24:48). Opening the writings is confirmation of Scripture. Naming them as witnesses is the threshold of Chapter 49. The same scene may contain both acts. The acts remain two files.

2 Peter 1 exhibits the same neighbourhood. Verses 16–18 are the eyewitness unit already examined. Verses 19–21 turn to the prophetic word. The turn is the letter's own movement from mountain to writing. This chapter may name that turn as the seers' Lord not displacing the prophetic word. It may not close Chapter 30 again, and it may not close Chapters 49–50 by treating the letter as the finished doctrine of apostolic office.

The remaining work of Movement VI is therefore still the remaining work: Christ and the apostolic witness; then the authority of the apostolic testimony. Confirmation of received Scripture does not perform those tasks in advance.

# 48.12 The Scriptures He Received: Law, Prophets, Psalms

The body He names is not an undefined “Bible” in the later English sense, and it is not a private anthology.

Luke 24:44 records His own grouping:

> **“These are the words which I spake unto you, while I was yet with you, that all things must be fulfilled, which were written in the law of Moses, and in the prophets, and in the psalms, concerning me.”**

Three designations stand together.

**The law of Moses** — **νόμος Μωϋσέως (*nomos Mōyseōs*)** — is the Torah, the written fivefold book associated with Moses. He cites Deuteronomy in temptation. He cites Exodus at the bush. He cites Genesis on man and woman. He speaks of Moses as a writer whose writings concern Him (John 5:46). Moses is not a rumour. Moses is a writing.

**The prophets** — **προφῆται (*prophētai*)** — are the prophetic writings as a body, including, in the Jewish grouping of His time, both the former prophets of the historical books and the latter prophets of the named prophetic voices. He reads Isaiah in the synagogue. He cites Daniel. He speaks of “the scriptures of the prophets” in the passion (Matthew 26:56). The prophets are not atmosphere. They are writings that can be opened, read, and claimed as fulfilled.

**The psalms** — **ψαλμοί (*psalmoi*, G5568)** — stand as the named third. In the Jewish division the Writings are a larger group of which the Psalms are the head and the representative. His wording is “the psalms.” The wording is His. It confirms at least the Psalter as belonging to the writings concerning Himself. It is historically coherent with a threefold Jewish division of Law, Prophets, and Writings. Coherence is not yet Movement VII's closed list. It is reception of a known body under names His hearers already used.

Luke 24:27 uses a related pair:

> **“And beginning at Moses and all the prophets, he expounded unto them in all the scriptures the things concerning himself.”**

**All the scriptures** — **πάσαις ταῖς γραφαῖς (*pasais tais graphais*)** — is the body's name as Graphē. Moses and all the prophets are the starting-points of the exposition. The object is the writings. The subject of the writings, in His claim, is Himself.

The historical setting of Chapter 43 already placed Him inside Second Temple Judaism, among synagogues, among a people who possessed these writings. Reception is therefore not a later Christian discovery of the Old Testament. It is this Jew's life inside the writings of His people, now examined as the Truth's confirmation of those writings.

He also treats named figures and events of those writings as belonging to the real past, not as disposable fable. Jonah is a sign of three days and three nights in the whale's belly, and Nineveh's repentance is the measure of the present generation (Matthew 12:39–41; Luke 11:29–32). The queen of the south comes from the uttermost parts of the earth to hear Solomon's wisdom (Matthew 12:42). Daniel the prophet is named as the speaker of the abomination of desolation, with a charge to the reader (Matthew 24:15). David eats the shewbread (Matthew 12:3–4). Abel's blood is from the foundation of the world (Luke 11:51). Noah's days are the measure of the coming of the Son of man (Matthew 24:37–39). Confirmation of received Scripture includes this historical seriousness: the writings He opens are writings of persons and events He treats as having occurred. The observation is sometimes offered as His seal upon the genuineness of the earlier corpus. The warranted form of that observation, at this stage, is more precise. He confirms those writings by using them as standing accounts of what was, what was written, and what must yet be filled. He does not hereby close Movement VII's list or Movement VII's infallibility verdict. He does hereby refuse the reduction that the earlier writings were, in His mouth, mere edifying myth.

---

# 48.13 The Biblical Language: *Gegraptai*, *Gegrammenon*, *Graphē*

The record's own words must control the file.

**γράφω (*graphō*, G1125)** is the verb to write. The perfect passive **γέγραπται (*gegraptai*)** means that a writing has been made and stands. English “it is written” is adequate if the perfect is not flattened into a mere past. The writing remains. The cited sentence is treated as present.

The perfect participle **γεγραμμένον (*gegrammenon*)** names that which stands written. Luke 4:17: the place of the writing where it was written. Luke 22:37: “this that is written must yet be accomplished in me.” John 19:22, in a different scene, has Pilate saying, “What I have written I have written” — a secular parallel that at least shows the perfect's force as a standing written act. The Gospel's theological use is not thereby proved by Pilate. The grammar of standing writing is shared.

**γραφή (*graphē*, G1124)** is the noun already examined in Chapters 20–22. In the passages of this chapter it designates Scripture as writing: “this scripture” (Luke 4:21); “the scriptures” (Matthew 22:29; Luke 24:27, 45; John 5:39); “the scripture” in the singular as a particular writing or as the written Word in view. Context determines whether a particular passage or the body is meant. In either case the object is writing, not an oral rumour detached from a text.

**ἀναγινώσκω (*anaginōskō*, G314)** is the verb of reading. “Have ye not read” assumes a written object that can be read. The charge is not “have ye not heard a tradition.” It is “have ye not read.” Reading is the appointed act toward a writing.

**πληρόω (*plēroō*, G4137)** is the verb of filling and fulfilling. “This day is this scripture fulfilled in your ears” (Luke 4:21). “All things must be fulfilled, which were written…” (Luke 24:44). Fulfillment, in His mouth, is the filling-up of what the writing already contained, not the invention of a meaning the writing never had. Chapter 37 already distinguished genuine correspondence from vague retrospective claim. This chapter takes that distinction as a premise and examines His own use of the verb as confirmation of the writing's force.

The language is therefore not the later vocabulary of a council. It is writing, standing writing, Scripture as writing, reading, and fulfilling. Confirmation is the right conceptual name for the practice those words exhibit.

---

# 48.14 “It Is Written” as a Formula of Force

The formula **γέγραπται (*gegraptai*)** is not a flourish attached to sermons. It is the ordinary way this Person names the writings as the rule of a present case.

The force of the formula is fourfold.

First, **priority**. Something has already been written. The present speaker is not creating the sentence He cites. He is standing under it.

Second, **permanence**. The perfect treats the writing as standing. A cancelled writing would not be cited as the rule of the present.

Third, **publicity**. What is written is in principle available. “Have ye not read” is the sister-formula of the same architecture. The text is not an esoteric oracle known only to Him.

Fourth, **adjudication**. The cited writing is used to decide. Temptation is refused by it. Error is named by ignorance of it. Necessity in the passion is named by it.

Matthew 4 and Luke 4 place the formula at the beginning of the public ministry, in conflict. Matthew 26:24 and Mark 14:21 place it over the passion: “the Son of man goeth as it is written of him.” Luke 24:46 places it in the mouth of the raised Lord: “Thus it is written, and thus it behoved Christ to suffer, and to rise from the dead the third day.” The formula therefore spans the ministry's opening conflict, the ministry's end, and the ministry's sequel. It is not a local habit of one controversy.

A later note in the research tradition observes that “as it is written” functions as a guide by which a doctrine out of harmony with a written statement is exposed as false. The observation is an inference from the formula's adjudicative use, not a substitute for examining the scenes. This chapter examines the scenes. The inference is warranted as a description of His practice: the written sentence is treated as the measure of the present claim. The later hermeneutical labour of comparing Scripture with Scripture is Movement X. The present point is more basic. He uses what is written as force.

---

# 48.15 The Temptation Narratives (Matthew 4 / Luke 4)

Matthew 4:1–11 and Luke 4:1–13 present Jesus, after baptism, led into the wilderness to be tempted by the devil. The scene is not a classroom. It is conflict. The answers are not suggestions. They are refusals.

The first proposal, in Matthew's order, is that the Son command stones to become bread, after forty days' fasting. The answer is:

> **“It is written, Man shall not live by bread alone, but by every word that proceedeth out of the mouth of God.”**

The cited writing is Deuteronomy 8:3, Moses' interpretation of the manna: Israel was fed in order to learn that life is not by bread only, but by the word of the LORD. The wilderness setting of Deuteronomy is not accidental to the wilderness setting of the temptation. He answers a proposed act concerning bread by a written sentence concerning bread in the wilderness. The correspondence is the writing's own subject, not a distant flourish.

The second proposal, in Matthew's order, is that He cast Himself from the temple's pinnacle, with Psalm 91:11–12 cited by the tempter: He shall give His angels charge, they shall bear Thee up. The tempter also says “it is written.” Citation as such is therefore not the whole of confirmation. A writing can be quoted against its force. The answer is:

> **“It is written again, Thou shalt not tempt the Lord thy God.”**

The cited writing is Deuteronomy 6:16, which recalls Massah: Israel is not to put the LORD to the proof. Psalm 91 is not thereby abolished. It is refused as a license to manufacture a proof. The second “it is written” judges the use of the first. Confirmation includes interpretation. It is not the talisman of a detached clause.

The third proposal is the kingdoms of the world in exchange for worship. The answer is:

> **“Get thee hence, Satan: for it is written, Thou shalt worship the Lord thy God, and him only shalt thou serve.”**

The cited writing is Deuteronomy 6:13 (with the force of 6:13–14): fear the LORD, serve Him, and do not go after other gods. The proposed exchange is refused by a written sentence about worship and exclusive service.

Luke's order places the temple-proposal last and notes that the devil departed for a season. The order is a narrative selection. The three citations remain Deuteronomy. The formula remains “it is written.” The conflict remains a conflict answered by standing writing.

The scene establishes, as an opening architecture of the ministry, that this Person's first recorded public conflict after the baptism is met not by a new oracle detached from Moses, and not by a display of power as the first move, but by appeal to what is written. The Son, already identified in Chapter 44 as the Truth, answers as one under the writings of Moses. Confirmation is the right name.

---

# 48.16 Three Citations in Conflict: Deuteronomy as Binding Written Word

The three answers are not three decorations. They are one practice under three written sentences of the same book.

Deuteronomy is Moses' written instruction to Israel on the edge of the land. It is Law in the sense Luke 24:44 names. By answering from Deuteronomy, Jesus treats that writing as present force for the Son in the wilderness. The Law is not being used as a relic of a discarded covenant in this scene. It is the rule of the conflict.

Several conclusions follow from the scene as a scene, without yet drawing Movement VIII.

First, **the writings are usable as the rule of a present moral and spiritual conflict**. Life, testing God, and worship are settled by what is written.

Second, **the writings can be cited against their force**, as the tempter cites Psalm 91. Confirmation is therefore not mere quotation. It is quotation under the writing's actual claim. “It is written again” is the correction of a citation by a citation.

Third, **the Son does not treat His identity as a license to evade the writings**. The baptismal voice has already named Him beloved Son. The first proposal begins, “If thou be the Son of God.” The answer does not say that Sonship replaces Moses. The answer says that it is written. Identity and writing are not rivals in this scene.

Fourth, **the scene is not yet a canon list**. Three Deuteronomy sentences and a refused use of Psalm 91 do not enumerate the corpus. They do confirm Law and Psalm as writings that can be cited as “it is written.”

The temptation narratives are therefore not a slogan-source for “Jesus used the Bible.” They are the ministry's opening exhibition of confirmation as adjudicative appeal to standing writing, in conflict, by the One already identified as the Truth.

---

# 48.17 “Have You Not Read” as an Assumption of Binding Text

A second formula runs through the controversies: **οὐκ ἀνέγνωτε (*ouk anegnōte*)** — have ye not read?

The question is not a request for information. It is a charge. The writings were available. Reading was the appointed act. The error in view is an error against a text that could have been read.

Matthew 12:3–5, in a Sabbath controversy, records two such questions: have ye not read what David did when he hungered, and have ye not read in the law how the priests in the temple profane the Sabbath and are blameless? The appeal is to narrative in the former writings and to the law of the priests. The Sabbath dispute is not being settled by a private vision. It is being settled by writings the opponents already possess.

Matthew 19:4, in a dispute about divorce, records:

> **“Have ye not read, that he which made them at the beginning made them male and female, And said, For this cause shall a man leave father and mother, and shall cleave to his wife: and they twain shall be one flesh?”**

The cited writing is Genesis 1:27 and 2:24. The dispute is about present practice. The court of appeal is the written beginning. Moses' later concession, He says, was for hardness of heart. The written beginning remains the measure. Confirmation here is not a vague respect for “the tradition of our people.” It is an appeal to specified written sentences as decisive over a later practice.

Matthew 21:42:

> **“Did ye never read in the scriptures, The stone which the builders rejected, the same is become the head of the corner?”**

Mark 12:10 calls the same citation “this scripture.” The parable of the vineyard has just been spoken. The writing of Psalm 118:22–23 is then named as Scripture the hearers should have read. The rejected-stone text is treated as the written interpretation of the present rejection.

Matthew 22:31–32, to the Sadducees:

> **“But as touching the resurrection of the dead, have ye not read that which was spoken unto you by God, saying, I am the God of Abraham, and the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob? God is not the God of the dead, but of the living.”**

Mark 12:26 locates the writing: “have ye not read in the book of Moses, how in the bush God spake unto him.” The appeal is to Exodus 3:6, a writing in the Law, a particular scene, a particular wording: **I am**, not merely **I was**. He treats the written tense and the written name as having argumentative force. Movement VIII will later examine a similar appeal to written wording in John 10. The present point is the practice: the book of Moses is to be read; the wording is the argument; ignorance of the writing is the named error.

Luke 10:26, to a lawyer:

> **“What is written in the law? how readest thou?”**

The two questions belong together. What is written, and how do you read? Confirmation is not the abolition of reading as an act of understanding. It is the insistence that the written law is the place to look, and that reading is the hearer's responsibility.

The formula's assumption is therefore stable across Sabbath, marriage, rejection, resurrection, and the summary of the law: the writings are there to be read, and what they say has force for the present dispute.

Matthew 15:1–9 and Mark 7:5–13 place the same assumption over against tradition. The Pharisees ask why the disciples transgress the tradition of the elders. He answers by Isaiah: this people honours with the lips, but their heart is far; in vain they worship, teaching for doctrines the commandments of men. Then He names the contrast: laying aside the commandment of God, they hold the tradition of men; they reject the commandment of God, that they may keep their own tradition; they make the word of God of none effect through their tradition. The commandment in the scene is the written honour of father and mother, set against a vowed-gift practice. Confirmation here includes a ranking: what God commanded in writing measures tradition; tradition does not measure the writing. Movement IX will examine mishandling and the rise of secondary authorities in full. The present point is already in force. He confirms the received writings by refusing to let a later human rule evacuate them.

---

# 48.18 The Sadducees and Exodus 3: “Ye Do Err, Not Knowing the Scriptures”

Matthew 22:23–33, Mark 12:18–27, and Luke 20:27–40 present the Sadducees, who say there is no resurrection, proposing a reductio from levirate marriage. Seven brothers, one wife, whose wife shall she be?

The answer, in Matthew's wording, begins:

> **“Ye do err, not knowing the scriptures, nor the power of God.”**

Two ignorances are named, and they are named as the cause of the error. Not knowing the Scriptures is not a small gap in literary culture. It is the explanation of a doctrinal failure. Chapter 21 already listed this text among the New Testament's appeals to Scripture as authoritative written testimony. The present file takes the appeal as this Person's confirmation.

The Scriptures in view, as the next sentences show, include the book of Moses at the bush. The Sadducees are the party associated, in the ordinary historical description, with a restricted regard for the later writings and with denial of resurrection. He answers them from the Law they acknowledge, and from a scene they cannot treat as a later accretion of prophets they dispute. The strategy is confirmation of Moses as sufficient, in that controversy, to carry the living God as the God of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob — and therefore as not the God of the dead.

The argument is an argument from written wording. “I am” is taken as present relation, not as a terminated historical footnote. Whether every later reader would have drawn the same inference is a hermeneutical question Movement IX may examine. The present point is that He treats the written wording as having the right to correct a party's doctrine, and that the named cause of error is not knowing the Scriptures.

Confirmation here includes a negative: refusal of the writings, or ignorance of them, yields error. The positive is the same fact seen from the other side: knowing the Scriptures is the appointed way not to err in the matter at hand.

---

# 48.19 Marriage and Genesis: Written Origin as Settling Word

Matthew 19:3–9 and Mark 10:2–9 present a test concerning divorce. The Pharisees ask whether it is lawful for a man to put away his wife for every cause. The appeal to Moses' permission is anticipated. Jesus does not begin with the permission. He begins with the beginning.

Genesis is treated as a written account of what the Maker did and said. “He which made them at the beginning made them male and female.” The Maker's act is narrated in writing. The Maker's word is narrated in writing: “they twain shall be one flesh.” The inference He draws is that they are no more twain but one flesh, and that man is not to put asunder what God hath joined together.

Moses' bill of divorcement is then located as a concession to hardness of heart, “but from the beginning it was not so.” Two writings are not being set at war as if the corpus were incoherent. The later permission is being measured by the earlier written origin. Confirmation includes the ranking of texts by their place in the writing's own beginning. Chapter 38 already treated unity and continuity as properties of the corpus. This scene is the central witness performing that ranking in controversy.

The scene matters to the definition of confirmation for a further reason. The subject is not a “religious” topic detached from life. It is marriage. The writings are being used as the measure of a public institution. Authoritative written word, in His practice, is not confined to temple-ceremony. It reaches the beginning of man and woman.

# 48.20 The Synagogue Reading (Luke 4:16–21)

Luke 4:16–21 is a public scene of reception, reading, interpretation, and claim.

> **“And he came to Nazareth, where he had been brought up: and, as his custom was, he went into the synagogue on the sabbath day, and stood up for to read.”**

Custom is named. Reception is named. Nazareth is named. The synagogue is the public house of the writings. He stands up to read. Reading is the act of confirmation in its most ordinary public form.

The book of the prophet Isaiah is delivered to Him. He finds the place where it is written. The writing is a scroll that can be handed, opened, located, and read aloud. Luke then gives the words of Isaiah 61:1–2, with the force of 58:6 in the clause about liberty to the bruised:

> **“The Spirit of the Lord is upon me, because he hath anointed me to preach the gospel to the poor; he hath sent me to heal the brokenhearted, to preach deliverance to the captives, and recovering of sight to the blind, to set at liberty them that are bruised, To preach the acceptable year of the Lord.”**

He closes the book, gives it again to the minister, and sits down. The eyes of all in the synagogue are fastened on Him. He says:

> **“This day is this scripture fulfilled in your ears.”**

**This scripture** — **ἡ γραφὴ αὕτη (*hē graphē hautē*)** — is the writing just read. **Fulfilled** is **πεπλήρωται (*peplērōtai*)**, perfect: it stands fulfilled. **This day** and **in your ears** locate the fulfillment in a public hearing, not in a private vision.

The scene is therefore not a later narrator's fulfillment-formula attached to an event the speaker never joined to a text. The speaker Himself reads the prophet and claims the day. Interpretation is present: the writing is about this day and this reader. Confirmation is present: the writing is Scripture, it is read, and its force is claimed as now filled.

The following verses show that the claim is not received as obvious. They wonder at gracious words, then take offence at the local carpenter's son, then hear Elijah and Elisha used against the assumption that a prophet is for Nazareth only, then attempt to throw Him from the hill. Confirmation of Scripture is not the same act as persuasion of every hearer. The writings can be read in a synagogue that then rejects the reader. The rejection does not convert the reading into illustration. It exhibits the writings as a public court before which a claim is made, and as a court that does not automatically produce faith.

Chapter 45 already treated the Nazareth scene as belonging to the public career. This chapter takes the same scene as confirmation of a written prophet in public reading. The two uses are two functions of one narrative. They are not duplication. Identity of scene is not identity of assignment.

---

# 48.21 Isaiah Opened in Public: Fulfillment Claimed of a Written Scroll

The object in Luke 4 is not “prophecy” in the abstract. It is a scroll, a place in the writing, a public reading, and a claim that this writing stands fulfilled.

Isaiah is a named prophet. The book is delivered. The place is found. The words are those of anointing, good news to the poor, release, sight, liberty, and the acceptable year. The claim is that these words are filled in the hearing of that Sabbath.

Two reductions fail immediately.

The first reduction says that Jesus used Isaiah as a motto for a social program, without treating the writing as Scripture. The text says “this scripture.” The reduction erases the word the scene uses.

The second reduction says that the church later invented the reading to make Jesus look like a fulfillment of Isaiah. The scene, as Luke writes it, contains offence, attempted violence, and the proverb of the prophet without honour in his own country. Invention that wishes to make a smooth messianic debut has chosen an unlikely gift. Chapter 47 already used retained friction as a mark against global fabrication of the seers. The same mark applies, with proportional force, to this public reading.

The scene does not, by itself, close Chapter 37's predictive-correspondence case for every Isaianic text. It establishes that this Person publicly treated a written prophet as Scripture and claimed its fulfillment of Himself in a named town on a Sabbath. That is confirmation.

---

# 48.22 Matthew 5:17–18: Not to Destroy but to Fulfill

The Sermon on the Mount opens its relation to the prior writings with a prohibition of a misunderstanding:

> **“Think not that I am come to destroy the law, or the prophets: I am not come to destroy, but to fulfil.”**

**The law or the prophets** is the pair already in use as a name for the received writings. **Destroy** — **καταλῦσαι (*katalusai*)** — is to loosen down, to abolish. **Fulfil** — **πληρῶσαι (*plērōsai*)** — is to fill up. The claim is that His coming is not the abolition of those writings. It is their filling.

Chapter 26 already recorded this text as part of Christ's relation to the earlier written revelation. Chapter 38 already used it as a mark of continuity. Chapter 45 already noted that the antitheses which follow — “Ye have heard that it was said… but I say unto you” — are a claim-class of authority, not yet this chapter's doctrine of confirmation. Those uses are premises. The present function is confirmation: He publicly refuses the role of destroyer of the Law and the Prophets, and He names fulfillment as the right description of His coming in relation to them.

The next sentence intensifies the claim toward the writing itself:

> **“For verily I say unto you, Till heaven and earth pass, one jot or one tittle shall in no wise pass from the law, till all be fulfilled.”**

Luke 16:17 is a sister-sentence: it is easier for heaven and earth to pass than for one tittle of the law to fail. The **jot** is the smallest Hebrew letter. The **tittle** is a small stroke of writing. The claim is about the written Law in its smallest marks, not about a vague “moral spirit of Moses.” He confirms the writings as writings.

This is not yet the infallibility verdict of Movement VII. It is not yet “the scripture cannot be broken” as Movement VIII's dedicated why. It is the public claim, by the One who is the Truth, that He has not come to abolish the Law and the Prophets, and that the written Law's smallest marks are not treated as disposable ornament. Confirmation of received Scripture includes that claim.

The antitheses that follow do not cancel the claim. “But I say unto you” intensifies murder into anger, adultery into lust, oath into simple truth, retaliation into endurance, and neighbour-love into love of enemies. Intensification is not abolition. The writings remain. The Son speaks as their fulfiller, not as their replacement. Chapter 45's claim-class and this chapter's confirmation-class meet here without merging. He speaks with His own authority, and He confirms the writings He fulfills.

---

# 48.23 Jot and Tittle: The Written Smallest Marks

Matthew 5:18's force is easy to flatten into rhetoric. The flattening must be refused.

A teacher who means only “the general idea of the Law matters” does not name jot and tittle. A teacher who means “I am founding a religion that has no need of Moses” does not swear that heaven and earth will pass before those marks pass. The sentence is a sentence about writing.

**Ἰῶτα (*iōta*)** in Matthew's Greek represents the Hebrew yod, the smallest letter. **κεραία (*keraia*)** is a horn, a small projection of a letter. The vocabulary is the vocabulary of a written text in a particular script, not of an oral gist.

The temporal clauses are two: till heaven and earth pass; till all be fulfilled. The Law's smallest marks are placed inside the world's standing and inside the filling of all that the writings contain. Fulfillment is again the right verb. The writings are not being frozen as a museum. They are being treated as marks that stand until their filling.

The later questions of how covenantal administrations relate to fulfillment — questions Chapter 38 already refused to treat as contradiction — remain later as hermeneutical labour. They do not erase the present confirmation. The Truth has publicly bound Himself to the writings as writings, down to the smallest marks, as that which He has not come to destroy.

# 48.24 John 5:39–47: The Scriptures Testify of Me; Moses Wrote of Me

John 5 is a controversy after a Sabbath healing. The Jews seek to kill Him because He has done this on the Sabbath and has made Himself equal with God (John 5:18). He answers with a discourse on the Son's works, the Father's raising of the dead, judgment, and witness. The Father's witness, John's witness, the works, and the writings are all named. Chapter 44 already used the revelatory architecture of the chapter. Chapter 45 already used the works-argument. This chapter takes the writings.

> **“Search the scriptures; for in them ye think ye have eternal life: and they are they which testify of me.”**

The Scriptures are an object of search. The opponents already assign them the function of life. He does not deny that function. He names their witness: they testify of Him. Confirmation here is not a vague respect. It is the claim that the received writings are witnesses in a legal architecture of testimony, and that their subject is Himself.

The charge follows: “And ye will not come to me, that ye might have life.” The writings and the Person are not rivals. Refusal of the Person is, in this argument, refusal of the writings' witness.

Then Moses:

> **“Do not think that I will accuse you to the Father: there is one that accuseth you, even Moses, in whom ye trust. For had ye believed Moses, ye would have believed me: for he wrote of me. But if ye believe not his writings, how shall ye believe my words?”**

**He wrote of me** is an explicit claim that Moses is a writer, that the writing is about Christ, and that belief in Moses and belief in Christ stand or fall together. **His writings** — **γράμμασιν (*grammasin*)** — are written letters, documents. **My words** are the Son's present speech. The argument is that refusal of the writings undercuts reception of the Son's words. The writings are not a ladder kicked away once the Son speaks. They are the appointed witness without which His words are not believed.

This is one of the chapter's clearest refusals of the reduction that His authority replaces Scripture. He places His words after Moses' writings in the order of the charge: if you do not believe the writings, how will you believe Me? Confirmation of received Scripture is here the condition He names for believing Him.

The claim that Moses wrote of Him is not, in this chapter, a catalogue of every Mosaic text so used. It is the explicit architecture: Moses as writer; writings as about Christ; unbelief toward the writings as unbelief toward Him. Movement VII may ask how a corpus is recognized. Movement VIII may ask why the Graphē cannot be broken. The present sentence is already in force: He confirms Moses' writings as writings that testify of Him, and He treats unbelief toward those writings as the explanation of unbelief toward Himself.

---

# 48.25 Searching and Refusing: The Writings as Witnesses Concerning Him

John 5:39's “search” may be read as indicative or as imperative in the underlying Greek. Either way the opponents are occupied with the Scriptures, and either way He says that those Scriptures testify of Him. Occupation with the writings is not the same act as receiving their witness.

The distinction matters to later Movements IX–X, where mishandling, unskillfulness, and labour will be examined. It belongs here only as a mark of confirmation: He does not treat the writings as a closed door. He treats them as witnesses whose subject can be refused by men who search them. Confirmation of Scripture is compatible with the fact that Scripture can be held and refused at once. The later diagnosis of that refusal is later. The present fact is that the Truth names the writings as His witnesses.

John 5 therefore joins three claims that this chapter must keep together:

1. The Scriptures testify of Him.
2. Moses wrote of Him.
3. Unbelief toward the writings explains unbelief toward His words.

The three claims are the opposite of a religion that begins by discarding Moses. They are also the opposite of a religion that treats Moses as a rival of the Son. The writings and the Son are placed in one architecture of witness. That architecture is confirmation.

# 48.26 Scripture Fulfilled: Passion, Betrayal, and the Necessity of What Is Written

The passion narratives do not treat the death as an accident later decorated with texts. They treat the death as the filling of what was written.

Matthew 26:54, in Gethsemane, when a sword has been drawn:

> **“But how then shall the scriptures be fulfilled, that thus it must be?”**

**Must** and **fulfilled** stand together. The writings are the named necessity. Rescue by the sword is refused because it would evacuate the writings.

Matthew 26:56:

> **“But all this was done, that the scriptures of the prophets might be fulfilled.”**

The clause is the narrator's in Matthew's form, immediately after Jesus' own question of 26:54. The narrator and the speaker agree. The prophets' writings are the named reason of the arrest as it unfolds.

Mark 14:21:

> **“The Son of man indeed goeth, as it is written of him: but woe to that man by whom the Son of man is betrayed!”**

Luke 22:37:

> **“For I say unto you, that this that is written must yet be accomplished in me, And he was reckoned among the transgressors: for the things concerning me have an end.”**

The cited writing is Isaiah 53:12. He applies it to Himself as a writing that must be accomplished in Him. Confirmation in the passion is not a later homily. It is the Lord's own naming of a written sentence as the necessity of His being numbered with transgressors.

John 13:18, of the betrayal:

> **“I speak not of you all: I know whom I have chosen: but that the scripture may be fulfilled, He that eateth bread with me hath lifted up his heel against me.”**

The cited writing is Psalm 41:9. The betrayal is placed inside a writing. The writing is not being used to excuse the betrayer. Woe remains. The writing is being used to name the event as fulfillment.

John 17:12 names the son of perdition as the one lost “that the scripture might be fulfilled.” The particular writing is not quoted in that clause. The architecture is the same: Scripture as the named filling of what happens in the passion-circle.

The passion therefore continues the practice of the wilderness and the synagogue: what is written has force, and the force here is necessity. Chapter 46 already established the death as the public end of the ministry and recorded “according to the scriptures” as the formula's own clause. This chapter attaches that clause to His own mouth. The formula is not a Pauline hobby absent from the Gospels. The Lord of the seers names the writings as the necessity of the way He goes.

# 48.27 “How Then Shall the Scriptures Be Fulfilled?”

Matthew 26:54 is the question that prevents a pious violence from presenting itself as faith.

The scene has swords. A servant's ear is wounded. Rescue is available as an image of loyalty. He refuses the image by asking how the Scriptures would then be fulfilled. The writings are the measure of the permissible act. An act that would empty the writings is refused, even if the act looks like defence of the Messiah.

Confirmation here has a moral edge. The writings do not exist to decorate a career that is then saved from them. The career is lived as their filling. The Truth does not evade the writings in order to survive. He goes as it is written of Him.

The question also protects the file from a reduction that says fulfillment-language is only the church's later way of coping with a failed messianic hope. In Gethsemane the speaker is not coping with a death that has already occurred. He is refusing a means of avoiding the death, and He is naming the writings as the reason of the refusal. The reduction must then claim that the church invented both the refusal and the question. Possible as a global suspicion. Costly as an account of a scene whose friction is the wound, the rebuke, and the flight of the disciples. The present chapter does not re-try Chapter 46's hinge. It notes that the hinge, in Matthew's Gethsemane, is already joined by the speaker to the Scriptures as necessity.

# 48.28 Temple, House of Prayer, and “Is It Not Written”

Mark 11:17, in the temple:

> **“Is it not written, My house shall be called of all nations the house of prayer? but ye have made it a den of thieves.”**

The cited writings are Isaiah 56:7 and Jeremiah 7:11. The act is a public confrontation in the temple courts. The measure of the confrontation is what is written. The house is the LORD's house in the writing. The nations belong to the writing's claim. The den of thieves is the writing's charge against a false trust in the temple.

Matthew 21:13 and Luke 19:46 give the same architecture with small differences of wording. The formula remains an appeal to writing. The temple is not being measured by a new private oracle. It is being measured by sentences already written against a house that has become a den.

The scene is confirmation in public space, against an institution that already claims to honour the writings. As in John 5, possession of the writings is not the same act as being measured by them. He confirms the writings by using them as the measure of the house. The later questions of mishandling belong to Movement IX. The present fact is the measure: it is written.

# 48.29 The Road to Emmaus (Luke 24:25–27)

Luke 24:13–35 presents two of them going to Emmaus on the first day of the week, reasoning over the things that have happened. Jesus Himself draws near. Their eyes are holden. They recount the prophet mighty in deed and word, the condemnation, the crucifixion, the third day, the women's vision of angels, the empty tomb, and the absence of His body. They had trusted that it had been He who should have redeemed Israel.

His first words to that account are not a new vision-report. They are a rebuke concerning the prophets and a necessity concerning the Christ:

> **“O fools, and slow of heart to believe all that the prophets have spoken: Ought not Christ to have suffered these things, and to enter into his glory?”**

**All that the prophets have spoken** is the named object of unbelief. Slowness of heart is the named fault. The suffering and the glory are the named necessity. The raised Lord does not treat the writings as a later optional commentary on Easter. He treats unbelief toward the prophets as the explanation of their despair, and the prophets' speech as the necessity of the suffering they have just described.

Then:

> **“And beginning at Moses and all the prophets, he expounded unto them in all the scriptures the things concerning himself.”**

**Beginning at Moses** is a starting-point in the writings, not in a private myth. **All the prophets** extends the body. **All the scriptures** names the object as Graphē. **The things concerning himself** is the subject He claims for those writings. **Expounded** — **διερμήνευσεν (*diermēneusen*)** — is interpretation. Confirmation after the raising is interpretation of the received writings as concerning Him, issued as the correction of seers who have already heard of the empty tomb and still have not believed the prophets.

Chapter 47 noted this scene as belonging to an appearance and left the Graphē-argument in its place. The appearance-file is closed as Chapter 47's object. The Graphē-argument is now due. The same walk is an eyewitness event and a confirmation event. The two assignments remain two. The walk is one.

The later breaking of bread opens their eyes. They remember that their heart burned while He opened to them the scriptures. Opening the Scriptures is the named cause of the burning, not a later pious caption. Confirmation is the Lord's own act upon the writings in the hearing of slow hearts.

# 48.30 Moses and All the Prophets: Beginning from the Writings Concerning Himself

Luke 24:27's sequence is the book's own sequence in miniature, now in the mouth of the raised Lord.

He does not begin with the empty tomb as a substitute for Moses. He begins at Moses. He does not treat the prophets as a miscellaneous anthology of religious genius. He takes all the prophets. He does not treat “concerning himself” as a single proof text. He expounds the things concerning Himself in all the Scriptures.

The claim is therefore comprehensive in scope and particular in subject. Comprehensive: Moses, all the prophets, all the Scriptures. Particular: the things concerning Himself, especially, as 24:26 has just said, the suffering and the glory.

This is not yet a verse-by-verse commentary of the corpus. Luke does not record the particular texts of that walk. The absence of a transcript is not the absence of the act. The act is named: exposition of the writings as concerning Him, beginning at Moses.

The research tradition's observation that the whole Bible centers around the person and work of Jesus Christ, with predictions in the earlier writings and fulfillment in the later, is an inference this scene warrants as architecture, not as a license to invent a list of fulfillments Luke does not give. The scene warrants the architecture: the raised Lord treats the received writings as the place where His suffering and glory were already spoken, and He treats slowness to believe those writings as the fault in the seers' despair.

# 48.31 Law, Prophets, and Psalms (Luke 24:44–47)

The upper-room scene repeats the Emmaus architecture in a more explicit grouping, now to the gathered company, with the marks of a body still in view.

He shows them His hands and His feet. He eats. Chapter 47 already took those marks as eyewitness events of the risen Christ. Then He says:

> **“These are the words which I spake unto you, while I was yet with you, that all things must be fulfilled, which were written in the law of Moses, and in the prophets, and in the psalms, concerning me.”**

The grouping is His. The necessity is His: **must be fulfilled**. The subject is His: **concerning me**. The time-stamp is His: this is what He already said while He was yet with them. Confirmation is not an Easter invention in this sentence. It is the raised Lord's identification of His prior teaching with the filling of the writings.

> **“Then opened he their understanding, that they might understand the scriptures, And said unto them, Thus it is written, and thus it behoved Christ to suffer, and to rise from the dead the third day: And that repentance and remission of sins should be preached in his name among all nations, beginning at Jerusalem.”**

Four elements stand in order.

First, **He opens their understanding**. The writings do not automatically yield their sense to slow hearts. The opener is the Lord of the writings. Later labour in the Word will be Movement X. The present fact is that understanding of the Scriptures is His gift in this scene, not a replacement of the Scriptures by a gift that has no writing.

Second, **“thus it is written.”** The formula of force now covers suffering, rising the third day, and the preaching of repentance and remission among the nations. The earliest formula's “according to the scriptures,” already established in Chapter 46 as the claim's own clause, here sounds in the Lord's own mouth as “thus it is written” over the same hinge.

Third, **it behoved Christ**. Necessity is again the writings' force, not a later tragic reading of a political failure.

Fourth, **the nations**. The writings, in His opening, already contain the preaching to the nations. Confirmation of received Scripture is not a retreat into a merely ethnic library. He reads the writings as having that reach.

Luke 24:44–47 is therefore the chapter's most explicit single unit: the body named (Law, Prophets, Psalms); the subject named (concerning Me); the formula named (thus it is written); the hinge named (suffer and rise the third day); the mission named (repentance and remission among the nations). Confirmation of received Scripture, after the raising, is this unit.

# 48.32 The Raised Lord Opens What Is Written

The opener of the writings is the same Person whose seers Chapter 47 examined.

The fact has evidential force inside Movement VI without yet being Movement VIII. The Truth, raised, attested by positioned witnesses, treats the received writings as the standing account of what had to occur, and He opens those writings as the sense of His own career. The writings and the Person interpret each other in the record's own argument: the writings concern Him; He confirms the writings. Chapter 44 already recorded, without completing this file, that the writings testify of Him. This chapter completes the Movement VI line: He, for His part, confirms them.

Opening is not abolition. Opening is not replacement. Opening is not yet the later claim that the Graphē is the primary written authority for all subsequent doctrine. Opening is the risen Lord's act of making the received writings understood as concerning Himself. The authentication line is that act, performed by this Person, upon these writings, in the hearing of the seers.

Luke 16:31, spoken before the passion in the parable of the rich man and Lazarus, already joined the writings to the raising as a moral architecture:

> **“If they hear not Moses and the prophets, neither will they be persuaded, though one rose from the dead.”**

The sentence is a parable's conclusion, not a narrative of Easter. Its architecture is nevertheless the same architecture Luke 24 then exhibits in history: Moses and the prophets are to be heard; a raising does not replace that hearing. Confirmation of received Scripture is here placed, by Jesus Himself, as the hearing without which even resurrection does not persuade. Chapter 49 will examine the apostolic witness of the raising. It will not be entitled to treat that witness as a substitute for Moses and the prophets. The Lord has already refused the substitute.

# 48.33 “According to the Scriptures” Now Attached to His Own Confirmation

1 Corinthians 15:3–4 recites, as of first importance, that Christ died for our sins according to the scriptures, and that He rose again the third day according to the scriptures. Chapter 46 recorded the clause as the formula's own claim of correspondence and left this file in its place.

The clause is now attached to His own practice.

The attachment is an inference, and is marked as an inference. Paul does not, in 1 Corinthians 15:3–4, quote Luke 24. The inference is that the earliest formula's “according to the scriptures” and the raised Lord's “thus it is written” over suffering and rising the third day are the same architecture: the hinge corresponds to what was written, and the Lord of the hinge is the one who says so.

The inference is warranted by correspondence of content, not by a shared sentence. Content: death, third day, writings. Speaker in Luke: the raised Jesus. Speaker in Paul: the received tradition of the church of the 50s. The tradition is older than the letter. The Gospel scene is the narrative form of the same claim that the writings already contained the hinge. Confirmation of Scripture is therefore not a late overlay upon a hinge that was first preached without writings. The hinge, as first preached, is already “according to the scriptures,” and the Lord of the hinge, as Luke writes Him, already says “thus it is written.”

Acts 2 will argue from Psalm 16 and Psalm 110. That argument is apostolic preaching, the neighbourhood of Chapters 49–50. It is noted here only as continuity: the seers who heard Him open the writings then preach from the writings. Continuity is not yet the finished doctrine of their authority.

# 48.34 2 Peter 1:19 as the Letter's Turn, Not Inspiration-Doctrine

2 Peter 1:16–18 has been examined as eyewitness of majesty. The next sentences turn:

The prophetic word is not being discarded because the mountain was seen. It is being named as that to which the readers should take heed. The seers' Lord and the prophetic word are not rivals. The letter's movement is the same movement as Luke 24's neighbourhood: seeing does not replace writing.

Verses 20–21 then speak of prophecy's origin: no prophecy of the scripture is of any private interpretation; holy men of God spake as they were moved by the Holy Ghost. Chapter 30 already examined inspiration as a doctrine of production. This chapter does not re-try that doctrine. The central witness's seers do not treat their seeing as the abolition of Scripture. They treat Scripture as the word to which the church should take heed.

That is the neighbourhood of confirmation. It is not yet Chapter 49's office. It is not yet Movement VIII's gate.

# 48.35 How This Relates to Movement V and Chapters 43–47 Without Circularity

The circularity to be refused is this:

> **Scripture is authoritative because Jesus confirms it; Jesus is known to be the Truth only because Scripture is already the primary written authority.**

That circle would steal Movement VIII and empty Movement V.

The actual sequence is this.

Movement V warranted the biblical writings as authentic written testimony of the claims under investigation. That warrant was cumulative: historical testimony, eyewitness as a category, manifestation, prophecy, unity, external corroboration, manuscripts, convergence, and explicit reasoning from evidence to warrant. It was not the Graphē-gate.

Chapters 43–47 then used that testimony to locate the Person, identify Him as the Truth, examine the claims-and-works conjunction, examine the hinge, and examine the seers. Those operations did not assume that the Graphē is primary written authority. They assumed that the writings may be examined as authentic testimony.

This chapter uses the same testimony to examine how that Person treated the writings already in force. The confirmation is a further authentication line because of who He has been shown to be inside that testimony. The Truth's confirmation of the received writings bears upon those writings as confirmed by Him. The bearing is real. It is not yet the later conclusion that the Graphē is the primary written authority by which all else is tested. That later conclusion will require canon, inspiration's guarantees, infallibility, John 10's why, and John 17:17. Sequence continues. Circularity is refused.

The writings therefore function in this chapter as the authentic testimony of His practice, and His practice, so testified, confirms the writings He received as binding written word. Two functions, one corpus, no stolen gate.

# 48.36 Competing Claim: Optional Illustration

The reduction says:

> **Jesus quoted the Jewish Scriptures as a teacher quotes familiar lines. The citations are illustration, atmosphere, or rhetorical colour. They are not confirmation of binding written word.**

The reduction fails for specified reasons.

First, **the formula is adjudicative**. “It is written” in the wilderness refuses proposed acts. “Have ye not read” charges error. “How then shall the scriptures be fulfilled” names necessity. Illustration does not ordinarily generate necessity, rebuke, and refusal.

Second, **the hearers are held responsible for reading**. A decorative citation does not produce “ye do err, not knowing the scriptures.” Error is explained as ignorance of writings that were there to be known.

Third, **He ranks His words after Moses' writings in John 5:47**. A man who treats quotations as colour does not say that unbelief toward Moses' writings explains unbelief toward His own words.

Fourth, **the raised Lord begins at Moses and all the prophets**. Easter is not used as a replacement for the writings. It is used as the occasion of opening them. Illustration that has become the sense of the career, the necessity of the passion, and the content of post-resurrection instruction is no longer illustration. It is confirmation.

Fifth, **the tempter's citation of Psalm 91 is refused by another writing**. The scene is aware of citation as a possible abuse. Confirmation is the right use, not the mere presence of a quoted clause.

Familiarity of the writings in a Jewish setting is true and is already a premise of reception. Familiarity does not convert adjudicative appeal into décor. The reduction fails.

# 48.37 Competing Claim: Creative Displacement of Binding Text

The reduction says:

> **Jesus used the Scriptures as raw material for a new meaning He created. The text is a springboard. Binding force belongs to His creativity, not to what is written.**

The reduction fails for specified reasons.

First, **He refuses to destroy the Law or the Prophets**. A springboard that abolishes the board is the thing He tells them not to think.

Second, **jot and tittle** are the opposite of a springboard. Smallest marks of writing are named as that which will not pass until all is fulfilled.

Third, **the Sadducean argument turns on written wording** — “I am,” the bush, the book of Moses. A springboard does not need the tense of Exodus 3.

Fourth, **“it is written again”** judges a use of Scripture by Scripture. Creativity that has no binding text cannot be judged by another text. The wilderness scene assumes that writings may correct a citation.

Fifth, **John 5:39–47** treats the writings as witnesses whose subject is already Himself, not as blank pages awaiting a new author. Search the Scriptures: they testify of Me. The testimony is the writings' own, in His claim, not a meaning He is licensed to invent against them.

Interpretation is real. He expounds. He applies. He claims fulfillment. Interpretation as exposition of what is written is confirmation's third act. Interpretation as displacement of what is written is the reduction. The record exhibits the former and, in Matthew 5:17, names the latter as the misunderstanding to be refused. The reduction fails.

# 48.38 Competing Claim: Confirmation Equals Canon List

The reduction says:

> **If He confirmed Scripture, He issued a canon. If He issued no catalogue of titles, He confirmed nothing. The file is either Movement VII already finished, or it is empty.**

The reduction fails as a false dichotomy.

Confirmation is authoritative treatment of writings in use. Canon is the later question of which writings constitute the recognized authoritative corpus. A man may live under Moses, the prophets, and the psalms, quote them as “it is written,” and open them after His raising, without enumerating Esther, without adjudicating later Christian disputes, and without performing the recognition-history of councils.

Luke 24:44 is a grouping, not a synod. Grouping Law, Prophets, and Psalms confirms a received body under names His hearers knew. Silence about a title is not, by itself, a later canonical decision. Citation of a title is not, by itself, a later canonical decision. Movement VII remains the proper file of corpus and recognition.

The research tradition notes that certain later writings commonly called apocryphal were not recognized by the Jews as Scripture and were not quoted by Christ and the apostles as such. The observation may be recorded as a historical claim relevant to reception: His citations, in the record we have, fall inside the writings Israel already treated as Scripture. The observation may not be converted, in this chapter, into a closed list or into the whole argument of canonical recognition. That conversion would be Movement VII performed by a footnote.

The dichotomy therefore fails on both horns. Confirmation is not empty. Confirmation is not a synod. The reduction fails.

# 48.39 Competing Claim: Confirmation Equals Infallibility or Movement VIII

The reduction says:

> **To say that He confirmed Scripture is already to say that Scripture cannot be broken, that the Word is truth, and that the Graphē is the primary written authority. Anything less is hesitation.**

The reduction fails by collapsing levels the TOC exists to keep distinct.

Confirmation of received Scripture is this Person's practice of treating the writings He received as binding written word. That practice is already a central authentication line of Movement VI, stated here with full force. Full force at this level is not the later why of John 10, not the later identification of Word and truth in John 17:17, and not the later conclusion that the Graphē is the primary written authority.

John 10:34–36 is an instance of the practice and a later dedicated argument. The instance may be named. The dedicated argument may not be stolen. “Cannot be broken” is a verdict that must rest on what Scripture is, as the locked guidance for Chapters 62–63 requires, not merely on the fact of quotation. This chapter supplies the practice. Movement VIII will supply the why.

Infallibility is a conclusion from God, revelation, inspiration, authentication, and corpus. Several of those premises are in hand. Corpus as recognized canon is not. The Graphē-gate is not. Pronouncing infallibility here would be a stolen later chapter, not a braver present one.

Sequence is not hesitation. The present line is in force. The later verdicts remain later because they are different questions. The reduction fails.

# 48.40 Competing Claim: His Authority Replaces Scripture

The reduction says:

> **“But I say unto you” replaces “it is written.” The Son's authority makes the prior writings obsolete. Confirmation is the wrong name for a career of supersession.**

The reduction fails for specified reasons.

First, **Matthew 5:17 is the prohibition of that thought**. Think not that I am come to destroy. The antitheses follow the prohibition. They may not be read as if the prohibition had not been spoken.

Second, **John 5:47 ranks His words after Moses' writings in the order of belief**. If ye believe not his writings, how shall ye believe my words? Replacement would invert the charge.

Third, **Luke 16:31** places hearing Moses and the prophets as the hearing without which a raising will not persuade. The Son who will Himself rise has already refused the logic that a raising replaces Moses.

Fourth, **the temptation answers** are the Son's authority exercised as submission to what is written, not as exemption from it. Sonship is the tempter's premise. “It is written” is the Son's answer.

Fifth, **the raised Lord opens the writings rather than discarding them**. Easter is the occasion of exposition, not of archive.

Chapter 45 already established that He speaks with distinctive authority. That claim-class remains. This chapter adds that the same Person confirms the writings. Authority to fulfill is not authority to abolish. The two classes meet in one career. They do not cancel. The reduction fails.

# 48.41 Competing Claim: Later Church Retrojection of Citations

The reduction says:

> **A historical teacher existed. The church, needing to bind Him to Israel's Scriptures, invented the citations, the synagogue reading, the Emmaus exposition, and “thus it is written.” Confirmation is a literary product of decades.**

The reduction fails for specified reasons.

First, **Chapter 43 already destroyed the vacuum and the coreless legend**. A located Jewish man, a short public career, crucifixion under Pilate, a movement in the 50s — these remain. Retrojection upon a core must then explain the content, the date, and the form of the confirmation-claims.

Second, **the earliest formula already contains “according to the scriptures”** over death and raising, delivered at Corinth's founding, written in the 50s, originating in the first years after the crucifixion, as Chapter 46 established. The hinge as first preached is already a writings-hinge. Retrojection that needs decades to attach Jesus to Moses has less time than it wants.

Third, **the form is not the ordinary form of a smooth apologetic invention**. Nazareth takes offence and attempts violence. Gethsemane includes a sword, a wound, and a rebuke. Emmaus includes fools and slow hearts. The Sadducees are answered from a text whose inference is not the easiest popular proof of resurrection. Invention that wishes to make Jesus the obvious darling of all Scripture-readers has chosen friction.

Fourth, **multiple streams carry the practice**. Synoptic temptation; Synoptic “have ye not read”; Lukan synagogue and Emmaus and upper room; Johannine “the scriptures testify of me” and “Moses wrote of me”; the Pauline formula. Independent idiom with the same species is the same evidential fact earlier chapters used of identity and of seeing. Retrojection must then be multiple, early, and coincident.

Fifth, **the opponents share the court of appeal**. Pharisees, Sadducees, and scribes are not being introduced to the writings by Him. He asks whether they have not read. A church inventing a Jesus who quotes Scripture in a vacuum might still do so. A church inventing controversies whose force depends on a shared Jewish court of appeal is inventing the obvious historical setting Chapter 43 already established. The setting makes confirmation the expected act of a Jewish teacher who is also more than a teacher. It does not make the act a late fiction.

The community remembered, preached, and wrote. Memory is not the same act as the creation, within a generation, of a Lord who never treated Moses as binding. The reduction fails as an explanation of the first-century conjunction.

# 48.42 Reason Through the Evidence

The reasoning may now be stated so that it can be inspected.

**Step 1.** The biblical writings are warranted as authentic written testimony of the claims under investigation (Movement V). The Person exists, was crucified under Pilate, is identified as the Truth, is authenticated in the ministry's claims-and-works conjunction, is proclaimed as raised on the third day, and is attested by positioned witnesses of what was seen and heard (Chapters 43–47). Those are earned premises.

**Step 2.** Confirmation of Scripture, in this argument, is this Person's treatment of already-written Holy Writ as binding written word: received, quoted, interpreted, and appealed to as authoritative. It is not optional illustration. It is not a canon list. It is not the infallibility verdict. It is not yet Movement VIII's finished Graphē-doctrine. It is not yet apostolic office.

**Step 3.** The body He names is the Law of Moses, the prophets, and the psalms. The language He uses is standing writing, Scripture as writing, reading, and fulfilling. The formula of force is “it is written,” with “have ye not read” as its sister-charge.

**Step 4.** Representative scenes exhibit the practice in context. Temptation is answered by Deuteronomy as standing writing. Controversies are adjudicated by “have ye not read,” including Genesis on marriage and Exodus 3 on the living God. Named figures of the earlier writings are treated as belonging to the real past. Tradition is ranked under the commandment of God in writing. The synagogue of Nazareth is a public reading of Isaiah claimed as fulfilled this day. Matthew 5:17–18 refuses destruction of the Law and the Prophets and names jot and tittle. John 5:39–47 names the Scriptures as witnesses concerning Him and Moses as a writer whose writings must be believed if His words are to be believed. The passion is named as the necessity of what is written. The raised Lord rebukes slowness to believe the prophets, begins at Moses and all the prophets, and opens Law, Prophets, and Psalms concerning Himself: thus it is written, thus it behoved Christ to suffer and to rise the third day.

**Step 5.** The earliest formula's “according to the scriptures” corresponds to that opening. 2 Peter 1:19 turns from eyewitness of majesty to the prophetic word as a light to which the readers should take heed. Seeing does not replace writing.

**Step 6.** The live reductions fail as global accounts: illustration; creative displacement; confirmation as canon list or as stolen Movement VIII; replacement of Scripture by His authority; later retrojection as global origin of the practice.

**Step 7.** Therefore the investigator is warranted, with full force, in concluding that the authentic testimony presents Jesus Christ as confirming the Scriptures He received as binding written word, and that this presentation is a central authentication line of Movement VI, the next established operation after the seers.

The form of reasoning is the same form the book has used: abductive historical and textual warrant. The writings say this. They are authentic testimony. The Person exists, has been identified, authenticated, examined at the hinge, and attested by seers. His practice toward the received writings is specified, public, adjudicative, and continued after the raising. The reductions fail. The authentication line follows. It is not Euclidean demonstration. It is not impression. It is the exhibited bearing of the record upon the proposition.

# 48.44 The Warranted Conclusion Stated with Full Force

# 48.47 From Confirmation of Scripture to Christ and the Apostolic Witness

The next necessary question is not a new curiosity. It is the question created by the confirmation now in force.

He has confirmed the writings. He has also appointed witnesses. Luke 24 places both acts in one room: the Scriptures opened; “ye are witnesses of these things.” John 5 placed the writings among a larger architecture of witness: John, the works, the Father, the Scriptures. The seers of Chapter 47 are not being asked to replace the writings. They are being asked to bear witness of the One who confirmed the writings and who was raised according to them.

The question is therefore:

> **What is Christ's relationship to the apostles, and what is the authority of their testimony concerning Him, now that He has been located, identified, authenticated, examined at the hinge, attested by seers, and shown to confirm the Scriptures He received?**

If the apostles stood alone as a later institution, Movement VI would end in office without the Lord's sending. If His confirmation of Scripture stood as the last word, Movement VI would end before the witnesses He named as witnesses. The remaining files are the remaining files: Christ and the apostolic witness; then the authority of the apostolic testimony.

That question is not a retreat from the writings. It is the writings' Lord examined in His relation to those He sends. The investigation therefore moves from:

> **It is written; have ye not read; thus it is written concerning Me.**

to:

> **Whom did He appoint as witnesses, and with what authority do they testify of Him?**

That is the proper open of the next chapter of Movement VI. The confirmation line was never meant to be the last word of the movement.
