# ONE SOURCE OF TRUTH

## The Doctrine of the Graphē

# Chapter 66 — Authority of Scripture Versus Authority of Interpretation

> **Movement IX — When Authoritative Truth Is Mishandled**

> **Central objective:** Distinguish the authority of the text from the fallibility of the interpreter — the Graphē’s standing as primary written authority from a reader’s or community’s claim about what the text means — so that an authoritative truthful writing is not converted into a grant that every interpretation of it is authoritative, and so that the fallibility of readers is not converted into a grant that all readings are equal. # 66.3 Define the Question: Two Authorities That Must Not Be Collapsed

**Authority of Scripture versus authority of interpretation**, in this chapter, is the dedicated account of two objects that neighbourhood invites us to treat as one: the Graphē’s standing as primary written authority, already earned, and the standing a reader or community claims for a statement of what that writing means.

The phrase is easily abused. In ordinary speech “authority of interpretation” may mean that interpretation is illegitimate, that every reading is as binding as the text, that no reading can be known to be correct, that an office is a second Graphē, that a community’s consensus is the writing’s living voice, that this chapter is already a catalog of errors, or that this chapter is already the labour of rightly dividing. None of those senses is this chapter’s object.

Four elements belong to the definition.

First, **two objects**. The writing is one object. The later act of stating what the writing means is another. Collapse of the two is the error this chapter exists to refuse. Separation of the two into a despair that no meaning can be reached is the opposite error this chapter also exists to refuse.

Second, **authority as standing, used of each object in its own place**. “Authority of Scripture” names the Graphē’s already-earned standing as primary written measure. “Authority of interpretation” names the standing claimed for a later statement of sense — claimed by a reader, a teacher, an office, a school, a tradition, or a community. The second standing is not, by being claimed, the first standing.

Third, **fallibility of the interpreter as compatible with infallibility of the record**. The reader may err. The record does not fail as what it actually communicates. Compatibility is the doctrine. Transfer of non-failure from record to reader is not the doctrine. Despair of all reading because readers can err is not the doctrine.

Fourth, **not those other claims**. The definition excludes, as not this object: a retry of Chapter 65; a rewrite of Chapter 57 or Chapter 58; a catalog of specific misreadings; a treatment of theology, philosophy, tradition, and institution as systems; a finished account of secondary sources becoming primary; tergiversation, accepted heresies, aridity, and *apeiros* as finished files; Movement X’s labour-files; the First Principles as this file’s achievement. Those exclusions are part of the definition. They are not a later apology.

Thus:

> **The question of this chapter is not why the Graphē is the primary written authority, why the record does not fail, or how later readers commit the several species of misreading. Those questions have been examined or remain later. The question is how the authority of the text is to be distinguished from the fallibility of the interpreter in the sense just defined.**

# 66.7 Define “Authority of Scripture” at This Stage

**Authority of Scripture**, in this argument, is the Graphē’s already-earned standing as the primary written authority: standing to govern judgment as God’s identified, authenticated, inspired, unbreakable, truthful writing — the decisive written measure among written authorities for knowing, testing, reasoning through, teaching, discerning, and living truth.

Chapter 65 already defined that standing and drew it as Movement VIII’s last word. The present file does not invent a second sense of “authority of Scripture.” It uses the gate as the first of two objects now distinguished.

Four notes belong to the definition at this stage.

First, **standing of the writing, not standing of the later claim**. A writing does not become authoritative by attracting interpreters. Interpreters become answerable because the writing already stands. Chapter 65 already said this as a note of the gate. The present file uses it as the first object’s location: the seat of this authority is the Graphē, not the reader.

Second, **already earned, not retried**. The warrant for this standing remains the conjunction already drawn: God who cannot lie; revelation given and authenticated; God-breathed writing; corpus, recognition, and standing as written Word; Christ’s appeal to what is written as court; unloosenability because of what it is; the Father’s word identified as truth. The present file cites that conjunction. It does not re-prove it.

Third, **attached to what the writing actually communicates**. Chapter 56 required speaker-distinction. Chapter 57 attached non-failure to the record of what it actually communicates. Authority of Scripture is authority of that communication, not a licence to treat every later inference as if it were the communication.

Fourth, **the measure of later speech, including later speech about itself**. Isaiah 8:20 already sent a people to the law and to the testimony. The Bereans already searched the scriptures whether those things were so. The writing that is primary written authority is the court of claims, including claims that say “this is what the writing means.”

Thus:

> **Authority of Scripture, at this stage, is the Graphē’s standing as primary written measure, already earned, used here as the first of two objects. Prestige is not the definition. The reader is not the seat. The later claim is not the writing.**

# 66.8 Define “Interpretation” at This Stage

**Interpretation**, in this argument, is the later act of stating what a writing means: the reader’s or community’s account of the sense, scope, relations, and force of what has been written.

The word is easily abused. In ordinary speech “interpretation” may mean a licence to replace the text, a confession that no meaning can be reached, a professional guild’s property, a spiritualizing that abandons what the writing communicates, or a ban on all stating of sense. None of those senses is this chapter’s object.

Four notes belong to the definition at this stage.

First, **an act, not the writing**. The writing is what is written. Interpretation is what is later said about what is written. Nehemiah 8:8 records both in one scene: they read in the book in the law of God distinctly, and gave the sense, and caused them to understand the reading. Reading the book is one operation. Giving the sense is another. The second is not, by existing, a crime. It is an act whose object is the first.

Second, **accountable, not sovereign**. To state sense is to make a claim that can be true or false of the writing. Accountability is the shape of the act. Sovereignty over the writing is not the shape of the act. Understanding is not authority over the text — the locked core distinction, now used as the definition’s second note.

Third, **possible, not automatic**. If the writing actually communicates, it can be understood. Chapter 57 already refused despair on that ground: infallibility of the record is a reason to labour at understanding, not a reason to despair of understanding. The present definition inherits that refusal. Interpretation is possible because the writing communicates. It is not automatic because the reader is a reader.

Fourth, **not those later files**. Defining the act is not cataloguing the ways the act fails. It is not examining systems that replace the act’s object. It is not examining *spoudazō* as the diligence the act requires. Those remain later.

The biblical vocabulary that neighbours the act may be named without becoming a lexicon-file. ἑρμηνεύω (*hermēneuō*, G2059) and its compounds appear where speech or writing is rendered or explained. νοέω and γινώσκω appear where understanding and knowing are in view. ἐπίλυσις (*epilusis*, G1955) appears in 2 Peter 1:20 in a production-context already controlled as “private unloosing” of prophetic origin, not as a ban on readers stating sense. Those terms illuminate. They do not replace the definition: interpretation is the later act of stating what the writing means.

Thus:

> **Interpretation, at this stage, is the accountable act of stating the writing’s sense. It is not the writing. It is not a licence. It is not a despair. It is not yet the catalog of its failures.**

# 66.9 Define “Authority of Interpretation” at This Stage

**Authority of interpretation**, in this argument, is the standing claimed for that later act: the claim that a particular statement of sense binds as the writing binds, or binds instead of the writing, or binds as a court the writing must now obey.

The phrase does not name a second Graphē that the investigation has discovered. It names a claim that later speakers make. The claim may be tacit: “Scripture is authoritative; therefore my reading is.” The claim may be official: an office, school, or community presents its sense as the writing’s living voice. The claim may be despairing in form and still a claim of authority: “all readings are equal, therefore no writing can judge my reading.” In each form, a later statement of sense is being granted a standing that belongs to the writing, or a standing that evacuates the writing as measure.

Three clarifications belong to the word at this stage.

First, **claimed standing, not earned standing of the Graphē**. Chapter 65 earned the writing’s standing. The present definition does not earn a parallel standing for interpreters. It names the standing interpreters claim. Naming a claim is not granting the claim.

Second, **not the denial that a correct reading has force as a correct reading**. A statement that states what the writing actually communicates has the force of a true account of the writing. That force is derivative. It is the force of correspondence to the measure, not the force of becoming the measure. A true map does not become the territory. A true reading does not become the Graphē.

Third, **not already Chapters 68–69**. An office, a tradition, a commentary, or a community may be the speaker who claims this standing. The present definition names the claim. The later files examine those speakers as systems and as displacements. Neighbourhood is not arrival.

Thus:

> **Authority of interpretation, at this stage, is the standing claimed for a later statement of sense. The claim is not the Graphē’s standing. A correct reading’s derivative force is not a second Graphē. The later system-files remain later.**

# 66.10 Why the Two Must Remain Distinct

The two objects must remain distinct because they are not the same thing, and because collapsing them destroys the gate just drawn.

They are not the same thing. The Graphē is God’s identified, authenticated, inspired, unbreakable, truthful writing. A later statement of sense is a human account of that writing. Chapter 14 already distinguished God’s Word from human accounts about God. Chapter 57 already distinguished the record from interpretation. Chapter 65 already refused to confer primary written standing upon every later claim about the text. The present file states the reason those distinctions were required: an authoritative truthful text does not make every interpretation of it authoritative.

They must remain distinct because the gate would otherwise become a blank check. If the writing’s standing migrated automatically into every later claim made in its name, then “primary written authority” would name the last speaker who quoted the writing, not the writing. Isaiah 8:20 would send a people to the last quotation rather than to the law and the testimony. The Bereans would be ignoble for testing a sermon by the writings. Galatians 1:8–9 would be unintelligible: another gospel would be as authoritative as the gospel already received, provided it quoted the writings. The record does not say those things.

They must remain distinct because the later investigation of mishandling would otherwise have no object. If text and interpretation were one, there would be no such thing as a failed reading of an authoritative text. There would be only rival texts. Chapter 67 would have nothing to examine except a quarrel of equally primary writings. The locked assignment of that later chapter presupposes the distinction this chapter draws.

They must remain distinct because knowledge of the writing would otherwise be denied. If distinction meant that no later statement can correspond to the writing, the writing would have failed as communication. Chapter 57 already refused that despair. The present file refuses it as a misuse of the distinction. Distinction preserves both objects. Collapse spends one of them.

Thus:

> **The two must remain distinct because they are two, because the gate is a gate of the writing rather than a blank check for later speech, because mishandling is otherwise unintelligible, and because communication is otherwise denied. Distinction is the doctrine. Collapse is the error.**

# 66.11 The Transfer Fallacy Named

The first live failure is **transfer**.

Transfer says:

> **The Graphē is authoritative; therefore my reading is authoritative. The record does not fail; therefore I do not fail when I handle the record. The Father’s word is truth; therefore my sentence about that word is truth.**

The inference is invalid. Authority of the measure is not authority of every measurement. Chapter 57 already named the analogue: an infallible measuring-line does not make every measurement correct; it makes incorrect measurement detectable in principle. Detectability is not automatic correctness. The present file names the same inference as the first live failure of Movement IX’s opening.

Transfer may be personal: a private reader baptizes a private reading. Transfer may be official: an office baptizes an official reading. Transfer may be communal: a people baptizes a received reading. The form does not rescue the inference. In each form, the writing’s standing is being treated as a property that migrates into the later speaker by the act of speaking about the writing.

The writings refuse the migration. “Ye do err, not knowing the scriptures” is intelligible only if possessors of the writings can err. “Which they that are unlearned and unstable wrest, as they do also the other scriptures” is intelligible only if writings that already stand can be handled so as to destroy the handler. “Let God be true, but every man a liar” is intelligible only if divine standing and human speech remain two. Those sentences will be examined as biblical record below. They are named here as the reason transfer is a fallacy and not a piety.

Transfer often presents itself as a defence of Scripture. The defence is unnecessary. The writing is not under attack when the reader is denied the writing’s standing. The writing is under attack when the reader is granted the writing’s standing, because the measure has then been replaced by the last measurement.

Thus:

> **Transfer fails. The writing’s standing does not migrate into the later speaker by the act of speaking about the writing. Detectability of error is not automatic correctness.**

# 66.12 The Despair Fallacy Named

The second live failure is **despair**.

Despair says:

> **Readers err; therefore all readings are equal. The interpreter is fallible; therefore the writing cannot function as a measure. Distinction of text from interpretation is the abolition of knowledge.**

The inference is invalid. Fallibility of the reader is a fact about the reader. It is not a fact about the writing. Chapter 57 already refused the corresponding despair: a record that does not fail is the reason labour is worth performing; if the record failed as record, labour would have no stable object. The present file names the same inference as the second live failure of Movement IX’s opening.

Despair may be sceptical: no reading can be known to correspond. Despair may be pluralist: every reading is as good as every other because each is a reading. Despair may be pious in form: “we are only interpreters,” offered as a reason that the writing may no longer judge a reading. The form does not rescue the inference. In each form, the reader’s fallibility is being treated as a property that migrates into the writing, or as a property that abolishes the writing as court.

The writings refuse the migration. They command searching. They command hearing Moses and the prophets. They command not adding and not diminishing. They exhibit giving the sense as a real act. They exhibit opening of understanding as a real gift toward writings that already stand. They exhibit wresting as a real crime against writings that already stand. Those sentences are unintelligible if all readings are equal. A crime against a writing presupposes that the writing has a sense that can be violated. A gift of understanding presupposes that the writing has a sense that can be understood.

Despair often presents itself as a defence of humility. The defence is unnecessary. Humility is not under attack when a reading is denied equality with every other reading. Humility is under attack when the writing is denied the power to judge readings, because the reader has then been made safe from the measure.

Thus:

> **Despair fails. Fallibility of the reader does not migrate into the writing, and it does not make all readings equal. A writing that communicates can be understood, and a reading can be judged by that writing.**

# 66.13 Fallibility of the Reader Compatible with Infallibility of the Record

The two live failures are refused by one compatibility.

The record does not fail as truthful divine writing of what it actually communicates (Chapter 57, used not retried). The reader who handles that record does not become incapable of error by the act of reading (Chapter 57’s limit, now the dedicated object). Those two sentences are compatible. They are not rivals.

Compatibility is required by the objects. Infallibility, in the sense already controlled, attaches to the inspired writing as produced under the divine operation. Reading is not that operation. The reader is not θεόπνευστος (*theopneustos*, G2315) by opening the book. Skill, diligence, context, grammar, cross-reference, and sound judgment remain the reader’s labour. Those operations will occupy later movements. They are named here only to locate the compatibility: the writing’s non-failure is not the reader’s non-failure, and the reader’s failure is not the writing’s failure.

Compatibility is required by the record’s own warnings. If the reader were infallible by reading, “ye do err, not knowing the scriptures” would be impossible. If the record failed as record whenever a reader failed as reader, wresting would unmake standing. Chapter 52 already established that wresting does not unmake standing. The present file uses that conclusion: the letters remain Scripture while being wrested; the law remains the law while being unknown; the writing remains the measure while being mishandled.

Compatibility is required by the gate. If the writing is the primary written authority, it remains the measure when the reader fails. If the reader’s failure unmade the writing’s standing, the gate would be a function of the last successful reading. The gate is not that function. The gate is the writing’s rank. Rank persists through failed use. Chapter 65 already said that the Bereans’ use may fail and that the standing does not fail with the use. The present file uses that sentence as the compatibility’s already-earned form.

What compatibility does not do is evacuate knowledge. The writing actually communicates. Communication can be received. A correct reading is possible because the object is a communicating writing, not a sealed charm. Possibility is not automatic success. Automatic success is transfer. Denial of possibility is despair. Compatibility refuses both.

Thus:

> **Fallibility of the reader is compatible with infallibility of the record. The writing’s non-failure is not the reader’s. The reader’s failure is not the writing’s. Knowledge remains possible. Transfer remains invalid. Despair remains invalid.**

# 66.14 Understanding Is Not Authority Over the Text

The locked core distinctions include a sentence this chapter must now use as doctrine:

> **Understanding ≠ authority over the text.**

Understanding is reception of what the writing communicates. Authority over the text would be standing to bind, alter, replace, or evacuate the writing as measure. Those are two objects. A reader may understand and remain under the writing. A reader may claim to understand and be claiming authority over the writing. The claim is not the understanding.

The distinction is forced by the gate. If understanding conferred authority over the text, the best reader would become the higher court. The Dedication controversy would then be unintelligible as court: the Truth would have appealed to what is written only until a later understanding replaced the writing as court. He appealed to the writing against hearers who already had understandings. Their understandings were not the court. The writing was the court.

The distinction is forced by the act of giving the sense. Nehemiah 8:8’s giving of the sense is toward understanding the reading. The sense is given so that the book is understood. The book is not given so that the sense-givers become the book. A true sense-giving succeeds as understanding. It does not succeed as replacement.

The distinction is forced by the later files without writing them. Rightly dividing, when it is examined, will be accurate handling of the word of truth, not authority to rewrite the word of truth. *Spoudazō*, when it is examined, will be diligence toward the writing, not diligence as a title to rule the writing. Those files remain later. The present point is already full: understanding, even when genuine, remains under the text it understands.

Thus:

> **Understanding is reception, not rule. A correct reading sits under the writing. It does not become the writing’s higher court by being correct.**

# 66.15 Quotation Is Not the Writing

The locked core distinctions include two further sentences this chapter must now use:

> **Quotation ≠ ignorance.**
>
> **Quotation ≠ complete understanding.**

Quotation of an authoritative text can convey genuine knowledge. Chapter 77 will examine that proposition as a dedicated file. The present file requires only what the distinction of authorities requires: to quote the writing is not to be the writing, and it is not, by itself, to have stated the writing’s sense correctly.

Quotation is not ignorance. A man may know the words and know their sense. The Sadducees in Matthew 22 are not rebuked for quoting. They are rebuked for not knowing the scriptures they handle. Possession of words without knowledge of their sense is one failure. Absence of words is another. The first failure is not cured by further quotation alone.

Quotation is not complete understanding. A man may cite a sentence and miss its relations, its speaker, its scope, or its force. 2 Peter 3:16 will be examined below as wresting of writings that include things hard to be understood. Hardness is not unintelligibility. It is a feature of the writings that makes quotation without understanding a live danger.

Quotation is not the writing. The cited words, in the writing, are the writing’s words. The same words, in a later mouth, are a later use. The use may be faithful. The use may be wresting. Faithfulness is judged by the writing, not by the fact of citation. Chapter 65 already refused to treat every later claim about the text as interchangeable with the text. The present file uses that refusal: a slogan that uses the writing’s words is not, by using them, the writing.

Thus:

> **Quotation can convey knowledge. Quotation is not the Graphē. Quotation is not, by itself, understanding. The writing remains the measure of the citation.**

# 66.16 Community Claim Is Still a Claim

A later statement of sense may be spoken by one reader or by many. Number does not change the object.

A community may receive the writings. Reception is not interpretation’s becoming the writing. Chapter 52 already established that canon is recognition of writings that already stand, not a later vote inventing authority. Chapter 57 already established that infallibility of Scripture is not infallibility of the church. The present file uses those conclusions: a people’s claim about what the writing means remains a claim about the writing. Consensus is not God-breathed character. Antiquity is not inspiration. Institutional location is not the seat of the gate.

The distinction is not contempt for the assembly. The writings themselves speak of teaching, of elders, of a people receiving the word, of a people searching the scriptures. Those operations remain real. They remain under the writing. A community that sits under the Graphē is a community in its proper rank. A community that sits as the Graphē’s living voice, such that the writing now obeys the community’s sense, has claimed the second object as if it were the first.

Unverifiable stories of a particular council inventing the Bible’s meaning as a library of official senses are not used here as premises. Chapter 52 already refused Nicaea-as-canon-origin as an unverified origin-story. The present file does not invent a replacement story about a council inventing interpretation. It uses the order already earned: the writing stands; later speech about the writing is later speech; number of speakers does not convert later speech into the writing.

Thus:

> **A community’s claim about the writing is still a claim about the writing. Reception is not replacement. Consensus is not the Graphē.**

# 66.17 Positive Warrant: The Gate Does Not Transfer

The first member of the positive warrant is the gate already drawn.

Chapter 65 established that the Graphē is the primary written authority by which truth is known, tested, reasoned through, taught, discerned, and lived. “Primary” named firstness among written measures. It did not name the infallibility of interpreters. It did not name interchangeability of later claims with the text. Those limits were the shape of the gate. They are now the first member of the distinction.

If the Graphē is the decisive written measure, later speech is measured. A statement of sense is later speech. Therefore a statement of sense is measured by the Graphē, not the reverse. The inference is direct. It does not require a catalog of misreadings. It does not require a system-file. It requires only the gate and the definition of interpretation as a later act.

The gate was drawn as conjunction: God who cannot lie; revelation given and authenticated; God-breathed writing; corpus and standing; Christ’s appeal to what is written as court; unloosenability because of what it is; the Father’s word identified as truth. That conjunction attaches to the writing. It does not attach, by the same warrant, to a later mouth. The later mouth is not the God who cannot lie. The later mouth is not the authenticated making-known. The later mouth is not θεόπνευστος as writing. The later mouth is not the corpus. The later mouth is not the court of John 10. The later mouth is not unloosenable because of what it is. The later mouth is not “thy word.” Transfer would require a second conjunction that the investigation has not drawn and that the writings refuse.

Thus:

> **The gate does not transfer. Primary written authority is the writing’s rank. A later claim about the writing is later speech under that rank.**

# 66.18 Positive Warrant: The Record Does Not Fail; the Reader May

The second member of the positive warrant is the compatibility already named from Chapter 57.

Because the God who cannot lie has made known, because all Scripture is God-breathed as writing, because inspiration’s guarantees are already in force, and because these writings have been authenticated and identified as the scriptural corpus, Scripture is an infallible record in the defined sense: it does not fail as truthful divine writing of what it actually communicates. That verdict attaches to the inspired writing, not to every later copy as autograph, not to the church as church, and not to the reader as reader.

The present file uses the last attachment as warrant for the distinction. If the verdict attached to the reader, Movement IX would have no object: mishandling would be impossible. If the verdict attached to no object but only to a pious mood, Movement VIII would have no gate. The verdict attaches to the writing. The reader remains a reader. Therefore authority of Scripture and authority of interpretation are two.

The second member also blocks despair. Non-failure of the record is why a reading can be judged. A failed reading is failed of a stable object. A correct reading is correct of a stable object. Stability of the object is the record’s non-failure. Instability of the reader is the reader’s fallibility. Both sentences stand.

Thus:

> **The record’s non-failure and the reader’s fallibility are the second member. They yield two objects, not one, and not none.**

# 66.19 Positive Warrant: God True, Every Man a Liar

The third member of the positive warrant is the ranking already in the writings and already used as shape in Chapter 58.

Romans 3:4: “Let God be true, but every man a liar.” The sentence will be examined below as biblical record. It is named here as warrant. Divine standing and human speech are two. The ranking is not cynicism about all human speech. It is a refusal to transfer divine standing to the human speaker as human speaker. The interpreter is a human speaker about the writing. His speech is tested by the writing. The writing is not tested by his office, his sincerity, his tradition, or his majority.

Chapters 10–12 already established why God is true: He is the ground of truth; He is truthful, faithful, and immutable; falsehood is incompatible with Him. Those conclusions remain premises. The present member does not re-prove them. It applies them to the two objects: the Graphē, as His writing, participates in that truthfulness as record of what it actually communicates; the interpreter, as man, does not participate in that truthfulness merely by interpreting.

The ranking is the reason transfer fails as theology and not only as logic. Transfer treats the interpreter as if the interpreter were, in the act of interpreting, the God who cannot lie. The writings do not grant that treatment. The ranking is also the reason despair fails as theology and not only as logic. Despair treats the writing as if the writing were, in the act of being read, every man a liar. The writings do not grant that treatment.

Thus:

> **God true, every man a liar, is the third member. The writing is received on the first side of that ranking. The interpreter remains on the second side as interpreter. The ranking is not contempt. It is the distinction’s source-side form.**

# 66.20 Positive Warrant: The Writing as Court of Later Speech

The fourth member of the positive warrant is the writing’s already-exhibited function as court.

Chapter 61 established that Christ reasons from what is written as the court of a capital dispute. Chapter 65 established that this court-function, joined to the preceding argument, yields primary written authority. The present file uses the court-function as warrant for the distinction: a court is not the counsel who appears before it. A later statement of sense appears before the writing. It does not become the bench by appearing.

The biblical record of measuring-function already exhibited in Chapter 65 — “it is written”; to the law and to the testimony; they have Moses and the prophets; search the scriptures; all Scripture profitable; received not as the word of men but as it is in truth the word of God; do not add and do not diminish — is used here, not retried as a second gate. Those sentences show later speech judged by the writing. They do not show the writing judged by later speech. The distinction is the grammar of that court.

If the interpreter’s claim were the court, John 10 would be His impression against theirs. He cited what is written. If the interpreter’s claim were the court, Isaiah 8 would send the people to the last official sense. It sends them to the law and the testimony. If the interpreter’s claim were the court, Galatians 1 would exempt the apostle from the deposit. It binds even an angel to the gospel already received.

Thus:

> **The writing as court is the fourth member. Later sense-giving appears before that court. It does not become that court by appearing, by quoting, or by gathering a people.**

# 66.21 Biblical Record: Romans 3:4

Romans 3:4 states the ranking the third member used:

> “Let God be true, but every man a liar; as it is written, That thou mightest be justified in thy sayings, and mightest overcome when thou art judged.”

The sentence stands in an argument about Jewish advantage, the oracles of God, and whether unbelief makes the faith of God without effect (Romans 3:1–8). Paul’s answer is that God remains true though every man be a liar. Human falsehood does not unmake divine truthfulness. The citation of Psalm 51:4 locates the ranking in earlier Scripture: God is justified in His words.

The present use is distinction, not a second file on justification, and not a retry of Chapters 10–12. The sentence exhibits two objects in one breath. God is true. Every man is a liar. The interpreter, as man, belongs to the second clause as interpreter. The Graphē, as His sayings written, belongs to the first clause as the writing of the God who is true. Transfer would rewrite the sentence: let God be true, and therefore the interpreter also. Despair would rewrite the sentence: every man is a liar, and therefore God’s sayings also. The text writes neither rewrite.

What the sentence does not do here is equally important. It does not teach that no human sentence can correspond to the truth. “Every man a liar” is ranking against transfer of divine standing, not a claim that correspondence is impossible. A true human confession remains possible. David’s own psalm is such a confession in the citation’s source. Possibility of correspondence is not migration of standing.

Thus:

> **Romans 3:4 warrants the ranking the distinction requires: God true; the interpreter, as man, not thereby true. Human falsehood does not unmake the writing. The writing’s truth does not migrate into the interpreter as interpreter.**

# 66.22 Biblical Record: Matthew 22:29 — Ye Do Err, Not Knowing the Scriptures

Matthew 22:29, with Mark 12:24, records the Truth’s verdict upon possessors of the writings:

> “Ye do err, not knowing the scriptures, nor the power of God.”

The occasion is the Sadducees’ question about the resurrection, using Moses’ writing about a brother’s wife (Matthew 22:23–28; cf. Deuteronomy 25:5–6). They have a text. They have a case built from a text. He does not say they err because they lack a book. He says they err, not knowing the scriptures, nor the power of God. He then cites Exodus 3:6 as written: “I am the God of Abraham, and the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob,” and reasons that God is not the God of the dead, but of the living (Matthew 22:31–32). Chapter 48 already used the passage as confirmation that He holds hearers responsible for reading. The present use is distinction.

Two objects are on the page. The scriptures are there to be known. The hearers err. Error is explained as ignorance of writings that were already in hand, and as ignorance of the power of God those writings present. Possession is not knowledge. Quotation of Moses in the question is not knowledge of Moses as the writings require. The writings remain the measure. The reading remains fallible.

The passage also refuses despair. He does not say the scriptures cannot be known. He says they do not know them, and He exhibits what knowing looks like: appeal to what was spoken to them by God, in a writing they already received, with the writing’s own force used as court. Error is detectable because the writings communicate. Detectability is not the Sadducees’ success. It is the writings’ standing against their use.

What the passage does not do here is become Chapter 67’s catalog. The Sadducean error about resurrection is a specimen that the distinction is live, not the finished species-file of misreading. Context failure, inference from one ordinance against the rest of the writings, and ignorance of God’s power will be examined as modes later. They are not examined as a catalog now.

Thus:

> **Matthew 22:29 warrants the distinction: writings already in hand can be unknown; hearers can err; the writings remain the court of the error. Possession is not knowledge. Error is not the writings’ failure.**

# 66.23 Biblical Record: 2 Peter 3:16 — Wresting the Scriptures

2 Peter 3:15–16 locates Paul’s letters with the other scriptures and names a handling that destroys the handler:

> “And account that the longsuffering of our Lord is salvation; even as our beloved brother Paul also according to the wisdom given unto him hath written unto you; as also in all his epistles, speaking in them of these things; in which are some things hard to be understood, which they that are unlearned and unstable wrest, as they do also the other scriptures, unto their own destruction.”

Chapters 51–52 already used the paragraph as category and recognition: the letters stand with the other scriptures; wresting does not unmake standing. The present use is distinction. Writings that already stand can be wrested. Wresting is a later act upon writings that have sense. The unlearned and unstable are readers. The writings are the object. Destruction is the readers’. The object remains.

Three features belong to the distinction.

First, **hardness is not unintelligibility**. “Some things hard to be understood” names a property of the writings, not a confession that the writings fail to communicate. Hardness makes labour necessary. It does not make all readings equal. Chapter 67 will examine deficient understanding. Chapter 73 will examine *apeiros*. Chapter 78 will examine *spoudazō*. Those files remain later. Hardness is named here only as evidence that interpretation is a real act with a real object, not as those later files performed.

Second, **wresting is interpretation claiming the text while violating it**. The verb’s force in this sentence is distortion of writings already classed as scripture. Distortion presupposes a sense that can be twisted. If all readings were equal, wresting would be only another reading. The text calls it wresting, and calls its end destruction. Equality of readings is not the record’s account.

Third, **the same act is done to the other scriptures**. Distortion is not a special fate of later letters. It is a live danger of Holy Writ as Holy Writ. The distinction is therefore not a New Testament novelty. It is the writings’ own account of later hands upon writings that stand.

What the paragraph does not do here is become Chapter 67’s catalog of methods, and it does not become Chapter 52 rewritten. Standing remains Chapter 52’s object. Species of mishandling remain Chapter 67’s object. The present object is two things on the page: scriptures that stand; readers who wrest.

Thus:

> **2 Peter 3:16 warrants the distinction: writings that stand can be wrested; wresting is the readers’ act and the readers’ destruction; hardness is not the abolition of sense; equality of readings is not the record’s account.**

# 66.25 Biblical Record: Nehemiah 8:8 — Reading and Giving the Sense

Nehemiah 8:8 records both objects in one public act:

> “So they read in the book in the law of God distinctly, and gave the sense, and caused them to understand the reading.”

The scene is the assembled people hearing the book of the law (Nehemiah 8:1–8). The book is read distinctly. Sense is given. Understanding of the reading is the aim. The present file does not invent unverifiable details of the platform, the crowd’s size, or a reconstructed office-history beyond what the record states. It takes the three verbs as they stand.

**They read in the book.** The object is the writing already in hand. Distinct reading is not replacement of the book. It is the book made audible as the book.

**They gave the sense.** Giving the sense is interpretation in the definition already controlled: the later act of stating what the writing means. The act is recorded as a proper act, not as a crime. The crime would be giving a sense that is not the book’s. The record does not say they became the book. It says they gave the sense of the reading.

**They caused them to understand the reading.** Understanding is reception of the reading. The reading remains the object understood. Understanding is not authority over the text. The people are not granted standing to rewrite the law by having understood it.

The scene therefore refuses both live failures in one paragraph. Transfer is refused: sense-givers remain sense-givers; the book remains the book. Despair is refused: sense can be given; understanding can be caused; the writing is not a sealed charm. Chapter 67 will examine how sense-giving fails. Chapter 84 will examine the discipline of interpretation. Those files remain later. The present force is the two objects exhibited as two, and as compatible.

Thus:

> **Nehemiah 8:8 warrants treating interpretation as a real accountable act toward a writing that remains the object. Giving the sense is not becoming the book. Understanding the reading is not ruling the reading.**

# 66.26 Biblical Record: Luke 24:25–27, 44–45 — Opening Understanding

Luke 24 records the risen Lord charging slowness and then opening understanding toward writings that already stood.

Verses 25–27:

Verses 44–45:

# 66.28 Biblical Record: 2 Peter 1:20 — Origin of Prophecy, Not a Ban on Reading

2 Peter 1:20–21 has already been controlled as production-doctrine:

The misuse fails the paragraph’s own next sentence. Verse 21 gives the reason: the prophecy came not by the will of man; men spoke as moved by the Holy Ghost. The “for” locates the claim in origin, not in a ban on later readers. ἐπίλυσις (*epilusis*, G1955), already handled in this manuscript as unloosing of prophetic origin, is the production-term, not a later prohibition of giving the sense. Private unloosing names how prophecy did not come. It does not name a later rule that the writings may not be understood.

The misuse would also contradict the letter’s own next movement. 2 Peter 3:16 names wresting of writings that include things hard to be understood. Hardness and wresting are unintelligible if stating sense is forbidden. Forbidden sense-giving would make wresting and faithful reading equally impossible. The letter does not write that impossibility.

The present file therefore holds two sentences together. Prophecy of Scripture is not of private unloosing as to origin (Chapters 30, 57, used not retried). Readers may and must state sense as later act, accountable to writings that already stand (Nehemiah 8:8; Acts 17:11; Luke 24:45). Those sentences are compatible. They are not rivals. Collapse of verse 20 into a ban on interpretation is transfer’s opposite error: not the reader becoming the writing, but the writing becoming unreadable in order to protect the writing. The writings do not require that protection.

Thus:

> **2 Peter 1:20 warrants origin-doctrine already earned: prophecy is not of private unloosing. It does not ban later accountable sense-giving. Production and interpretation remain two operations.**

# 66.32 Reconstructing the Aletheia Proposition: Graphē as Premiere Convention, Not Interpreter as Graphē

**What the claim means.** “Pillows in” names dependence and rest: the experience takes its measure from the writing. “Premiere convention” names the writing as first among conventions for hearing. Persons and organizations are not excluded from the measure by being large. They are not included in the writing’s standing by being large.

**Premises.** The Graphē is the primary written authority (Chapter 65). The record does not fail as what it actually communicates (Chapter 57). God is true; every man is a liar (Romans 3:4, used above). Human accounts about God are to be tested (Chapter 14).

**What does not follow here.** That *apeiros*, tergiversation, accepted heresies, and aridity have been examined as dedicated files. That *spoudazō* has been examined. # 66.42 Reason Through the Evidence

The inference can now be exhibited.

**Premise 1.** The Graphē is the primary written authority by which truth is known, tested, reasoned through, taught, discerned, and lived — the decisive written measure among written authorities (Chapter 65, used not retried).

**Premise 2.** Scripture, as inspired authenticated corpus, is an infallible record: it does not fail as truthful divine writing of what it actually communicates; that verdict attaches to the writing, not to the interpreter (Chapter 57, used not retried).

**Premise 3.** Authority of the written Word is not infallibility of interpreters; God true, every man a liar, remains the ranking (Chapter 58; Romans 3:4, used not retried as a second ontology-file).

**Premise 4.** Interpretation is the later act of stating what the writing means; that act is real, accountable, and not the writing (definition at this stage; Nehemiah 8:8; Luke 24:45).

**Premise 5.** The writings themselves exhibit later speech judged by the writing, and exhibit later speech failing while the writing stands: ye do err, not knowing the scriptures; wresting unto destruction; searching whether those things were so; searching without coming; not above that which is written; even an apostle or an angel judged by the deposit; the commandment laid aside by tradition (Matthew 22:29; 2 Peter 3:16; Acts 17:11; John 5:39–40; 1 Corinthians 4:6; Galatians 1:8–9; Mark 7).

**Premise 6.** Truth is not sincerity, not popularity, not institutional consensus; the thing described is not the description of the thing; later speech about the word is not the word (Chapters 1–3, 14, 65, used not retried).

**Inference.** Therefore the authority of Scripture and the authority of interpretation are two objects. The writing’s standing does not transfer automatically to a reader’s or community’s claim about what the text means. Fallibility of the reader is compatible with infallibility of the record. An authoritative truthful text does not make every interpretation of it authoritative. The fact that readers err does not make all readings equal. A correct reading has derivative force as correspondence; it does not become a second Graphē. An incorrect reading remains incorrect; it does not become the writing by quoting, by occupying an office, or by gathering a community. The investigator is warranted, with full force, in receiving this as Movement IX’s first word. Chapter 67 remains the next question.

**Alternative explanations refused.** That Scripture’s authority baptizes the reader’s reading. That all readings are equal because readers err. That this file is already Chapters 67–73. That this file is Chapter 65 retried or Chapter 57 rewritten. That 2 Peter 1:20 bans stating sense. That distinction abolishes knowledge. Those alternatives fail the join, the biblical record of two objects, and the premises already in force.

Thus:

> **The inference is inspectable. The distinction follows from the conjunction of the gate, the record’s non-failure, the ranking of God and man, the definition of interpretation as later act, and the writings’ own exhibitions. The alternatives fail as accounts of that conjunction.**
