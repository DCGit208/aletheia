# 70.3 Define the Question: Tergiversation and Doctrinal Turning Aside

**Tergiversation**, in this chapter, is doctrinal turning aside: the movement of a person or a people from a truth the Graphē has given, toward another object that claims the place of that truth.

The English word is not a biblical hapax to be imported as a new revelation. It is the locked assignment’s name for an act the writings already describe. Latin *tergiversari* joins the back (*tergum*) and a turning (*versari*): to turn the back, to shift, to desert a side one had. The argumentative force of the name, controlled by the assignment, is departure. Equivocation, shifting language, and desertion of a post are neighbouring senses of the same English word. They belong here only so far as they are operations of leaving a given truth while often keeping its words. They do not become a separate essay on rhetoric.

The phrase is easily abused. In ordinary speech “turning aside” may be heard as any change of mind, so that every correction is already a betrayal. “Doctrinal” may be heard as a sneer at teaching, so that every doctrine is already a turn from Christ. “Departure from truth” may be heard as departure from a party’s settled sense, so that the party’s governor is silently installed as the truth. None of those senses is this chapter’s object.

Four elements belong to the definition.

First, **a movement from a given truth**. The writings do not describe tergiversation as the first hearing of a people who have never been told. They describe a turning out of a way commanded, a removal from him who called, a departing from the faith, a turning of the ear from the truth, a forsaking of the right way, a drifting from things heard, an erring from the truth. The “from” is part of the object. A question not yet answered is not yet this movement. A truth not yet given is not yet this leaving.

Second, **toward another object that claims the place**. The calf is called the god who brought Israel up. The other message is called a gospel. The fables receive the ear that was for the truth. The speaker draws disciples after himself. The turn is doctrinal because what is left and what is taken are matters of what is believed, taught, heeded, and obeyed as the word. A change of circumstance that leaves the same confession standing is not, by itself, this chapter’s object.

Third, **causes and mechanisms, not a bare condemnation**. The locked assignment asks what causes the turning and by what operations it proceeds, and which intellectual failures permit it. A verdict that only says “it is evil to turn” has not performed the file. The causes are the conditions the writings name. The mechanisms are the operations by which the movement is carried. The intellectual failures are the failures of hearing, understanding, learning, stability, consent, and heed that the writings attach to the permission of the departure.

Fourth, **not those other claims**. The definition excludes, as not this object: a retry of Chapter 69’s displacement; accepted heresies as the normalization and institutional acceptance of error; aridity as the loss of reasonability; *apeiros* as unskillfulness in the Word; Movement X’s labour-files; the First Principles as this file’s achievement. Those exclusions are part of the definition. They are not a later apology.

Thus:

> **The question of this chapter is not what happens when secondary sources govern meaning, and not yet how error becomes the accepted doctrine of a body, how reasonability is lost, or how skill is gained. Those questions have been examined or remain later. The question is what causes doctrinal turning aside, by what mechanisms it proceeds, and which intellectual failures permit departure from truth.**

# 70.4 The Biblical Acts the Name Collects

The writings do not use one Greek word for every departure. The chapter collects acts the assignment names as one object, and it keeps the words distinct so that a lexicon is not mistaken for the argument.

**סוּר (*sur*, H5493).** To turn aside, to depart. Exodus 32:8 and Deuteronomy 9:12, 16 use it for Israel’s quick turning out of the way the Lord commanded. Deuteronomy 11:16 uses it for the turn that follows a deceived heart and issues in the service of other gods. The word names a leaving of a commanded way. It does not, by itself, name a later council.

**ἐκτρέπω (*ektrepō*, G1624).** To turn out of a path, to turn aside. First Timothy 1:6: some have turned aside unto vain jangling. First Timothy 5:15: some are already turned aside after Satan. First Timothy 6:20: avoiding profane and vain babblings is the commanded contrary of the turn. Second Timothy 4:4: they shall be turned unto fables. The word names the aside-movement. The object turned unto — vain talk, Satan, fables — is supplied by the clause, not by the verb alone.

**ἀποστρέφω (*apostrephō*, G654).** To turn away. Second Timothy 4:4: they shall turn away their ears from the truth. Titus 1:14: commandments of men that turn from the truth. The organ can be named. The ear turns. A commandment can be the instrument that turns. The truth is the “from.”

**ἀφίστημι (*aphistēmi*, G868).** To withdraw, to depart, to fall away. First Timothy 4:1: some shall depart from the faith. Luke 8:13: in time of temptation they fall away. Hebrews 3:12: an evil heart of unbelief, in departing from the living God. The word names a standing-away. “Some” in 1 Timothy 4:1 is part of the grammar. The text does not say that every hearer departs.

**μετατίθημι (*metatithēmi*, G3346).** To transfer, to transpose, to remove to another place. Galatians 1:6: ye are so soon removed from him that called you unto another gospel. The movement has a terminus a quo and a terminus ad quem. The English “removed” must not be emptied of that transfer. The chapter does not rest a doctrine on an unresolved middle-voice dispute. The marvel, the speed, the from, and the unto are explicit either way.

**παραρρέω (*pararreō*, G3901).** To flow beside, to drift past. Hebrews 2:1, only here: lest at any time we should let them slip. The lexical picture is of the things heard flowing past the hearer who does not hold them. The King James “slip” is the translation in force in the quotation. The argumentative force is loss of what was heard by failure to give the more earnest heed. Drift is a mechanism of turning. It is not a softer word that makes the loss unreal.

**ἀστοχέω (*astocheō*, G795).** To miss the mark, to deviate. First Timothy 1:6: having swerved. First Timothy 6:21: have erred concerning the faith. Second Timothy 2:18: who concerning the truth have erred. The miss is concerning a named object — the charge’s end, the faith, the truth — and in 2 Timothy 2:18 it is filled with a sentence: the resurrection is past already. Missing the mark is not a vague mood. It is a deviation that can be stated.

**στρεβλόω (*strebloō*, G4761).** To twist, to wrench, as by torture. Second Peter 3:16: which they that are unlearned and unstable wrest. The mechanism of handling is a distortion. The subjects of the verb are named: the unlearned and the unstable. The end is named: their own destruction. Hard things in Paul’s epistles are named in the same sentence and are not the verb’s subject.

These acts are one assignment because each is a leaving of a given truth. They are not one lexeme. A calf in the camp, a transfer unto another gospel, an ear turned to fables, a drift from things heard, and a twisted sentence are exhibitions of one object in different courts. Collapsing the words into a single technical term would hide the mechanisms. Leaving them unjoined would hide the assignment.

Thus:

> **Tergiversation, in this argument, is the doctrinal turning the writings exhibit as *sur*, *ektrepō*, *apostrephō*, *aphistēmi*, *metatithēmi*, *pararreō*, *astocheō*, and *strebloō*: a movement from a given truth toward another object that claims its place. The words stay distinct. The object they serve in this file is one.**

# 70.5 Distinguish Objects That Must Not Be Collapsed

The investigation fails at the first step if it treats different objects as one.

**This turning and Chapter 69’s governance.** This chapter examines departure. Chapter 69 examined the occupation of the seat of sense. A governor can be the condition under which departure is called faithfulness. The condition does not make the turning a reprint of the occupation, and the turning does not make the occupation unstated. A person can turn by an itching ear where no commentary has been installed as the court. A court can be governed and still contain a hearer who has not left the truth. To treat this file as Chapter 69 retried would hide the movement inside another account of the seat. To omit the movement in order to protect the displacement-file would leave the displacement unjoined to the fifth word Movement IX assigns.

**Turning and the five modes of misreading.** Chapter 67 catalogued poor perception, deficient understanding, faulty inference, context failure, and improper handling. Those modes can be instruments of a turning: a wrested sentence is an improper handling that carries a departure. The instrument is not the movement. A misreading can be corrected while the reader is still facing the truth. A turning is the leaving. To treat this file as Chapter 67 retried would convert a departure into a sixth mode. To ignore the modes would pretend the departure has no intellectual carriage. The modes remain the catalog. This chapter uses them as instruments already identified, in the service of a different object.

**The turner and the troubler.** Galatians names both the ones who are removed and the some who trouble and pervert. Acts 20 names wolves from outside and men arising from within who draw disciples away. The agency that draws and the agency that goes are both in the record. A psychology that makes every turner a pure victim erases the rebuke. A psychology that makes every turner a pure originator erases the drawers, the bewitchers, and the privy introducers. The chapter keeps both agencies.

**Departure from truth and disagreement not yet settled.** The truth in this file is truth the writing has given: a commanded way, a received gospel, sound doctrine, the faith, the things heard. A question the writings have not closed is not, by the mere fact of disagreement, a tergiversation. Acts 15 exhibits a dispute brought to the word and to the witnesses. The exhibition is the contrary of a turn only so far as the dispute is submitted to what is written and to what God has done, rather than settled by the louder party. The chapter does not write a polity of councils. It refuses the reduction that every doctrinal difference is already a leaving.

**The turning and the truth’s survival.** Mark 7’s making of none effect, already used in Chapter 69, is compatible with the Scripture’s inability to be broken. The same compatibility holds here. Men can leave a truth. They cannot, by leaving it, make it cease to be the truth. The calf does not become the God who brought them up because they say so. Another gospel does not become the gospel because it wears the name. The departure succeeds as a movement of persons. It fails as an ontological defeat of the truth.

**Intellectual failure and excuse.** “Permit,” in the assignment, names the opening through which departure proceeds. It does not name a pardon. The unlearned and unstable wrest unto their own destruction. The ones who understand neither what they say nor whereof they affirm are not thereby innocent of the vain jangling. Identifying the failure is the assignment. Baptizing the turner is not.

**This chapter and later files.** Accepted heresies, aridity, *apeiros*, scholarly labour, *spoudazō*, and the First Principles remain later. A turning may be the place where a later pathology appears. Appearance is not those files written now. Second Peter 2:1 uses αἵρεσις (*hairesis*, G139) for destructive heresies privily brought in. The mechanism of that bringing-in belongs here. The account of how an erroneous doctrine becomes normalized, and why a body’s acceptance does not transform error into truth, belongs to Chapter 71.

Thus:

> **Doctrinal turning aside is a movement from a given truth toward another claimant of its place. It is not the displacement retried, not a sixth mode of misreading, not every disagreement, not the abolition of the truth left, not an excuse, and not the later files under another name.**

# 70.7 The Sequence of Levels: One Clean Scope Section

What this chapter does not settle is a matter of sequence, not of doubt about what it does settle.

The investigation proceeds by levels. Each level answers its question with full force. The next level remains next because it is a different question.

**Already in force.** Truth is conformity to reality. God is the ground of truth and cannot lie. Revelation has been given and authenticated. Scripture as writing is God-breathed. The record does not fail as truthful divine writing of what it actually communicates. The Scripture cannot be broken. The Graphē is the primary written authority. The authority of that writing has been distinguished from the authority claimed for a later statement of sense. Transfer fails. Despair fails. Scripture is misread in five inspectable modes, and the writing remains the measure of those modes. Theology, philosophy, tradition, and institution are secondary systems. They may serve. They do not become primary written authority by identity, academy, antiquity, or recognition. When secondary sources govern the meaning of Scripture, the seat of sense is occupied and the test is reversed. The writing is not broken by that occupation. Service is not that event. Recognition of the corpus is not creation of the corpus. Human accounts about God are accountable.

**This level.** Given those premises, doctrinal turning aside has causes and mechanisms the writings name, and intellectual failures of understanding, learning, stability, consent, endurance of sound doctrine, and heed permit departure from truth. The truth left remains the measure. Continuance in what was learned and earnest heed to what was heard are real contraries. The warrant is full at this level. It is not postponed until Chapter 71 in order to keep the present turning weak, and it is not inflated into a normalization-file, a dryness-file, an unskill-file, or a labour-file in order to make the turning sound larger than a departure.

**Next levels, named once.** Accepted heresies belong to Chapter 71. Aridity and the loss of reasonability belong to Chapter 72. *Apeiros*, unskillfulness in the Word, belongs to Chapter 73. Scholarly labour, perception as a dedicated stage-file, reasoning through the Word as a labour-file, quotation and knowledge as a dedicated file, *spoudazō*, exercise, maturity, rightly dividing, and readiness for the Six First Principles belong to Movement X. Those files remain later.

**Limits that belong to this level itself, not to later hesitation.** This turning is not a second statement of governance. This turning is not a claim that every hearer departs. This turning is not a claim that intellectual failure is the only biblical cause, or that the heart’s unbelief, lust, and deceit are absent from the same paragraphs. This turning is not a pardon issued to the unlearned. This turning is not a denial that return is intelligible: the truth remains, and the writings speak of converting the one who errs from it. This turning is not a history of later parties. Using Chapters 57, 65, 66, 67, 68, and 69 is not rewriting them. A cause is not a sneer at teaching. A mechanism is not a sneer at the gospel’s real power to trouble a church. An intellectual failure is not a sneer at the intellect, and it is not a decree that study is the whole obedience. Those limits are part of what the turning-file is. They are not a stack of clauses designed to drain the positive conclusion.

The chapter will therefore state the positive warrant with full force, locate the limits as belonging to the doctrine rather than as apologies, and name later files once as later files. It will not repeat, under every paragraph, that the present conclusion is not proof of a later conclusion. Sequence is already the account of that difference.

Thus:

> **The present account of doctrinal turning aside is in force at this level. Later verdicts remain later because they are different questions. Limits named here are the shape of the doctrine, not a delayed doubt about the doctrine.**

# 70.8 Define “Cause” and “Mechanism” at This Stage

**Cause**, in this argument, is a condition the writings themselves attach to the departure as what produces it, occasions it, or opens the way for it.

**Mechanism**, in this argument, is the operation by which the movement is carried: the transfer, the substitution, the leaven, the twist, the heap, the shift of heed, the drift, the drawing-after, the privy introduction, the carrying off.

The two are not a metaphysic of causation imported from elsewhere. They are the assignment’s words for relations the texts already draw. “Who hath bewitched you” names an agent. “A little leaven leaveneth the whole lump” names an operation. “They will not endure sound doctrine” names a condition of the hearers. “Having itching ears” names the organ and its appetite. “Which some professing have erred concerning the faith” names a profession in connection with a miss. The chapter does not add a hidden psychological layer behind these clauses. It reads the clauses as the causes and the mechanisms.

A cause can be a condition without being a sufficient excuse, and without being the sole cause in every case. The record is plural. An evil heart of unbelief is named. A deceived heart is named. Lusts are named. A transfer of heed to seducing spirits is named. Not understanding what one affirms is named. Unlearned instability is named. The plurality is the doctrine at this level. A single-cause theory would be a system laid on the texts.

A mechanism can be traced without a claim that the tracer has entered the turner’s secret thoughts beyond what the text says. Aaron’s sentence about the fire and the calf is in the text. The Galatians’ removal is in the text. The ear’s turning is in the text. Where the text stops, the mechanism stops. Invention of motives the text does not state is not insight. It is a further turning, in which a theory finishes what the writing did not say.

Thus:

> **A cause is a condition the writings attach to departure. A mechanism is an operation by which the departure is carried. Both are read from the clauses. Neither is a pardon, a single-cause system, or a journey behind the text.**

---

# 70.9 Define “Intellectual Failure” at This Stage

**Intellectual failure**, in this argument, is a failure of the knowing, hearing, understanding, learning, stability, consent, or heed that the writings attach to departure from truth.

The phrase is the assignment’s, and it must not be allowed to drift into a compliment or an insult. It is not a claim that the turner is stupid. It is not a claim that the turner is merely academic and therefore harmless. It is not a claim that the mind fails while the heart stays pure. It is the name for those failures the texts locate in understanding, learning, steadfastness of mind, consent to sound words, endurance of doctrine, and the direction of the ear.

The failures this chapter identifies, and will warrant from the passages, are these:

- understanding neither what one says nor whereof one affirms, while desiring to be a teacher (1 Timothy 1:7);
- not consenting to wholesome words, knowing nothing, and doting about questions (1 Timothy 6:3–4);
- professing oppositions of falsely named knowledge and erring concerning the faith (1 Timothy 6:20–21);
- not enduring sound doctrine, and turning the ear from the truth (2 Timothy 4:3–4);
- missing the mark concerning the truth by a sentence the truth does not say (2 Timothy 2:18);
- being unlearned and unstable, and therefore able to wrest writings that are hard (2 Peter 3:16);
- failing to give the more earnest heed to the things heard, so that they drift (Hebrews 2:1);
- hearing the word and not mixing it with faith, so that the hearing does not profit (Hebrews 4:2);
- erring in connection with not knowing the scriptures (Matthew 22:29, used now as the join of not-knowing and error, not as a second Sadducean file).

These are intellectual in the strict sense that they are failures of the apprehension and the handling of a word. They are never, in the paragraphs where they appear, a separate world from the heart. The same 2 Timothy 4 joins the failure to endure doctrine with lusts. The same Hebrews 3 joins departing with an evil heart of unbelief. The same 1 Timothy 4 joins departure from the faith with a seared conscience. The identification keeps the join. It refuses the split.

“Permit” means that where these failures stand, the departure has an opening the writings recognize. A man who does not understand what he affirms can turn aside into vain jangling and carry others. A hearer who will not endure sound doctrine can heap teachers for an itching ear. An unlearned and unstable reader can twist a hard sentence unto destruction. The permission is the opening. The destruction, the overthrow of some, and the curse on another gospel are the texts’ account of what the opening is not: it is not a mitigation.

Dullness of hearing and inexperience in the word of righteousness stand in Hebrews 5:11–14, in the neighbourhood of this subject. The dedicated account of that dullness as loss of reasonability is Chapter 72. The dedicated account of ἄπειρος (*apeiros*, G552) is Chapter 73. The present definition does not borrow those files in order to sound complete. It identifies the failures the assignment can warrant now.

Thus:

> **An intellectual failure, here, is a failure of understanding, learning, stability, consent, endurance of sound doctrine, or heed, which the writings attach to departure. It permits the turning as an opening. It does not excuse the turning, and it does not travel alone, apart from unbelief, lust, and deceit, in the paragraphs that name it.**

---

# 70.10 Define “Departure from Truth”

**Departure from truth**, in this argument, is the leaving of what is the case as the writing has given it, in favor of another account that is not the case.

Truth has already been defined as conformity to reality, not as the property of a sincere speaker and not as the vote of an institution. Departure from truth is therefore not departure from a feeling, and not departure from a brand. It is departure from the reality the Graphē communicates: the way commanded, the gospel of Christ, the faith, the sound doctrine, the things heard.

The writings mark the object left with prepositions and with nouns. From the way. From him that called you. From the faith. From the truth. From the living God. From one’s own steadfastness. The nouns are not interchangeable slogans. The way is the commanded path. The faith, in 1 Timothy 4:1 and 6:21, is the deposit in view in those charges. The truth, in 2 Timothy 4:4 and James 5:19, is the truth the ear leaves and the truth from which a brother errs. The living God, in Hebrews 3:12, is the person departed from by unbelief, in a paragraph about hearing his voice. The chapter does not flatten these into one vague “religion.” It treats them as departures from a given divine word and from the God who spoke it.

A departure can retain the vocabulary of the thing left. “These be thy gods, O Israel, which have brought thee up” retains the history of the exodus and assigns it to the calf. “Another gospel” retains the word gospel. Fables are received by ears that had a truth to turn from. The retention of words is a mechanism, not a proof that no departure occurred. Chapter 66 already established that quotation is not the Graphē. The present use is the further point: a turning often quotes, renames, and continues to say “gospel,” “gods who brought us up,” and “truth,” while the object has been exchanged.

Thus:

> **Departure from truth is the leaving of what the writing has given as the case. Retained vocabulary does not prevent the leaving. It is one of the ways the leaving disguises itself.**

---

# 70.11 Positive Warrant: The Truth Turned From Remains the Measure

The first member of the positive warrant is that the truth departed from remains the measure of the departure.

This follows from premises already in force, and the writings of turning confirm it rather than suspend it. The record does not fail as truthful divine writing of what it actually communicates. The Scripture cannot be broken. The Graphē is the primary written authority. A movement of persons away from a sentence does not edit the sentence. Galatians 1:8–9 attaches the curse to the other preaching because the gospel already preached is still the measure, even if the mouth is “we” or an angel. Exodus 32:8 names the calf a turning out of the way commanded, which is intelligible only if the way still exists to be the measure of the turn. Second Peter 3:16’s wresting is wresting of the scriptures, not a discovery that the scriptures had no sense until the unstable supplied one. James 5:19 can speak of erring from the truth and of being converted because the truth is still there.

The warrant is not a comfort that makes the turning small. It is the ground on which the turning can be called a turning at all. If the leavers redefined truth by leaving, the word “departure” would be a vanity, and every path would be true for those on it. Chapters 1–3 already refused that vanity. The present chapter inherits the refusal and applies it to the act of turning. The calf is false because the Lord brought them up. The other gospel is not another of the same kind because the gospel of Christ has been received. The fable is a fable because the truth was the ear’s proper object. Detection is possible because the measure stands.

Thus:

> **The truth turned from remains the measure. The turning does not break it, does not become it, and does not make the departure true. That survival is the first member of the warrant, stated with full force.**

---

# 70.12 Positive Warrant: The Writings Name Causes, So the Turning Is Inspectable

The second member of the positive warrant is that the causes and mechanisms are named, and are therefore inspectable.

The writings do not say, “Some turned, and the reason is hidden in a liberty the text will not discuss.” They say: they turned quickly; they made a calf; they said these were the gods who brought them up; they were removed unto another gospel; some troubled them and would pervert it; they were bewitched not to obey the truth; a persuasion not from the caller hindered them; a little leaven leavened the lump; some swerved and turned aside unto vain jangling, desiring to be teachers and understanding neither what they said nor whereof they affirmed; some shall depart from the faith, giving heed to seducing spirits and doctrines of devils; they will not endure sound doctrine; after their own lusts they will heap teachers, having itching ears; they will turn away their ears from the truth and be turned unto fables; their word will eat as a gangrene; concerning the truth they have erred, saying the resurrection is past; commandments of men turn from the truth; hearers let the things heard slip when they do not give earnest heed; an evil heart of unbelief departs; sin’s deceit hardens; philosophy and empty deceit carry off; perverse speech draws disciples after the speakers; false teachers privily bring in destructive heresies; the unlearned and unstable wrest; the unstable are led away and fall from their steadfastness.

An investigator who treats the turning as an opaque fate has not yet read the clauses. An investigator who adds causes the clauses do not contain has left the record for a theory. The positive warrant is the middle the texts themselves occupy: the turning can be examined. Its causes can be stated. Its mechanisms can be followed. The statement is accountable to the sentences, in the same way Chapter 67’s modes were accountable to the exhibitions and Chapter 69’s governance was accountable to the seat of sense.

Forewarning belongs to this member. The Spirit speaketh expressly that some shall depart (1 Timothy 4:1). Paul warns night and day before the wolves and the drawers arise (Acts 20:29–31). Peter writes, “seeing ye know these things before, beware” (2 Peter 3:17). The warning is intelligible because the danger is describable in advance. A turning that could not be described until after it had redefined truth could not be warned against as a leaving of truth. The warnings treat the truth as still known and the departure as still recognizable.

Thus:

> **The writings name the causes and the mechanisms. The turning is inspectable. Forewarning is possible because the departure is a describable leaving of a known truth. That inspectability is the second member of the warrant, stated with full force.**

---

# 70.13 Positive Warrant: Continuance Is a Real Contrary, So Turning Is Not Fate

The third member of the positive warrant is that continuance, heed, and conversion are real contraries, so the turning is not the necessary end of having heard.

Second Timothy 3:14 commands: continue thou in the things which thou hast learned and hast been assured of, knowing of whom thou hast learned them. The command stands in the next breath after evil men and seducers waxing worse, deceiving and being deceived. The worsening is real. The continuance is commanded to a man who has learned. Hebrews 2:1 commands the more earnest heed to the things heard, as the act that answers the drift. Hebrews 3:12 commands, take heed, lest there be in any of you an evil heart of unbelief. Second Peter 3:17 commands, beware, lest ye also, being led away, fall from your own steadfastness, and verse 18 commands, grow in grace and in the knowledge of our Lord and Saviour Jesus Christ. James 5:19–20 speaks of a brother erring from the truth and of one converting him. Second Timothy 2:25–26 speaks of repentance to the acknowledging of the truth, and of recovery out of the snare.

These contraries are not Movement X written in advance. They are the texts’ own opposite of the turning, required here because a cause that was fate would not be a cause the hearer is commanded to refuse. The labour of *spoudazō*, the exercise of the word, and the approved workman remain later. What is in force now is narrower and already explicit: the same writings that describe departure command the hearer to continue, to heed, to beware, and they speak of return to the truth as a real act. “Some shall depart” is not “all must depart.” “Lest ye also” is not “ye also inevitably.”

The contrary also protects the previous files. Chapter 66 refused despair, the inference that because interpreters err, no reading is true. The present member refuses the cousin inference that because hearers turn, steadfastness is a slogan. Steadfastness is a relation to a truth that still stands, held by heed and by continuance in what was learned. It is not a personality trait the text forgot to define. It is not the infallibility of the hearer. Chapter 57 already located infallibility in the record. The hearer who continues is still a hearer. He continues in a word able to make wise unto salvation, not in his own permanence.

Thus:

> **Continuance in what was learned, earnest heed to what was heard, beware of being led away, and conversion of the one who errs are real contraries of turning aside. The turning is not fate. That contrary is the third member of the warrant, stated with full force.**

---

# 70.14 What the Turning Is: A Movement From a Given Truth

The exhibitions, taken together, show one movement.

There is a given. The way has been commanded. The gospel has been preached and received. The faith has been the object of a charge. Sound doctrine has been the thing to endure. The things have been heard. The right way has been there to forsake. Steadfastness has been one’s own to fall from. The truth has been there to err from.

There is a movement. Quickly turned aside. Removed. Departed. Turned away the ear. Turned unto fables. Let slip. Fell away. Forsook. Wrested. Erred. Drawn away. Carried off.

There is another object in the place. A molten calf assigned the exodus. Another gospel. Vain jangling. Seducing spirits and doctrines of devils. Fables. A sentence: the resurrection is past already. Philosophy and empty deceit after the tradition of men. Disciples after the speaker. The way of Balaam. The error of the wicked.

The movement is doctrinal because the exchange is an exchange of what is believed, taught, and heeded as the word of God. It is not first a change of taste in indifferent things. Where the texts include lust, gain, and wages, they include them as bound up with the doctrinal exchange — itching ears that will not endure sound doctrine, men supposing that gain is godliness, Balaam loving the wages of unrighteousness while forsaking the right way. The moral freight is part of the doctrinal turning. It does not reduce the turning to a private vice with no teaching in it.

Thus:

> **The turning is a movement from a given truth to another object that claims its place. The given, the movement, and the claimant are all in the exhibitions. That three-part shape is what tergiversation is in this chapter.**

---

# 70.15 What Does Not Constitute the Turning

Several neighbouring events are visible in the same writings and are not this object.

**First hearing is not turning.** A people who have not yet been told the way cannot yet have turned out of it. The calf narrative is a turning because the way had been commanded. Galatians is a turning because the gospel had been received and Christ had been set forth before their eyes. The chapter does not call ignorance of an unpreached word a tergiversation.

**Correction toward the writing is not turning.** Apollos, in Acts 18:26, is taught the way of God more perfectly. The movement is toward the truth he had not yet held accurately. To call every change a desertion would make repentance impossible and would make James 5:19’s conversion a second fall. The direction decides. Toward the given truth is not away from it.

**Service under the writing is not turning.** Sense given under the book, the deposit held, the teacher opening the scripture, the prophecy proved — Chapter 69’s goods — are not departures. A hearer who is helped toward the text has not, by being helped, turned. The turning begins where the help, the custom, the teacher, or the experience becomes the object in the truth’s place, or where the hearer leaves the truth by the mechanisms this chapter names.

**A hard sentence is not itself a turning.** Second Peter 3:16 says some things are hard to be understood, and then names a different act: the unlearned and unstable wrest them. Difficulty is a feature of some texts. Wresting is the act of the unstable. The chapter inherits Chapter 67’s refusal to make hardness into the writing’s fault.

**The survival of the truth is not the absence of the turning.** Because the gospel still stands, a careless inference says the Galatians’ removal does not matter. The curse is in the paragraph. Because the scriptures can still be wrested only if they still exist, a careless inference says wresting is harmless study. The clause says unto their own destruction. Gravity is part of what the turning is. The later files will examine how error becomes accepted, how reasonability fails, and how unskill stands. They are not required before the curse, the destruction, the overthrow of some, and the not entering can be named as the texts’ own weight.

Thus:

> **First hearing, correction toward the writing, service under the writing, and the hardness of some sentences are not tergiversation. The survival of the truth does not make the turning weightless. The turning is the movement away.**

---

# 70.16 Biblical Record: Exodus 32 and Deuteronomy 9 — Quickly Turned Aside Out of the Way

> “They have turned aside quickly out of the way which I commanded them: they have made them a molten calf, and have worshipped it, and have sacrificed thereunto, and said, These be thy gods, O Israel, which have brought thee up out of the land of Egypt.”

**Context.** The speaker is the Lord, to Moses, on the mountain, after the covenant words have been given. The people below ask Aaron for gods to go before them, because “as for this Moses, the man that brought us up out of the land of Egypt, we wot not what is become of him” (Exodus 32:1). Aaron makes the calf. The people worship and assign the exodus to it. Deuteronomy 9:12 and 9:16 repeat the charge in Moses’ rehearsal: they are quickly turned aside out of the way which the Lord commanded them, and have made a molten image. Aaron’s later sentence to Moses is, “I cast it into the fire, and there came out this calf” (Exodus 32:24).

**What is explicit.** A way had been commanded. The turning is out of that way. The turning is quick. The product is a made god. The saying assigns to the calf the act the Lord had done. The delay of the mediator is the people’s stated reason for the request. Aaron’s report to Moses deletes the making and narrates a spontaneous emergence.

**What the passages warrant for this turning.** The cause is not the absence of a word. The word has just been given. The cause includes a refusal to wait on the mediator who is still the Lord’s servant, and a demand for a visible god to go before them. The mechanism is substitution: the history of redemption is retained in the sentence and transferred to the work of their hands. Speed is a feature of the mechanism. A turning does not need a long age of decay in order to be a turning. The false causal story is also in the record. “There came out this calf” is an account that hides the tools. Concealment of agency is part of this exhibition. It is not yet a universal law that every turner narrates his turn as an accident. It is what this text shows a maker doing when he explains the departure.

**What the passages do not warrant here.** That every later use of a visible sign is a calf. Chapter 69 already distinguished a sign that serves under the word from a wonder that governs it. That the full story of Israel’s later kings is this chapter’s subject. The exhibition is the quick turn from the commanded way. That speed proves the covenant word was never given. The charge is that they turned out of the way commanded. The way is the premise of the charge.

Thus:

> **Exodus 32 and Deuteronomy 9 exhibit a quick turning out of a commanded way, by substitution of a made god for the Lord who brought them up, with the redemption-sentence retained and reassigned, and with a report that hides the making. Speed does not erase the given word. It shows that departure can be swift.**

---

# 70.17 Biblical Record: Deuteronomy 11:16 and Isaiah 30:9–11 — The Deceived Heart and the Demand for Smooth Things

> “Take heed to yourselves, that your heart be not deceived, and ye turn aside, and serve other gods, and worship them.”

> “This is a rebellious people, lying children, children that will not hear the law of the LORD: which say to the seers, See not; and to the prophets, Prophesy not unto us right things, speak unto us smooth things, prophesy deceits: get you out of the way, turn aside out of the path, cause the Holy One of Israel to cease from before us.”

**Context.** Deuteronomy 11 is Moses’ charge in the land they are entering, after the recital of what their eyes have seen. The warning is a sequence: a deceived heart, a turning aside, a service of other gods. Isaiah 30 addresses a people who will not hear the law, who instruct the seers and the prophets to stop right things and to supply smooth things and deceits, and who command the prophets to turn aside out of the path and to remove the Holy One from before them.

**What is explicit.** The heart’s deception stands first in Moses’ sequence, and the turning is the next clause, and the service of other gods is the issue. Isaiah’s people refuse the law, refuse right things, request smooth things and deceits, and command a turning out of the path. The path is assumed. The request is to leave it. The Holy One is to cease from before them. The speech is their speech to the prophets.

**What the passages warrant for this turning.** Deuteronomy 11:16 gives a cause-order in one sentence. Deception of the heart permits the turn. The turn issues in another service. The intellectual and moral failure is not a later theory about the verse. It is the verse’s own order. “Take heed to yourselves” makes the sequence a warned contingency, not a fate. Isaiah 30 exhibits the mechanism from the hearers’ side of the ear. They do not merely fail to find the path. They instruct the speakers to abandon right speech for smooth speech. The demand for what pleases is a cause of the turning they request. Second Timothy 4:3 will name the same pattern as itching ears and heaped teachers. The pattern is already in the prophets: right things refused, smooth things chosen, the path told to move.

**What the passages do not warrant here.** That every pleasant sentence is a deceit. Smoothness is condemned as the substitute for right things, not as a property of all clear speech. The gospel can be spoken plainly and still be the truth. That the loss of reasonability as a dedicated condition of aridity has been written. The deceived heart and the refusal of right things are the present causes. The dryness-file remains Chapter 72.

Thus:

> **A deceived heart, then a turning, then another service: that order is explicit. A people can also demand the turning, by refusing right things and hiring smooth ones. The path does not move because the hearers are tired of it. The hearers command the speakers to leave it.**

---

# 70.18 Biblical Record: Galatians 1:6–9 — Removed Unto Another Gospel

> “I marvel that ye are so soon removed from him that called you into the grace of Christ unto another gospel: which is not another; but there be some that trouble you, and would pervert the gospel of Christ. But though we, or an angel from heaven, preach any other gospel unto you than that which we have preached unto you, let him be accursed. As we said before, so say I now again, If any man preach any other gospel unto you than that ye have received, let him be accursed.”

**Context.** The speaker is Paul, to the churches of Galatia, at the opening of the letter, before the body of the argument. He has just identified the Father and the Lord Jesus Christ as the source of grace and peace. The marvel interrupts the thanksgiving. The measure he invokes is the gospel he and those with him preached, and the gospel the Galatians received. Chapter 69 used this paragraph for another gospel governing under the gospel’s name, and reserved the causes and mechanisms of the removal for this file.

**What is explicit.** The removal is from him that called you into the grace of Christ. The removal is unto a ἕτερον (*heteron*, G2087) gospel, a different one. That different one is οὐκ ἄλλο (*ouk allo*, G243): it is not another gospel of the same kind. Some trouble them and would pervert the gospel of Christ. The perversion is judged by the gospel preached and received, and the judgment is a curse, repeated, and it binds even if the mouth is “we” or an angel from heaven. The removal is “so soon.”

**What the passage warrants for this turning.** The mechanism is transposition. Persons are moved from the caller to a different message that claims the gospel’s name and is denied the gospel’s identity. The cause includes an identifiable agency: some that trouble and would pervert. The Galatians are not described as inventing the other gospel in a vacuum. They are described as removed, and the removers are in the sentence. Their own movement is also in the sentence: Paul marvels at them, not only at the troublers. Both agencies stand. The retained word “gospel” is the disguise. The lexical distinction between a different gospel and another of the same kind is the text’s way of saying that vocabulary did not preserve identity. Speed, as in Exodus 32, is a feature. A church that has received the grace of Christ can be removed quickly. The reception is the premise of the marvel. It is not cancelled by the speed.

**What the passage does not warrant here.** That every doctrinal disagreement is another gospel. The text identifies a perversion of the gospel of Christ, measured by the gospel received. That the curse is a mood rather than a judgment. The word is repeated so that it will not be heard as a mood. That the causes have been exhausted by naming the troublers. The next paragraphs of Galatians fill the mechanism further. This section establishes the transfer, the false sameness, the troubling agency, and the speed. That how such a perversion becomes the ordinary doctrine of a later age has been written. The removal is the object. Normalization remains Chapter 71.

Thus:

> **Galatians 1:6–9 exhibits removal from the one who called, unto a different message that keeps the name gospel and is denied the reality. Troublers pervert. The called are moved. The received gospel remains the measure, and the curse shows the movement’s weight. Speed shows that a true reception can be left quickly.**

---

# 70.19 Biblical Record: Galatians 3:1–3 and 5:7–9 — Bewitched, Hindered, and Leavened

> “O foolish Galatians, who hath bewitched you, that ye should not obey the truth, before whose eyes Jesus Christ hath been evidently set forth, crucified among you? This only would I learn of you, Received ye the Spirit by the works of the law, or by the hearing of faith? Are ye so foolish? having begun in the Spirit, are ye now made perfect by the flesh?”

> “Ye did run well; who did hinder you that ye should not obey the truth? This persuasion cometh not of him that calleth you. A little leaven leaveneth the whole lump.”

**Context.** Galatians 3 opens the argument from their own reception of the Spirit. Christ has been placarded before their eyes as crucified. The alternative Paul refuses is completion by the flesh after a beginning in the Spirit. Galatians 5 looks back on a race that was being run well. The hindrance is a not-obeying of the truth. The persuasion is not from the caller. Leaven is the figure of the small contrary working through the whole lump. Galatians 4:9 adds the direction he will not let them beautify: turning again to the weak and beggarly elements, the desire to be in bondage again.

**What is explicit.** A bewitching (βασκαίνω, *baskainō*, G940) is asked as the cause of not obeying the truth, in the face of a public portrayal of Christ crucified. The Spirit was received by the hearing of faith. The present folly is seeking to be made perfect by the flesh. They did run well. Someone hindered them (ἐγκόπτω, *enkoptō*, G1465), cut into their course, so that they should not obey the truth. The persuasion (πεισμονή, *peismonē*, G3988) is not from the one who calls. A little leaven leavens the whole lump.

**What the passages warrant for this turning.** The intellectual failure is not the absence of evidence. Christ was set before their eyes, and they heard, and they received the Spirit by that hearing. The failure is not obeying the truth they were shown, and treating a later addition as the completion of what the hearing began. The mechanism has three operations that can be named without a theory. Bewitchment: a fascination that fixes the eye elsewhere than on the Christ who was placarded. Hindrance: a cutting-in on a course that was already true. Leaven: a small contrary teaching that does not need to replace every sentence at once in order to work through the lump. The persuasion’s origin is part of the test. A persuasion not from the caller is not a development of the call. It is a different source. “Ye did run well” forbids the inference that the race was imaginary. The prior running is the premise of the question “who did hinder you.”

**What the passages do not warrant here.** That every growth in understanding is a fleshly completion. The text distinguishes a beginning in the Spirit by the hearing of faith from a completion by the flesh. Movement X will examine labour in the word. Labour that hears the same gospel is not this hindrance. That leaven has been examined as the whole doctrine of accepted heresy. The figure shows how a little contrary spreads through a body. The dedicated account of institutionalized error remains Chapter 71. The figure is used here as a mechanism of turning, not as that file.

Thus:

> **The Galatians were hindered from obeying a truth already set before their eyes. Bewitchment, a persuasion not from the caller, and a little leaven are the mechanisms. Prior running and prior hearing are the premises of the rebuke, not illusions cancelled by the fall.**

---

# 70.20 The Mechanisms in Galatia Joined

The Galatian exhibitions are one turning seen in its parts.

The from is the caller and his grace, the gospel received, the hearing of faith, the race run well. The unto is another gospel, the flesh as the completer, the weak and beggarly elements, a persuasion from elsewhere. The agents are some that trouble and pervert, a “who” that bewitched, a “who” that hindered. The intellectual failure is not obeying the truth in the presence of evidence, and receiving a different message as if it were the same gospel or the needed completion. The moral and doctrinal weight is the curse, the foolishness named as foolishness, and the bondage desired again.

Chapter 69’s question — what voice finishes the sense — is not restated as the whole of this join. A voice can finish the sense and the hearer can still be asked, as a further question, why he moved. Galatians answers with transfer, perversion, bewitchment, hindrance, and leaven. The answer uses the governance of another gospel as a premise, the premise Chapter 69 established, and asks the question that premise made necessary: how the churches moved.

The join also refuses two reductions that the paragraph itself cannot host. It cannot host the reduction that the Galatians were only victims, because they are rebuked for not obeying and for seeking completion by the flesh. It cannot host the reduction that there were no troublers, because the troublers are the subject of the curse as truly as the churches are the subject of the marvel. Turning aside, in this cardinal exhibition, is a relation between a given gospel, a people who leave it, and speakers who carry the leaving.

Thus:

> **Galatia’s turning is removal plus perversion plus non-obedience of a placarded truth, carried by bewitchment, hindrance, and leaven. The churches move. The troublers carry. The received gospel measures both.**

# 70.21 Biblical Record: 1 Timothy 1:3–7 — Swerved, and Turned Aside Unto Vain Jangling

> “As I besought thee to abide still at Ephesus, when I went into Macedonia, that thou mightest charge some that they teach no other doctrine, neither give heed to fables and endless genealogies, which minister questions, rather than godly edifying which is in faith: so do. Now the end of the commandment is charity out of a pure heart, and of a good conscience, and of faith unfeigned: from which some having swerved have turned aside unto vain jangling; desiring to be teachers of the law; understanding neither what they say, nor whereof they affirm.”

**Context.** Paul charges Timothy at Ephesus. The charge is that some teach no other doctrine (ἑτεροδιδασκαλέω, *heterodidaskaleō*, G2085) and give no heed to fables and endless genealogies. The end of the commandment is love from a pure heart, a good conscience, and faith unfeigned. From that end, some have swerved and turned aside. Their desire is to be teachers of the law. Their condition is that they understand neither what they say nor the matters about which they confidently affirm (διαβεβαιόομαι, *diabebaioomai*, G1226).

**What is explicit.** Other-doctrine and heed to fables are the objects of the charge. The swerving (ἀστοχέω, *astocheō*) is from the commandment’s end. The turning aside (ἐκτρέπω, *ektrepō*) is unto vain jangling (ματαιολογία, *mataiologia*, G3150). The desire to teach is explicit. The absence of understanding is explicit, both of their own sentences and of their subject matter.

**What the passage warrants for this turning.** The intellectual failure is stated in the same breath as the turning, and it is stated as their condition, not as a spectator’s insult. They want the office of teacher. They do not understand their own affirmations. The mechanism is a swerve from the commandment’s end — love, conscience, unfeigned faith — into speech that ministers questions. Heed to fables is already forbidden in the charge, so the fable is not an innocent curiosity that later files must wait to mention. It is a named alternative to godly edifying which is in faith. The failure permits the departure in the precise sense of the text: from the end, by missing it, into empty talk, while the mouth continues to affirm. Confident affirmation without understanding is the intellectual form of this turning. It is not yet the dedicated account of the unskillful babe, and it is not a licence to despise teaching. The charge assumes that true teaching exists and that other-doctrine is a deviation from it.

**What the passage does not warrant here.** That every question is vain jangling. The text distinguishes questions ministered by fables and endless genealogies from edifying which is in faith. Inquiry that stays under the commandment is not this swerve. That *apeiros* has been opened because a teacher lacks understanding. The lack is identified here as the condition joined to this turning. The lexical file of unskillfulness in Hebrews 5:13 remains Chapter 73.

Thus:

> **Some turn aside from the commandment’s end unto vain talk by missing the mark, desiring to teach what they do not understand. The intellectual failure is the text’s own description of their condition. Confident speech is not understanding, and it is not innocence.**

# 70.22 Biblical Record: 1 Timothy 4:1–2 — Departing from the Faith by a Transfer of Heed

> “Now the Spirit speaketh expressly, that in the latter times some shall depart from the faith, giving heed to seducing spirits, and doctrines of devils; speaking lies in hypocrisy; having their conscience seared with a hot iron.”

**Context.** The charge to Timothy continues. The Spirit’s utterance is marked as express, not as a conjecture. The time is “latter.” The subject is “some,” not all. The movement is departure from the faith. The participle gives the manner: giving heed (προσέχω, *prosechō*, G4337) to seducing spirits and to doctrines of devils. The speakers of the lies are characterized by hypocrisy and by a seared conscience. Verses 3–5 locate the doctrines in forbidding to marry and commanding to abstain from meats, against the creature received with thanksgiving. Verses 6–7 return Timothy to the words of faith and of good doctrine, and to refusal of profane and old wives’ fables.

**What is explicit.** Departure from the faith is a stated future fact concerning some. The manner is a transfer of heed. The new objects of heed are seducing spirits and doctrines of demons. The speech is lying, the manner hypocritical, the conscience cauterized. The express speech of the Spirit is the warrant that this is not an accident the churches could not have been told.

**What the passage warrants for this turning.** The mechanism is the transfer of heed. The faith is left by attending elsewhere. Heed is an intellectual and spiritual act: the mind’s attendance. What one attends to becomes the operative teacher. Chapter 69 established that an experience or a spirit-claim does not govern the writing. The present function is the departure itself: giving heed to those claims is how some leave the faith. The seared conscience shows that the intellectual transfer is not a clean academic choice sitting above the person. The same sentence holds the doctrines and the burned conscience together. Forewarning is part of the cause’s exposure. Because the Spirit speaks expressly, the departure can be recognized as the thing warned, not baptized as a new leading. “Some” keeps the contrary available: departure is not the definition of the latter times. The latter times contain the warning and the charge to be a good minister of the words of faith.

**What the passage does not warrant here.** That every unusual teaching is a doctrine of demons. The text names heed to seducing spirits and to teachings that forbid what God created to be received. The criterion is the faith and the creature’s goodness, not a suspicion of every new sentence. That the chronology of “latter times” has been built into a calendar. The phrase locates the warning. It does not, in this chapter, authorize a grid of ages. That aridity is the name of the seared conscience. The searing is in the text as the moral condition joined to the lying speech. The dedicated loss of reasonability remains Chapter 72.

Thus:

> **Some depart from the faith by transferring heed to seducing spirits and to demonic doctrines. The transfer is the mechanism. Hypocrisy and a seared conscience are in the same act. Express warning makes the departure recognizable rather than mysterious.**

# 70.23 Biblical Record: 1 Timothy 6:3–5 and 6:20–21 — Consent, Empty Knowledge, and Missing the Faith

> “If any man teach otherwise, and consent not to wholesome words, even the words of our Lord Jesus Christ, and to the doctrine which is according to godliness; he is proud, knowing nothing, but doting about questions and strifes of words, whereof cometh envy, strife, railings, evil surmisings, perverse disputings of men of corrupt minds, and destitute of the truth, supposing that gain is godliness: from such withdraw thyself.”

> “O Timothy, keep that which is committed to thy trust, avoiding profane and vain babblings, and oppositions of science falsely so called: which some professing have erred concerning the faith.”

**Context.** First Timothy 6 contrasts other-teaching with consent (προσέρχομαι, *proserchomai*, G4334, to come to) to sound words, the words of the Lord Jesus, and the doctrine according to godliness. The man who will not come to those words is conceited, knows nothing, and is diseased (νοσέω, *noseō*, G3552) about controversies and word-fights. The mind is corrupted and destitute of the truth. Gain is supposed to be godliness. The closing charge is to keep the deposit and to turn away from profane empty-talk and from the oppositions of the falsely named knowledge (ἀντιθέσεις τῆς ψευδωνύμου γνώσεως). Some, by professing that, have missed the mark concerning the faith. Grace be with those who are with Timothy. The faith is not missed by the charge. It is missed by the profession.

**What is explicit.** Other-teaching is defined by non-consent to wholesome words. The intellectual condition is knowing nothing, joined to pride and to a sickness about questions. Destitution of the truth is the result named, not a preliminary insult. The deposit is to be kept. The falsely named knowledge is to be avoided. Profession of it is connected, in the relative clause, with erring concerning the faith.

**What the passages warrant for this turning.** The intellectual failure that permits this departure is the refusal to come to sound words, together with a knowledge that is a name without the thing. Chapter 68 already refused to identify “falsely named knowledge” with a later academic faculty, and already refused the knowledge-claim primary rank under the deposit. The present function is the miss: professing the oppositions is how some err concerning the faith. The mechanism is substitution of a named knowledge for the deposit, and of word-fights for consent to the words of Christ. “Knowing nothing” does not mean the man is silent. It means the controversies are not knowledge. The supposition that gain is godliness ties the doctrinal turning to a moral mis-seeing: what is profitable is renamed godliness. That renaming is a small exhibition of the larger pattern. The truth is left, and a proxy wears its dignity.

**What the passages do not warrant here.** That controversy about the meaning of a sentence is itself destitution of the truth. The text’s object is other-teaching that will not come to wholesome words, and oppositions that miss the faith. Testing a teaching by the words of Christ is the consent the verse requires, not the disease it condemns. That the deposit-keeping is already the full account of the approved workman. Second Timothy 2:15 remains Movement X. The present imperative is the contrary of this miss: keep the trust, avoid the babbling.

Thus:

> **Departure here is other-teaching that will not consent to sound words, and a professed knowledge that misses the faith. Pride, ignorance, and destitution of the truth are one condition. The deposit remains keepable. The profession of its rival is the miss.**

# 70.24 Biblical Record: 2 Timothy 4:3–4 — The Ear That Will Not Endure

> “For the time will come when they will not endure sound doctrine; but after their own lusts shall they heap to themselves teachers, having itching ears; and they shall turn away their ears from the truth, and shall be turned unto fables.”

**Context.** Paul charges Timothy to preach the word in an opportune and an unopportune time, to reprove, rebuke, and exhort, with longsuffering and doctrine. The reason includes a coming time. Sound doctrine (ὑγιαίνουσα διδασκαλία, teaching that is healthy) is the thing they will not endure. The alternative construction is a heap of teachers (ἐπισωρεύω, *episōreuō*, G2002), gathered according to their own lusts, to serve itching ears (κνήθω, *knēthō*, G2833). The result is two turnings of the same organ: the ear turned away (ἀποστρέφω) from the truth, and the hearers turned aside (ἐκτρέπω) unto fables (μῦθος, *mythos*, G3454).

**What is explicit.** The failure begins as a refusal to endure healthy teaching. The criterion of the teachers they gather is their own desires. The ear is the organ named twice. The from is the truth. The unto is fables. The heap is their own act: they shall heap to themselves.

**What the passage warrants for this turning.** This is a cardinal exhibition of the intellectual failure the assignment asks to be identified. Endurance of sound doctrine is a capacity of the hearer toward a teaching. Its refusal is not a neutral change of curriculum. It is the cause of the ear’s turning. The mechanism has ordered steps the verse itself orders. They will not endure. Therefore they accumulate teachers. The principle of selection is lust, felt as an itch in the hearing. The teachers are numerous because the itch is not answered by the one healthy doctrine. Then the ear leaves the truth. Then the person is turned unto fables. The fable does not need to refute the doctrine point by point if the ear has already been removed. Supply follows demand. Isaiah 30’s “speak unto us smooth things” is the same mechanism in another court: the hearer orders the speaker off the path. The lust does not make the failure non-intellectual. The itch is in the ear, the organ of doctrine. The doctrine refused is the intellectual object. The desire is the selector. The text holds both, and a reading that keeps only the lust or only a disembodied mistake has cut the sentence.

**What the passage does not warrant here.** That every plurality of teachers is a heap. The church has teachers. The heap is defined by the principle of selection: one’s own lusts, against endurance of sound doctrine. That fables have been catalogued as a literary genre to be despised wherever a narrative appears. The text’s fables are the destination of an ear that left the truth. Second Peter 1:16 already refused cunningly devised fables as the apostolic contrary, in a file already in force. The present point is the destination of the turned ear. That the loss of reasonability as aridity is the itch. The itch is the present mechanism. Chapter 72 remains the dedicated dryness.

Thus:

> **The ear turns from the truth unto fables when sound doctrine is not endured and teachers are heaped to satisfy desire. The refusal, the heap, the itch, the turning of the ear, and the fable are one mechanism in the text’s own order.**

# 70.25 Biblical Record: 2 Timothy 2:16–18 — Vain Babbling That Misses the Mark and Overthrows

> “But shun profane and vain babblings: for they will increase unto more ungodliness. And their word will eat as doth a canker: of whom is Hymenaeus and Philetus; who concerning the truth have erred, saying that the resurrection is past already; and overthrow the faith of some.”

**Context.** Timothy is charged to remind, to charge men not to strive about words to no profit, and to study to shew himself approved, a workman that needeth not to be ashamed, rightly dividing the word of truth. The labour and the right division are Movement X’s texts. This chapter does not open them. It uses the adjacent warning for the mechanism that makes the labour necessary, without performing the labour-file. Profane empty-voices (κενοφωνία, *kenophōnia*, G2757) increase unto more ungodliness. Their word eats as a gangrene (γάγγραινα, *gangraina*, G1044). Two men are named. Their error concerning the truth is a sentence: the resurrection has already occurred. The effect is the overthrow of the faith of some.

**What is explicit.** Babbling is not static. It increases. The word of the babblers consumes. The miss concerning the truth is specifiable as a doctrinal sentence. The sentence has an effect on others: some have their faith overthrown. The names make the warning concrete. This is not a rumour about “some people somewhere.”

**What the passage warrants for this turning.** The mechanism is a false sentence about a true object. The resurrection is a truth the apostolic gospel holds. “Already past” is a relocation of that truth into a completed past that cancels the hope still held. Chapter 67 would recognize a faulty handling. The present function is the departure: concerning the truth they have erred, and the error overthrows. The intellectual failure is the miss, filled with content. A turning of this kind can be quoted, taught, and spread. The gangrene is the figure of spread without a new argument at every inch. Increase unto more ungodliness is the text’s own causal clause: the babbling is shunned because it grows. The overthrow of “some” again refuses fate. The word eats. It does not thereby eat every faith in the room. The charge to shun assumes that the empty talk can be refused.

**What the passage does not warrant here.** That rightly dividing has been taught because the verse before the warning commands it. The command is acknowledged as the contrary and left in its file. That every error about the timing of a promise is this sentence. The warrant is the structure: a specifiable miss concerning the truth, with a consuming spread and an overthrow. That naming the men is a licence to build the chapter from later gossip about them. The text gives the names, the sentence, and the effect. That is the exhibition.

Thus:

> **A doctrinal turning can be a single false sentence about a true object, increasing as empty talk and consuming as gangrene, until the faith of some is overthrown. The miss is concerning the truth. The sentence is public enough to be refused.**

# 70.26 Biblical Record: 2 Timothy 3:13–14 — Deceiving and Being Deceived, and the Contrary of Continuance

> “But evil men and seducers shall wax worse and worse, deceiving, and being deceived. But continue thou in the things which thou hast learned and hast been assured of, knowing of whom thou hast learned them; and that from a child thou hast known the holy scriptures, which are able to make thee wise unto salvation through faith which is in Christ Jesus.”

**Context.** The paragraph has described men of a corrupt mind, reprobate concerning the faith, who will not proceed far because their folly shall be manifest, set against the background of Paul’s own doctrine, manner of life, and sufferings. The worsening is of evil men and of impostors (γόητες, enchanters, seducers). The double participle is the mechanism: deceiving, and being deceived. The contrary imperative is continuance in the things learned and assured, with knowledge of the persons from whom they were learned, and with the holy scriptures as the writings known from childhood, able to make wise unto salvation.

**What is explicit.** Worsening is real. The deceiver is also deceived. Continuance is commanded, not suggested as a temperament. The object of continuance is “the things which thou hast learned and hast been assured of.” The ground includes knowing the teachers and knowing the scriptures. The scriptures’ ability is to make wise unto salvation through faith in Christ Jesus. The next verses, already established in the inspiration-files, assign all Scripture’s profitability. They are not re-opened here.

**What the passage warrants for this turning.** Mutual deception is a mechanism. The man who leads others aside is himself a man led aside. The turning is not only a strategy of the clear-eyed against the simple. It is a worsening in which the seducer is inside the deceit he spreads. That does not reduce his agency. “Deceiving” is his act. “Being deceived” is his condition. The intellectual failure and the moral worsening are one waxing. The positive warrant of this section is the contrary’s content. Continuance has an object: things learned, things of which one has been assured, teachers one can name, scriptures one has known. Steadfastness is not a mood floating above the word. It is remaining in a given teaching whose source can be identified and whose writing can make wise. The ability of the scriptures is part of why the turning is not fate. The word the seducer leaves is the word able to keep the hearer wise. This is not yet the scholarly impetus as a programme. It is the text’s own placement of the holy writings on the side of continuance.

**What the passage does not warrant here.** That knowing a teacher’s name is a sufficient ground. The same letter has already said that an apostle’s mouth is cursed if it preaches another gospel, and Chapter 66 is in force. “Knowing of whom thou hast learned them” is joined to the scriptures, not substituted for them. That every worsening is irreversible. The imperative “continue thou” is addressed into the world where the worsening is happening. The command is the text’s refusal of inevitability for the one who has learned.

Thus:

> **Seducers worsen by deceiving and by being deceived. The contrary is continuance in things learned and assured, in the company of the scriptures that are able to make wise unto salvation. The turning has a specifiable opposite because the truth has a specifiable given.**

# 70.27 Biblical Record: Titus 1:10–14 — Commandments of Men That Turn from the Truth

> “For there are many unruly and vain talkers and deceivers, specially they of the circumcision: whose mouths must be stopped, who subvert whole houses, teaching things which they ought not, for filthy lucre’s sake. One of themselves, even a prophet of their own, said, The Cretians are alway liars, evil beasts, slow bellies. This witness is true. Wherefore rebuke them sharply, that they may be sound in the faith; not giving heed to Jewish fables, and commandments of men, that turn from the truth.”

**Context.** Titus is left in Crete to set in order what is lacking and to ordain elders. The elder holds fast the faithful word as he hath been taught, so that he may be able by sound doctrine both to exhort and to convince the gainsayers. The gainsayers are many. Their teaching subverts whole houses. The motive named is shameful gain. The remedy is sharp rebuke unto soundness in the faith, and the content of the refusal is heed given neither to Jewish fables nor to commandments of men that turn away from the truth (ἀποστρεφομένων τὴν ἀλήθειαν).

**What is explicit.** Vain talk and deceit subvert households. The teaching is “things which they ought not.” Gain is a motive. Soundness in the faith is the aim of the rebuke, so the subversion is not treated as the end of the story for the rebuked. The human commandments are characterized by their effect: they turn from the truth. Fables are the other object of forbidden heed.

**What the passage warrants for this turning.** Chapter 68 ranked tradition under the writing. Chapter 69 examined tradition governing the meaning of a commandment. The present function is the turning-effect of the human commandment: it is a mechanism that turns people from the truth. A commandment is an intellectual and moral instrument. It tells a house what to heed. When its content is a fable or a human rule in the truth’s place, the heed is the departure. Subversion of whole houses shows that the mechanism is not only private. A teaching can turn a household. That scale is still not Chapter 71. Subversion is the present damage. The account of how an error becomes the normal, accepted doctrine of an institution, and why acceptance does not make it true, is the next file. Gain as a motive joins the intellectual instrument to a corruption of aim, as 1 Timothy 6 joined gain and godliness. The sharp rebuke unto soundness is the contrary inside the same paragraph. The mouth is stopped so that the faith may be sound. The turning is not humoured as an alternative health.

**What the passage does not warrant here.** That every commandment taught in a church is a commandment of men. The elders are commanded to hold the faithful word and to exhort by sound doctrine. The human commandment that turns is the one that does not hold that word. That “they of the circumcision” authorizes a racial or perpetual indictment. The text identifies a class of deceivers then at work, specially of that party, and it quotes a Cretan about Cretans as a witness Paul says is true about the lie then in view. The warrant is the mechanism — vain teaching, gain, fables, human commandments that turn from the truth — not a people condemned as a people. That Jewish fables have been identified with the Old Testament scriptures. The scriptures are the faithful word’s ground in the previous verses and throughout the book already written. The fables are what the rebuke refuses in favor of soundness in the faith.

Thus:

> **Human commandments and fables turn from the truth when they are heeded in its place. The mechanism subverts houses and can be paid for. The contrary in the same charge is soundness in the faith by a rebuke that stops the mouth. Household damage is not yet the normalization-file.**

# 70.28 Biblical Record: Hebrews 2:1 — Drift by Failure of Earnest Heed

> “Therefore we ought to give the more earnest heed to the things which we have heard, lest at any time we should let them slip.”

**Context.** Hebrews 1 has set the Son above the angels as the one through whom God has spoken in these last days, the heir, the brightness of the Father’s glory, the one who sat down on the right hand of the Majesty on high. Chapter 2 opens on that “therefore.” The things heard are the word spoken by the Lord and confirmed, which the following verses will not let the readers neglect. The danger is παραρρυῶμεν (*pararreō*), that the things heard flow past, that they slip. The appointed prevention is more abundant heed (προσέχειν περισσοτέρως).

**What is explicit.** The object in danger of being lost is “the things which we have heard.” The loss is a drifting or slipping, not first a signed renunciation. The prevention is a greater heed. The obligation is “we ought.” The “lest at any time” marks a real contingency.

**What the passage warrants for this turning.** Drift is a mechanism of doctrinal turning that does not always announce itself as a new gospel. The things heard are not denied in a manifesto. They are not held, and they pass. The cause is the failure of earnest heed. Heed is the intellectual and moral attendance already seen in 1 Timothy 4:1, there transferred to seducing spirits, here withheld from the things heard. The same verb-family of attendance explains both the loud departure and the quiet one. Neglect is sufficient. A rival system is not required for this particular mechanism, though a rival can occupy the attention heed denies to the word. The positive warrant is in the infinitive: the drift is what heed answers. If turning of this kind were fate, “we ought” would be rhetoric. The text makes it obligation. The greatness of the word spoken by the Son, already the chapter’s premise, is why the heed is “the more” earnest. The measure of the attention is the dignity of what was heard. This chapter does not re-prove that dignity. It uses it as the reason the drift is a departure from a great given, not a minor forgetfulness.

**What the passage does not warrant here.** That every failure of memory is apostasy. The text’s object is the things heard as the word of so great salvation. Ordinary human forgetfulness of an indifferent detail is not the sentence. That the labour of study has been specified because heed is commanded. The command identifies the contrary of drift. The method of diligence, *spoudazō*, remains Chapter 78. That Hebrews 6:4–6 has been opened because the warning continues in the letter. The falling-away passage and the foundation-list sit in the neighbourhood of the First Principles and are not this section’s assignment. The drift of 2:1 is sufficient for the mechanism now in view.

Thus:

> **Doctrinal turning aside includes a drift in which the things heard slip because earnest heed was not given. The mechanism can be quiet. The obligation to heed shows that the quiet loss is neither trivial nor fated.**

# 70.29 Biblical Record: Hebrews 3:7–4:2 — Departing by an Evil Heart of Unbelief

> “Take heed, brethren, lest there be in any of you an evil heart of unbelief, in departing from the living God. But exhort one another daily, while it is called To day; lest any of you be hardened through the deceitfulness of sin. For we are made partakers of Christ, if we hold the beginning of our confidence stedfast unto the end.”

> “For unto us was the gospel preached, as well as unto them: but the word preached did not profit them, not being mixed with faith in them that heard it.”

**Context.** The Holy Ghost’s word in Psalm 95 is the text: today, if ye will hear his voice, harden not your hearts, as in the provocation, when the fathers proved and saw his works forty years, and did not enter into the rest because of unbelief. The writer turns that word on the “brethren.” The danger is an evil heart of unbelief (καρδία πονηρὰ ἀπιστίας) in the act of departing (ἀποστῆναι, from ἀφίστημι) from the living God. The hardening instrument is the deceitfulness of sin (ἀπάτη τῆς ἁμαρτίας). The profit of the word heard depends on its being mixed with faith. The fathers’ bodies fell in the wilderness, and “they could not enter in because of unbelief” (Hebrews 3:19).

**What is explicit.** Departing from the living God is the act an evil heart of unbelief performs. The heart is addressed to hearers called brethren. Daily exhortation is the appointed resistance to hardening. Sin deceives, and the deception hardens. The word preached to the fathers did not profit them. The reason is not that the word was unspoken. The reason is that it was not mixed with faith in the hearers. They had seen the works. They did not enter. Unbelief is the named barrier.

**What the passages warrant for this turning.** The cause is unbelief toward a word heard and toward works seen. This is the chapter’s refusal to make intellectual failure a substitute for the heart. The heart’s unbelief is a cause of departing. It is intellectual in the sense that it is a response to a heard word and to a known day: today, if ye will hear his voice. It is not intellectual in the thin sense of a miscalculation apart from the person. The deceitfulness of sin is a mechanism of hardening that makes the departure feel reasonable while it closes the hearing. Deceit is a cognitive moral act: sin lies, and the heart hardens under the lie. The wilderness exhibition supplies duration, as Exodus 32 supplied speed. They saw his works forty years. Length of exposure is not itself heed. The same record therefore refuses two lazy accounts: that only the sudden turn is real, and that long association with the word is itself faithfulness. Mixture with faith is the condition of profit. Hearing without that mixture is a cause of the word’s unprofitableness in the hearer, not a defect in the word’s truth. Chapter 57 stands. The record did not fail. The hearers did not enter.

**What the passages do not warrant here.** That every believer who fears, or every church that suffers, has an evil heart of unbelief. The warning is “lest there be in any of you,” a guarded contingency, with exhortation as the help. That the rest of God has been doctrinally mapped beyond the use of this warning. The entrance refused is the text’s weight. A geography of the rest is not this chapter. That daily exhortation is a technique of institutional control. The exhortation is mutual, among brethren, while it is called today, and its content is the hearing of his voice. It is the contrary of isolated hardening.

Thus:

> **Departing from the living God proceeds from an evil heart of unbelief, hardened by sin’s deceit, in the face of a word heard and works seen. Hearing that is not mixed with faith does not profit, and the unprofitableness is in the hearers. Duration of exposure does not replace faith. The word they heard remains the word.**

# 70.30 Biblical Record: Luke 8:13 — Hearing, Joy, No Root, and Falling Away

> “They on the rock are they, which, when they hear, receive the word with joy; and these have no root, which for a while believe, and in time of temptation fall away.”

**Context.** Jesus interprets the parable of the sower. The seed is the word of God. The wayside hearers lose it to the devil before believing. The rock hearers receive it with joy, believe for a while, and in time of temptation fall away (ἀφίστανται). The thorn hearers are choked with cares, riches, and pleasures, and bring no fruit to perfection. The good ground hears, keeps, and brings forth fruit with patience.

**What is explicit.** Hearing occurs. Reception with joy occurs. Belief for a while occurs. Root is absent. Temptation is the season of the falling away. The falling away is from a belief that had a “while,” not from a silence.

**What the passage warrants for this turning.** The mechanism is a reception that does not root, meeting a temptation. Joy at hearing is not the same as root. The intellectual and moral failure is the word not held deeply enough to remain when the test comes. The cause of the fall, in the sentence, is the conjunction of no root and temptation. This exhibition answers the claim that a prior believing was proof the later fall was unreal, and the claim that a later fall proves the prior hearing never happened. The text affirms both the while of belief and the fall. Exodus and Galatians showed speed after a real given. This verse shows a while of belief and then a fall. Tempo varies. The structure does not: a word received, then a leaving. Temptation is a cause the text names. It does not, in this verse, specify every form temptation takes. The specification would be a different study. The warrant is the conjunction itself.

**What the passage does not warrant here.** That joy is suspicious. The good ground also receives the word. Joy is not the differentia. Root and fruit and patience are. That the four soils have been made into a full anthropology of conversion. The verse is used for the mechanism of falling away after a joyful, temporary belief. The rest of the parable is acknowledged and not developed into a treatise.

Thus:

> **A hearer can receive the word with joy, believe for a while, and fall away in temptation because there is no root. The prior reception is real in the text. The fall is real in the text. Joy without root is not continuance.**

# 70.31 Biblical Record: Colossians 2:4–8 — Beguiled and Carried Off

> “And this I say, lest any man should beguile you with enticing words. For though I be absent in the flesh, yet am I with you in the spirit, joying and beholding your order, and the stedfastness of your faith in Christ. As ye have therefore received Christ Jesus the Lord, so walk ye in him: rooted and built up in him, and stablished in the faith, as ye have been taught, abounding therein with thanksgiving. Beware lest any man spoil you through philosophy and vain deceit, after the tradition of men, after the rudiments of the world, and not after Christ. For in him dwelleth all the fulness of the Godhead bodily. And ye are complete in him.”

**Context.** Paul has said he would have them know the mystery of God, even Christ, in whom are hid all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge. The “this I say” guards them from being beguiled (παραλογίζομαι, *paralogizomai*, G3884) with persuasive speech (πιθανολογία, *pithanologia*, G4086). He has seen their order and the steadfastness of their faith. The walk is to match the reception: as ye received Christ Jesus the Lord, so walk in him. The warning is lest any man carry them off as spoil (συλαγωγέω, *sylagōgeō*, G4812) through philosophy and empty deceit, according to the tradition of men, according to the rudiments of the world, and not according to Christ. The reason is fullness and completeness in him.

**What is explicit.** Enticing speech can beguile. Steadfastness of faith in Christ is a present good he beholds, not a fiction. The norm of the walk is the Christ they received. The spoiling instrument is philosophy and vain deceit. The norm of that instrument is human tradition and worldly rudiments. The excluded norm is Christ. The doctrinal reason the spoiling is needless and false is that fullness dwells in him and they are complete in him.

**What the passage warrants for this turning.** Chapter 68 ranked philosophy as a secondary inquiry that must not replace revelation, and ranked tradition as a delivery that must not become primary. The present function is the carrying off. Philosophy and empty deceit are a mechanism of doctrinal turning when they move the hearer from Christ, whom he has received, to a wisdom after men and after the world’s elementary principles. The intellectual failure is consent to a persuasive speech that offers what the text says is already hid in Christ, as though Christ were not enough. “Not after Christ” is the criterion. A philosophy that is after Christ, tested by the writing and by the fullness that dwells in him, is not this spoiling. The text does not ban thought. It bans a thought that carries the church off from the one in whom they are complete. Beguiling is the intellectual operation: plausible speech (pithanology) that reasons beside the mystery. The steadfastness he beholds is the contrary already in the church, and the imperative is to walk as they received. Reception is again the premise. The turning would be a spoiling of people who have received, not the description of people who never had Christ.

**What the passage does not warrant here.** That the word philosophy, wherever it appears in later centuries, is this verse’s subject. The verse identifies a philosophy and an empty deceit characterized by human tradition, worldly rudiments, and not-according-to-Christ. Chapter 68’s rank stands. This chapter adds the carrying off as a turning-mechanism, not a new history of schools. That “complete in him” has been developed into the later doctrinal system of any particular note. The clause is the text’s reason against the spoiling. It is not a grid.

Thus:

> **Hearers who have received Christ are carried off when persuasive speech draws them through a philosophy and an empty deceit after men and after the world’s rudiments, and not after Christ. Fullness in him is the reason the supplement is a turning. Steadfastness is a real good the warning is meant to preserve.**

# 70.32 Biblical Record: Acts 20:28–32 — Perverse Speech That Draws Disciples After the Speakers

> “For I know this, that after my departing shall grievous wolves enter in among you, not sparing the flock. Also of your own selves shall men arise, speaking perverse things, to draw away disciples after them. Therefore watch, and remember, that by the space of three years I ceased not to warn every one night and day with tears. And now, brethren, I commend you to God, and to the word of his grace, which is able to build you up, and to give you an inheritance among all them which are sanctified.”

**Context.** Paul speaks to the elders of Ephesus. He has preached the kingdom and the whole counsel of God, and he is pure from the blood of all men because he has not shunned to declare it. He charges them to take heed to themselves and to the flock, over which the Holy Ghost has made them overseers, to feed the church of God. Then he names a future he knows: wolves entering, and men arising from the elders’ own company. The speech is perverse, distorted (διεστραμμένα, from διαστρέφω, *diastrephō*, G1294). The purpose clause is the mechanism’s aim: to draw away (ἀποσπάω, *apospaō*, G645) the disciples after them. The charge is to watch and to remember three years of warning. The commendation is to God and to the word of his grace, able to build up.

**What is explicit.** The danger is double in origin: from outside, and from among yourselves. The means is speech that is twisted. The aim is not the disciples’ good under the word. The aim is a following after the speakers. The warning preceded the event by a long and tearful ministry. The word of God’s grace is able to build the church up. The elders are accountable to watch.

**What the passage warrants for this turning.** The mechanism is drawing-after. A doctrinal turning is carried when perverse speech redirects the disciple’s adherence from the word to the speaker. Chapter 69 examined the teacher as a governor of meaning. The present function is the aim of the speech: ἀποσπᾶν τοὺς μαθητὰς ὀπίσω αὐτῶν, to draw the disciples away after themselves. The intellectual instrument is distorted speech. The moral aim is a personal following. An elder’s office does not prevent the mechanism. “Of your own selves” is the text’s refusal of the comfort that the danger is always foreign. The wolves are real, and the internal arising is real. Forewarning is again part of the warrant. Three years of warning make the turning a thing that could be watched for, not a sudden mystery. The positive member is explicit: the word of his grace is able to build up. The church is not commended to the elders’ personalities as the final shelter. It is commended to God and to that word. The ability of the word is the contrary of the drawing-after. A speaker who draws after himself has ceased to commend the disciples to the word and has made himself the destination.

**What the passage does not warrant here.** That every strong teacher is a wolf. The differentia is the perverse speech and the aim of drawing after oneself, not force of conviction or clarity of doctrine. Paul himself taught night and day. The difference is the destination: the counsel of God, or the speaker’s own following. That “after my departing” has been made a calendar of church history. The clause is the time after his absence from Ephesus. It is not, in this chapter, a dispensational hinge. That the word’s ability to build up has been turned into a claim that no watch is needed. The watch and the commendation are both commanded. The word’s ability is why the watch has a refuge, not why the watch is unnecessary.

Thus:

> **Turning is carried by distorted speech whose aim is to draw disciples after the speaker, from outside the flock or from among the elders. Long warning makes the danger watchable. The word of God’s grace is able to build up, and that ability is the contrary of a teacher who makes himself the destination.**

# 70.33 Biblical Record: 2 Peter 2:1–2 and 2:15 — Privily Brought In, and the Right Way Forsaken

> “But there were false prophets also among the people, even as there shall be false teachers among you, who privily shall bring in damnable heresies, even denying the Lord that bought them, and bring upon themselves swift destruction. And many shall follow their pernicious ways; by reason of whom the way of truth shall be evil spoken of.”

> “Which have forsaken the right way, and are gone astray, following the way of Balaam the son of Bosor, who loved the wages of unrighteousness.”

**Context.** Peter has said that prophecy came not by the will of man, but holy men of God spake as they were moved by the Holy Ghost. He then sets false prophets among the people and false teachers among you in parallel. The bringing-in is privy (παρεισάγω, *pareisagō*, G3919): brought in beside, alongside. The objects are αἱρέσεις ἀπωλείας, heresies of destruction, rendered “damnable heresies.” The denial is of the Lord that bought them, the δεσπότης (*despotēs*) who purchased them. Swift destruction is theirs. Many will follow. The way of truth is blasphemed by reason of them. Verse 15 describes those who have abandoned the straight way (καταλείποντες εὐθεῖαν ὁδόν) and wandered, following Balaam’s way, who loved the wages of unrighteousness.

**What is explicit.** False teachers are a stated future among the readers, on the pattern of false prophets in Israel. The method is covert introduction beside, not only open replacement announced as replacement. The content includes denial of the Master who bought them. Many follow. The way of truth is spoken against because of the followers and the teachers. The same people have forsaken a way that was straight. Balaam’s way is a following motivated by wages.

**What the passages warrant for this turning.** The mechanism of covert introduction belongs to this chapter. A doctrinal turning is often carried by what is brought in alongside the faith, so that the departure feels like an addition until the denial is plain. “Beside” is the force of the compound. The forsaking of the straight way is the turning proper: there was a right way, and they left it, and they went astray. Wages name a cause joined to the forsaking, as gain was joined to the subversion of houses. “Many shall follow” records a following. It does not yet examine how an erroneous doctrine becomes the normalized, institutionalized doctrine of a body, and it does not yet answer why that acceptance cannot turn error into truth. Those are Chapter 71’s sentences. The present warrant stops at the mechanism: privy bringing-in, denial, forsaking of the straight way, a following that causes the way of truth to be evil spoken of. The way of truth remains the way of truth while it is evil spoken of. Slander does not relocate it. That is the first member of the positive warrant, applied to a text that could be misread as if the way had been ruined by the followers.

**What the passages do not warrant here.** That the word *hairesis* has been given its full chapter. The destructive heresies are the cargo of the privy act. Their acceptance as the ordinary doctrine, and the impotence of acceptance to make them true, remain the next assignment. That “the Lord that bought them” has been made the occasion of a system about the extent of redemption beyond what this turning requires. The clause identifies the one denied. The denial is the doctrinal turning in view. A separate treatise on purchase is not this section. That Balaam’s story has been retold from outside the letter. The letter gives the feature it needs: he loved the wages of unrighteousness, and they followed that way after forsaking the straight one.

Thus:

> **False teachers carry doctrinal turning by bringing destructive teaching in beside the faith, even to denying the Master who bought them, and by forsaking the straight way. Many follow, and the way of truth is slandered without ceasing to be the way. The following is visible. The normalization of error is the next chapter.**

# 70.34 Biblical Record: 2 Peter 3:15–18 — Unlearned and Unstable Wresting

> “And account that the longsuffering of our Lord is salvation; even as our beloved brother Paul also according to the wisdom given unto him hath written unto you; as also in all his epistles, speaking in them of these things; in which are some things hard to be understood, which they that are unlearned and unstable wrest, as they do also the other scriptures, unto their own destruction. Ye therefore, beloved, seeing ye know these things before, beware lest ye also, being led away with the error of the wicked, fall from your own stedfastness. But grow in grace, and in the knowledge of our Lord and Saviour Jesus Christ.”

**Context.** Peter writes of the Lord’s longsuffering and of the day of the Lord, against scoffers who walk after their own lusts. He joins Paul’s epistles to that subject and calls them scriptures in the same sentence that warns about the other scriptures. Some things in them are hard to be understood (δυσνόητα). The wrestlers are the unlearned (ἀμαθεῖς, *amathēs*, G261) and the unstable (ἀστήρικτοι, *astēriktos*, G793). The verb is twist (στρεβλόω). The range is “as they do also the other scriptures.” The end is their own destruction. The readers know these things beforehand. The danger is being led away with (συναπαχθέντες, from συναπάγω, *synapagō*, G4879) the error (πλάνη, *planē*, G4106) of the lawless, and falling (ἐκπίπτω, *ekpiptō*, G1601) from their own steadfastness (στηριγμός, *stērigmos*, G4740). The contrary command is growth in grace and in the knowledge of the Lord and Saviour.

**What is explicit.** Hardness of some things is conceded. The subjects who wrest are characterized by lack of learning and by lack of fixedness. The wresting is not confined to Paul. It is their manner with the scriptures. The end is destruction. Prior knowledge is the ground of the beware. The leading-away is a real possibility for the beloved. Steadfastness is “your own,” a possession one can fall from. Growth in grace and knowledge is the positive command.

**What the passage warrants for this turning.** This is the assignment’s central identification of intellectual failure. The failure that permits the twist, and the destruction that follows the twist, is named as unlearnedness and instability. *Amathēs* is not *apeiros*, and this chapter does not pretend it is. It is the text’s own word for the unlearned whose handling wrenches what is hard. *Astēriktos* is the unfixed, the unstable, the mind not set. Together they are the condition under which difficulty becomes an occasion of distortion. The text separates the difficulty from the distortion by the grammar. “Which” are hard. “Which they” wrest. A hard sentence can be left hard, and handled as hard, without being twisted. The intellectual failure is the combination of ignorance of the proper learning and absence of stability, issuing in a violent handling. Chapter 67 already used improper handling as a mode. The present function is the departure: the wresting is unto destruction, and the next verse warns of being led away and of falling from steadfastness. The mechanism of being led away with the error of the wicked is social and doctrinal at once. Error has a company. The unstable are carried with it. Prior knowledge is why the carrying is a falling from steadfastness and not an original innocence. “Seeing ye know these things before” is the inspectability of the second positive warrant, in the imperative mood.

**What the passage does not warrant here.** That “unlearned” means unintelligent, or that learning in the later academic sense is the salvation of the reader. The contrast in the letter is with the wresting of the scriptures, and the growth commanded is growth in grace and in the knowledge of Christ. Movement X will examine the labour by which that knowledge is sought in the word. This verse identifies the failure. It does not write the curriculum. That every difficult sentence has been declared twisted. Hardness is not wresting. That Paul’s letters have been ranked below “the other scriptures,” or above them. The sentence calls the wresting of Paul the same kind of act as the wresting of the other scriptures, which places the epistles with the scriptures as objects of the same abuse and the same dignity as writings. Chapter 53’s account of apostolic authority is in force and is not retried. That steadfastness is the hearer’s infallibility. It is a standing one is warned not to fall from. The record remains the infallible thing. The hearer remains the one who can be led away.

Thus:

> **The intellectual failure that permits wresting is being unlearned and unstable. Hardness is not the cause. The twist is. The twist runs to destruction, and the unstable can be led away with the error of the wicked and fall from their own steadfastness. Knowing beforehand is the ground of the warning. Growth in the knowledge of Christ is the contrary.**

# 70.35 Biblical Record: James 5:19–20 — Erring from the Truth, and Conversion

> “Brethren, if any of you do err from the truth, and one convert him; let him know, that he which converteth the sinner from the error of his way shall save a soul from death, and shall hide a multitude of sins.”

**Context.** James closes the letter. The prior verse has spoken of a brother who errs (πλανάω) from the truth (ἀπὸ τῆς ἀληθείας), and of one who turns him back (ἐπιστρέφω, *epistrephō*, G1994). The converter turns a sinner from the error of his way. The effect is saving a soul from death and covering a multitude of sins. The address is “brethren,” and the “if any of you” places the erring inside the company addressed.

**What is explicit.** Erring from the truth is a real possibility among the brethren. Conversion back is a real act of another. The one converted is described, in the next clause, as a sinner in the error of his way. The stakes are death and a multitude of sins. The truth is the “from” of the wandering and the destination of the return.

**What the passage warrants for this turning.** Departure from truth can occur inside the brotherhood and can be reversed by a turning back to the truth. The positive warrant’s third member is not an inference from silence. It is this sentence. The truth does not vanish when a brother errs from it. If it vanished, conversion to it would have no object. The gravity is also in the sentence. The error is a way whose end, unconverted, is death. Harmlessness is not available. The converter’s act is not described as the invention of a new truth. It is a turning of the wanderer back. Second Timothy 2:24–26 stands with this exhibition and is not a second theme: the servant of the Lord, in meekness instructing those that oppose themselves, looks for repentance unto the acknowledging of the truth, and for recovery out of the snare of the devil, who had taken them captive. Acknowledging the truth is the intellectual contrary of the error. Recovery is the movement contrary of the capture. The chapter does not write a technique of pastoral restoration. It receives the texts’ assertion that return is a real movement because the truth is a real object.

**What the passage does not warrant here.** That every doctrinal question is “the error of his way.” The text names erring from the truth. Chapter 70’s definition already refused to make every unsettled question a tergiversation. That conversion is automatic, or that every wanderer is in fact brought back. The sentence is conditional: if any err, and one convert him. The possibility is warranted. A census of restorations is not. That the death in view has been doctrinally subdivided beyond the text’s warning. The weight is that the error is not a small alternative lifestyle. The precise account of death’s kinds is not required for that weight and is not opened here.

Thus:

> **A brother can err from the truth, and a brother can convert him back. The truth remains the object of the return. The error is grave enough to be a way toward death. Return is intelligible because departure did not redefine the truth departed from.**

# 70.36 Biblical Record: Matthew 22:29 as the Join of Not-Knowing and Erring

> “Ye do err, not knowing the scriptures, nor the power of God.”

**Context.** The Sadducees, who say there is no resurrection, pose the case of the seven brothers and the one wife. Jesus answers. Chapter 67 used the sentence for deficient understanding. Chapter 69 used the scene for a doctrine that pre-decides what the text may mean. The present function is neither a second catalog entry nor a second displacement entry. It is the join the verb and the participle state between erring and not knowing.

**What is explicit.** πλανᾶσθε, ye do err, ye are deceived, ye wander. The participle μὴ εἰδότες names not-knowing of the scriptures and not-knowing of the power of God as the condition in which the error is identified. The correction is a sentence they are told they have read: I am the God of Abraham, and the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob.

**What the passage warrants for this turning.** Not-knowing the scriptures is an intellectual failure joined, by the speaker who is the truth, to the act of erring. The join is the coherent biblical form of the claim that ignorance of the writing permits doctrinal departure. It does not say that not-knowing is the only cause in every passage this chapter has examined. It says that in this exhibition the error and the not-knowing are one rebuke. The power of God is joined to the scriptures, so the failure is not a merely literary gap. A doctrine that makes the resurrection absurd has wandered, and the wandering is located in not knowing what was spoken and not knowing what God can do. The intellectual failure is culpable in the mouth of Jesus. He does not treat it as a misfortune that excuses the denial. He treats it as the reason they err. “Have ye not read” returns them to a text they possessed. Possession without knowing is the failure. Quotation of a levirate law, which they did use, was not knowledge of the scriptures that bore on the question.

**What the passage does not warrant here.** That every error in the world reduces to one unread verse. The sentence is the diagnosis of this denial. Other exhibitions in this chapter name lust, deceit, heed transferred, and wages, and those causes are not erased by this participle. That the Sadducean party has been examined as an accepted heresy in the sense of Chapter 71. The party’s doctrine was already in force as a system that governs meaning. The new use is the join of not-knowing and erring.

Thus:

> **Erring is joined by Christ to not knowing the scriptures and not knowing the power of God. The intellectual failure permits the departure in the sense that it is the condition he names inside the error. It does not excuse the error, and it does not become the only cause the rest of the canon is allowed to name.**

# 70.37 The Intellectual Failures Identified

The failures may now be stated as a list the passages warrant, not as a list a system prefers.

**Failure to understand what one affirms.** The desire to be a teacher of the law, joined to understanding neither one’s sentences nor one’s subject, permits the turn into vain jangling. Speech continues. Knowledge of the speech does not.

**Failure to consent to wholesome words.** Other-teaching that will not come to the words of the Lord Jesus and to the doctrine according to godliness is the condition of the man who knows nothing and is destitute of the truth. Non-consent is a decision of the mind against the sound word.

**Failure of learning and of stability in the face of what is hard.** The unlearned and the unstable wrest the hard things and the other scriptures. The hardness is given. The learning and the fixedness are the conditions whose absence permits the twist.

**Failure to endure sound doctrine.** The healthy teaching is the object not borne. The failure is the first clause in the mechanism that ends at fables. A hearer who will not endure the doctrine will not stay with the truth the doctrine states.

**Failure of heed.** Heed withheld from the things heard permits drift. Heed transferred to seducing spirits and doctrines of demons is the manner of departing from the faith. Heed given to fables and to commandments of men is the manner of being turned from the truth. Heed is the common intellectual act. Its direction is the difference between continuance and departure.

**Failure of knowing the scriptures one can possess.** Erring, in the Sadducean exhibition, is not-knowing what has been written and what God can do. Possession of a quotable statute is not that knowledge.

These failures permit departure. They are openings through which the movement passes. In the same canonical neighbourhood, and often in the same sentence, the writings name an evil heart of unbelief, the deceitfulness of sin, lusts, a seared conscience, the love of wages, and hypocrisy. The identification of the intellectual failures is the assignment. The erasure of the other causes would be a mutilation of the paragraphs. The erasure of the intellectual failures, on the ground that only vice matters, would be the opposite mutilation. The texts teach the join.

The failures do not pardon. Destruction, overthrow, curse, not entering, and a soul in danger of death are the weights attached to the wanderings these failures permit. A man who does not understand what he affirms is still accountable for the affirmation. An unstable wrestler is not a harmless beginner. The beginner’s dedicated diagnosis, as babe and as *apeiros*, is a later chapter. The wrestler’s end, in this chapter’s text, is his own destruction.

Thus:

> **The intellectual failures that permit departure from truth are failures of understanding what is affirmed, of consent to sound words, of learning and stability before what is hard, of endurance of sound doctrine, of the direction of heed, and of knowing the scriptures one possesses. They stand with unbelief, lust, deceit, and gain. They open the turning. They do not excuse it.**

# 70.38 Mechanism: Substitution That Keeps the Sentence

One operation recurs often enough to be named as a mechanism across exhibitions.

The calf receives the sentence that belongs to the Lord: these brought thee up. The different gospel receives the name that belongs to the gospel received. The fable receives the ear that belongs to the truth. The resurrection “already past” receives the doctrinal dignity that belongs to the resurrection hoped. The falsely named knowledge receives the reverence that belongs to the deposit. The speaker receives the following that belongs to the word of God’s grace. The human commandment receives the heed that belongs to the truth.

Substitution keeps a word, a history, a hope, or a reverence, and moves it onto another object. The keeping is why the turning can be sincere in its vocabulary and false in its object. Sincerity has already been refused as a determinant of truth. The present point is mechanical. Retained language is how departure travels inside a people who would resist an announced abandonment. Aaron’s “there came out this calf” is substitution at the level of explanation: the effect is narrated without the act. Galatians’ “which is not another” is the apostolic refusal of substitution at the level of the name. The name “gospel” was kept. The identity was not.

Detection follows the first positive warrant. Ask what the sentence belonged to, and what it is now assigned to. If the exodus is assigned to the calf, the sentence has been substituted. If the gospel received is replaced by a different message under the same name, the name has been substituted. If the ear’s object is a fable, the organ has been substituted away from the truth. The test is the writing’s own given, not a hearer’s impression that the words still sound holy.

Thus:

> **Substitution keeps the holy sentence, the holy name, or the holy reverence, and assigns it to another object. The keeping disguises the turning. The assignment is the turning. The given truth detects the exchange.**

# 70.39 Mechanism: The Small Contrary That Spreads

A second operation is the leaven and the gangrene.

Galatians: a little leaven leavens the whole lump. The persuasion is not from the caller, and the hindrance is a cutting-in on a good race, not necessarily a demolition of every doctrine on the first day. Second Timothy 2: the empty talk increases unto more ungodliness, and the word eats as a gangrene, and the faith of some is overthrown by a sentence. Second Peter 2: heresies are brought in beside, and many follow, and the way of truth is evil spoken of. Titus: whole houses are subverted.

The mechanism is spread by a small, adjacent, or increasing contrary. The turning does not always arrive as a complete alternative system delivered on a single morning. It arrives as a persuasion, a sentence, a privy cargo, a teaching that ought not to be taught, and it works through. The figure of leaven is the text’s, and the figure of gangrene is the text’s. Both mean that the quantity at the start is a poor measure of the effect at the end. “Little” and “beside” and “increase” are the relevant marks.

This mechanism is why a people can be removed “so soon” and also why a household can be subverted without the whole church having voted. The scale varies. The operation is the working-through of a contrary that was allowed a place. Chapter 71 will ask how such a contrary becomes the accepted doctrine of a body and why the acceptance is not alchemy. The present chapter establishes the spread as a mechanism of the turning itself. The lump is leavened by the little. The some are overthrown by the word that eats. The many follow. Those are already effects of the mechanism, stated here as effects, and not as the normalization-file.

Thus:

> **A little contrary, a single false sentence, a cargo brought in beside, can spread through a lump, a household, or a company of disciples. Smallness at the start is not innocence. Spread is a mechanism of turning aside.**

# 70.40 Mechanism: Selection of Teachers by the Ear’s Appetite

A third operation is the heap.

Second Timothy 4:3–4 orders it. Non-endurance of sound doctrine issues in a gathering of teachers according to one’s own lusts, for itching ears, and the ears turn from the truth unto fables. Isaiah 30:10–11 is the same operation in the imperative mood of the hearers: say to the prophets, do not prophesy right things; speak smooth things; turn aside out of the path. Acts 20:30 is the operation from the speaker’s side: the speech is shaped to draw disciples after the speaker. The two sides meet. Hearers heap teachers who will scratch the ear. Speakers distort speech to become the destination of the disciples.

The intellectual failure inside the mechanism is the replacement of sound doctrine by appetite as the criterion of whom to hear. The criterion is a doctrinal criterion even while it is a lustful one, because what is accepted or refused is doctrine. Healthy teaching is the thing not endured. Fables are the thing turned unto. The number of teachers is a symptom. One teacher who turns the ear is enough for the mechanism in a single hearer. The heap is the text’s picture of an appetite that one healthy voice will not satisfy. The remedy in the charge is not a smaller heap chosen by the same itch. The remedy is preaching the word, reproving, rebuking, exhorting, with doctrine. That charge is the contrary. It is not yet the full discipline of interpretation.

Governance from Chapter 69 can feed this mechanism. A teacher who already finishes the sense is a ready candidate for a heap, because the ear that will not endure the writing will endure a mouth that makes the writing comfortable. The feeding is a condition. It is not the identity of the two files. A hearer can heap teachers in a field where no single commentary has been installed as the official court. The itch is sufficient to gather them.

Thus:

> **Teachers are selected by the ear that will not endure sound doctrine, and speakers shape speech to draw disciples after themselves. Appetite replaces the healthy teaching as the criterion of hearing. The fable is the destination. The word preached and the word of grace are the contraries.**

# 70.41 Mechanism: Transfer and Withholding of Heed

A fourth operation is the direction of προσέχω (*prosechō*), heed.

First Timothy 4:1: departing from the faith, giving heed to seducing spirits and doctrines of devils. First Timothy 1:4: neither give heed to fables and endless genealogies. Titus 1:14: not giving heed to Jewish fables and commandments of men that turn from the truth. Hebrews 2:1: give the more earnest heed to the things heard, lest they slip. Deuteronomy 13, already used in Chapter 69 for the sign that must not govern the commandment, names the mechanism of turning in the verb of the tempter’s aim: he has spoken to turn you away, to thrust you out of the way which the Lord thy God commanded you to walk in. The commanded response is not to hearken. Hearkening to the sign-bearer, when the message is other gods, is the heed that carries the thrust.

Heed is the mechanism’s hinge because it is the act that aims the mind. Withheld from the things heard, it permits drift. Given to another spirit, another doctrine, another fable, or another commander, it carries departure. The faith is not usually left by a philosophical proof that nothing is true. It is left by attendance moving house. The positive commands are therefore commands about attendance: heed the things heard; do not heed the fables; do not hearken to the wonder that revises the commandment. The intellectual character of the failure is this aim of the mind. A man is where his heed is. If his heed is at the fable, he has turned, even if the scroll is still in the room.

Chapter 69’s reversed test is the institutional form of a captured court. The present mechanism is the personal and congregational form of captured attention. They can coincide. They are named as mechanism and as governance because the assignment separated the questions. A court can reverse its test while a remnant’s heed remains on the writing. A hearer’s heed can move while the official court has not yet installed a governor. The hinge is still heed.

Thus:

> **Heed carries the turning when it is transferred to another doctrine, another spirit, a fable, or a sign that thrusts out of the way, and when it is withheld from the things heard. Where the mind attends, the person is. Drift and departure are the two failures of that attendance.**

# 70.42 Mechanism: Twist, Beguilement, and the Plausible Word

A fifth operation is distorted handling that still sounds like reason.

Second Peter 3:16: wresting. Colossians 2:4: beguiling with enticing words. Acts 20:30: speaking perverse things. Second Timothy 2:18: a sentence that relocates the resurrection. Matthew 4, already in force from Chapter 69, exhibited a true writing cited to produce a false step, answered by what is written again. The present use of that exhibition is narrow: a turning can be carried by a scripture twisted, not only by a scripture denied. The tempter’s “it is written” is a mechanism of leading aside. The Lord’s “it is written again” is the restoration of the path. The discipline of that restoration as a labour remains Movement X. The existence of the mechanism belongs here, because doctrinal turning aside is not limited to people who throw the writing away. It includes people who wrench it.

The intellectual failures that permit the wrench are the ones 2 Peter names, unlearned and unstable, and the one 1 Timothy 1 names, not understanding what one affirms, and the one Matthew 22 names, not knowing the scriptures. Plausible speech exploits the failure. Pithanology is speech built to persuade. It becomes beguilement when it moves the hearer from Christ, in whom the treasures of wisdom are hid, to a wisdom not after him. The hearer’s duty is not to become unpersuadable. It is to hold the faithfulness of the word he has been taught, and to test the persuasion’s origin, as Galatians tests a persuasion that cometh not of him that calleth you. A plausible word from the wrong source is a mechanism. A true word from the writing is not suspect because it is clear.

Thus:

> **Turning is carried by wresting, by enticing speech, and by a true line used for a false step. The twist is permitted by unlearned instability, by affirmation without understanding, and by not knowing the scriptures. Plausibility is not proof. The writing, including what is written again, remains the measure of the plausible word.**

# 70.43 Governance as a Condition, Not as the Whole Cause

Chapter 69 is a premise, and it must be used in its own force without being enlarged into this file or shrunk into a preamble that did no work.

Where a commentary, a tradition, an institution, a teacher, a system, or an experience governs the meaning of Scripture, the hearer is in a condition where leaving the truth can be called fidelity to the governor. The tradition that makes a commandment of none effect trains a people to turn aside from the duty while quoting the duty’s vocabulary. The other gospel that governs a church is already the destination of a removal. The dream that is told as the word trains the ear to heed a fable. These are real conditions. They explain why a turning under a governor often feels like obedience. The feeling is the disguise substitution produces when the governor supplies the substituted object.

The condition is not the whole cause. The same writings describe turnings in which the decisive clause is the hearer’s non-endurance, the hearer’s itch, the hearer’s unbelief, the hearer’s instability, the hearer’s hearkening, the hearer’s desire to be a teacher without understanding. A governor may be absent, or may be resisted, and the person may still turn. A governor may be present, and a hearer may still search whether the things are so, as the Bereans did with a true teacher, an exhibition already in force as service and as the contrary of captured heed. The existence of the governor does not force every member through the turning. “Some” and “lest any of you” and “who did hinder you” keep agency visible on more than one side.

To say “governance is the only cause” would reprint Chapter 69 as Chapter 70 and would silence the ear, the heart, the wages, and the wresting. To say “governance is irrelevant to turning” would ignore the destination the governor provides and the disguise it offers. The accurate relation is condition and mechanism, not identity. Governance can supply the object turned unto, the vocabulary retained, and the social permission to call the leaving faithfulness. The movement of the person or the people is still the turning, and it still has the causes this chapter has identified.

Thus:

> **A governing secondary source is a condition under which departure is easily named faithfulness. It can supply the substitute and the disguise. It is not the whole cause, and its absence is not the absence of danger. The turning remains the movement, with its own causes and its own intellectual failures.**

# 70.44 The Five Modes as Instruments, Not as This File Retried

Poor perception, deficient understanding, faulty inference, context failure, and improper handling can each carry a departure. The Sadducees’ not-knowing is deficient understanding joined to erring. The wresting of a hard text is improper handling joined to destruction. A citation torn from its place, as in the temptation, is context failure used as a step off the path. A conclusion that the resurrection is already past is a faulty inference, or a faulty assertion, concerning the truth, with overthrow as its effect. Perception that will not see Christ placarded, and will not see the works of forty years, is poor perception joined to not obeying and to not entering.

The catalog is used, not rewritten. No sixth mode is added. The modes answer how a mind mishandles a writing. This chapter answers how a person or a people move from a truth, and which of those mishandlings, together with unbelief, lust, deceit, and misdirected heed, permit the movement. A mode can be repented of while the person is still turned toward the writing, as correction is not tergiversation. A mode becomes an instrument of tergiversation when it is the carriage of a leaving: the twist by which one falls from steadfastness, the not-knowing by which one denies what God said, the non-endurance by which the ear changes objects.

The distinction preserves both files. Chapter 67 does not become a footnote, and Chapter 70 does not become a repetition. The reader who has learned the modes now sees what they can carry. The reader who is learning the turning is not asked to re-earn the modes in order to trust the carriage.

Thus:

> **The five modes are instruments by which a turning can be carried. They are not themselves the definition of turning, and this chapter is not their second catalog. A mishandling carries tergiversation when it is the means of leaving a given truth.**

# 70.45 Reconstructing the Bible-Introduction Proposition: Classes Who Find the Writing Hard, and the Unstable Who Wrest

A reconstructible proposition from the Bible Introduction materials lists classes who find the Bible hard to understand: the ignorant of its contents; the lazy; the biased contrary to its doctrines; rebels who have listened to religious teachers who explain it away; the uninterested, under a theory that it is hard; the worldly whom it condemns; unstable souls who wrest it to their own destruction; deceivers who use it for their own interests; those whom Satan blinds; and those who refuse to be converted. The same materials say that these reasons are willful, and that people do not have to be in that condition.

The proposition is not adopted as “the source says.” It is tested.

**What is claimed.** Difficulty in understanding is located in the readers’ conditions rather than in a writing that cannot speak. Among those conditions are bias, teachers who explain the writing away, wresting by the unstable, and use of the writing for one’s own interests. The conditions are treated as avoidable.

**Premises already in force.** The record communicates (Chapter 57). Misreading is the reader’s failure in named modes (Chapter 67). Teachers who explain the writing away govern rather than serve (Chapter 69). Second Peter 3:16 distinguishes hard things from wresting by the unlearned and unstable. Acts 20 distinguishes a teacher who draws disciples after himself. Galatians distinguishes troublers who pervert from churches who are moved and are still rebuked.

**Biblical correspondence.** Matthew 15, which the materials cite for bias, exhibits honour of God with the mouth and a heart far from him, teaching for doctrines the commandments of men. That correspondence holds: a prior commitment contrary to the commandment is a condition of not hearing. Second Peter 3:16 holds as the materials’ seventh class, and it is this chapter’s own text: unstable wresting unto destruction. Second Corinthians 4:2 refuses handling the word of God deceitfully, which corresponds to the class of deceivers who use the writing for their own interests, and it corresponds to drawing-after and to wages. Teachers who explain the writing away correspond to the troublers, the privy introducers, and the mouths that must be stopped.

**What follows.** The coherent remainder is received. Doctrinal turning aside is permitted by conditions of the hearer and of the teacher, not by a writing that has failed to be a word. Bias against the doctrine, heed to teachers who void the word, wresting by the unstable, and self-interested handling are real openings, and they match mechanisms this chapter has established from the texts themselves. The materials’ insistence that these conditions are not a fate imposed by the writing coheres with the commands to heed, to continue, and to beware.

**What does not follow, and is not adopted.** A single psychology in which every turning is nothing but willfulness, so that hindrance, bewitchment, being led away, and being deceived drop out of the record. Galatians rebukes the churches and names the hinderer. Second Peter warns the beloved lest they be led away. Second Timothy 3 joins deceiving with being deceived. Agency of the turner is true. Agency of the carrier is true. A total willfulness erases the second. A total victimhood erases the first. Neither is adopted. The claim that the only alternative to these classes is “simply reading and believing,” said in a way that denies hard things and denies that Christ and the apostles expounded the writings, is not adopted. Second Peter 3:16 affirms hard things. Luke 24 affirms expounding. Movement X remains the labour. This chapter receives only the remainder that belongs to its question: the openings are in the hearers and the handlers, the writing remains the measure, and the openings are not one simplified will.

Thus:

> **The reconstructible list is received where it locates wresting, bias, explaining-away, and self-interested handling in men rather than in a speechless writing. A single willfulness that erases hinderers, and a simplicity that erases hard texts and real expounding, are not adopted.**

# 70.46 Reconstructing the Bible-Introduction Proposition: “As It Is Written” as the Detection of the Turn

A further reconstructible proposition from the same materials states that “as it is written” is one of the safest guides to get truth and to discover fallacy, and that any doctrine out of harmony with any written statement of Scripture is false.

The proposition is not adopted as “the source says.” It is tested. Chapter 69 already received the direction of the test and refused a mechanical counting of verses as a new governor. The present test asks only what the proposition contributes to the detection of turning.

**What is claimed.** A doctrine that does not stand with the writing is a fallacy, and the writing is the instrument by which the fallacy is discovered.

**Premises.** Isaiah 8:20: if they speak not according to this word, it is because there is no light in them. Galatians 1:8–9: the other gospel is judged by the gospel received. Second Timothy 2:18: the sentence “the resurrection is past already” is an error concerning the truth. Chapter 69: the test restored asks whether the source speaks according to the writing.

**Biblical correspondence.** The exhibitions of turning are detected by a given word. The calf is exposed because the way was commanded and the Lord brought them up. The different gospel is exposed because the received gospel is still preached as the measure. The fable is exposed because the truth is still the ear’s proper object. The relocated resurrection is exposed because the truth concerning the resurrection is still the apostolic gospel. Detection is comparison with what is written and with what was received as that writing’s communication.

**What follows.** Disharmony with the writing is how a doctrinal turning is known as a turning and not as a development. A teaching that cannot stand with the given truth has moved. The movement may still call itself the faith. The detection does not depend on the teacher’s tone, the teaching’s age, or the number of its followers. “Many shall follow” does not change the test. This is the first positive warrant in the form of a criterion: the truth turned from measures the doctrine that claims to replace it.

**What does not follow, and is not adopted.** That any isolated clause, torn from the corpus, is enough to declare a doctrine false. The tempter’s “it is written” was disharmony manufactured by isolation, and Chapter 67 named that manufacture as a mode. That a margin’s rule of two or three verses is the detection-method. Chapter 69 refused that rule as a governor, and this chapter does not reinstall it as a detector. The full labour of holding passages together remains Movement X. The coherent remainder is the direction: what is written detects the fallacy of a doctrine that has turned from it. The detector is the writing, not a private counting superimposed on the writing.

Thus:

> **A doctrinal turning is detected by disharmony with the truth the writing has given. “As it is written” is received as that direction of detection. Isolation of a clause, and a mechanical count of clauses, are not adopted as the method.**

# 70.47 Reconstructing the Bible-Introduction Proposition: Dullness Before What Was Delivered

A further reconstructible proposition, in notes on Hebrews 5:11–14, states that the many things hard to utter are hard because the hearers are dull, not because the things are mysteries; that dullness (νωθρός, *nōthros*, G3576) is slowness and sloth; that the hearers have been in the way long enough that they ought to be teachers and instead need the first principles again; and that the unskilful are the inexperienced, dull of hearing and inexperienced in the word.

The proposition is not adopted as “the source says.” It is tested only for what may be used now.

**What is claimed.** Slowness to grasp what was delivered is a condition of the hearers. Time spent is not the same as growth. Inexperience in the word is the condition of those who need milk.

**What may be used now.** The claim that a delivered doctrine can be slow to be grasped because of the hearers coheres with Hebrews 2:1 and 3:12–4:2, already used: the things heard can slip, and the word heard can fail to profit when not mixed with faith. Time is not heed, as forty years of seeing was not entrance. That coherence belongs to this chapter’s causes. A people can have the word within reach and still turn, because dull hearing does not hold what was delivered.

**What may not be used as finished.** The dedicated account of dullness as the loss of reasonability is Chapter 72. The dedicated account of *apeiros* and of the babe is Chapter 73. The first principles as a foundation to be gone on from are the book’s end, not this file. The materials’ further claim that milk-feeding was a common metaphor among sacred and profane writers is a historical remark, not a premise this chapter needs. The present use stops at the coherence already stated: dullness of hearing permits a word to be present and unheld. The pathology of that dullness, and the skill that answers it, are later questions the turning itself now justifies asking. They are not answered by being mentioned.

Thus:

> **Dull hearing can leave a delivered word unheld, and time in the way is not itself grasp. That coherence is received. Aridity, *apeiros*, and the first principles are not written by the reception.**

# 70.48 Reconstructing the Aletheia Proposition: The Asserted Causal Chain, Tested

A reconstructible proposition from the project’s own doctrinal materials states that the absence of reasonability, understood as sound judgment, and the presence of unskillfulness are responsible for aridity and for tergiversation among persons and among age-old organizations ostensibly Christian; that damnable accepted heresies called doctrines ensue; and that the lack of skill is blamed on failure to realize the scholarly impetus of the scripture and to labour with the diligence the writing requires.

The proposition is not adopted as “the source says.” It is tested. Earlier chapters reserved this chain as a pathology not yet earned. This chapter is the file in which tergiversation is the assigned object. The test asks what part of the chain the present evidence establishes, and what part remains in the files still later.

**What is claimed.** A causal order: failed judgment and failed skill produce dryness, turning, and then accepted heresies treated as doctrines. The root blamed is failure to recognize that the writing demands labour and failure to give that labour. Persons and organizations that claim the Christian name are both in view.

**Premises.** Intellectual failures permit departure (the present argument, warranted from the passages). Not-knowing the scriptures is joined to erring (Matthew 22:29). Unlearned instability wrests unto destruction (2 Peter 3:16). Non-endurance of sound doctrine ends at fables (2 Timothy 4:3–4). Organizations and houses are not exempt (Acts 20:30; Titus 1:11; Chapter 68’s refusal of the Christian name as a shield). Truth is not created by institutional acceptance (Chapters 1–3, already in force).

**Biblical correspondence.** The correspondence that holds is the correspondence between intellectual failure and departure. The writings do attach not-understanding, not-knowing, not-learning, instability, non-consent, non-endurance, and misdirected heed to turning aside. They do attach that attachment to persons, to churches, to households, to elders arising from within, and to priests who left the way. An organization that calls itself Christian is not, by the name, protected from the turning. “Ostensibly” is a just caution, and Chapter 68 already warranted it: saying is not being. The correspondence that does not hold, as a finding of this chapter, is the single ordered chain in which aridity is the first product, tergiversation the second, and accepted heresies the third, with *spoudazō* as the proved root. The writings examined here are plural in their causes. They do not say “predominantly” as a statistic. They do not use the word aridity. They do not, in these passages, open the lexical file of *apeiros*. They do not, in these passages, perform the labour-chapter of diligence. A chain that arranges later chapter-titles into one sentence of blame is a map of the book’s remaining questions. It is not a conclusion those questions have already delivered.

**What follows.** The coherent remainder is received with full force at this level. Intellectual failures permit doctrinal turning aside, among persons and among bodies that claim the name of Christ. The failures do not excuse the turning. The name does not prevent it. The ensuing of accepted heresies, the dryness of reasonability, the diagnosis of unskill, and the labour of diligence are real next questions, because a turning that is permitted by not-knowing and not-enduring raises them. Raising them is this chapter’s transition, not this chapter’s theft.

**What does not follow, and is not adopted.** The adverb “predominantly,” as though the heart’s unbelief, the lust of the ear, the wages of Balaam, and the deceit of sin had been weighed and found minor. They stand in the texts beside the intellectual failures. The blame-line that treats *spoudazō* as already demonstrated is not adopted. The word will have its chapter. Using it now as the finished root would close Movement X inside a leaflet’s sentence. The identification of “damnable accepted heresies” with the leaflet’s own later constructions is not adopted, and it is not adopted in the opposite direction either: this test does not declare those constructions to be the heresies of 2 Peter 2:1. It refuses both the self-exemption and the premature verdict.

Thus:

> **The reconstructible chain is received where it joins intellectual failure to doctrinal turning, for persons and for bodies that claim Christ’s name. It is not adopted as a finished sequence in which aridity, accepted heresies, unskillfulness, and the labour of diligence have already been established. Those remain later. The plurality of causes in the writings remains in force.**

# 70.49 Reconstructing the Aletheia Proposition: The Warning Does Not Certify the Warner’s Grid

A second reconstructible feature of the same materials is that, having named tergiversation as the fruit of unskill and of failed judgment, they proceed to state what repentance, faith, baptisms, resurrection, and judgment are to mean, including counts of baptisms, sequences of resurrections and raptures, and a map of the underworld, and they attach those constructions to the passages by citation.

The feature is not adopted as “the source says.” It is tested as an illustration of this chapter’s object, not as a biography and not as a verdict of motives. Chapter 69 already refused the grid the seat of sense. The present question is different: whether a warning against turning aside certifies the warner, and whether a construction laid on the warning can itself be a turning.

**What is happening in the materials.** A true concern — that persons and churches turn aside from the writing — is stated. Then a system is spoken as the sense of the passages about the first principles and the last things. If that system is received as the cure of tergiversation because it warned against tergiversation, the warning has been substituted for the test. The mechanism of substitution, already established, applies to the warner. A holy word, here the word about not turning, is retained, and a grid is assigned the dignity of the cure. The grid may contain sentences the writing teaches. Those sentences remain the writing’s where they correspond. The grid as a grid is still under the writing. It turns, in the sense this chapter has defined, if it moves the hearer from what the passages give to what the grid must have them mean, and if the hearer calls that movement faithfulness to a warning about faithfulness.

**Premises.** Substitution keeps the holy sentence and reassigns it. The truth turned from remains the measure. A true sentence inside a source does not legitimize the source’s government. Chapter 69 established the last point for the seat of sense. The present premise is the turning, not the seat.

**What follows.** A document that warns against doctrinal turning is itself capable of being an instrument of turning, if its constructions become the object in the writing’s place. The warning can be true while a construction attached to the warning is false. Each sentence meets the writing on its own. The true warning does not canonize the next paragraph. The false paragraph, where it is false, does not erase the true warning. This is the chapter applying its own conclusion to a source it has used. The application is required by honesty. A book that tested every other teacher’s sentence and exempted its research materials would be performing the drawing-after it has just described: disciples after the source, rather than hearers before the writing.

**What does not follow here.** That every sentence in the materials has been adjudicated as a turning. This chapter does not write that commentary. It refuses the grid the offices of cause-list and of cure. That the First Principles have been taught or corrected. The materials name them. The teaching of them is the book’s end, after the labour-movement. That the reader is left without a measure because a source was refused the office. The measure is the Graphē, which is why the warning could be received in the previous section at all.

Thus:

> **A warning against tergiversation does not certify the warner’s system. The system turns if it becomes the object in the writing’s place. True warning and unadopted grid can stand in one document. Each sentence meets the writing. The grid is neither the cause-list nor the cure.**

# 70.50 Competing Claim: The Writing Causes the Turning Because It Is Hard

> **Some things in the writings are hard. Therefore the writing itself causes men to turn aside, and departure is the writing’s fault. If the word were clear, no one would wrest it, drift from it, or exchange it for a fable.**

The claim fails.

Second Peter 3:16 states the hardness and, in the same breath, assigns the wresting to the unlearned and unstable, unto their own destruction. The grammar does not make “hard things” the subject of “wrest.” Hebrews 2:1 assigns the slipping to the failure of earnest heed, after a chapter on the Son’s superiority, not after a confession that the word of salvation cannot be held. Galatians 3:1 assigns the not-obeying to a bewitching in the face of Christ evidently set forth, not to a Christ who was never shown. Hebrews 4:2 assigns the unprofitableness of the heard word to its not being mixed with faith, not to its not being true. Deuteronomy 30:11–14, in a book this chapter has used for the commanded way, says the commandment is not hidden and not far off, but is nigh, in the mouth and in the heart, that it may be done. The present use of that nearness is limited: the turning in Deuteronomy’s narrative is not explained by an absent word. The word was nigh, and they turned.

Chapter 57 established that the record does not fail as communication. Chapter 67 established that misreading is the reader’s mode, not the writing’s silence. The present claim is the same reduction aimed at turning rather than at misreading. It is refused again because the turning-texts themselves refuse it. Hardness is real in some sentences. It is a reason for heed, for stability, and for the labour a later movement will command. It is not a cause that relocates guilt from the wrestler to the writing. If hardness caused the turning, the stable and the heedful would turn as necessarily as the unstable, and the commands to beware and to continue would be commands to do the impossible. The commands treat continuance as possible in the same letter that treats some things as hard.

Thus:

> **Hardness is not the cause of doctrinal turning aside. The writings assign the twist, the drift, and the exchange to failures of learning, stability, heed, and obedience, in the presence of a word that still speaks. The writing remains the measure of the turning. It is not the author of it.**

# 70.51 Competing Claim: Governance Is Already the Whole Pathology of Turning

> **Chapter 69 showed that secondary sources govern meaning. Therefore the causes of turning have already been written. Men turn because a note, a tradition, a house, a teacher, a system, or an experience sits in the seat. A further file is a repetition with a darker name.**

The claim fails.

Chapter 69 established an event: the seat of sense is occupied and the test is reversed. It reserved, in express words, the causes and mechanisms of the Galatians’ removal, the causes of a party’s turning, and the dedicated pathology of departure. Reservation was not coyness. It was the difference between a court under a governor and a person moving from a truth. The present file has used the event as a condition and has refused it as the whole cause. Ears itch where they have not yet installed a commentary. Hearts unbelieve in a wilderness where the word is heard daily. Unstable men wrest writings in their own hands. Speakers draw disciples after themselves out of the eldership, which is a turning inside an office, not only a turning under a foreign note.

If governance were the whole cause, the texts that name the hearer’s non-endurance, the hearer’s unbelief, and the hearer’s instability would be decorative. They are not decorative. They are causal clauses. If governance were irrelevant, the texts that show a perverted gospel, a human commandment, and a fable receiving the ear would be equally decorative. The competing claim keeps one set and cancels the other. The chapter keeps both, in the relation already stated: condition, not identity.

Thus:

> **Governance of meaning is a condition that disguises departure as fidelity. The causes and mechanisms of the departure are a further question, and the writings answer it with clauses Chapter 69 did not spend. This file is not that file reprinted.**

# 70.52 Competing Claim: Every Disagreement Is Tergiversation

> **Truth is one. Therefore every doctrinal disagreement is a turning aside from truth. The sooner a difference is named tergiversation, the safer the church. To wait is to be unstable.**

The claim fails.

The definition required a movement from a truth the writing has given. A difference about a question the writing has not settled in the terms the accuser prefers is not yet that movement. The Bereans disagreed with the possibility that Paul’s word should be received without search, and the disagreement was noble because it tested the word by the scriptures. Paul and Barnabas disagreed sharply about a companion, and the text does not call the disagreement another gospel. Aquila and Priscilla corrected Apollos toward a more perfect knowledge of the way, and the correction was not a desertion. If every difference is tergiversation, correction is treason, and James’s convertor is committing the act he cures.

The opposite error, that no difference is ever a turning, has already been refused by the curse, the gangrene, and the denial of the Master. The present claim is the inflated error. It turns the category into a weapon and thereby performs a mechanism this chapter has named: perverse speech used to draw disciples after the speaker who controls the weapon. A charge of turning must itself be according to the writing. It must identify the given truth, the movement, and the object that has claimed its place. Without those, the charge is a fable about the brother, and the ear that enjoys it is the itching ear.

Thus:

> **Tergiversation is departure from a truth the writing has given toward another claimant of its place. Disagreement is not yet that departure. A charge that skips the given, the movement, and the claimant is itself a distorted speech.**

---

# 70.53 Competing Claim: The Turning Is Harmless Because the Truth Still Stands

> **The first warrant says the truth remains the measure. Therefore the turning does not matter. The Bible is still true, the gospel is still the gospel, and those who leave it hurt only their opinions. Steadfastness is preferable, not necessary.**

The claim fails.

The survival of the truth is what makes the leaving a real leaving of a real object. It is not what makes the leaving cheap. Galatians attaches a curse to the other gospel and marvels at the churches. Second Peter attaches destruction to the wresting and swift destruction to the false teachers, and says many follow. Second Timothy attaches the overthrow of the faith of some to a single false sentence. Hebrews attaches not entering to unbelief, and attaches the slipping of the things heard — the so great salvation spoken by the Lord — to neglected heed. James attaches death to the unconverted error of the way. Exodus attaches the breaking of the tables and the judgment that follows to the quick turn. The way of truth can be evil spoken of and still be the way, and the speaking-against is still a damage the text bothers to name.

Chapter 69 refused the cousin claim that governance is harmless because the writing still stands. The same refusal holds for the turning. Gravity at this level is part of what departure is. The later chapters do not have to complete their work before a curse is a curse. Harmlessness is a doctrine the turning-texts do not teach.

Thus:

> **The truth’s survival measures the turning and condemns it. It does not cushion it. Curse, destruction, overthrow, exclusion from the rest, and death are the texts’ own account of the weight.**

---

# 70.54 Competing Claim: Turning Is Fate, So the Commands Are Rhetoric

> **The Spirit says some shall depart. Wolves shall enter. Men shall arise. Seducers shall wax worse. Therefore departure is the predetermined story of hearers, and “continue,” “beware,” and “take heed” are the apostle’s sentiment, not a real contrary.**

The claim fails.

“Some” is not “all.” “Lest” is not “when.” “Who did hinder you” is a question asked of people who did run well, and a question expects a resistance. “Ye therefore, beloved, seeing ye know these things before, beware” addresses the knowers as people who can be warned because they know. The express foretelling of departure is a revelation of a danger, and the letters that contain it are written to churches in order that the danger be refused. A fate that no heed could touch would make the infinitives unintelligible. The chapter has already stated the third positive warrant. The competing claim denies the verbs.

The claim also confuses the certainty of a warned event, in the case of those who transfer heed and heap teachers, with the necessity that every hearer be among them. First Timothy 4:1 is compatible with 4:6, in which Timothy is a good minister, nourished up in the words of faith. Both stand. The departure of some is not the job of all. To preach the warning as if it closed the nourishment is to turn the warning into the fable.

Thus:

> **Forewarned departure is a real danger with real subjects. It is not the definition of every hearer. Continuance, heed, and beware are the writings’ own contraries, addressed to people who have learned and who know. The imperatives are part of the doctrine.**

# 70.55 Competing Claim: Only the Heart, or Only the Intellect

> **Either departure is a moral disease of the heart, and talk of intellectual failure is an excuse learned from the academy; or departure is an intellectual mistake, and talk of lust, unbelief, and deceit is a preacher’s excess. Choose.**

The claim fails in both directions. It is a false dichotomy the paragraphs will not host.

Second Timothy 4:3 names lusts and names sound doctrine in one mechanism. The itch is in the ear that hears teaching. Hebrews 3:12 names an evil heart of unbelief in the departing, inside a sermon on hearing his voice. Hebrews 4:2 names a word heard and faith not mixed with it. First Timothy 4:1–2 names doctrines of demons and a seared conscience. First Timothy 1:7 names not-understanding as the condition of the ones who turned aside, without calling that condition a virtue. Second Peter 2:15 names wages together with forsaking the right way. Matthew 22:29 names not-knowing the scriptures as the condition of the error, in the mouth of the judge, not in the mouth of an apologist.

A reading that keeps only the heart must delete “not knowing,” “unlearned,” “understanding neither what they say,” and “will not endure sound doctrine.” A reading that keeps only a disembodied intellect must delete lust, unbelief, deceit, wages, and the seared conscience. The assignment asked for the intellectual failures, and they have been identified. The identification included their join with the heart because the join is textual. An excuse is what the dichotomy offers, in either direction: the mind failed but the man is good, or the man is bad and so there is nothing to understand about how he left the truth. The texts offer a causes-account instead of an excuse.

Thus:

> **Intellectual failures and the heart’s unbelief, lust, and deceit permit departure together. Deleting either set cuts the sentences. The failures are identified without being pardons, and the vices are named without making the turning unintelligible.**

# 70.56 Competing Claim: Speed Proves There Was Never a Truth to Leave

> **They turned quickly. They were so soon removed. They believed only for a while. Therefore the prior state was counterfeit, and the doctrine of turning is a mistake about names. Nothing was left, because nothing had been had.**

The claim fails as a general account, because it contradicts the premises the speed-texts themselves state.

Exodus 32: the way was commanded, and the turning is out of it. The charge is unintelligible if there was no way. Galatians 1: they were called into the grace of Christ, and they received a gospel, and the removal is from the caller unto another gospel. Galatians 5: ye did run well. Galatians 3: Christ was set forth before their eyes, and they received the Spirit by the hearing of faith. Luke 8:13: they hear, receive the word with joy, and for a while believe, and then fall away. Hebrews 3: the fathers heard, and saw the works forty years. Second Peter 3:17: fall from your own steadfastness, addressed to those who know these things before.

Some passages in the writings distinguish a profession that was never the thing. They do not authorize the deletion of “out of the way,” “received,” “did run well,” “for a while believe,” and “your own steadfastness.” Where the text says the prior possession was real, the turning is a leaving of a real possession. Where the text withholds that premise, this chapter does not invent it. The speed-claim invents a universal negative the exhibitions will not bear. It also makes the marvel at Galatia theatrical. One does not marvel that a people left a caller they never had, on the ground that they left so soon. The soon is grievous because the calling was theirs.

The claim’s grain of truth is narrower and already kept: a joyful hearing is not a root, and a name is not a being. That truth does not convert every quick departure into evidence that the word was never given. It converts joyful hearing without root into a warned condition, which is what Luke 8:13 is.

Thus:

> **Speed and brevity are features of some turnings, not proofs that the given truth was imaginary. The way commanded, the gospel received, the race run, the while of belief, and the steadfastness possessed are the texts’ premises. The turning is a leaving of those premises, not a retroactive denial that they were stated.**

# 70.57 Competing Claim: This Chapter as Already Chapters 71–73 or Movement X

> **Once the turning has been named, the heresies have been judged, the dryness has been diagnosed, the unskillful have been described, and the labour of study has been commanded. Leaven is already accepted heresy. The itching ear is already aridity. Not-understanding is already *apeiros*. “Give the more earnest heed” is already *spoudazō*. The later chapters are surplus.**

The claim fails.

The locked TOC distinguishes the assignments in distinct sentences. This chapter examines the causes and mechanisms of doctrinal turning aside and identifies the intellectual failures that permit departure from truth. Chapter 71 examines how erroneous doctrines become normalized or institutionalized, and why institutional acceptance does not transform error into truth. Chapter 72 examines aridity in connection with deficient intellectual engagement, poor judgment, and failure to labour properly in the Word. Chapter 73 develops *apeiros*, inexperienced and unskillful in the word of righteousness. Movement X examines scholarly labour, *spoudazō*, rightly dividing, and readiness for the First Principles. Distinction of assignments is not a delay of nerve. It is the knowledge-flow.

A pious form of collapse says: the effects are visible, so write the later files now. Visible effects are the warrant that the turning is grave. They are not the normalization, the loss of reasonability, or the formation of skill. Chapter 69 already refused the collapse when it was offered as “the displacement is already the pathology.” This chapter refuses the remaining half aimed at itself: the turning is not already the acceptance-file, the dryness-file, the unskill-file, or the labour-file.

“Many shall follow” has been used as a following that slanders the way of truth. The itching ear has been used as the mechanism by which teachers are heaped and the truth is left. The use is not the finished account of aridity. Not-understanding what one affirms has been used as an intellectual failure that permits vain jangling. The use is not the finished account of Hebrews 5:13. Earnest heed has been used as the contrary of drift. The use is not the finished account of diligence, endeavour, and study. Hebrews 6:1–2 has not been opened as the foundation-list. Second Timothy 2:15 has not been opened as the approved workman.

Thus:

> **This chapter is not already Chapters 71–73 or Movement X. The causes and mechanisms of turning aside are the object. Later questions remain later.**

# 70.58 Reason Through the Evidence

The inference can now be exhibited.

**Premise 1.** The Graphē is the primary written authority. The record does not fail as truthful divine writing of what it actually communicates. The Scripture cannot be broken. Truth is conformity to reality and is not created by belief, popularity, or institutional acceptance (Chapters 1–3, 57, 62–63, and 65, used not retried).

**Premise 2.** The authority of that writing is distinct from the authority claimed for a later statement of sense. Transfer fails. Despair fails. Quotation is not the Graphē (Chapter 66, used not retried).

**Premise 3.** Scripture is misread in five inspectable modes. The writing remains the measure. A correct reading remains possible. An incorrect reading remains incorrect (Chapter 67, used not retried).

**Premise 4.** Theology, philosophy, tradition, and institution are secondary systems. They may serve. None becomes primary written authority by Christian identity, academic status, antiquity, or institutional recognition (Chapter 68, used not retried).

**Premise 5.** When commentaries, traditions, institutions, teachers, systems, or experiences govern the meaning of Scripture, the seat of sense is occupied and the test is reversed. The writing is not broken. Service is not the event (Chapter 69, used not retried).

**Premise 6.** The writings themselves exhibit doctrinal turning aside and name its causes and mechanisms. A commanded way is left quickly by substitution of a made god, the redemption-sentence retained (Exodus 32; Deuteronomy 9). A deceived heart issues in turning and in another service; right things are refused and smooth things demanded (Deuteronomy 11:16; Isaiah 30:9–11). Churches are removed from the caller unto a different gospel that is not another of the same kind, troubled and perverted by some, bewitched and hindered from obeying a placarded truth, leavened by a little contrary (Galatians 1; 3; 5). Some swerve from the commandment’s end into vain jangling, desiring to teach what they do not understand (1 Timothy 1:3–7). Some depart from the faith by giving heed to seducing spirits and doctrines of demons (1 Timothy 4:1–2). Other-teaching refuses consent to wholesome words; a falsely named knowledge is professed, and some err concerning the faith (1 Timothy 6). Ears that will not endure sound doctrine heap teachers by lust and turn from the truth unto fables (2 Timothy 4:3–4). Empty talk increases and consumes; a false sentence about the resurrection overthrows the faith of some (2 Timothy 2:16–18). Seducers deceive and are deceived; continuance in the things learned, in the company of the scriptures, is commanded (2 Timothy 3:13–15). Human commandments and fables turn from the truth and subvert houses (Titus 1). Things heard slip when earnest heed is not given (Hebrews 2:1). An evil heart of unbelief departs from the living God; sin’s deceit hardens; the word heard does not profit when not mixed with faith (Hebrews 3–4). Joyful hearing without root falls away in temptation (Luke 8:13). Philosophy and empty deceit carry off from Christ those who have received him (Colossians 2). Perverse speech draws disciples after the speakers (Acts 20). Destructive teaching is brought in beside; the straight way is forsaken (2 Peter 2). The unlearned and unstable wrest hard things unto destruction and can be led away from their own steadfastness (2 Peter 3:16–18). A brother can err from the truth and be converted (James 5:19–20). Erring is joined to not knowing the scriptures (Matthew 22:29).

**Premise 7.** Reconstructible Introduction and Aletheia propositions, tested rather than cited as authority, cohere where they locate wresting, bias, explaining-away, and self-interested handling in men, where they detect fallacy by what is written, where they treat dull hearing as a condition that can leave a word unheld, and where they join intellectual failure to turning among persons and among bodies that claim Christ’s name. They do not cohere, and are not adopted, where they reduce every turning to bare willfulness, where they deny hard texts or real expounding, where they install a verse-count or a dispensational grid as the method, where they treat a causal chain through aridity, accepted heresies, *apeiros*, and *spoudazō* as already finished, or where the warner’s grid is received as the cure because the warner named the disease.

**Premise 8.** Competing reductions fail: the writing’s hardness as the cause; governance as already the whole pathology; every disagreement as tergiversation; harmlessness; fate; the heart against the intellect as a forced choice; speed as proof that nothing was ever given; this file as already Chapters 71–73 or Movement X.

**Inference.** Therefore doctrinal turning aside is an inspectable movement from a truth the Graphē has given toward another object that claims its place. Its causes and mechanisms are the ones the writings name: substitution, leaven and spread, selection of teachers by appetite, transfer and withholding of heed, twist and beguilement, covert introduction, drawing-after, drift, and departure by unbelief. The intellectual failures that permit it are failures of understanding, consent, learning, stability, endurance of sound doctrine, heed, and knowing the scriptures one possesses. Those failures join unbelief, lust, deceit, and gain, and do not excuse the movement. The truth left remains the measure. Continuance, earnest heed, and conversion remain real contraries. The investigator is warranted, with full force, in receiving this as Movement IX’s fifth word.

The inference is not a leap from “hearers can err” to “every difference is a desertion.” The exhibitions supply the given, the movement, and the claimant. The inference is not a leap from “the turning is grave” to “the later pathology-files and the labour-files have been written.” Sequence forbids that leap. The inference is not a leap from “a source warned against turning” to “the source’s system is the account of turning.” Testing forbids that leap.

Thus:

> **The join is inspectable: primary writing + distinguished interpretation + catalogued misreading + ranked secondary systems + governance of meaning already exhibited + the writings’ own clauses of removal, swerving, heed transferred, ears turned, ways forsaken, and wresting = a warranted account of the causes and mechanisms of doctrinal turning aside, and of the intellectual failures that permit departure from truth, at this level and not beyond it.**

# 70.60 The Warranted Conclusion Stated with Full Force

The conclusion may now be stated without postponement and without inflation.

Doctrinal turning aside is the movement of a person or a people from a truth the Graphē has given toward another object that claims that truth’s place.

**The causes** the writings attach to that movement include: a deceived heart; refusal to wait on the word given and demand for a substitute; troubling and perverting speech; bewitchment and hindrance; a persuasion not from the caller; desire to teach without understanding; transfer of heed to seducing spirits and demonic doctrines; refusal to endure sound doctrine; lust that selects teachers; empty talk that increases; human commandments and fables; failure of earnest heed; an evil heart of unbelief; the deceitfulness of sin; temptation meeting a rootless reception; philosophy and empty deceit not after Christ; the aim of drawing disciples after the speaker; wages; privy introduction of destructive teaching; unlearned and unstable wresting. The list is plural because the record is plural.

**The mechanisms** by which the movement is carried include: substitution that keeps the holy sentence and reassigns it; the small contrary that leavens, consumes, and spreads; the heaping of teachers by the ear’s appetite; the transfer and the withholding of heed; the twist, the enticing word, and the true line used for a false step; the covert bringing-in beside the faith; the drawing of disciples after the speaker; the drift of things heard; the departure of unbelief in the face of a word heard. These operations are readable from the clauses. They are not a psychology invented behind the clauses.

**The intellectual failures** that permit the departure are failures of understanding what one affirms, of consent to wholesome words, of learning and stability before what is hard, of endurance of sound doctrine, of the direction of heed, and of knowing the scriptures one possesses. They permit as openings. They stand in the same paragraphs as unbelief, lust, deceit, hypocrisy, and gain. They do not excuse the turner. The unlearned wrestler goes to his own destruction. The affirm-er who does not understand is still the man who turned aside unto vain jangling.

**The truth turned from remains the measure.** The calf does not become the Lord. The different gospel does not become the received gospel. The fable does not become the truth. The slandered way remains the way. The Scripture is not broken by the leaving. A following, even of many, does not transform the departure into truth. That last sentence is the neighbour of Chapter 71, stated here only as the survival of the measure, not as the doctrine of institutional acceptance.

**Continuance is a real contrary.** Continue in the things learned. Give the more earnest heed. Take heed lest there be in any of you an evil heart of unbelief. Beware, knowing beforehand. Grow in grace and in the knowledge of the Lord. Convert the one who errs from the truth. The word of God’s grace is able to build up. The holy scriptures are able to make wise unto salvation. “Some” depart. The some is not the all. The turning is not fate, and it is not harmless.

This continues Movement IX. It is not Chapter 69 retried. It is not Chapters 71–73. It is not Movement X. That is this chapter’s conclusion, stated here with full force.

Thus:

> **The causes and mechanisms of doctrinal turning aside have been examined, and the intellectual failures that permit departure from truth have been identified. The turning is in force. Later questions remain later because they are different questions.**

# 70.61 Apply the Standard

The standard has been applied.

A movement that stands in the neighbourhood of the faith is now to be asked not only, “What voice is finishing the sense?” — Chapter 69’s question — but, “What truth was given, who or what has moved from it, and what object has claimed its place?” Was the way commanded, and has a substitute received its sentence? Was the gospel received, and has a different message kept the name? Was sound doctrine the thing to endure, and has the ear ordered fables? Was Christ received, and has a persuasive speech carried the hearer off from him? Was the straight way there, and has it been forsaken for wages? Was the writing in the hands, and has it been wrested because the handler was unlearned and unstable?

The questions are practical without becoming a tribunal’s handbook, a history of parties, or a technique of interpretation. A people who confess that Scripture is primary may still be removed from the gospel they received. A people who have run well may still be hindered. A hearer who once received the word with joy may still have no root when temptation comes. An elder may still speak perverse things to draw disciples after himself. A warning against turning may still be stapled to a grid that turns. A hard sentence may still be left hard without being twisted, and a twisted sentence may still be judged by the writing it twisted.

The standard also protects. A correction toward the writing is not a desertion. A teacher who opens the scripture and commends disciples to the word of grace is not a wolf. A question not settled by the writing is not yet a fable. A hearer who continues in the things learned is not credited with infallibility and is not accused of turning because others turned. Service remains service. Steadfastness remains a real relation to a truth that stands. The protection is the same fact as the judgment: the writing is the measure of both the continuance and the departure.

The standard is the Graphē. Tergiversation is the movement away from what it has given. The intellectual failures are openings in the hearer and the handler. The openings do not move the standard.

Thus:

> **The standard has been applied: the causes and mechanisms of doctrinal turning aside, and the intellectual failures that permit departure from truth, have been identified as Movement IX’s fifth word, not announced as a slogan, and not postponed as if the turning were weak.**

# 70.62 From Tergiversation to Accepted Heresies

A turning, now controlled as an inspectable departure with named causes, still leaves a further question.

The turning-file is in force. Persons and peoples leave a given truth by substitution, spread, appetite, heed, twist, covert introduction, drawing-after, drift, and unbelief. Intellectual failures permit the leaving and do not excuse it. The truth remains the measure. Those sentences are the cleared ground. They are not yet the dedicated examination of how erroneous doctrines become normalized or institutionalized, or of why institutional acceptance does not transform error into truth.

If the turning were not stated, the next question would still be what causes departure. The turning has been stated. If this chapter were already the account of accepted heresies, the next file would be repetition. This chapter is not that account. The next question is therefore the acceptance the locked assignment names: Accepted Heresies.

“Many shall follow their pernicious ways” has been read as a following. A following can thicken into a normal doctrine, a test of membership, a teaching that no longer has to be brought in beside because it has become the house’s own voice. The thickening is not yet examined. A little leaven leavens the lump. The leavened lump, received as the faith and defended as the faith, is a further object. The present file had to establish the leaven as a mechanism of turning. It must not steal the account of acceptance in order to make the turning sound like a sociology of institutions. The next file must not steal this turning as if the causes had not been stated.

That is the proper open of the next chapter. The turning was never meant to be a slogan that finishes Movement IX, and it was never meant to deny that a departure can become the ordinary doctrine of a body. It was meant to be the naming of the movement and of the failures that permit it, sufficient to justify taking the next question, which is the question the movement itself forces once acceptance, and not only departure, is the object: how do erroneous doctrines become normalized or institutionalized, and why does institutional acceptance not transform error into truth?

The next file must not treat this turning as if the causes had not been described. This file must not treat that acceptance as if Chapter 71 were already written. Naming the neighbourhood is not performing the examination.
