# 63.3 Define the Question: Why Scripture Cannot Be Broken

**The why**, in this chapter, is the dedicated account of why ἡ γραφή cannot be λυθῆναι, given that “broken” has already been controlled as the loosing of the writing’s force as court so as to evacuate the appeal.

The why is easily abused. In ordinary speech “why the scripture cannot be broken” may mean a preacher’s motto (“because Jesus said so”), a historical boast that copies have survived kingdoms, a claim that every later tradition is thereby certified, a psychological remark that opponents will fail to refute a text, or a claim that Chapters 64–65 are already finished because a why exists. None of those senses is this chapter’s object.

Four elements belong to the definition.

First, **why, as ground of the already-controlled sense**. The object is not a second explanation of λυθῆναι. The object is why that incapacity belongs to the writing. Sense is already in force. Ground is the new object.

Second, **from what Scripture is**. The locked assignment requires that the conclusion rest on what Scripture is: authoritative written truth. The Graphē, as already identified, is God’s truthful written Word — God-breathed, non-failing as record of what it actually communicates, standing as that writing. The why is drawn from that identity. It is not drawn from a later ecclesiastical stamp, and it is not drawn from the mere occurrence of a quotation.

Third, **not merely from the fact that Jesus quoted the clause**. His saying it is the assertion. The assertion is not dispensable. It is not the sole warrant. A why that stopped at “because He said so” would leave the authority of the writing unexplained, and would convert the Truth’s verdict into a circular proof of itself.

Fourth, **not those other claims**. The definition excludes, as not this object: a retry of Chapter 62’s sense; John 17:17 as finished Graphē-doctrine; the Graphē-gate; a grant that every later interpreter’s use of a verse inherits this standing; a claim that every later copy is physically unbreakable; a rewrite of Chapters 56–58 as if Movement VII had not already been stated. Those exclusions are part of the definition. They are not a later apology.

Thus:

> **The question of this chapter is not what “the scripture cannot be broken” means in the argument, how the a fortiori is drawn, or how God’s Word is truth as John 17:17 will state it. Those questions have been examined or remain later. The question is why the scripture cannot be broken, from what Scripture is.**

# 63.8 Assertion Distinguished from Ground: Christ’s Saying It Is the Assertion

The locked assignment forbids a particular stop:

> **Do not rest the conclusion merely on the fact that Jesus quoted it.**

The stop is not a slight upon His quotation. His quotation is the argument. His parenthetical is the assertion. The stop is a slight upon a why that never leaves the quotation.

**Assertion**, in this chapter, is what Chapter 62 already established: He asserts, in the indicative, that the scripture cannot be broken. Speaker, object, and dispute are explicit. The assertion is the Truth’s verdict in the court He opened. It is load-bearing in the a fortiori. It is not garnish.

**Ground**, in this chapter, is why the assertion is true of the writing. Ground is the identity of the Graphē as God’s truthful written Word, already earned: God who cannot lie; inspiration of the writing; infallibility of the record; authority of the written Word. Ground is not a rival to the assertion. Ground is what the assertion is about.

The two must not be inverted.

If assertion is treated as ground, the argument runs: He said the scripture cannot be broken; therefore it cannot; therefore His argument from it is sound. The last step uses the first as its own proof. Circularity of that form is the forbidden stop.

If ground is treated as making the assertion dispensable, the argument runs: the doctrine of Scripture is already complete; John 10:35b adds nothing; Movement VIII is ornament upon Movement VII. That inversion fails the unit. He does not leave the writing’s standing unsaid in the dispute. He asserts it. The Truth’s verdict upon the writing, in the court, is part of the biblical record of the why. It is not the whole why, and it is not nothing.

The right order is therefore:

> **His saying it is the assertion. The why is the doctrine of the writing. The assertion is the Truth’s verdict upon that writing. The verdict does not replace the doctrine. The doctrine does not erase the verdict.**

Christ’s identity as the Truth is already in force (Chapter 44). His confirmation of received Scripture is already in force (Chapter 48). Those premises prevent a further confusion: that His assertion is an unverified religious claim waiting for this chapter to become true. His assertion is the assertion of the One already identified. The present file does not authenticate Him. It locates what His assertion does in the why: it is verdict, not sole warrant.

Thus:

> **Assertion and ground are related and not identical. He said it. That is the assertion. The Graphē is God’s truthful written Word. That is the why. Neither may be collapsed into the other.**

The phrase names the identity. It does not finish the gate.

# 63.10 The Character of God as Source-Side Ground

The first member of the why is the character of God, already established.

God is the ground of truth (Chapter 10). He is truthful, faithful, and immutable (Chapter 11). Falsehood is incompatible with Him: it is impossible for God to lie (Chapter 12). Numbers 23:19, Titus 1:2, and Hebrews 6:18 remain the biblical record of that impossibility, used rather than rewritten.

What that character does in the present why is source-side.

If the Graphē is His writing, then the writing cannot be His lie. A lie is falsehood offered as truth. To loose the writing as court — to set it aside, annul it, make it void so as to evacuate an appeal that treats it as standing truth — would be to treat His writing as if it could be made to fail as truth in the dispute. That treatment cannot succeed as an act upon the writing if the writing is His. It can be attempted. Attempt is not success. Chapter 62 already distinguished attempt from success as an act upon ἡ γραφή. This chapter names why success is denied: the source cannot lie.

The inference is not:

> **God cannot lie; therefore every document that names God is unbreakable.**

That would collapse divine truthfulness into documentary authentication, which the book has refused from Chapter 11 onward. Authentication of this corpus remains already in force (Movements V–VII). The present member is: given that this writing is His, the source-side impossibility of falsehood is why the writing cannot be loosed as if it were false or disposable truth.

The inference is also not:

> **God cannot lie; therefore John 10:35b needs no Christ and no court.**

The Dedication dispute is the place of the assertion. Source-side ground does not erase the place. It answers why the writing used as court in that place cannot be recused as broken.

Faithfulness and immutability belong here as well, used not retried. A source who could change into falsehood, or who could abandon what He has written, would leave the writing’s standing hostage to later alteration of the source. The God already identified does not so change and does not so abandon. His writing, as His, is not recusable on the ground that He has become other than true.

Thus:

> **The character of God is source-side ground of the why: if this is His writing, it cannot be loosed as court as if He could lie, change into falsehood, or abandon what He has written. Authentication of the writing remains already in force. Source-side is not a blank check for every claimant of the title.**

# 63.11 Inspiration as the Writing-Side Ground

The second member of the why is inspiration, already established.

All Scripture is God-breathed: πᾶσα γραφή θεόπνευστος (2 Timothy 3:16), established in Chapters 30–31 and used as to its guarantees in Chapter 56. Inspiration is the divine operation concerning Scripture as writing. It is not identical with revelation, recording, transmission, or canon. Those distinctions remain in force.

What inspiration does in the present why is writing-side.

The Graphē is not merely a human report about God that happens to be useful in court. It is God-breathed writing. Chapter 56 already warranted that the inspired record is a truthful record of what it actually communicates, and that it has authority as Scripture to teach, reprove, correct, and train. This chapter uses that warrant: a God-breathed writing cannot be loosed as court-force without being treated as if θεόπνευστος named an honorific rather than an operation.

The reconstructible argument from the Bible Introduction’s treatment of inspiration is this, tested rather than adopted as a slogan: inspiration secures truth and unity in the record, not sameness of words; it superintends the communicating and recording of truth; it records with accuracy the language of God and of other speakers without converting every speaker into God. That argument has already been examined as to what it warrants (Chapters 30–31, 56). The present use is: a record so produced is not a disposable illustration in a blasphemy-trial. To break it in the controlled sense would be to treat God-breathed writing as voidable human opinion.

2 Peter 1:20–21 remains in force as the prophetic side of the same writing-side: prophecy of Scripture is not of private interpretation as private origin; holy men spoke as they were moved by the Holy Ghost. Private will is not the producer of the standing writing. Private will is therefore not the recuser of it either. An opponent may refuse. Refusal is not the writing’s becoming a private product.

Inspiration’s limits remain the shape of the doctrine. Inspiration of the writing is not inerrancy of every later copy. Inspiration is not infallibility of every interpreter. Inspiration is not membership for every religious writing that claims the title. Those limits, already named, are not drains upon the why. They prevent the why from becoming a blank check.

Thus:

> **Inspiration is writing-side ground of the why: the Graphē is God-breathed writing, a truthful record of what it actually communicates. To loose it as court is to treat θεόπνευστος as an empty title. Limits as to copies, interpreters, and claimants remain the shape of the doctrine.**

# 63.12 Infallibility of the Record as the Non-Failure Ground

The third member of the why is infallibility of the record, already established.

Chapter 57 defined infallibility, in this argument, as the warranted conclusion that the inspired scriptural record does not fail as truthful divine writing of what it actually communicates. The object of that verdict is the inspired scriptural record already identified as the corpus, not every later copy, not every interpreter, not the church as church, and not every later claim about Scripture.

What infallibility does in the present why is non-failure.

λυθῆναι, in the controlled sense, is success at making the writing fail as court-force: setting it aside, annulling it, making it void so that the written case no longer stands. If the record does not fail as truthful divine writing of what it actually communicates, then that success is denied as an act upon the record. The Dedication appeal treats Psalm 82:6 as communicating a standing naming. Non-failure of the record as to what it actually communicates is why that naming cannot be made void as premise.

The inference is not a flattening of genre. Infallibility is indexed to actual communication: speaker, sense, and kind of claim. Chapter 57 already required speaker-distinction. This chapter uses that index: Psalm 82:6, as God’s written address in the psalm’s argument, is not a recorded lie of a false speaker. It is the writing Christ cites as “I said.” Non-failure attaches to that communication as what it is.

The inference is also not a blank check for every tradition that quotes the record. Infallibility of the record is not infallibility of later use. The why attaches to ἡ γραφή, not to the opponents’ charge, and not to a later school’s paraphrase.

If the record could fail as truthful writing, the a fortiori would have a failing premise. Chapter 61 already showed that the lesser case must remain standing. Thus:

> **Infallibility of the record is non-failure ground of the why: the inspired scriptural record does not fail as truthful divine writing of what it actually communicates. Success at λυθῆναι would be success at making it fail as court. That success is denied of the record. Copies, interpreters, church, and tradition remain distinguished.**

# 63.13 Authority of the Written Word as Standing Already Named

# 63.15 The Positive Chain: Character, Inspiration, Infallibility, Assertion

The members may now be joined as one chain rather than as a heap.

**Because** God is true, faithful, immutable, and cannot lie, genuine divine communication is trustworthy as His communication.

**Because** this corpus has been authenticated as given revelation written, the communication under investigation is not an unexamined claimant.

**Because** all Scripture is God-breathed, the writing is His writing, not a later honorific attached to human genius.

**Because** the inspired record does not fail as truthful divine writing of what it actually communicates, the writing does not fail as the case it actually states.

**Because** the Graphē has standing as that writing, the citation is court, not decoration.

**Therefore** the writing’s force as court cannot be loosed, set aside, annulled, or made void so as to evacuate an appeal that treats it as standing truth. That is why the scripture cannot be broken.

**And** the Truth, in the Dedication controversy, asserts that very incapacity as the standing-clause of His a fortiori from Psalm 82:6. His assertion is the verdict. The chain is the why.

The chain is a conjunction. Chapter 58 already showed the four identity-members as a conjunction for the written Word’s truthfulness and authority. This chapter adds the assertion as the forensic verdict upon that conjunction, and draws the why of λυθῆναι from the whole.

The chain is not a new independent proof of God, inspiration, or canon. It is the why-function of premises already in force.

Thus:

# 63.16 Why Force as Court Cannot Be Loosed Without Denying What It Is

The thesis’s second joint may now be inspected.

**To loose the writing as court**, in the controlled sense, is to treat the written case as disposable: recusable, annullable, voidable, so that the appeal no longer has a standing premise.

**To deny what it is** is to treat the Graphē as not God’s truthful written Word — as not His, or as His lie, or as failing record, or as decoration without standing.

Those two acts are one act under honest names.

If the writing is His, loosing it as if it were not His is denial of origin.

If the writing is truthful as His communication, loosing it as if it could be made false in the dispute is denial of His truthfulness.

If the writing is God-breathed, loosing it as if θεόπνευστος were empty is denial of inspiration.

If the writing does not fail as record of what it actually communicates, loosing the communicated case as void is denial of non-failure.

If the writing has standing as court, loosing it as colour is denial of standing.

The why is therefore not a metaphor. It is identity-protection in the forensic sense. Unloosenability is not an extra property glued onto a book. It is what follows from being this writing when this writing is used as court.

The opponents’ charge required, in effect, that God-vocabulary used of a man be blasphemy in their mouths’ application, even though their law writes God-vocabulary of those to whom the word of God came. To sustain that charge they would have to break the scripture in the controlled sense: recuse the written lesser case. Recusal of that case is recusal of what the writing is in the argument: standing Holy Writ. The statement denies that recusal as a successful act upon ἡ γραφή. The why is that such recusal would be denial of the writing’s identity.

They may still refuse. Refusal is recorded. Stones are recorded. Refusal is not the writing’s becoming other than what it is. Denial as a speech-act of opponents is possible. Denial as a successful redefinition of ἡ γραφή is what cannot be done.

Thus:

> **Force as court cannot be loosed without treating the Graphē as not God’s truthful written Word. That treatment can be attempted. It cannot succeed as an act upon the writing. Unloosenability is the forensic consequence of identity, not an extra slogan.**

# 63.17 What “Denying What It Is” Would Require

Honesty about the inference requires stating what a successful loosing would cost.

To succeed at λυθῆναι, as an act upon ἡ γραφή, the opponent would have to have at least one of the following as true:

1. **That God can lie**, so that His writing may be treated as false or disposable in court. Chapter 12 has refused this.
2. **That this writing is not His**, so that loosing it is not loosing His word. Movements IV–VII have identified this corpus as Scripture, authenticated, inspired, and standing as His writing. The Dedication appeal treats it as “your law,” already received. This option is not available inside the dispute as He frames it, and it is not available in the book’s earned identification.
3. **That inspiration does not warrant truthfulness of the record**, so that God-breathed writing may still be voided as failing report. Chapter 56 has refused the claim that inspiration guarantees nothing about truth.
4. **That the record can fail as truthful divine writing of what it actually communicates**, so that the written case may be discarded as a failing premise. Chapter 57 has refused this as to the inspired record.
5. **That standing is later prestige rather than origin**, so that the citation is colour and may be dropped without remainder. Chapter 58 has refused prestige as origin.

A successful loosing would therefore require revoking the chain. The why is that the chain is already in force. Revocation is not a live option inside the investigation; it is the cost of the competing claim.

This is not a threat. It is an account of implication. If someone denies that the scripture cannot be broken, the investigation asks which member is being denied. Vague denial is not an alternative explanation. It is an unlocated dissent.

The Dedication opponents need not have named these members. They needed, in practice, to recuse the written case in order to keep the charge. Practical recusal is the forensic form of denial. He answers practical recusal with the identity-assertion: the scripture cannot be broken.

Thus:

> **Successful loosing would require denying God’s truthfulness, or the writing’s identity as His, or inspiration’s guarantee, or the record’s non-failure, or standing as origin. Those denials have already been refused. The why is that cost made explicit.**

# 63.23 Reconstructing the Aletheia Proposition: Unchallenged Authority of Holy Writ

A reconstructible proposition from the Aletheia Libraries’ treatment of the Graphē is this: Holy Writ is writing of unchallenged, unquestionable authority — Scripture as the written thing to which John 10:35 is attached as naming that standing.

The manuscript does not write “the source says.” It reconstructs and tests.

**What is claimed.** Graphē / Holy Writ / Scripture has standing that may not be challenged as if it were ordinary human writing.

**What the claim means.** Unchallenged authority is not the absence of opponents. Opponents are in John 10 with stones. Unchallenged, if it is true, means the writing is not recusable as broken court: its standing is not a matter of later human license.

**What would warrant it.** Precisely the identity this chapter has named: God’s truthful written Word, God-breathed, non-failing as record, asserted as unloosenable by the Truth in court.
