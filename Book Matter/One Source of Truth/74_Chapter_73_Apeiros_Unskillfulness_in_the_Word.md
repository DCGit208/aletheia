# 73.3 Define the Question: Ἄπειρος, Unskillfulness in the Word

**Ἄπειρος (*apeiros*)**, in this chapter, is the condition Hebrews 5:13 names: inexperienced, unskillful, in the word of righteousness. The one who partakes of milk is ἄπειρος λόγου δικαιοσύνης. The locked assignment’s English for that condition is unskillfulness in the Word.

**Unskillfulness in the Word**, in this chapter, is that condition read at the level the book has earned. The object of the unskill is a word. The word, in this argument, is not a hobby beside the Graphē and not a feeling called righteousness apart from what is written. It is the word of righteousness the sentence names, handled as the Word the preceding movements have identified: the primary written authority, which remains the measure when the handler is unskilled.

The English phrase is not a new revelation imported beside the verse. It is the locked assignment’s name for the condition the verse states. “Unskillful” is the English the common translation gives for ἄπειρος in this hapax. “Inexperienced” is the lexical force of the privative of trial. Both belong to the definition. Neither is a sneer added after the lexicon.

The phrase is easily abused. In ordinary speech “unskilled” may be heard as a slight against the uncredentialed, so that every hearer without an office is *apeiros* by status. “Inexperienced” may be heard as calendar youth, so that every new believer is guilty of the charge the time-clause levels at those who ought already to be teachers. “In the Word” may be heard as inexperience in religious atmosphere, so that skill in a room, a crowd, or a house is counted as the skill the verse names. None of those senses is this chapter’s object.

Four elements belong to the definition.

First, **a privative of trial in a specified word**. Ἄπειρος is the alpha-privative of πεῖρα (*peira*), trial, attempt, experience. The genitive λόγου δικαιοσύνης names the sphere of the missing trial. The person is not untried in general. He is untried in the word of righteousness. Skill, in this sentence, is what trial in that word produces. Its absence is the condition.

Second, **a diet that exhibits the privative**. The subject is πᾶς ὁ μετέχων γάλακτος, everyone who partakes of milk. The milk is not a metaphor the chapter imposes. It is the sentence’s own subject, carried from the preceding clause: they have become such as have need of milk, and not of solid food. Partaking of milk is the exhibition of the unskill. It is not, by itself, a complete doctrine of spiritual diet. The paragraph supplies the contrast. This chapter reads the contrast far enough to define the unskill, and it leaves the dedicated doctrine of maturity and strong meat in its own file.

Third, **a classification the sentence gives**. Νήπιος γάρ ἐστιν: for he is a babe. Immaturity is not an insult added by the investigator. It is the sentence’s own ground of the milk and the inexperience. The relationship is grammatical before it is homiletical.

Fourth, **a use that fails the word**. Inability to handle the Word appropriately is what the unskill and the immaturity mean for the act of handling. The hearers need to be taught again the elements of the beginning of the oracles of God. The writer has many things to say, and utterance is hard because of the hearers. Solid food belongs to the mature. The unskilled babe uses milk where the word in question requires a handling he does not have. “Appropriately” means according to what the word is and what the time requires. It does not mean according to a method written in a later chapter.

Thus:

> **The question of this chapter is not what the dryness is, and not yet what diligence, exercise, maturity, or right division are as methods. Those questions have been examined or remain later. The question is what *apeiros* is, and how lack of skill, immaturity, and inability to handle the Word appropriately stand together.**

# 73.4 The Three Members Named by the Assignment

The assignment names a development and a relationship. The relationship has three members. They are distinguished here so that a missing credential is not treated as already the babe, and so that inability is not treated as a property of the writing.

**Lack of skill.** Skill, at this stage, is acquired capacity to deal with a specified object, produced by trial in that object. Lack of skill is ἄπειρος in the word of righteousness: the trial has not been had, and the capacity the trial produces is not there. The mark is unacquaintance by use. The hearers of Hebrews have heard enough that the writer can speak of time and of teaching again. The lack is not the silence of a person never addressed. It is inexperience in the word after address, exhibited as a milk-diet where solid food belongs.

**Immaturity.** Immaturity, at this stage, is the νήπιος the sentence names. A babe, in this clause, is the one whose condition explains the milk and the inexperience. The mark is undeveloped judgment relative to the word that is due, not a count of years. The same paragraph has already said that for the time they ought to be teachers. The babe-status in view is compatible with time passed. It is the classification of those who have become such as need milk.

**Inability to handle the Word appropriately.** Handling, at this stage, is the act of taking, teaching, discerning, and using the word according to what it says. Inability is the failure of that act at the level the time and the word require. The mark is a word present and a handler who cannot take the discourse that belongs to it. Hebrews 5:12’s “ye have need that one teach you again” and Hebrews 5:14’s assignment of solid food to the trained are the paragraph’s own account of the inability. The positive method of handling — diligence, exercise, right division, the discipline of interpretation — remains later. The inability is readable now because the paragraph states a need, a diet, and a discernment the milk-partaker does not have.

The relation among the three is the paragraph’s own relation, not a machine. The babe is the ground (νήπιος γάρ). The unskill is the condition of the milk-partaker in the word of righteousness. The inability is the use: milk rather than solid food, elements again rather than the discourse the writer has to say, senses not trained to discern. A chapter that defined a Greek adjective and never reached immaturity and handling would have performed half the assignment. A chapter that wrote the labour by which skill is gained would have performed a later assignment.

Thus:

> **Lack of skill is inexperience in the word of righteousness. Immaturity is the babe the sentence names as the ground. Inability to handle the Word appropriately is the use that follows: the word due is not taken. The three are one diagnosis. None of them is the obscurity of the Graphē, and none is yet the dedicated file of diligence or of maturity.**

# 73.5 The Biblical Words the Name Collects

The verse does not offer a loose English cluster. The chapter collects the words of Hebrews 5:12–14 that bear on unskill, and it keeps them distinct so that a lexicon is not mistaken for the argument. Neighbouring words that illuminate the relationship are collected with them. Words whose dedicated files are later are named as neighbours and not opened as those files.

**ἄπειρος (*apeiros*, G552).** Without experience of, unacquainted with, untried in. Hebrews 5:13, only here in the New Testament. The form is nominative masculine singular, predicate of πᾶς ὁ μετέχων γάλακτος. The construction takes the genitive of the thing untried: λόγου δικαιοσύνης. The word is the alpha-privative of πεῖρα (*peira*), trial or experience. It is not, in this verse, the classical homograph that means boundless, which privatives a limit (πεῖρας) rather than a trial. The argumentative force is inexperience in a specified word. The common rendering “unskilful” names the capacity the inexperience withholds. Section 73.19 holds the lexical distinction at length.

**μετέχω (*metechō*, G3348), in the participle μετέχων.** To partake, to share in. Ὁ μετέχων γάλακτος is the one who partakes of milk. The common translation “useth milk” carries the ongoing diet, not a single sip. The subject of the unskill is a partaker, and the partaking is of milk.

**γάλα (*gala*, G1051).** Milk. In 5:12 and 5:13 it is the diet of those who have become needy of it and not of solid food. The same Greek word appears in 1 Corinthians 3:2, where the apostle fed milk and not meat because they were not able to bear meat. The image is shared. The charges are not identical, and section 73.23 keeps them distinct.

**λόγος δικαιοσύνης.** Word of righteousness. Λόγος in 5:11 has already named the writer’s discourse: πολὺς ἡμῖν ὁ λόγος, we have much to say. Δικαιοσύνης specifies the discourse as having to do with righteousness. The phrase is the object of the inexperience. It is not a second topic beside “the Word” invented by the assignment. Unskillfulness in the Word, as this book names the condition, is unskill in this word, under the identification of the Word already earned.

**νήπιος (*nēpios*, G3516).** An infant; figuratively, the immature. Νήπιος γάρ ἐστιν classifies the milk-partaker. The usage in this clause is the ground of the diet and the unskill. A traditional lexical analysis hears a privative of speech. The argument of this chapter rests on the use in the sentence, not on the etymology. The canon-wide diagnosis of the babe, without reduction to age or institutional status, remains a later file. The present use is the classification Hebrews 5:13 requires.

**ὀφείλω (*opheilō*), in ὀφείλοντες, and διὰ τὸν χρόνον.** Owing; by reason of the time. Hebrews 5:12: ye ought to be teachers on account of the time. The obligation and the time are the setting in which milk is an indictment. They are not a separate essay on pedagogy.

**στοιχεῖα τῆς ἀρχῆς τῶν λογίων τοῦ θεοῦ.** The elements of the beginning of the oracles of God. Στοιχεῖα are elements, rudiments. Ἀρχή is beginning. Λόγιον is an oracle, a divine utterance. The hearers need to be taught these again. The content of the elements is the foundation-list the book reserves. This chapter names the need to be taught again. It does not teach the list.

**στερεὰ τροφή.** Solid food. The common translation “strong meat” names the same contrast. It belongs to the mature, not to the milk-partaker. The dedicated examination of maturity, skill, and strong meat remains later. The contrast is used here as the paragraph’s own frame for what the unskilled do not handle.

**τέλειος (*teleios*, G5046).** Mature, complete, of full age. In 5:14 the genitive τελείων names those to whom solid food belongs. The word distinguishes the paragraph’s contrary from the νήπιος. It does not, by being present, write the maturity-file.

**ἕξις (*hexis*, G1838), αἰσθητήριον (*aisthētērion*, G145), γυμνάζω (*gymnazō*, G1128), διάκρισις (*diakrisis*, G1253).** Habit or condition produced by practice; faculty of perception; to train; distinguishing. Hebrews 5:14 joins them: by reason of practice the faculties have been trained for the discernment of good and evil. They name the contrary of the unskilled babe inside the paragraph. The practice itself, as the exercise of the Word, remains a later file. The discernment is stated. The method is not written.

**νωθρός (*nōthros*, G3576).** Dull, sluggish. Already examined in Chapter 72 as dullness of hearing. It remains the first mark. It is collected here only so that the second mark is not mistaken for a reprint of the first.

These words are one assignment because the sentence joins the partaker of milk, the inexperience, the babe, and, in the flanking verses, the time, the elements, the solid food, and the trained faculties. They are not one lexeme. Collapsing ἄπειρος into νωθρός would steal Chapter 72. Collapsing τέλειος and ἕξις into the present definition would steal Movement X. Leaving ἄπειρος, νήπιος, and the inability unjoined would hide the assignment.

Thus:

> **Unskillfulness, in this argument, is the condition Hebrews 5:13 states with ἄπειρος, μετέχων γάλακτος, λόγος δικαιοσύνης, and νήπιος, read in the frame of time, elements, solid food, and trained discernment. The words stay distinct. The diagnosis they serve in this file is one. The labour-words are not borrowed in order to complete the cure.**

# 73.6 Distinguish Objects That Must Not Be Collapsed

The investigation fails at the first step if it treats different objects as one.

**This unskill and Chapter 72’s dullness.** Hebrews 5:11 and Hebrews 5:13 stand in one paragraph. The paragraph does not make them one object of one chapter. Dull of hearing has been examined. The loss of reasonability is in force. Unskillful in the word of righteousness is the present object. The two marks can belong to one babe, as the paragraph’s own sequence allows, and as a later section will receive when it tests the claim that babes have two characteristics. Belonging to one person is not identity of the marks. A hearer can be dull, and the account of inexperience in the word can remain a different question until it is asked. This chapter asks it. It does not re-ask the dryness.

**This unskill and Chapter 67’s improper handling.** Improper handling is a mode by which a later statement of sense fails. It is in force. Inability to handle the Word appropriately is the condition of the unskilled babe, not a sixth mode added to the catalog and not the catalog reprinted. The mode is an act that mishandles. The unskill is the inexperience and immaturity that leave the partaker on milk, unable to take the word the time requires. Using a passage about not knowing the Scriptures again is not repeating the mode-file. The new function is the skill-diagnosis.

**This unskill and the labour-files.** Scholarly impetus, the stages of perception and learning, reasoning through the Word as a method, the distinction between possessing words and understanding them, σπουδάζω (*spoudazō*), the exercise of the Word, maturity and strong meat, the babe and the unskillful as a canon-wide diagnosis, rightly dividing, the approved workman, the discipline of interpretation, and readiness for the Six First Principles remain later. Hebrews 5:14 names practice and maturity. Naming the contrary is not writing those files. Second Timothy 2:15 names a workman who handles the word. Naming the question that verse will answer is not developing the verb or the adverb of right division. Hebrews 6:1 speaks of leaving the word of the beginning and going on. Naming the sequel is not teaching the foundation.

**Lack of skill and lack of office.** The hearers ought to be teachers by reason of the time. “Teachers” in that clause is the obligation the time created, not a certificate the unskill is defined as missing. A person can hold an office and be ἄπειρος in the word of righteousness. A person can lack an office and handle the word. The verse’s subject is the partaker of milk, not the untitled.

**Lack of skill and calendar youth.** Time passed is what makes the milk a charge. Youth as such is not the charge. A newborn’s milk is a different sentence. An old habit of milk, after one ought to have been a teacher, is this sentence. Age does not create the unskill, and age does not cure it.

**Immaturity and the child of humility.** Matthew 18 speaks of becoming as little children in order to enter the kingdom. The child there is the pattern of turning and lowliness. The νήπιος of Hebrews 5:13 is the ground of a milk-diet and of inexperience in the word. The writings use child-words for more than one object. The investigation keeps the objects apart.

**Inability and unreadability.** The unskilled cannot handle the word appropriately. The word remains what it is. Chapters 62–63, 65, 66, and 72 already forbid the inference that a failed handler makes the writing fail. This chapter inherits the prohibition and applies it to skill: inexperience is in the partaker, not in the word as a word that cannot be tried.

**The first hour and the become-such.** John 16:12, Acts 8:31, and 1 Peter 2:2 each know a limit on what a hearer can bear or understand. The limit of an hour not yet come, the limit of a first reading that asks for a guide, and the limit of a newborn who is commanded to desire milk are not the charge against those who have become needy of milk when they ought to be teachers. Collapsing them would make every beginning a sin and every request for a guide a confession of ἄπειρος in the accusatory sense.

**This chapter and the end of the book.** Movement IX closes when this word is stated. The book continues. A closed movement is not a closed manuscript.

Thus:

> **Unskillfulness in the Word is inexperience in the word of righteousness, with the immaturity and the inability the paragraph joins to it. It is not dullness reprinted, not the mode-catalog reprinted, not the labour-files under another name, not the absence of office, not calendar youth, not the humble child, not the unreadability of the Graphē, and not every beginning.**

# 73.7 Premises That May Be Used, and Premises That May Not

The chapter may use, as already in force:

- Truth as conformity to reality, distinct from belief, popularity, institutional consensus, and sincerity as a determinant of truth (Chapters 1–3).

- The call to reason as the Lord’s own call (Chapter 7), used now as the standing invitation that unskill has not cancelled.
- Knowing God by revelation rather than by speculation; human accounts about God as themselves to be tested (Chapters 13–14).

- Infallibility of the record as not infallibility of the interpreter, the church as church, or every later tradition (Chapter 57). An unskilled handler is not an infallible handler, and the record’s non-failure is not his skill.
- The Scripture cannot be broken (Chapters 62–63), used now as the reason an unskilled partaker does not break the writing or make it unhandleable in principle.
- The Graphē as the primary written authority (Chapter 65). Handling is measured by that authority. Unskill does not move the measure.
- Authority of Scripture as distinct from authority of interpretation; transfer and despair already refused; a correct reading remains possible (Chapters 66 and 67). Improper handling may be the act the inability performs. The five modes are not re-catalogued.
- Theology, philosophy, tradition, and institution as secondary systems (Chapter 68). A system’s Christian name, academic status, antiquity, or recognition does not constitute skill in the word of righteousness.

- Doctrinal turning aside as an inspectable movement, with intellectual failures that permit and do not excuse (Chapter 70). - Aridity as the dryness of engagement, judgment, and labour, with dullness of hearing located in the hearers (Chapter 72). Νωθρός remains that file’s word. The present file begins at ἄπειρος.

The chapter may use neighbouring passages that exhibit lack of skill, immaturity in the relevant sense, or inability to handle the word. It may not treat those passages as a substitute for Hebrews 5:13, and it may not treat Movement X as already performed.

Thus:

# 73.8 The Sequence of Levels: One Clean Scope Section

What this chapter does not settle is a matter of sequence, not of doubt about what it does settle.

The investigation proceeds by levels. Each level answers its question with full force. The next level remains next because it is a different question.

**Already in force.** Truth is conformity to reality. God is the ground of truth and cannot lie. Revelation has been given and authenticated. Scripture as writing is God-breathed. The record does not fail as truthful divine writing of what it actually communicates. The Scripture cannot be broken. The Graphē is the primary written authority. The authority of that writing has been distinguished from the authority claimed for a later statement of sense. Transfer fails. Despair fails. Scripture is misread in five inspectable modes, and the writing remains the measure of those modes. A correct reading remains possible. An incorrect reading remains incorrect. Theology, philosophy, tradition, and institution are secondary systems. They may serve. They do not become primary written authority by identity, academy, antiquity, or recognition. When secondary sources govern the meaning of Scripture, the seat of sense is occupied and the test is reversed. The writing is not broken by that occupation. Service is not that event. Doctrinal turning aside has causes and mechanisms the writings name. Intellectual failures permit departure and do not excuse it. The truth turned from remains the measure. Continuance is a real contrary. Erroneous doctrines become normalized and institutionalized by operations the writings exhibit, and institutional acceptance does not transform error into truth. Refusal remains possible. Aridity is the dryness of engagement, judgment, and labour. The loss of reasonability is the failure of sound judgment toward a Word that remains clear. Dullness of hearing is in the hearers. The hearer is not excused by the dryness. Sound judgment is an inspectable discipline. The Lord calls his people to reason. Recognition of the corpus is not creation of the corpus. Human accounts about God are accountable.

**This level.** Given those premises, ἄπειρος is unskillfulness in the word of righteousness: the milk-partaker is inexperienced in that word, for he is a babe, and the relationship of lack of skill, immaturity, and inability to handle the Word appropriately is the diagnosis the paragraph states. The Word remains handleable. The unskill is inspectable because the writing states it. The contrary — maturity with faculties trained to discern — is real in the same paragraph, and is named here without being methodized. After the time in which one ought to be a teacher, the condition charges the partaker. The warrant is full at this level. It is not postponed until Movement X in order to keep the present unskill weak, and it is not inflated into a labour-file in order to make the diagnosis sound like the cure.

**Next levels, named once.** The scholarly impetus of Scripture, perception and learning as stages, reasoning through the Word as labour, quotation and knowledge, σπουδάζω, the exercise of the Word, maturity and strong meat, the babe and the unskillful as a canon-wide diagnosis, rightly dividing, the approved workman, the discipline of interpretation, and the passage from the Graphē to the Six First Principles belong to Movement X. The Six First Principles themselves remain beyond that movement’s gate. Those files remain later.

**Limits that belong to this level itself, not to later hesitation.** This unskill is not a second statement of aridity. This unskill is not a claim that every hearer is a babe, or that the newborn’s milk is guilt, or that humility is immaturity, or that a first reading which asks for a guide is the charge of Hebrews 5:12. This unskill is not a claim that the writing is unhandleable in principle, or that a correct handling has become impossible. This unskill is not a claim that office, credential, eloquence, or the age of an institution is the skill, and it is not a claim that their absence is the unskill. This unskill is not a single-cause theory in which Chapters 70–72 are demoted into effects of a leaflet’s syntax. This unskill is not a manual of study and not a foundation-list. Using Chapters 57, 62–63, 65, 66, 67, 68, 69, 70, 71, and 72 is not rewriting them. An unskilled handler is not a sneer at the beginner. A babe in this sentence is not a sneer at the child. An inability is not a decree that the word has no sense. Those limits are part of what the unskill-file is. They are not a stack of clauses designed to drain the positive conclusion.

One scope section is enough. Later sections will not re-plead these limits as though the conclusion were still tentative. Where a particular passage does not carry a further inference, that local limit will be stated at that passage. The local limit protects the exhibition. It does not weaken the level.

Thus:

> **The sequence is: truth, record, primary writing, distinguished interpretation, catalogued misreading, ranked secondary systems, governance, turning, acceptance, and aridity already in force; unskillfulness in the Word at this level; the labour of the Word at the next movement. The unskill is warranted now. Movement IX closes on this word.**

# 73.9 Define “Lack of Skill” at This Stage

Lack of skill, at this stage, is the condition ἄπειρος names in its construction with the genitive.

Πεῖρα is trial. The privative says the trial has not been had. The genitive says where. The missing trial is not missing experience of weather, war, trade, or the management of a gathering. It is missing experience of the word of righteousness. The English “unskilful” is warranted because the capacity to deal with a word is what trial in that word produces, and the sentence denies the capacity by denying the trial. The English “inexperienced” is warranted because it stays with the privative and does not leap to a theory of ignorance as stupidity or as never having heard a sentence.

The hearers are not described as people to whom the oracles have never come. They need to be taught again. Again is πάλιν. The elements have been in reach. The lack of skill is unacquaintance by use after the word has been set before them, in the form of a diet that never leaves milk. A person who has never been addressed is not the subject of “teach you again.” The condition is stall or regression in handling, not the innocence of a first hearing.

Skill is not fluency, and lack of skill is not silence. A partaker can use the words of milk, and can be busy with the elements, and still be ἄπειρος in the word of righteousness. The skill denied is the skill of that word, the word that stands with solid food and with discernment of good and evil. Recitation of rudiments is compatible with the unskill. It can be the form the unskill wears.

Thus:

> **Lack of skill is inexperience in the word of righteousness: trial in that word not had, and the capacity the trial produces not there, in those who have already been addressed and who partake of milk.**

# 73.10 Define “Immaturity” at This Stage

Immaturity, at this stage, is νήπιος as Hebrews 5:13 uses it.

The clause is explanatory. Νήπιος γάρ ἐστιν gives the ground of the preceding description. Everyone who partakes of milk is inexperienced in the word of righteousness, for he is a babe. The babe is not a second accusation stacked on a skills-test the investigator invented. The babe is why the milk and the inexperience belong to him. He is on milk because he is an infant in this matter. He is untried in the word of righteousness because he is an infant in this matter.

The infant in view is measured by the time-clause, not by the cradle. For the time, they ought to be teachers. They have become such as need milk. Γεγόνατε, ye are become, names a condition arrived at. Immaturity here can be the failure to leave infancy, and it can be the return to a diet that had been outgrown. The sentence’s “become” forbids the flatter account in which nothing has happened and the hearers are simply young. Something has come to be: a need of milk, where teachers were owed.

Immaturity is undeveloped capacity relative to the word that is due. It is not a measurement of intelligence, and it is not a moral compliment. The figure of the infant says that the partaker is not in a condition to take the solid word. It does not say that the partaker cannot think, and it does not say that the partaker is charming. The charge is the diet and the inability, in the face of the time.

Other biblical uses of child-words judge other objects. They will be distinguished in the record. They are not allowed to redefine this clause in advance of that distinction.

Thus:

> **Immaturity is the babe-status Hebrews 5:13 assigns as the ground of the milk and the inexperience: a condition arrived at, measured by the time in which teachers were owed, and relative to the word of righteousness.**

# 73.11 Define “Inability to Handle the Word Appropriately” at This Stage

Inability to handle the Word appropriately, at this stage, is the use the paragraph describes.

The writer has many things to say, and they are hard to utter because the hearers are dull. That dullness is Chapter 72’s finding, used now as the neighbouring reason utterance fails. The present member is the handling. They have need that someone teach them again the elements of the beginning of the oracles. They have become needy of milk and not of solid food. Everyone in that diet is unskilled in the word of righteousness, for he is a babe. Solid food belongs to the mature, to those who by practice have their faculties trained to discern good and evil.

Handling appropriately would be taking the word at the level the time and the discourse require: teaching rather than being taught the elements again; solid food rather than milk; discernment of good and evil rather than an untrained faculty. The inability is the gap between that handling and the partaker’s condition. He handles milk. He does not handle the word of righteousness. The word is in the argument. The skill to take it is not in him.

“Appropriately” is fixed by the paragraph, not by a later technique. Appropriate handling is handling that answers to what the word is — the oracles of God, the discourse of righteousness, the solid food — and to what the time has made due. Inappropriate handling, in this file, is the milk-diet and the second teaching of elements where teachers were owed. It is not yet the finished account of right division, and it is not a list of interpretive rules.

The inability is in the partaker. The many things can be said. They are hard to say to these hearers. The solid food is real food. It belongs to those whose faculties have been trained. The existence of the food and of the mature is the paragraph’s witness that the word can be handled. The inability qualifies the babe. It does not qualify the word as a word without sense.

Thus:

> **Inability to handle the Word appropriately is the milk-partaker’s failure to take the word of righteousness at the level the time requires. The word remains sayable, teachable, and discernible. The failure is the handler’s.**

# 73.12 Positive Warrant: The Word Remains Handleable

The first member of the positive warrant is the handleability of the Word.

Handleability is not a hope added after the diagnosis in order to soften it. It is a consequence of what the Word has already been shown to be, confirmed by the paragraph’s own grammar.

The record does not fail as truthful divine writing of what it actually communicates. The Scripture cannot be broken. The Graphē is the primary written authority by which truth is known, tested, reasoned through, taught, discerned, and lived. A correct reading remains possible. An incorrect reading remains incorrect. The writing is not dry. Hardness of utterance is in the hearers. Those sentences are in force. An unskilled partaker is one more handler. He does not become a property of the writing. If inexperience in the word made the word untryable in principle, the mature of verse 14 would be a fiction, and the command to go on, which this chapter does not expound, would be a mockery. The paragraph does not speak that way. It withholds solid food from the babe and assigns it to the mature. Assignment to the mature is an affirmation that the food can be taken.

The word of righteousness is a word. Words that are true can be tried by those who will try them. The privative ἄπειρος locates the missing trial in the partaker. A privative of the handler is not a privative of the object. The object remains the sort of thing in which trial is possible. That is what it means for the Word to remain handleable.

This member does not say that handling is easy, or that every sentence is equally plain, or that no guide is ever required, or that the elements are contemptible. It says that unskill is not the unreadability of the Graphē. The diagnosis is severe because the word can be handled and, in these hearers, is not.

Thus:

> **The Word remains handleable. Unskillfulness qualifies the partaker of milk. It does not qualify the writing as a writing that cannot be tried. The mature in the same paragraph are the warrant’s own exhibit.**

# 73.13 Positive Warrant: The Unskill Is Inspectable Because the Writing States It

The second member of the positive warrant is the inspectability of the condition.

Inspectability is not a mood of suspicion toward hearers. It is the fact that Hebrews 5:13 states a condition in public words: everyone who partakes of milk is inexperienced in the word of righteousness, for he is a babe. The statement can be read. The genitive can be seen. The γάρ can be seen. The diet can be seen in the previous verse, and the contrary diet in the next. A diagnosis that could not be checked against the sentence would be a sneer. This diagnosis is a sentence.

The neighbouring exhibitions are likewise public. Corinth is told that the apostle could not speak to them as to spiritual, but as to babes, and that they were not able to bear meat. The Ephesians are told to be no longer infants, carried about by every wind of teaching. The Sadducees are told that they err, not knowing the Scriptures. The unlearned and unstable wrest the writings. These are statements. They can be set beside Hebrews 5:13 and distinguished from it. The distinctions are part of the inspection. A single insult called “unskilled,” applied without a text, would not be this warrant.

The condition is therefore not an invisible essence guessed from a person’s manner. It is a relation to the word: milk where solid food belongs, inexperience where trial was due, infancy where teachers were owed, inability where discernment is the mark of the mature. A hearer, a teacher, or a body can be asked the paragraph’s questions. Have you become such as need the elements again? Are you partaking of milk in the matter where the word of righteousness is the discourse required? Are you a babe in that word by the sentence’s own test? The questions are practical because the writing is specific.

Thus:

> **The unskill is inspectable because Hebrews states it and because the writings exhibit the neighbouring forms of inability. The diagnosis is a relation to the word, available to be read, not a temper the investigator feels.**

# 73.14 Positive Warrant: The Contrary Is Real, So Unskill Is Not the Nature of the Word

The third member of the positive warrant is the contrary.

Hebrews 5:14 does not describe a hypothetical class invented to shame the readers. It states to whom solid food belongs: to the mature, those who by reason of practice have their faculties trained to discern good and evil. The contrary of the milk-partaker is a real handling. Practice produces a condition. Faculties can be trained. Discernment of good and evil is a capacity the paragraph recognizes.

The contrary proves two things that belong to this level, and it does not prove the method.

It proves that unskill is not the nature of every hearer of this word. The paragraph that charges the babe acknowledges the mature. If everyone who heard were ἄπειρος by the mere fact of hearing, the contrast would collapse. The contrast stands. Unskill is a condition of the partaker of milk, not the definition of the audience as such.

It proves that the word is the sort of object in which practice has something to train. Discernment is πρὸς διάκρισιν καλοῦ τε καὶ κακοῦ, toward the discrimination of good and evil. The faculties are trained for a judgment the word sustains. A word that could not be handled would not be the object of a training the paragraph treats as actual.

The contrary does not, at this level, specify the exercises, the stages of learning, the verb of diligence, or the rule of right division. Διὰ τὴν ἕξιν names practice as the reason the faculties are trained. Naming the reason is the paragraph’s own contrast. Writing the practice as a course would be the exercise-file. The warrant uses the existence of the contrary. It does not spend the contrary as a syllabus.

The contrary also protects the beginner who is not this charge. Because unskill is not the nature of the word, a first honest milk is not a defeat of the word. Because the mature exist by practice, the milk of the newborn is aimed at something. The aim is stated by the contrast. The path is later.

Thus:

> **The contrary is real: solid food belongs to the mature whose faculties are trained to discern. Unskill is not the nature of the Word and not the fate of every hearer. The method of the training remains the next movement’s question.**

# 73.15 What the Unskillfulness Is

The three members of the warrant and the three members of the assignment now yield the definition the file exists to state.

Unskillfulness in the Word is the condition of everyone who partakes of milk: he is inexperienced in the word of righteousness, for he is a babe, and he is therefore unable to handle that word as the time and the discourse require. Lack of skill is the missing trial. Immaturity is the ground the sentence gives. Inability is the use. The Word remains the measure and remains handleable. The condition is public in the paragraph. The mature are the living contrary, named and not methodized.

The condition belongs to Movement IX because it is a way authoritative truth is mishandled: the word is possessed as milk, taught again as elements, left untried in its righteousness-discourse, and handled below what it is. The mishandling does not become a second authority. It does not make the milk into the solid food. It does not make the babe the measure of what the word may say. The writing still says what it says. The partaker does not yet handle it appropriately.

The condition closes the movement because the locked sequence ends the pathology here. Distinction, misreading, secondary rank, governance, turning, acceptance, dryness, and unskill are the movement’s eight words. The eighth states the handler’s lack of skill in the word itself. What answers the lack is not a ninth pathology. It is labour. Labour is the next movement.

Thus:

> **Unskillfulness in the Word is ἄπειρος in the word of righteousness: inexperience, infancy, and inability to handle, in a Word that remains handleable. That is Movement IX’s last word.**

# 73.16 What Does Not Constitute the Unskillfulness

The definition excludes, by the distinctions already drawn, the objects that resemble it and are not it. They are listed once so that the exhibitions can be read against a stable fence.

A dull ear, already judged, is not by itself this file. A misreading in one of the five modes is not by itself this file. A secondary system, a governing commentary, a turning, an accepted error, and a dry judgment are premises of the world in which unskill does its damage. They are not the definition of ἄπειρος.

Calendar youth is not the unskill. Absence of office or of a school’s credential is not the unskill. Eloquence is not the skill, and lack of eloquence is not the unskill. Skill in gathering a crowd, ordering a house, or preserving an institution is not skill in the word of righteousness. The newborn’s commanded desire for milk is not the charge. The humility of the little child is not the babe of this clause. The hour in which hearers cannot yet bear what has not been given is not the “become such.” The first reader who asks for a guide is not thereby the partaker whom time accuses. The boundless, as a philosophical privative of limit, is not this adjective. Ignorance as stupidity, and ignorance as never having heard, are not the inexperience of those who must be taught again. Possession of a Bible, recitation of its sentences, and long residence in a body that prints the Scriptures are not the trial the privative denies.

The exclusions keep the positive definition pointed. They are the fence around Hebrews 5:13, not a second essay.

Thus:

> **What constitutes the unskill is the milk-partaker’s inexperience in the word of righteousness, his babe-status as the sentence’s ground, and his inability to handle that word appropriately. The neighbouring conditions and the flattering substitutes do not constitute it.**

# 73.17 Biblical Record: Hebrews 5:12 — The Time, the Milk, and the Elements

The sentence of verse 13 is carried by verse 12. The record begins there, not in order to teach the elements, but in order to see what kind of milk the unskill belongs to.

Καὶ γὰρ ὀφείλοντες εἶναι διδάσκαλοι διὰ τὸν χρόνον, πάλιν χρείαν ἔχετε τοῦ διδάσκειν ὑμᾶς τινὰ τὰ στοιχεῖα τῆς ἀρχῆς τῶν λογίων τοῦ θεοῦ, καὶ γεγόνατε χρείαν ἔχοντες γάλακτος καὶ οὐ στερεᾶς τροφῆς.

For indeed, owing to be teachers by reason of the time, you again have need of someone to teach you the elements of the beginning of the oracles of God, and you have become having need of milk and not of solid food.

**Speaker, audience, occasion.** The writer of Hebrews is withholding a discourse he judges his readers unready to hear. The audience is addressed as people who have had time. The occasion is the turn from the high-priestly subject, hard to utter because they are dull, to the charge that explains the dullness in the language of diet and of rudiments. Chapter 72 has already placed the dullness. This verse places the time and the diet that verse 13 will interpret.

**The time.** Διὰ τὸν χρόνον is not a vague “eventually.” It is the ground of the obligation. Ὀφείλοντες εἶναι διδάσκαλοι: you are under obligation to be teachers. The obligation has a cause, and the cause is the time. Time in the oracles creates a debt of teaching. The debt has not been paid. The clause is the reason milk is not kindness in this paragraph. Kindness to a beginner is a different text. Here the time has already spoken.

**Again.** Πάλιν governs the need to be taught. The teaching required is a second teaching. The elements are not news. They are what must be laid down another time because the first laying did not yield teachers. “Again” is part of the charge. It is also part of the protection of the word: the elements can be taught. The inability is not a darkness in which even the beginning cannot be said. It is a return to the beginning when the time called for teaching others.

**The elements.** Τὰ στοιχεῖα τῆς ἀρχῆς τῶν λογίων τοῦ θεοῦ are the rudiments of the beginning of God’s oracles. The genitives stack a limitation: not the whole counsel as the writer’s present discourse, but the starting elements of the divine utterances. The book’s later gate will name a foundation of repentance from dead works and of faith toward God, and the teachings that follow that pair. This verse does not list them. What it warrants now is that the hearers’ need is a need to be retaught the beginning of the oracles, and that this need is the opposite of the teacher-obligation the time created.

**Become.** Γεγόνατε χρείαν ἔχοντες states a perfect: you have become people who have need. The need is a condition that has come about. The translation “are become such as have need of milk, and not of strong meat” holds the contrast inside the becoming. Solid food is what they do not need, in the writer’s diagnosis, because they are not in a condition to take it. The diagnosis is not a compliment to simplicity. It is an account of a diet that matches an inability.

**What the verse contributes to the three members.** The time and the unpaid obligation of teaching show that lack of skill, when verse 13 names it, is not the inexperience of the newly addressed. The milk, set against solid food, is the diet the inability wears. The elements taught again are the handling they can receive and the handling that accuses them. Immaturity is not yet named. Verse 13 will name it. Verse 12 has made the milk a moral and intellectual fact about hearers who had time.

**Local limit.** The verse does not warrant a syllabus of the elements, a history of how long “the time” is in years, or a verdict that every congregation of long standing has become infantile. It warrants the charge in the form the writer brings it: time, obligation, again, milk, not solid food.

Thus:

> **Hebrews 5:12 sets the milk of verse 13 inside a time that owed teachers, a second teaching of the elements, and a becoming-needy of milk rather than solid food. The unskill will be the interpretation of that diet, not a replacement of it.**

# 73.18 Biblical Record: Hebrews 5:13 — Ἄπειρος in the Word of Righteousness

Πᾶς γὰρ ὁ μετέχων γάλακτος ἄπειρος λόγου δικαιοσύνης, νήπιος γάρ ἐστιν.

For everyone who partakes of milk is inexperienced in the word of righteousness, for he is a babe.

**The first γάρ.** The clause explains verse 12. They have become needy of milk and not of solid food, for everyone who partakes of milk is ἄπειρος in the word of righteousness. The diet is not an arbitrary image. It is evidence of a condition, and it is the condition’s exhibition. Whoever lives on that diet, in the sense the paragraph means, stands in the privative. The πᾶς admits no special partaker who uses milk, in this accusatory sense, and is nevertheless skilled in the word of righteousness. The universality is inside the definition of this diet, not a census of every Christian household.

**The predicate.** Ἄπειρος is predicate. It is not a noun meaning “an amateur” as a social class. It describes the partaker. The description is governed by the genitive. He is not ἄπειρος in the absolute, as though the word meant “incompetent at life.” He is ἄπειρος of the word of righteousness. Take away the genitive and the verse’s charge disappears. Any account of unskillfulness in the Word that forgets the word of righteousness has forgotten the only object the adjective has in Scripture.

**The word of righteousness.** Λόγος in the previous breath was the writer’s many things to say. The natural contextual force of λόγου δικαιοσύνης is that discourse, specified as the teaching that has righteousness as its concern, the solid word over against the milk of the elements. A hearer can be occupied with rudiments and be untried in that discourse. The phrase also bears, by the range of λόγος and δικαιοσύνη, the sense of speech or teaching whose character is righteousness. What the context will not bear is a reduction to polished manners, or a reduction to a righteousness the partaker feels without a word. The unskill has a verbal object. The assignment’s “in the Word” is faithful to that object when the Word is the writing in which this discourse lives, and unfaithful to it when “the Word” is loosened into religious surroundings.

The further inference, that the phrase is a title for the Melchizedek-discourse because Hebrews 7:2 will gloss Melchizedek as king of righteousness, is possible and not explicit. Verse 11’s “of whom” already points the withheld discourse at the priest after the order of Melchizedek. The unskill is therefore occasioned by unreadiness for that discourse. The principle the verse states is wider than the occasion: everyone who partakes of milk is unskilled in the word of righteousness. The occasion shows the principle under strain. The principle is what the assignment develops. The priesthood-chapter is not this file.

**Partaking.** Μετέχων is present and continuous in force. The unskill is joined to a diet one is on, not to a moment of ignorance instantly repented. The common translation “useth milk” is right to hear a use. Use of milk is the life of the condition. It is not yet the exercise-file’s account of using the word. It is the use that constitutes the unskill.

**What is explicit.** It is explicit that the milk-partaker is ἄπειρος of the word of righteousness, and that the explanation is that he is a babe. It is explicit that this explains the diet of verse 12. It is explicit that the condition is general for that partaker, πᾶς.

**Local limit.** The verse does not define the stages by which experience is gained. It does not list the doctrines inside “righteousness.” It does not say that milk is everywhere and always a sin. It says what the partaker of this milk is.

Thus:

> **Hebrews 5:13 states the unskill: the one who partakes of milk is inexperienced in the word of righteousness. The object of the privative is that word. The diet is the exhibition. The next clause gives the ground.**

# 73.19 Lexical Record: Ἄπειρος, Not the Boundless

The adjective requires a lexical account that the translation “unskilful” both serves and can obscure.

**Original script.** ἄπειρος.

**Transliteration.** *apeiros*.

**Lexical form.** ἄπειρος, -ον, a two-termination adjective in this use.

**Strong’s number.** G552.

**Basic lexical meaning.** Without trial or experience of a thing; unacquainted; unused to. With a genitive of the thing.

**The privative.** The alpha is privative. The noun in view is πεῖρα, a trial, an attempt, an experience obtained by trying. To be ἄπειρος is to be without that trial. Skill enters the English because a tried capacity is a skill, and an untried capacity, in a matter where trial was due, is unskillfulness. The Greek does not contain a second root meaning “craft.” The craft-word is the true inference from the privative in this context, and it is the inference the common translation made. It is a sound inference. It is not a license to detach “skill” from trial in the word and reattach it to any competence the reader admires.

**The homograph.** Classical Greek also knows ἄπειρος as boundless, endless, the privative of a limit. That adjective belongs to a different composition, alpha with πεῖρας or πεῖραρ, a limit or end. It is the word later philosophy used for the infinite. It is not the word functioning in Hebrews 5:13. The genitive of a discourse, the diet of milk, and the classification “babe” are nonsense if the adjective means infinite. The partaker is not being called immeasurable. He is being called untried. A reading that imported the philosophical infinite would be a collision of homographs, impressive and empty.

**Grammatical form in the verse.** Nominative singular masculine, agreeing with the partaker, predicate, with the genitive of the sphere. There is no article on ἄπειρος. The construction is “is inexperienced,” not “is the inexperienced one” as a known office.

**Biblical usage.** The New Testament uses this adjective once. There is no second apostolic sentence to dilute the context. The hapax does not make the word doubtful. It makes the context decisive. Hebrews 5:12–14 is the usage. Milk, solid food, babe, mature, practice, and discernment fix the sense more tightly than a wander through classical boundlessness.

**“Inexperienced, that is, ignorant.”** A common lexicon-line, and the line a later leaflet repeats, equates the privative with ignorance and then with unskillfulness. The equation is intelligible as a gloss and false as an identity. Inexperience in a word yields a not-knowing of that word by trial. In that limited sense the partaker is ignorant of the word of righteousness: he does not know it as one who has handled it. The hearers are not ignorant as the unaddressed are ignorant, because they must be taught again. They are not ignorant as the unintelligent are called ignorant, because the charge is a diet and a time, not a measurement of mind. Ἀγνοέω and ἀμαθής are other words, available when the writings mean not-knowing or want of learning. Ἄπειρος was available for want of trial. The gloss “that is, ignorant” may stay as a consequence. It may not replace the privative.

**Argumentative significance.** The lexical force keeps the assignment honest. Unskillfulness in the Word is want of trial in the word of righteousness. Every competing account that relocates the want — into youth, office, crowd, institution, or the writing’s obscurity — has moved the genitive.

Thus:

> **Ἄπειρος is want of trial, constructed with the word of righteousness, rendered unskilful because trial produces the capacity to handle. It is not the boundless. “Ignorant” is a consequence-gloss, not a second definition, and not a verdict of stupidity or of never having heard.**

# 73.20 Biblical Record: The Γάρ and the Νήπιος

The second γάρ is the relationship’s hinge. Νήπιος γάρ ἐστιν.

**What γάρ does.** It gives the ground. The milk-partaker is inexperienced in the word of righteousness because he is a babe. The inexperience is not an accident beside an otherwise mature handler who happens to prefer simple food. The diet and the privative belong to him as infant. Immaturity is not a pastoral mood added to a skills-word. It is the clause without which the skills-word is unexplained inside the verse.

**What νήπιος means here.** The word’s use is infant, and, by figure, the person whose capacity is still the infant’s capacity. In this sentence the figure is fixed by milk and by the contrast with τέλειος in the next verse. The babe is the one for whom milk is the fitting diet and for whom the word of righteousness is untried. Fitting, in this paragraph, is accusatory, because verse 12 has established the time. The same word can be fitting without accusation when the hearer is newly begotten. That use is a different sentence. Here the infant is the explanation of a failure to be what the time required.

**What the relationship is, in the clause itself.** Lack of skill and immaturity are joined by γάρ as condition and ground. He is unskilled, for he is a babe. The inability is the diet the first γάρ explained: milk, not solid food, elements again. Three members, two conjunctions, one partaker. The investigator does not invent the join. The investigator refuses to break it.

**What the join is not.** It is not the claim that every immature person, in every biblical sense of child, is ἄπειρος in the word of righteousness. It is not the claim that lack of skill and immaturity are synonyms so that either word can be dropped. Drop the babe, and the ground is gone. Drop the unskill, and the milk is an image without the privative that interprets it. Drop the inability, and the diet of verse 12 and the solid food of verse 14 have nothing to do, which is to say the verse has been cut out of its paragraph. The relationship is a distinction inside a unity. The unity is one partaker’s condition. The distinction is ground, privative, and use.

**The traditional etymology.** Lexicons often analyze νήπιος as a privative of speech, one who does not speak. If the analysis is sound, it is a further figure of undeveloped capacity, interesting beside a word-skill the babe does not have. The verse does not argue from the etymology. An unskilled handler may speak a great deal. The Hebrews are not accused of silence. They are accused of a diet. The chapter does not rest the doctrine on the derivation.

**Local limit.** The clause warrants the join for the milk-partaker in this paragraph. It does not warrant the full account of how the writings use νήπιος, παιδίον, and βρέφος across the canon. That account, and the refusal to reduce the diagnosis to age or to institutional status, is a later assignment. This clause is the nucleus that later account will have to preserve, not the account itself.

Thus:

> **The second γάρ makes immaturity the ground of the unskill. The babe explains the milk-partaker’s inexperience in the word of righteousness. Lack of skill and immaturity are one condition with two members, and the inability is the use the paragraph has already named.**

# 73.21 Biblical Record: Hebrews 5:14 — The Paragraph’s Own Contrary, Named and Not Methodized

Τελείων δέ ἐστιν ἡ στερεὰ τροφή, τῶν διὰ τὴν ἕξιν τὰ αἰσθητήρια γεγυμνασμένα ἐχόντων πρὸς διάκρισιν καλοῦ τε καὶ κακοῦ.

Solid food is for the mature, for those who by reason of practice have their faculties trained for the discernment of both good and evil.

**Δέ.** The particle sets the contrary. Milk belongs to the account of the babe. Solid food belongs to the mature. The paragraph is not a rebuke without a positive pole. The positive pole is stated in a genitive of possession and a description of faculties.

**The mature.** Τέλειος here is full-grown relative to the infant. The common translation “of full age” is right if age is understood as the maturity verse 12’s time failed to produce, and wrong if it is understood as a birthday. The mature are identified by their faculties, not by their years.

**Practice.** Διὰ τὴν ἕξιν: on account of the habit, the condition that practice produces. The faculties are γεγυμνασμένα, having been trained. The perfect participle matches the perfects of the charge. The babe has become needy of milk. The mature have had their faculties trained. Both are conditions arrived at. One is the unskill. The other is the contrary. The preposition διά gives practice as the reason of the training. That is as far as this chapter takes the reason. The exercise of the Word is the file that will ask what the practice is. Stealing it now would turn a contrast into a course.

**Discernment.** The training is toward διάκρισις of good and evil. Handling, in the paragraph’s own contrary, includes discrimination. The inability of the babe is not only a failure to enjoy harder sentences. It is a failure to discern. Chapter 72 has already established poor judgment as a dryness. This verse is not that establishment repeated. It is the skill-side of discernment: faculties trained, by practice, for a distinction the unskilled do not make. The two files meet in the act of discerning and remain two files. Dryness was the loss of the judgment. Unskill is the untrained faculty of the babe in the word. A mature discernment is what the unskill lacks. The way the faculty is trained is not said beyond the noun ἕξις.

**What the verse contributes.** It completes the relationship. Inability to handle appropriately is inability relative to a real ability the paragraph describes: taking solid food, with trained faculties, unto discernment. Lack of skill is the privative of the trial that such practice is. Immaturity is the opposite of τέλειος. The three members are legible across three verses, and verse 14 is their contrast, not their replacement.

**Local limit.** The verse does not warrant a psychology in which the soul has an organ corresponding to each bodily sense, offered as doctrine. Αἰσθητήρια are faculties of perception. The sentence uses them for discernment of good and evil. A further anatomy of the soul is not the text. The verse also does not warrant the claim that the mature are beyond teaching, or that instruction is a mark the investigator may add to the two the text gives. The text gives maturity and trained discernment. Additions are not the contrary.

Thus:

> **Hebrews 5:14 names the contrary: solid food, the mature, faculties trained by practice unto discernment. The contrary shows what appropriate handling is. It does not write the practice, the maturity-doctrine, or the exercise-file.**

# 73.22 The Three Members Joined

The paragraph can now be stated as the relationship the assignment requires.

**Lack of skill** is ἄπειρος λόγου δικαιοσύνης. The milk-partaker has not the trial of the word of righteousness. That is Hebrews 5:13’s predicate.

**Immaturity** is νήπιος γάρ ἐστιν. The privative belongs to him because he is a babe. The time of verse 12 measures the babe as one who ought to have been a teacher and has become needy of milk. That is the ground.

**Inability to handle the Word appropriately** is the use running through verses 12 and 14 and interpreted by verse 13. He must be taught the elements again. He partakes of milk and not of solid food. He does not have the trained discernment to which solid food belongs. The word of righteousness is the word he does not handle. The elements are the word he can only receive again, not teach.

The order of dependence inside the verse is ground, then condition, then — by the frame — use. He is a babe; therefore he is unskilled in that word; therefore, as verse 12 has already said and verse 14 will contrast, he is on milk and not on the food of the mature. The order of discovery in the paragraph is the reverse: the writer meets a dullness, finds a diet that has become milk, and interprets the diet as unskill grounded in infancy. Both orders are honest. The dependence is the babe as ground. The exhibition is the diet. The name of the condition, for this book, is unskillfulness in the Word.

The three are not interchangeable labels. A man may be immature in the sense of newly begotten and not be this unskill, because the time has not yet created the debt of teaching. A man may lack a skill in some other art and not be ἄπειρος of this word. A man may be unable to bear a word that has not yet been given, and the inability is then the hour’s limit, not the babe’s diet. The join holds where the three appear as the paragraph joins them: one partaker, one word, one time, one ground.

Understood this way, the relationship answers the question Chapter 72 left open. Dullness was the first mark. Unskill is the second. The second is not a restatement of hearing that fails to consider. It is inexperience in the word the hearing was for, grounded in a babe-status the time accuses, issuing in a handling that stays at milk. A hearer can carry both marks. The marks remain two.

Thus:

> **Lack of skill, immaturity, and inability to handle the Word appropriately stand as predicate, ground, and use of one milk-partaker in the word of righteousness. The join is the paragraph’s. The distinctions inside the join keep later files and different child-sentences from being swallowed.**

# 73.23 Biblical Record: 1 Corinthians 3:1–2 — Babes Who Could Not Receive

Καὶ ἐγώ, ἀδελφοί, οὐκ ἠδυνήθην λαλῆσαι ὑμῖν ὡς πνευματικοῖς ἀλλ’ ὡς σαρκίνοις, ὡς νηπίοις ἐν Χριστῷ. γάλα ὑμᾶς ἐπότισα, οὐ βρῶμα· οὔπω γὰρ ἐδύνασθε. ἀλλ’ οὐδὲ ἔτι νῦν δύνασθε.

And I, brothers, could not speak to you as to spiritual, but as to carnal, as to babes in Christ. I fed you with milk, not with meat, for you were not yet able. But neither yet now are you able.

**What the passage exhibits.** Paul names a relationship this chapter recognizes: babes, milk, inability to receive the word he would have spoken. Νήπιοι ἐν Χριστῷ are infants who are in Christ. The infancy is not outsider-status. It is a condition inside faith. Milk was given because they could not bear meat. The inability is οὔπω, not yet, and it persists: οὐδὲ ἔτι νῦν, not even now. Time has passed between the feeding and the letter, and the ability has not arrived. That persistence is the point of contact with Hebrews’ “become” and “by reason of the time.”

**What the passage adds.** It adds that the inability can be the reason a true teacher withholds meat and gives milk. Milk, in the apostle’s past act, was an adaptation to their condition, not the goal of the apostleship. The goal is visible in the reproach that they are still unable. Appropriate handling by the teacher included milk for those who could not bear meat. Appropriate condition in the hearer did not include remaining unable. The distinction protects the relationship from a crude reading in which every cup of milk is disobedience, including the cup a faithful teacher gives to the unable.

**What the passage does not collapse.** Corinthians adds σαρκίνοις, fleshly, and the following verses will speak of jealousy and strife as the proof that they are still fleshly and walk as men. Hebrews 5:13 does not ground the unskill in party-strife. The relationship of babe, milk, and inability is shared. The accompanying moral exhibition is Corinth’s. Importing jealousy as the definition of ἄπειρος would add a ground the Hebrews clause does not give. Refusing the shared relationship because the accompanying sins differ would break a real parallel.

**Local limit.** The passage does not warrant a doctrine that the spiritual are a class who no longer need the Scriptures, or that “meat” is a secret teaching beside the gospel. The meat is what Paul would have spoken and could not. The passage also does not write the method by which the unable become able. It states the inability and its persistence.

Thus:

> **First Corinthians 3 exhibits babes in Christ, fed with milk because they could not bear meat, and still unable. The relationship of immaturity and inability is confirmed. The fleshly strife of Corinth is not the definition of ἄπειρος. The teacher’s adaptive milk is not the hearer’s excuse for remaining unable.**

# 73.24 Biblical Record: 1 Peter 2:2 — The Milk That Is Commanded

Ὡς ἀρτιγέννητα βρέφη τὸ λογικὸν ἄδολον γάλα ἐπιποθήσατε, ἵνα ἐν αὐτῷ αὐξηθῆτε.

As newborn babes, long for the pure milk of the word, that you may grow thereby.

**The words are different.** Βρέφος, a newborn infant, and ἀρτιγέννητα, newly begotten, are not νήπιος as Hebrews 5:13 uses it. The verb is ἐπιποθήσατε, long for, an imperative. The milk is τὸ λογικὸν ἄδολον γάλα, the pure milk that belongs to the word. The purpose-clause that the sentence certainly contains is growth. A wider Greek text adds εἰς σωτηρίαν, unto salvation, at the end of the purpose. The addition is not in the common English of the verse, and this chapter does not hang the distinction on it. Milk here is commanded, and the command is aimed at growth.

**What the distinction does.** It prevents the relationship in Hebrews from becoming a hatred of milk. The writings command the newly begotten to desire the word’s milk. Desire is the appropriate handling of the word at that hour. Growth is the purpose, which means the milk is not a permanent identity. Hebrews addresses those who by reason of time ought to be teachers and have become needy of milk and not of solid food. Peter addresses those who are to long for milk in order to grow. One sentence accuses a stalled or relapsed diet. The other commands a beginning diet ordered to growth. Both can be true. Neither is the other.

**What a collapse would do.** If 1 Peter 2:2 were read as Hebrews 5:13, the newborn’s obedience would be unskill in the accusatory sense, and the imperative would be a command to sin. If Hebrews 5:13 were read as 1 Peter 2:2, the charge against those who ought to be teachers would soften into a tenderness the time-clause does not offer. The relationship this chapter defends includes both texts: milk is the proper desire of the newly begotten, and milk is the indictment of the partaker who should have been a teacher. Immaturity that longs and grows is not the immaturity that has become the diet where solid food belongs.

**Local limit.** Where the Greek includes “unto salvation,” that phrase orders the growth; it is not a license to rewrite Hebrews’ “word of righteousness” as the slogan “basis for salvation,” and it is not a soteriology to be developed here. With or without the phrase, the milk of this verse is ordered to growth, which is a real end. The end does not make the later refusal of solid food innocent.

Thus:

> **First Peter 2:2 commands newborn babes to long for the pure milk of the word in order to grow. That milk is obedience. Hebrews’ milk, after the time that owed teachers, is the unskill. The relationship stands only if the two milks are kept apart.**

# 73.25 Biblical Record: 1 Corinthians 14:20 — Children in Understanding

Ἀδελφοί, μὴ παιδία γίνεσθε ταῖς φρεσίν, ἀλλὰ τῇ κακίᾳ νηπιάζετε, ταῖς δὲ φρεσὶν τέλειοι γίνεσθε.

Brothers, do not be children in your understandings, but in evil be infants, and in your understandings be mature.

**The distinction the verse itself draws.** Παίδια in the understandings is forbidden. Νηπιάζειν in evil is commanded. Τέλειοι in the understandings is commanded. The same letter that called the Corinthians babes in Christ now refuses them the right to be children in judgment, and it uses the infant-verb for a different object: malice, where infancy is a good. Childlikeness is not one biblical commodity.

**What it contributes to the relationship.** Immaturity in Hebrews 5:13 is immaturity relative to the word of righteousness, kin to being children in understanding, not kin to being infants in evil. The inability to handle the word is an understanding-failure, in the family of φρήν, the mind that judges. It is not a failure to be harmless. A handler can be gentle and be ἄπειρος in the word. A handler can be called to innocence in evil and still be obliged to be mature in understanding. The verse makes the obligation explicit with τέλειοι, the same maturity-word Hebrews 5:14 uses for those who take solid food.

**Local limit.** The verse belongs to an argument about tongues, prophecy, and the mind in the assembly. This chapter does not expound that argument. It uses the verse’s own distinction among child-words. It does not claim that Hebrews 5:13 and 1 Corinthians 14:20 are the same occasion.

Thus:

> **The writings forbid childhood in understanding, command infancy in evil, and command maturity in understanding. The unskill of Hebrews is of the first and third sort. It is not a failure to be an infant in malice, and child-language in Scripture is not one accusation.**

# 73.26 Biblical Record: Matthew 18:3 — The Child Who Is Not This Babe

Καὶ εἶπεν, Ἀμὴν λέγω ὑμῖν, ἐὰν μὴ στραφῆτε καὶ γένησθε ὡς τὰ παιδία, οὐ μὴ εἰσέλθητε εἰς τὴν βασιλείαν τῶν οὐρανῶν.

And he said, Truly I say to you, unless you turn and become as the little children, you will not enter the kingdom of heaven.

**The object.** The child here is παιδίον, and the verb is become. The becoming is a turning. The saying answers the disciples’ question about who is greatest. The child is set in the midst as the rebuke of greatness. Lowliness, reception of a child, and the refusal of scandal carry the paragraph. Nothing in the paragraph says that the child is inexperienced in the word of righteousness, or that entry into the kingdom is a return to milk after one ought to have been a teacher.

**Why the distinction is required for the relationship.** If immaturity in Hebrews were the same object as this child, the charge of ἄπειρος would be a charge against the very turning Jesus requires, and the obligation to become teachers by reason of the time would contradict the obligation to become as little children. The writings do not contradict themselves. They use child-figures for different acts. One act is turning from greatness. The other is remaining, or becoming again, unable to handle the word. Humility is not unskill. Unskill is not humility. A proud teacher can be skilled in the word and fail Matthew 18. A humble hearer can be ἄπειρος in the word of righteousness and fail Hebrews 5. Each sentence keeps its own force.

**Local limit.** The passage does not warrant a sentimentality in which childlikeness excuses ignorance of the Scriptures. It warrants a turning. The turning is not the diet of milk.

Thus:

> **Matthew 18’s little child is the pattern of turning and lowliness. The νήπιος of Hebrews 5:13 is the ground of unskill in the word. Becoming as a child in the kingdom-saying is not the inability to handle the Word.**

# 73.27 Biblical Record: Ephesians 4:14 — Children Carried by Teaching

Ἵνα μηκέτι ὦμεν νήπιοι, κλυδωνιζόμενοι καὶ περιφερόμενοι παντὶ ἀνέμῳ τῆς διδασκαλίας ἐν τῇ κυβείᾳ τῶν ἀνθρώπων, ἐν πανουργίᾳ πρὸς τὴν μεθοδείαν τῆς πλάνης.

That we may be no longer infants, tossed and carried about by every wind of teaching, by the sleight of men, in craftiness toward the scheming of error.

**The infant and the teaching.** Νήπιοι here are those whom every wind of διδασκαλία can carry. The infancy is defined by inability to stay put under teaching. The goal of the gifts in the preceding verses is a mature man, unto the measure of the stature of the fullness of Christ, with the explicit negative that the body be no longer infants of this sort. Immaturity and inability stand together again: the infant cannot handle teaching so as to discern its winds. Handling is not a luxury of scholars. It is the stability the infant lacks.

**The relation to earlier files.** Chapter 70 has already examined turning aside, and the winds of teaching are a mechanism by which hearers are carried from what they were given. This verse is not a second turning-file. The new function is the infant as the one who cannot handle. The sleight and the craft are real. They succeed against the νήπιος. They do not succeed by making the teaching-wind true. Chapter 71’s premise holds: a teaching that has become the wind of a body is not thereby the word. The present point is narrower: the infant condition is the condition in which that wind is not discerned.

**What it adds to inability.** Inability to handle the Word appropriately includes inability to test teaching by the word. The Ephesian infant is carried by teaching because he is an infant. The Hebrew babe is on milk because he is a babe and unskilled in the word of righteousness. The two are not the same sentence. They share the member this assignment calls inability. A handler who cannot discern good and evil, in Hebrews’ contrary, is the same kind of danger Ephesians names as being carried. The discernment is one. The files stay distinct: this chapter states the inability inside the unskill; it does not re-narrate the turn.

**Local limit.** The verse does not warrant a fear of all teaching, or a claim that teachers are the winds. The same paragraph gives teachers so that the body may grow. The wind is the teaching that the infant cannot judge. The judgment is the capacity. The method of the judgment is later.

Thus:

> **Ephesians 4:14 exhibits the infant as the one carried by every wind of teaching. Immaturity and inability to handle teaching stand together. The winds do not become truth by carrying him. The verse confirms the relationship. It does not retry the turning-file.**

# 73.28 Biblical Record: Matthew 22:29 — Erring by Not Knowing the Scriptures

Ἀποκριθεὶς δὲ ὁ Ἰησοῦς εἶπεν αὐτοῖς, Πλανᾶσθε μὴ εἰδότες τὰς γραφὰς μηδὲ τὴν δύναμιν τοῦ θεοῦ.

Jesus answered and said to them, You err, not knowing the Scriptures, nor the power of God.

**The occasion.** The Sadducees have brought a case about the resurrection, built from the levirate law, designed to make the resurrection absurd. They know a text well enough to wield it. Jesus’ answer is that they err, and the ground of the error is a double not-knowing: the Scriptures, and the power of God. He then cites what has been spoken by God, “I am the God of Abraham,” and charges them that they do not read what was said to them.

**Unskill, not mere absence of a Bible.** Μὴ εἰδότες is a not-knowing. It is not ἄπειρος, and the chapter does not pretend it is the same lexeme. The function in this file is the inability the not-knowing produces: they handle a commandment about marriage as a refutation of the resurrection, and the handling errs because they do not know the Scriptures they quote around. Possession of a case, and possession of a sentence of the law, is compatible with not knowing the Scriptures. That compatibility is the point of contact with the milk-partaker who has the elements and does not have the word of righteousness. Rudiments in the hand are not trial of the word.

**The second object.** “Nor the power of God” keeps the not-knowing from being a merely literary complaint. The resurrection is a thing God does. A handling that knows neither the writing nor the power will build clever cases and call them exegesis. This chapter does not develop a doctrine of power. It notes that Jesus joins the two objects, so that unskill in the Scriptures is not repaired by a cleverness that leaves God’s power out, and a claim of power that does not know the Scriptures is the other half of the same error.

**Local limit.** The verse does not warrant the claim that every error has the same cause, or that the Sadducees are the Hebrews. It warrants an exhibition: error in handling a question of the word is grounded, by the Truth’s own statement, in not knowing the Scriptures. Chapter 67’s modes may describe how such a case fails context and inference. The present function is the skill-fact underneath the mode: they do not know the writings they use.

Thus:

> **Matthew 22:29 exhibits inability as error grounded in not knowing the Scriptures, in men who could quote the law for a case. Wielding a sentence is not trial of the word. The not-knowing is kin to ἄπειρος and is not the same word. The error does not make the Scriptures fail.**

# 73.29 Biblical Record: 2 Peter 3:16 — The Unlearned Wrest

Ἐν αἷς ἐστιν δυσνόητά τινα, ἃ οἱ ἀμαθεῖς καὶ ἀστήρικτοι στρεβλοῦσιν ὡς καὶ τὰς λοιπὰς γραφάς πρὸς τὴν ἰδίαν αὐτῶν ἀπώλειαν.

In which are some things hard to understand, which the unlearned and unstable wrest, as they do also the other Scriptures, to their own destruction.

**Hardness without unreadability.** Peter has called Paul’s letters Scripture in the act of saying the unlearned wrest them as they wrest the other Scriptures. Δυσνόητα, hard to understand, is a real property of some things in the letters. Chapter 66 has already used hardness as evidence that interpretation is a real act with a real object, and has refused to make hardness into equal validity of all readings. The present function is the handler. The things are hard. The wresting is done by the ἀμαθεῖς καὶ ἀστήρικτοι, the unlearned and unstable. Hardness is not the license. The condition of the handler is the instrument of the wrest.

**Ἀμαθής and ἄπειρος.** Unlearned is not the same adjective as inexperienced. Want of learning and want of trial are neighbours. Both name a handler who is not furnished for the word in front of him. Peter adds instability, which Chapter 70 has already read as a failure that permits departure. This chapter uses the pair as an exhibition of inability: wresting is a handling, and it is inappropriate handling, and it is the act of the unlearned and unstable. The destruction is theirs. The Scriptures are not destroyed. “As also the other Scriptures” places Paul’s letters with the Graphē. The wrest is a mishandling of the primary written authority, not the birth of a second one.

**What the relationship gains.** Inability to handle appropriately can take the form of violence to the sense, not only the form of staying on milk. The babe of Hebrews does not take the solid word. The unlearned of Peter twist the hard word. Both are failures of handling. They are not one portrait. A partaker can be on milk without being a wrestler of Paul, and a wrestler can be busy with hard texts without the humility of one who knows he needs the elements. The assignment’s third member is wide enough to include both exhibitions and precise enough to keep them distinct. The Hebrew diagnosis remains the milk and the word of righteousness. Peter shows what inappropriate handling looks like when the unlearned touch what is hard.

**Local limit.** The verse does not warrant a ban on reading what is hard, or a claim that difficulty is the reader’s illusion. Some things are hard to understand. The verse also does not write the discipline that would keep a reader from wresting. It identifies who wrests, and what the wrest does to them.

Thus:

> **Second Peter 3:16 exhibits inappropriate handling as the wrest of the unlearned and unstable, practiced on writings that remain Scripture, including things hard to understand. Hardness does not authorize the wrest. Unlearned instability is kin to unskill and is not the definition of the milk-partaker.**

# 73.30 Biblical Record: Acts 8:30–31 — Inability That Asks for a Guide

The eunuch reads Isaiah. Philip asks, Ἆρά γε γινώσκεις ἃ ἀναγινώσκεις; Do you understand what you are reading? The answer is a question: Πῶς γὰρ ἂν δυναίμην ἐὰν μή τις ὁδηγήσῃ με; How should I be able, unless someone guide me? He asks Philip to come up and sit with him. The passage he reads is the one about the lamb led to the slaughter. Philip, beginning from that Scripture, announces to him the good news of Jesus.

**A real inability.** The man states an inability. Understanding the reading requires a guide. The scroll is in his hands. Possession of the writing is not yet understanding. That gap is the point of contact with unskill: a word can be present and unhandled. The assignment’s third member has a milder cousin here, and the cousin must be recognized so that the accusation of Hebrews is not applied to every open question.

**Why this is not the charge.** He asks. He desires a guide. He is not described as one who ought, by reason of time, to have been a teacher of this scroll and has become needy of milk. He is a reader on a road, at a text he does not yet see, met by a messenger. The inability is the occasion of guidance, and the guidance begins from the Scripture and leads to Jesus. The word was handleable. The handling came by a guide he requested. Nothing in the narrative rebukes him for the request. The narrative fulfills the request.

**What the distinction protects.** Inability to handle is not one moral fact. There is an inability that asks, and there is an inability that has become the diet of those who owed teaching. The first is met by exposition that starts from the writing. The second is charged by Hebrews 5:12–13. Treating every “how can I?” as ἄπειρος in the accusatory sense would make the eunuch’s question a sin and Philip’s sitting with him a condoning of it. Treating the Hebrew charge as nothing more than the eunuch’s question would erase the time-clause. The relationship stays sharp: lack of skill is culpable where the time and the diet make it the babe’s condition, and a first inability that seeks a guide is the word being honored, not refused.

**Local limit.** The passage does not warrant a rule that no one may understand a text without an official guide, and it does not warrant a rule that guides are unnecessary because the word is clear. It warrants this scene: a reader asked, a guide opened the writing, the writing preached Christ. The discipline of how guides should handle texts is later. The scene’s contribution now is the distinction inside inability.

Thus:

> **Acts 8 exhibits an inability that asks for a guide, and a guide who begins from the Scripture. The word was handleable. The request was not the milk-charge of those who ought to be teachers. Inability that seeks is not ἄπειρος as Hebrews accuses it.**

# 73.31 Biblical Record: John 16:12 — Cannot Bear Them Now

Ἔτι πολλὰ ἔχω ὑμῖν λέγειν, ἀλλ’ οὐ δύνασθε βαστάζειν ἄρτι.

I have yet many things to say to you, but you cannot bear them now.

**The likeness.** The sentence sounds like Hebrews 5:11–12. Many things to say; hearers who cannot bear them. Both speakers limit present speech by the hearers’ capacity. The likeness is real and is the reason the verses are often fused. The fusion is the error.

**The difference.** Ἄρτι, now, is the limit Jesus names. The hour is before the cross has done its work in their sight and before the Spirit, whom he is promising, has come to guide them into all the truth. The inability is indexed to what has not yet been given and to what they cannot yet carry. It is not indexed to a time that already obligated them to be teachers and found them relapsed onto milk. “Now” looks toward a soon. Hebrews’ “by reason of the time” looks toward a debt already due. One is sequence in the giving of revelation to the apostles in the last hours before the Passion. The other is a charge against hearers of a letter who have had the oracles and have not come to teaching.

**What the relationship gains by the difference.** Inability to bear a word is sometimes the wisdom of the speaker, matching speech to an hour. That inability does not make the hearers ἄπειρος in the accusatory sense. Jesus does not call the eleven babes who partake of milk when they ought to be teachers. He says they cannot bear these things now, and he promises the further guidance. Appropriate speech included restraint. The restraint does not redefine Hebrews’ rebuke as the same restraint. Where the time has created the debt, continued inability is the unskill. Where the hour has not yet come, inability is the hour.

**Local limit.** The verse does not warrant a permanent silence about anything hard, on the ground that hearers cannot bear it. The “now” has a promised after. It also does not warrant treating every withholding of teaching as obedience to John 16:12. Withholding is obedience when the hour is the reason. Withholding is the other fact when the hearers have become dull and milky after time. Chapter 72 located the hardness of Hebrews 5:11 in the hearers. This verse locates a limit in the hour. Both locations are biblical. They are not one location.

Thus:

> **John 16:12 is inability indexed to an hour not yet come. Hebrews 5:13 is unskill indexed to a time already due. Many things to say, and hearers who cannot bear them, are not yet the babe on milk. The hour and the debt are different grounds.**

# 73.32 Biblical Record: Isaiah 28:9–13 — Weaned from the Milk

Whom shall he teach knowledge? and whom shall he make to understand the message? Them that are weaned from the milk, and drawn from the breasts. For precept upon precept, precept upon precept; line upon line, line upon line; here a little, and there a little.

**The setting.** Isaiah 28 opens on the drunkards of Ephraim and turns to priest and prophet who reel with wine, err in vision, and stumble in judgment. Verses 9–10 stand in that controversy. The questions about who is fit to be taught, and the image of those weaned from milk, are followed by the repetitive syllables the common translation renders as precept upon precept and line upon line. Verse 11 answers that by stammering lips and another tongue he will speak to this people. Verse 13 repeats the syllables and says that the word of the Lord will be to them precept upon precept, that they may go and fall backward and be broken.

**What can be said without settling the speaker.** Readers have long divided on whether verses 9–10 are the prophet’s question — knowledge is for those weaned from milk, and the teaching comes line upon line — or the scoffers’ mimicry of the prophet as a teacher of infants, babbling small syllables. The Hebrew of the repeated words can be heard as sense, “precept” and “line,” and can be heard as mocking sound. The division is real. This chapter does not pretend it is closed, and it does not build the definition of ἄπειρος on a speaker-identification the text leaves contested.

**What the passage still exhibits.** Milk and weaning are already, inside a controversy about teaching knowledge, the images the text uses for who can be taught. A people whose priests stumble in judgment are in no position to despise elementary teaching, if the syllables are a taunt; and a people who will not hear are told that the word they refused will become the line upon line over which they fall, if the syllables are the message. In either reading, the milk-image is joined to the question of who is able to receive knowledge, and the sequel is judgment on those who will not be taught. That is enough for this file. It is an Old Testament exhibition, in the language of milk and weaning, that ability to be taught is a real condition, and that refusal of teaching does not leave the refuser in a harmless simplicity.

**What is not imported.** The passage is not quoted as a Hebrew equivalent of ἄπειρος. The Greek adjective remains a hapax with its own construction. Isaiah does not use the phrase “word of righteousness.” The contribution is the milk-image in a teaching-controversy, kept honest about the disputed voice. The contribution is not a second definition, and it is not a claim that line-upon-line repetition is the method of skill. Method remains later. If verse 10 is the scoffers’ baby-talk, it is certainly not a curriculum. If it is the prophet’s method, it is still not this chapter’s assignment to write the curriculum.

Thus:

> **Isaiah 28 sets milk and weaning inside a controversy about who can be taught knowledge, in a passage whose speaker is disputed and whose sequel is a fall. The image confirms that ability to be taught is real and that scorn or refusal is not innocence. It does not redefine ἄπειρος, and it does not supply the method.**

# 73.33 Biblical Record: Acts 18:24–26 — Mighty in the Scriptures, and the More Accurate Way

Apollos is described as a Jew, an Alexandrian, an eloquent man, mighty in the Scriptures, instructed in the way of the Lord, fervent in spirit, speaking and teaching accurately the things concerning Jesus, though he knew only the baptism of John. He spoke boldly in the synagogue. Aquila and Priscilla heard him, took him, and expounded to him the way of God more accurately. Ἀκριβέστερον is the comparative: more accurately. The common translation “more perfectly” carries the same correction in a softer English.

**Eloquence and might are not the skill of the whole way.** He is λόγιος, able in speech, and δυνατὸς ἐν ταῖς γραφαῖς, powerful in the Scriptures. Those are real descriptions. They do not prevent the narrative from recording a limit: he knew only the baptism of John. Skill in speaking and strength in the writings can stand with an incomplete holding of the way. The incompleteness was met by exposition, privately, by two hearers who had the more accurate way to set forth. He received it. The narrative then sends him on, and the brothers commend him, and he helps those who have believed, publicly, from the Scriptures, showing that Jesus is the Christ.

**Why this is not the Hebrew charge, and why it still serves the relationship.** Apollos is not said to have become dull, or to have owed a teacher’s office by wasted time and relapsed onto milk. He is instructed, accurate as far as he knows, fervent, and correctable. The inability, if the word is used, is a remaining inaccuracy, and it is healed by teaching he receives. The episode therefore protects the definition from two corruptions. It protects it from the claim that eloquence, public boldness, or being mighty in the Scriptures just is the skill Hebrews 5:13 means. He had those, and still needed a more accurate way. It protects it from the claim that any further need of teaching is the babe’s guilt. He needed teaching, and the need was not the indictment of one who ought already to have been past milk. Appropriate handling included his reception of the more accurate exposition. The word was handleable. Two tentmakers handled it toward him. He handled it afterward in public, from the Scriptures.

**Local limit.** The passage does not warrant a life of Apollos, a theory of John’s baptism, or a rule that private correction is the only way accuracy grows. It warrants the distinction: might in the Scriptures is not yet the whole skill, and a further accuracy received is not the milk-charge of Hebrews.

Thus:

> **Acts 18 exhibits a man mighty in the Scriptures and eloquent, who still needed the way expounded more accurately, and who received it. Eloquence and scriptural might are not ἄπειρος’s opposite by themselves. A received correction is not the guilt of the relapsed babe. The word was handleable in both directions.**

# 73.34 Biblical Record: Luke 24:45 — Opened Their Understanding

Τότε διήνοιξεν αὐτῶν τὸν νοῦν τοῦ συνιέναι τὰς γραφάς.

Then he opened their mind to understand the Scriptures.

**The scene’s own order.** He has said that all things written in the law of Moses, and the prophets, and the psalms, concerning him must be fulfilled. He has said that thus it is written, and thus it behoved the Christ to suffer and to rise, and that repentance and remission should be preached in his name. Then he opened their mind to understand the Scriptures. The opening is toward understanding τὰς γραφάς. The object is the writings. The act is his.

**What Chapter 72 already took.** Slowness of heart to believe all that the prophets had spoken is the dryness-file’s exhibition, Luke 24:25. This verse is not that reproach restated. The new function is the opening. A mind can be shut to the writings and can be opened to them. The shut mind was not evidence that the writings had no sense. The opened mind understands the writings. Understanding is a handling. It was possible. It was given.

**What the relationship gains.** Inability to handle is not a fate sealed in the nature of readers. The same chapter that records slowness records an opened mind. For this file, the point is skill’s object: the Scriptures are the sort of thing a mind can be opened to understand. Unskill is a real condition. It is not the last word about the writings, and it is not the last word about the mind. The opener, in this scene, is the risen Christ. This chapter does not turn the scene into a theory of illumination that bypasses the text. He opens the mind to understand the Scriptures, after he has spoken what is written. The writing remains the object. The opening is the reason the object is understood.

**Local limit.** The verse does not warrant a claim that no ordinary reading understands anything until a second experience is added, and it does not warrant a method of opening. It warrants the possibility and the object: minds are opened, and what they are opened to is the Scriptures. The labour by which a hearer seeks that understanding remains later.

Thus:

> **Luke 24:45 exhibits an opened mind whose object is the Scriptures. Inability is not the nature of the mind or of the writing. The opening follows words about what is written. Handling, as understanding the Scriptures, is possible.**

# 73.35 Biblical Record: Nehemiah 8:8 — Sense Given, Not a Method Written

So they read in the book in the law of God distinctly, and gave the sense, and caused them to understand the reading.

**The exhibition.** A book is read. The reading is distinct. Sense is given. Understanding of the reading is the result aimed at the people. The verbs are the handling of a present law. The people are not described as the measure of the law. The law is read to them, and the sense is given so that they understand the reading. Appropriate handling, in this narrative, is public, textual, and ordered to understanding. It is the contrary of a milk that never becomes understanding, and the contrary of a wrest that twists what is hard.

**Why it is cited and not developed.** The discipline of interpretation — perception, language, context, comparison, inference, and the rules of a careful reading — is a later chapter. Nehemiah 8:8 does not list those rules. It states that the book was read, the sense given, the reading understood. That statement is enough for the present warrant. It shows a people receiving appropriate handling of the Word, which confirms that appropriate handling is a real and public act. It does not become, in this file, the handbook of how to do it. To expand the verse into a method would be to write the later discipline in miniature and to claim a verse that does not contain the miniature.

**The unskill, seen from the contrary.** The ἄπειρος partaker is the one for whom this sort of act does not characterize his own relation to the word of righteousness. He partakes of milk. He does not occupy the place of one who, by trial, handles the word so as to give and receive its sense at the level due. The Levites’ act is not his identity. The possibility of the act is the rebuke of the claim that the word cannot be handled.

**Local limit.** The verse does not warrant a priesthood of interpreters without whom the book is dead, and it does not warrant the claim that giving the sense is an office above the book. They read in the book. The sense is the sense of that reading. The book remains primary. The handlers serve the reading. Chapter 69 has already distinguished service from governance. This verse is service.

Thus:

> **Nehemiah 8:8 exhibits appropriate handling as a distinct reading, a giving of the sense, and an understanding of the reading. The act confirms that the Word can be handled. The verse is not the discipline-file and not a second authority above the book.**

# 73.36 Biblical Record: 2 Timothy 3:15 — Known from Childhood, and Still a Word

Καὶ ὅτι ἀπὸ βρέφους τὰ ἱερὰ γράμματα οἶδας, τὰ δυνάμενά σε σοφίσαι εἰς σωτηρίαν διὰ πίστεως τῆς ἐν Χριστῷ Ἰησοῦ.

And that from infancy you have known the sacred writings, which are able to make you wise unto salvation through faith which is in Christ Jesus.

**From infancy.** Ἀπὸ βρέφους uses the newborn-word, not νήπιος as the accusatory babe. Timothy’s acquaintance with the sacred writings began in childhood. The acquaintance is stated as a good. It is the opposite of a rebuke. A life that has known the writings from the beginning is not thereby the condition Hebrews charges. The charge required time that should have produced teachers and produced, instead, a need of milk. Timothy’s infancy is the start of knowing. The Hebrew babe is the explanation of not handling. Infancy as the beginning of knowledge and infancy as the ground of unskill are different relations to time.

**Able to make wise.** Τὰ δυνάμενα is a participle of ability, predicated of the sacred writings. The writings are able to make wise unto salvation through faith in Christ Jesus. The ability is in the writings. This is the handleability-warrant in a Pauline sentence: the word is not a dead object waiting for a skilled caste to give it life. It is able. The next verses, already used in this book for God-breathed profit, will say that all Scripture is profitable for teaching, reproof, correction, and instruction in righteousness. This chapter does not re-open that argument. It uses 3:15 for two limited findings: early knowledge of the writings is a good, and the writings are able to make wise.

**What this does to a slogan.** A later leaflet will call the Word, in the babe’s second mark, “the basis for salvation.” Timothy’s sentence says the sacred writings are able to make wise unto salvation through faith in Christ. That is a real office of the writings. It supports the refusal to treat the word as a hobby. It does not rewrite λόγου δικαιοσύνης. Hebrews’ phrase remains the object of ἄπειρος. Paul’s phrase remains the office of the sacred writings in Timothy’s formation. The two sentences agree that the word is not trivial. They are not one phrase. Substituting “basis for salvation” for “word of righteousness” would trade the verse under exegesis for a neighboring truth, and would do it in the clause where the genitive must stay.

**Knowing and being skilled.** Timothy has known the writings from infancy, and Paul still charges him, in the letter, to continue in what he has learned and to do the work of an evangelist. Knowledge from childhood is not the end of obligation. For this file, the caution is enough: having known the sacred writings is not identical with the trial Hebrews says the milk-partaker lacks, and it is also not nothing. The distinction between possessing words and understanding their force is a later chapter. The present distinction is narrower and already visible: childhood knowledge is praised, and the milk-diet after teacher-time is charged. Both judgments can stand.

**Local limit.** The verse does not establish the faith through which the wisdom comes as a treatise, and it does not establish the First Principles. It establishes the writings’ ability and the goodness of early knowledge.

Thus:

> **Second Timothy 3:15 praises knowledge of the sacred writings from infancy and predicates ability of the writings: they can make wise unto salvation through faith in Christ. Early knowledge is not the Hebrew charge. The writings’ ability is not the partaker’s skill. The phrase does not replace “word of righteousness.”**

# 73.37 Why Unskill Does Not Break the Writing or Excuse the Hearer After Time

The exhibitions converge on two refusals that belong to the positive conclusion.

**The writing is not broken.** The Scripture cannot be broken. That premise was earned before any pathology of hearers. Unskill is a pathology of the partaker. The Sadducees’ not-knowing does not unsay the “I am” of the bush. The wrest of the unlearned does not make Paul’s letters cease to be Scripture. The eunuch’s puzzle does not make Isaiah fail; it makes a guide sit down. The opened mind understands the Scriptures because they are there to be understood. The Levites give the sense of the book because the book has a sense. The sacred writings are able to make wise. Hebrews assigns solid food to the mature because the food is real. If the unskill broke the writing, the charge would be a charge against God for having spoken in a word that trial cannot reach. The paragraph charges the partaker. The premise forbids the reversal.

**The hearer after time is not excused.** Διὰ τὸν χρόνον is the reason of the obligation to be teachers. Γεγόνατε is the condition they have entered. Πάλιν is the again of a teaching they should have been giving. The privative describes them. Nothing in the paragraph treats the milk as an innocent preference of the modest. Nothing treats the babe as a term of endearment that cancels the debt. Corinth’s “not even now are you able” is a reproach, not a pardon. The unlearned wrest to their own destruction, which is a consequence, not an excuse. Judicial forms of dullness, already examined, follow refusal and do not pardon the refusal. Unskill sits in the same moral world. Where the hearer is newly begotten, the milk is commanded and the excuse-question does not arise. Where the hearer asks for a guide at a first hard text, the guide is given and the excuse-question does not arise. Where the hour has not come, the speaker waits. Where the time has come and the diet is milk, the sentence accuses. The accusation is the form of the non-excuse.

**The two refusals protect each other.** If the writing were unhandleable, the accusation would be unjust, because the debt of teaching would be a debt no one could pay. If the hearer were excused by the mere existence of the writing’s survival, the accusation would be decorative, because unskill would be harmless wherever the text remained on the page. Chapter 72 refused both moves for dullness. This chapter refuses them for unskill. The word remains the measure. The partaker remains responsible after the time. A correct handling remains possible, as the mature, the opened mind, the more accurate way, and the given sense all witness.

Thus:

> **Unskill does not break the Scripture and does not excuse the hearer whom the time accuses. Handleability makes the charge just. The charge keeps handleability from being mistaken for harmlessness.**

# 73.38 Reconstructing the Bible-Introduction Proposition: Inexperienced, Only Here, Two Marks

A set of notes on Hebrews 5:13 says that the word rendered unskilful is the Greek *apeiros*, inexperienced, and that it occurs only here. It then names two characteristics of babes: dull of hearing, at Hebrews 5:11, and inexperienced in the Word, at Hebrews 5:13.

**What the proposition claims.** It claims a lexical identification, a hapax, and a two-mark account of the babe drawn from the paragraph.

**What the evidence is.** The identification of ἄπειρος as inexperienced matches the privative of πεῖρα and the genitive construction. The hapax is a fact of New Testament usage. The two marks are both in the paragraph: νωθροὶ ταῖς ἀκοαῖς in verse 11, ἄπειρος λόγου δικαιοσύνης in verse 13, and νήπιος as the classification that lets the notes call them characteristics of babes.

**What reasoning holds.** The lexical note is right to say inexperienced, and right to stop the New Testament survey at this verse. The two-mark note is right to refuse a one-word diagnosis. Dullness and inexperience are both said. Chapter 72 has already developed the first as aridity and the loss of reasonability, and has refused to let the second be stolen early. This chapter develops the second and refuses to let the first be stolen back. Receiving the two marks means keeping them two. The notes’ placement of both under the babe is a fair reading of the paragraph’s unity. It is not a warrant for one chapter to do both assignments.

**What is not adopted.** The notes do not, in this line, supply the boundless-homograph distinction, the limit on “ignorant,” or the relation of the genitive to “word of righteousness” as a discourse. Those belong to the lexical work this chapter has done, not to the notes as authority. The notes also do not, by being right about the two marks, become the measure of the paragraph. The paragraph is the measure. The notes are received where they restate it.

Thus:

> **The inexperienced-and-only-here note is received. The two characteristics are received as two: dull of hearing already established, inexperience in the Word established here. The notes are not the authority. The paragraph is.**

# 73.39 Reconstructing the Bible-Introduction Proposition: Unweaned Babes, and the Collapse of 1 Peter 2:2

The notes on Hebrews 5:12 say that the readers have been professed Christians long enough to be teachers, but because of laziness and dullness must be taught a second time the first simple doctrines; that they have not grown at all; that they are still unweaned babes, with a citation of 1 Corinthians 3:2 and 1 Peter 2:2; and that milk-feeding was a metaphor used by many writers, sacred and profane, who applied the infant to every beginner and meat to those who had learned the first principles.

**What holds.** “Long enough to be teachers” restates διὰ τὸν χρόνον and ὀφείλοντες εἶναι διδάσκαλοι. “Taught a second time” restates πάλιν. The citation of 1 Corinthians 3:2 is a real parallel, already used in this chapter for babes, milk, and persistent inability. Laziness and dullness, as a rendering of the νωθρός-neighbourhood, belong to the dryness-file’s findings about sloth and dull hearing. They may stand as the neighbouring condition. They are not a new analysis of ἄπειρος.

**What fails.** “Have not grown at all” flattens γεγόνατε. The perfect can include never having left infancy, and it can include having become needy again. The text accuses a condition arrived at. It does not give the investigator a universal inner history called “no growth whatever,” and it does not license that history as a verdict on every long-standing church. The charge is against the hearers addressed.

The joint citation of 1 Peter 2:2 with the rebuke collapses the commanded milk of the newly begotten into the unweaned guilt of those who ought to be teachers. The words differ. The speech-acts differ. One is an imperative to long for milk in order to grow. The other is a diagnosis of those who need milk and not solid food. Calling both “unweaned babes” in one breath teaches the collapse this chapter has refused.

“First simple doctrines” adds “simple” and specifies “of Christ” beyond the verse’s “elements of the beginning of the oracles of God.” The elements may be beginning-elements without being despised as simple, and their content is not taught here. The profane-and-sacred metaphor claim is a historical assertion this chapter does not need and does not verify. The biblical sentences carry their own milk-image. An unverified report about many writers is not a premise. Earlier in the movement that report was already set aside as colour. It stays aside.

**The result for the relationship.** The time, the again, and the Corinthian parallel help the account of inability after time. The “not at all,” the fusion with Peter, the added “simple,” and the profane parallel do not. A true observation about the time-clause does not carry the intensifications with it.

Thus:

> **The time-clause and the second teaching are received. The Corinthian parallel is received. “Not grown at all,” the fusion of 1 Peter 2:2 with the rebuke, the added simplicity, and the unverified profane metaphor are not adopted. The relationship does not need them.**

# 73.40 Reconstructing the Bible-Introduction Proposition: Full Age, and What the Notes Add

The notes on Hebrews 5:14 identify full age with the Greek *teleios*, one who has reached full maturity, and they name two characteristics of adults: capable of instruction, and powers of discrimination. They gloss the senses as the bodily organs and then as corresponding powers in the soul. They gloss exercised as athletic training, and discernment as the faculty of distinguishing.

**What holds.** Τέλειος as mature, over against the infant, is the paragraph’s contrast. Discrimination restates διάκρισις of good and evil. The faculty-word αἰσθητήριον does mean an organ or faculty of perception. The verb γυμνάζω does mean to train. Those glosses illuminate. They do not replace the clause.

**What is added.** “Capable of instruction” is not a phrase in the verse. The verse identifies the mature by trained faculties unto discernment, not by a second trademark called teachability. Teachability may be a fair inference from the wider letter, and it may be kin to Isaiah’s question about who can be taught. It is not one of two textual characteristics coordinate with discernment. Adopting it as if it were the Greek would put a note in the verse.

The move from bodily organs to a system of soul-senses that perfect the soul is a further anthropology. The verse uses the faculties for a discernment. It does not teach the system. Athletic nakedness as the root image of γυμνάζω is lexicography. It becomes a distraction if it is turned into a spirituality of stripping, and it becomes the exercise-file if it is turned into the method of training. This chapter keeps the verb as the perfect participle the verse has: trained. It does not write the gymnasium.

Those proposals are a discipline of interpretation. They are not the definition of ἄπειρος, and they are not the skill the milk-partaker lacks, stated as a list he failed to memorize. Adopting them here would write a later chapter and would install a grid as the handling the paragraph itself defines as trained discernment. The paragraph’s contrary is faculties trained by practice to discern good and evil. That contrary is received. The grid is not the contrary.

**A note on right division.** The same materials, at 2 Timothy 2:15, begin a gloss of the verb for cutting straight. That gloss belongs to the chapter on rightly dividing. It is not the cure this chapter is allowed to write. Mentioning that the materials offer it is enough to refuse the offer.

# 73.41 Reconstructing the Aletheia Proposition: Inexperienced, That Is, Ignorant, Unskillful

This closing chapter does not discover that Chapters 70–72 were symptoms of a cause the leaflet had already ranked first.

# 73.42 Reconstructing the Aletheia Proposition: The Babe, the Two Marks, and the Basis for Salvation

The same leaflet says that anyone unskilled in the word of righteousness is a *nēpios*, implying negation, figuratively a simple-minded person, an immature Christian, a babe, a child. It adds that the lack is a lack of cognitive skill, knowledge as a mental result of perception and learning and reasoning, and it names word exercise and word drill.

That join is received, because this chapter has argued it from the text and not from the leaflet. “Immature” is a fair English of the figure when it means undeveloped capacity relative to the word due. “Christian” specifies an audience. Hebrews addresses those who have the oracles and the time. The specification is intelligible. It is not a requirement that the Greek word apply only inside a later denominational sense of the name.

**Simple-minded, and the privative of speech.** “Implying negation” tracks a traditional analysis of νήπιος as a privative. The chapter has already declined to rest the doctrine on the etymology. “Simple-minded,” if it means undeveloped judgment, restates the figure. If it means dull of intelligence, it replaces a diet-charge with an insult the verse does not make. The milk-partaker may be able-minded and unskilled. Corinth’s babes were not accused of being unable to think. They were accused of being unable to bear meat, and of jealousy. The figure is immaturity. The slur is not adopted.

**The two marks.** Dull of hearing and unskilled in the Word are the two characteristics already received from the paragraph, in the order the movement assigned them. **The basis for salvation.** The relative clause “which is the basis for salvation” is the leaflet’s addition to “the Word.” Hebrews 5:13 says “word of righteousness.” Second Timothy 3:15 says the sacred writings are able to make wise unto salvation through faith in Christ. The addition gestures at a true office and misses the phrase under study. The word is not a hobby; the writings have a saving wisdom as their ability; the object of ἄπειρος remains the word of righteousness. “Basis for salvation” is not adopted as a substitute genitive. Adopting it would also tilt the file toward a soteriology and a foundation-content this assignment does not teach.

**Cognitive skill, perception, learning, reasoning, drill.** The lack is intellectual. That is received. Ἄπειρος in a word is not a lack of seating capacity in a room, and it is not a mood. It is want of trial in a discourse, and trial in a discourse is an act of the mind accountable to what is written. Chapter 6’s sound judgment is the standing account of that accountability. The further formula — knowledge as the mental result of perception and learning and reasoning — is the stage-file of the next movement. Word exercise and word drill are the exercise-file. The leaflet is right that the cure, when it comes, will be labour of the mind upon the word. The leaflet is not the author of that labour in this chapter. Receiving the cognitive character of the lack does not mean writing the stages and the drill. A lack of cognitive skill, stated with those expansions as if they were the definition, would smuggle the method into the diagnosis.

Thus:

> **The verse’s babe is received. “Simple-minded” is received only as undeveloped judgment, not as an insult to intelligence. The two marks stay two, and the first stays Chapter 72’s. “Basis for salvation” does not replace “word of righteousness.” The lack is intellectual. The stages and the drill are not written.**

# 73.43 Reconstructing the Aletheia Proposition: Persons, Organizations, and the Leaflet’s Order

The leaflet applies the babe to anyone, persons or organizations, unskilled in the word of righteousness. It has already set the damages among persons and among age-old organizations ostensibly Christianized.

**Persons.** Πᾶς ὁ μετέχων is personal and distributive. The sentence needs no institutional theory to reach the partaker. Every milk-partaker of the kind the paragraph means is the subject. That is received.

**Organizations.** A body can be described as unskilled when those who handle the word in its name are on the milk of this paragraph: owing, by the time, a teaching they do not give, needing the elements again, inexperienced in the word of righteousness. The description is a description of handling, not the birth of a second kind of babe with a corporate soul. Antiquity does not cure the description. This chapter adds the skill-form of the same refusal: an age-old body can be ἄπειρος in the word of righteousness. The age is not the trial. The printed Bible in the pew is not the trial. The leaflet’s “ostensibly” remains a useful caution, already taken in earlier files: a Christian name does not constitute the thing named. The test is the paragraph’s test, applied to the handling: milk or solid food, elements again or the word of righteousness, untrained faculty or discernment. **The order, once more, at the close.** Because this is the movement’s last word, the temptation is strongest to let the leaflet’s syntax become the movement’s conclusion: unskill, with the loss of reasonability, produced the dryness, the turning, and the accepted heresies. **What the leaflet still gets right, gathered.** The condition is inexperience and unskill in the word of righteousness. The unskilled partaker is a babe. The marks are two. Those are the leaflet’s true observations, and they are true because the paragraph and the premises say them.

Thus:

> **The partaker is the subject. A body’s handling can be unskilled, and the body’s age does not create the skill. The leaflet’s order does not become the movement’s causal finding. The true observations are kept because the text warrants them.**

# 73.44 Competing Claim: Unskill Is Calendar Youth

The reduction says:

> **Ἄπειρος means young. The newly converted are the babes, and the charge is a charge against being new.**

The reduction fails.

The time-clause exists in order to prevent it. Owing to be teachers by reason of the time is nonsense if the only subject is the person who has not had time. Γεγόνατε names a becoming. First Peter 2:2 commands the newly begotten to desire milk. Hebrews accuses those who need milk when solid food is the due. Youth can be the hour of the commanded milk. It is not the definition of the privative. An old partaker of milk is the clearest case of the charge, not the exception. A young hearer who longs for the word and grows is the clearest case of the other sentence.

The reduction flatters the long-standing. If unskill is youth, then years in the house are skill. The paragraph teaches the opposite relation: years are the reason the debt of teaching exists, and the debt can be unpaid.

Thus:

> **Unskill is not calendar youth. The time makes teachers due. Newness that desires the milk in order to grow is a different relation to the word.**

# 73.45 Competing Claim: Unskill Is the Absence of Office or Credential

The reduction says:

> **The unskilled are the unordained, the unschooled, the lay. Skill is the office or the certificate.**

The reduction fails.

The obligation to be teachers arises διὰ τὸν χρόνον, not διὰ τὸν τίτλον. The predicate ἄπειρος takes the genitive of the word, not the genitive of a diploma. Apollos was mighty in the Scriptures and eloquent, and still needed a more accurate exposition; the need was not the absence of a public voice, which he had. The Sadducees had standing enough to bring a case in the temple precincts, and they erred by not knowing the Scriptures. Office can coincide with unskill. Lack of office can coincide with a true handling, as when two hearers expound the way more accurately to the eloquent man.

The reduction also insults the obligation. If only the credentialed can be skilled, the debt that ordinary hearers become teachers is a debt the paragraph would be wicked to state. The paragraph states it. Teaching, in the clause, is the use of the word the time should have produced, not a guild.

Thus:

> **Unskill is not the absence of office. The genitive is the word of righteousness. Standing can be ἄπειρος. The time obligates teachers, not a caste.**

# 73.46 Competing Claim: The Word Cannot Be Handled

The reduction says:

The reduction fails.

It is the despair Chapter 66 refused and Chapter 72 refused again for dullness, now wearing the vocabulary of skill. The paragraph that names the babe names the mature in the next line. Solid food belongs to them. The writings are able to make wise. Minds are opened to understand the Scriptures. Sense is given so that a people understands the reading. A more accurate way can be expounded and received. Despair selects the privative and ignores the contrary the same author wrote in the same breath.

Hardness is real. Some things are hard to understand. Many things are hard to utter when hearers are dull. Hardness is a reason for labour and for honesty about the hearer. It is not a property of unreadability. If the word could not be handled, τέλειος would be an empty title and διάκρισις an empty goal. They are not empty in the sentence.

Thus:

> **The Word can be handled. The babe’s inability qualifies the babe. The mature, the opened mind, and the given sense are in the record. Despair is not the last word of the paragraph that accuses.**

# 73.53 Reason Through the Evidence

The inference can be stated in premises the preceding sections have made inspectable.

**Premise 1.** The record does not fail, the Scripture cannot be broken, and the Graphē is the primary written authority. Interpretation is not that authority. A correct handling remains possible. An incorrect handling remains incorrect. Secondary systems do not become primary by name, school, age, or recognition. Governance does not break the writing. Turning aside is real and inexcusable. Acceptance does not transform error into truth. Aridity is the loss of sound judgment toward a Word that remains clear, and dullness of hearing has been stated as that loss’s own mark.

**Premise 3.** Ἄπειρος is want of trial, with the genitive of the thing untried. In this verse the thing is the word of righteousness. “Unskilful” is the capacity the missing trial withholds. **Premise 5.** The Word remains handleable, because the privative qualifies the partaker, because the same paragraph assigns solid food to the mature, and because the wider record exhibits opened minds, given sense, a more accurate way received, and writings able to make wise. **Premise 6.** After the time that owed teachers, the condition does not excuse the hearer. Becoming needy of milk is a charge. **Premise 7.** Corinth confirms babe, milk, and persistent inability inside faith, without making Corinth’s strife the definition. Ephesians confirms the infant as unable to handle teaching’s winds. **Premise 8.** The Introduction’s lexical note and its two marks are coherent with the paragraph when the marks stay two and the intensifications are refused: not “no growth at all” as a universal history, not Peter’s milk fused with the rebuke, not a profane parallel as premise, not a soul-sense system, not a rule-list as the skill. The leaflet’s causal order, its substitute phrase “basis for salvation,” its stages, and its drill are not findings of this chapter.

**Premise 9.** The competing reductions fail: calendar youth; office and credential; an unhandleable word; room-skill; milk as always guilt; the humble child as this babe; harmlessness; the age of a body; this file as dryness reprinted or as labour already written.

The conclusion does not depend on a source’s authority. It depends on the paragraph, read with premises already in force and with exhibitions that survive distinction. The labour that answers the condition is not a hidden extra premise. It is the next question, forced by the condition and not answered inside it.

Thus:

> **The evidence warrants the definition, the relationship, the handleability, the non-excuse, and the closure of the movement. It does not warrant the cure-files. The inference is inspectable. The level is full.**

# 73.55 The Warranted Conclusion Stated with Full Force

# 73.56 What Movement IX Has Established

A movement-closing chapter owes the gathered account, not as a reprint of eight files, but as the one verdict their sequence warrants.

**The misreading.** The later act fails in poor perception, deficient understanding, faulty inference, failure of context, and improper handling. These are modes of an act. They are not defects of the writing’s truthfulness. An incorrect reading remains incorrect.

**The secondary systems.** Theology, philosophy, tradition, and institution may serve under the writing. Christian identity, academic status, antiquity, and institutional recognition do not promote them to primary written authority.

**The turning.** Doctrinal turning aside is a movement away from a truth the Graphē has given. The writings name causes and mechanisms. Intellectual failures permit the departure and do not excuse it. The truth left remains the measure. Continuance is a real contrary.

**The acceptance.** An erroneous doctrine can become a body’s ordinary teaching or the installed order of its belief, worship, and discipline. The reception does not transform the error into truth. Refusal of the multitude, the custom, and the suffered teacher remains possible.

**The dryness.** Aridity is the condition in which the Word given to be heard, judged, and laboured in does not yield sound judgment in the hearer.
