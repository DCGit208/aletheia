# 61.3 Define the Question: Christ's Reasoning From What Is Written

**Christ’s reasoning from what is written**, in this chapter, is the inspectable movement of inference in John 10:35–36 by which He proceeds from the written case — those to whom the word of God came were called gods, and the scripture cannot be broken — to the present charge against Him whom the Father sanctified and sent, not as a new oracle substituted for the writing, not as a pun on a vocabulary-item, and not as the whole of Movement VIII.

The phrase is easily abused. In ordinary speech “He reasoned from Scripture” may mean a preacher’s later habit, a license to treat every later inference as His, a curiosity about rabbinic form, or a claim that Chapters 62–65 are already finished because an argument exists. None of those senses is this chapter’s object.

Four elements belong to the definition.

First, **reasoning**. The unit is not a heap of clauses. It has a condition, a characterization, a standing-clause, and an application put as a question. Reasoning is the movement that joins those members so that the application follows from the written case. An assertion without that movement would be a restated claim. The present file is the movement.

Second, **from what is written**. The direction of the inference is from the Graphē to the charge, not from the charge to a convenient illustration. γεγραμμένον in verse 34 has already named the category. The present file asks what follows when the argument actually proceeds from that category: the writing supplies the lesser case; the writing’s standing is asserted; the present charge is tested by that case.

Third, **the weight assigned to the written text**. Weight, in this chapter, is the force He gives the writing in settling the dispute’s terms. He does not treat the text as colour around a self-authenticating shout. He treats it as the court. Weight so defined is not yet the finished why of unbreakability, and it is not yet the Graphē-gate. It is the force the argument itself assigns: the writing is what the charge must answer.

Fourth, **not those other claims**. The definition excludes, as not this object: the finished sense of “broken”; the dedicated why; John 17:17 as finished Graphē-doctrine; the Graphē-gate; a retry of Chapter 59’s scene; a retry of Chapter 60’s psalm; a grant that every later interpreter who reasons from a verse has Christ-level authority. Those exclusions are part of the definition. They are not a later apology.

Thus:

> **The question of this chapter is not whether John 10:34–36 is an argument from writing, what Psalm 82 argues, or why Scripture cannot be broken as a finished doctrine. Those questions have been examined or remain later. The question is how He reasons from the written wording to the present charge, and what weight He assigns to the written text in that reasoning.**

# 61.7 Reasoning From What Is Written Defined

**Reasoning from what is written**, in this chapter, is the operation by which a conclusion concerning a present charge is drawn from premises the writing itself supplies, such that the writing is the source of the lesser case and the measure against which the charge is tested.

The definition has four elements.

First, **premises supplied by the writing**. The lesser case is not invented in the moment as a new illustration. It is already written: “I said, Ye are gods.” The characterization is already the writing’s case as He reads it: those to whom the word of God came. A reasoning that used the writing only as a motto would not be reasoning from the writing. It would be reasoning beside the writing.

Second, **a movement to a conclusion**. Verse 36 is not a second unrelated speech. It is the application. The question “Say ye… Thou blasphemest…?” is the forcing of that application upon the opponents. Movement is what makes the unit an argument rather than two remarks.

Third, **from, not merely about**. One may speak about Scripture while reasoning from prestige, from stones, from institutional office, or from private experience. He speaks from Scripture: the first word of the answer is whether it is written; the application is tested by what that writing has already done.

Fourth, **not a later method-lecture as the object**. Movement X will teach the learner to reason through the Word as labour. This chapter reconstructs His reasoning in this controversy. Reconstruction of one argument is not the later curriculum of skill. The later curriculum will use this reconstruction as a specimen. It is not this specimen under another name.

Two false substitutes must be refused.

**Reasoning as a substitute for the writing.** A later habit may treat “reasoning” as the authority, with verses as optional support. That habit inverts the direction. Here the writing is the court. Reasoning is the following of what the court has said to the charge now made.

**The writing as a substitute for reasoning.** A later habit may quote a verse and call the quotation an argument. Quotation is not yet the movement of verses 35–36. He quotes, then He characterizes, then He asserts standing, then He applies. The present file is that sequence as logic.

Thus:

> **Reasoning from what is written is the movement from premises the Graphē supplies to a conclusion about the present charge, with the writing as source and measure. It is not reasoning beside the text, and it is not quotation without movement.**

# 61.8 The Logic of the Argument Defined

**The logic of Christ’s argument**, in this chapter, is the valid inferential structure of John 10:35–36: a condition that takes the citation as an admitted written calling, a characterization of those so called, an assertion of the writing’s standing, and an application that tests the blasphemy-charge against a greater case in the same order of consideration.

Logic here is not a later system imposed upon the text. It is the argument’s own joints.

The joints are these.

**Condition.** “If he called them gods…” The “if” of verse 35 is the logic of admission, not of doubt that the writing exists. Given verse 34’s “Is it not written,” the condition follows as: if that writing has so named them.

**Characterization.** “…unto whom the word of God came…” The persons of the lesser case are not an undefined “them.” They are those addressed by God’s word, already mapped in Chapter 60 onto Psalm 82’s judges under God.

**Standing.** “…and the scripture cannot be broken…” The writing that has so named them is Scripture, and that Scripture is treated as standing in force. The dedicated sense of “broken” remains later. The logical function is already this: the lesser naming is not a negotiable illustration.

**Application.** “Say ye of him, whom the Father hath sanctified, and sent into the world, Thou blasphemest; because I said, I am the Son of God?” The present charge is put as a question that forces comparison. The comparison is the argument’s conclusion-form: if the lesser stands, the charge against the greater does not.

The logic is therefore **from lesser written naming to greater present claim**, with the writing as the admitted court. That form will be named a fortiori in the next dedicated definition. The present definition needs only the joints: condition, characterization, standing, application.

A logic so defined is not a later creed’s first article. It is not a mystic’s assertion. It is not a proof-text card. It is the argument as an argument.

Thus:

> **The logic of the argument is the movement from an admitted written calling of those who received God’s word, through the asserted standing of that writing, to the testing of the blasphemy-charge against the Father’s sanctified and sent Son.**

# 61.9 Weight Assigned to the Written Text Defined

**Weight assigned to the written text**, in this chapter, is the force Christ gives the Graphē in this dispute: the writing functions as the court whose wording supplies the lesser case, whose standing is asserted as not to be set aside, and whose exhibit the charge must answer.

Weight is easily abused. It may be heard as the finished Graphē-gate, as a claim that every later copy is equally this court, as a claim that every interpretation has this force, or as a weak metaphor meaning “He liked the Bible.” None of those senses is this definition.

Four elements belong to the definition.

First, **court, not decoration**. Decoration is a quotation added after a conclusion already reached by other means. Court is that by which the conclusion is reached. He does not first shout an identity-claim and then ornament it with a psalm. He asks whether it is written, cites, characterizes, asserts standing, and then applies. The writing occupies the place of the court.

Second, **settling-material in the dispute**. Settling-material is not the later claim that the Graphē is the primary written authority for all later knowing, testing, teaching, and living. It is the narrower, already inspectable claim that in this controversy the terms of the charge are tested by what the writing has already said. The opponents are placed under their own law.

Third, **force of standing without the finished why**. He states that the scripture cannot be broken as a step that gives the lesser naming its non-negotiable character. That is weight. Why the writing has that character, from what Scripture is, remains Chapters 62–63. Using force is not explaining force’s ground.

Fourth, **not interpreter-infallibility**. The weight is assigned to the written text. It is not assigned to every later reading of the text. The distinction is already required by the object. Movement IX will examine mishandling. This chapter must already refuse to treat His use of the text as a blank check for later uses of the text.

Two false substitutes must be refused.

**Weight as mere emphasis.** A speaker may raise his voice at a quotation. Emphasis is not court. The argument’s structure, not a reconstructed tone, is the evidence of weight.

**Weight as later imprimatur-theory offered as this file’s achievement.** A later leaflet may speak of Holy Writ as unchallenged authority. That proposition, reconstructed, corresponds to the force this scene exhibits. Correspondence is not a license to treat a later formulation as the biblical sentence, and it is not Chapter 65 written early. The scene exhibits the force. The later gate remains the later gate.

Thus:

> **Weight assigned to the written text is the force of court and settling-material in this dispute: the Graphē supplies the case, stands as not to be set aside, and tests the charge. That weight is not decoration, not the finished why, and not the Graphē-gate.**

# 61.10 A Fortiori Defined: From the Written Lesser to the Greater Claim

**A fortiori**, in this chapter, is the argumentative form from the lesser to the greater in the same order of consideration: if a naming already stands in Scripture for those to whom the word of God came, then a greater claim, made by the One the Father sanctified and sent, cannot be treated as blasphemy on the mere ground that God-vocabulary has been used of a man.

The Latin tag names a form. The form does not depend upon the tag. John 10:35–36 does not contain the later Greek phrase πόσῳ μᾶλλον, “how much more.” Honesty requires that fact. The a fortiori is reconstructed from the structure, not quoted as an explicit adverbial. Reconstruction is inference. The inference is warranted by the comparison the question in verse 36 forces.

The form has four members.

**Lesser case.** Those to whom the word of God came were called gods by the writing itself. Chapter 60 has identified them as those who judge under God, mortal, charged with unjust judgment, children of the Most High rather than the Most High. The lesser is real office under God, not a fiction, and it is lesser than the Son’s claim.

**Greater case.** He whom the Father sanctified and sent into the world, who has said “I am the Son of God,” and who has already said “I and my Father are one.” The greater is not a later creed imported as a premise. It is the identification verse 36 itself gives, already prepared by the discourse’s works and oneness-claim.

**Shared order of consideration.** Both cases concern God-vocabulary used of those who are, in the opponents’ hearing, men. The charge is that a man has made himself God. The writing has already used God-vocabulary of men to whom the word of God came. The order is: what may be said, without blasphemy, of men in relation to God, given what Scripture has said.

**Conclusion-form.** If the lesser naming stands because it is written and the writing is not to be broken, then the charge against the greater does not stand as a mere inference from “a man has used God-vocabulary.” The question of verse 36 is that conclusion put to the opponents.

The form is sometimes named, in later discussion of biblical and Jewish argument, as an inference from the light to the heavy. That later name may be noted as a recognized pattern of reasoning. It is not a reconstructed academy, not a named teacher, and not a claim that the argument’s validity depends upon a school. Validity depends upon the premises and the comparison. Chapter 5 already established that comparison is an instrument of reason. This chapter uses that instrument on this text.

A fortiori so defined is not identity. The judges are not the Son. The Son is not merely a judge. The argument is that the charge fails, not that the two cases are the same case.

Thus:

> **A fortiori, here, is from the written lesser naming of those who received God’s word as “gods” to the greater claim of the sanctified and sent Son. The form is reconstructed from the structure. It is not an explicit “how much more” in the wording, and it is not identity of the two cases.**

# 61.11 Lexical Control of the Argument's Connectives

The argument’s joints have words. Those words must be controlled as the text uses them, not as a later system wishes.

**εἰ (*ei*) — “if.”** Verse 35 opens with a condition. In this context the condition is the logical consequence of verse 34, not a hypothetical invented to evade. “Is it not written…?” expects the writing’s existence. “If he called them gods” then names what that writing has done. The “if” is the hinge of admission: given the citation, this calling has occurred in the writing.

**ἐκείνους εἶπεν θεούς — “he called them gods.”** The subject of εἶπεν is the Speaker of the cited “I said,” God in the psalm’s presentation as already established. The object is those of the citation. θεούς is the wording of the citation, not a later metaphysical term offered as a substitute. “Called” treats the wording as a naming, a case, not as a metaphor the argument may discard.

**πρὸς οὓς ὁ λόγος τοῦ θεοῦ ἐγένετο — “unto whom the word of God came.”** Chapter 60 established the mapping onto Psalm 82’s “I have said.” This chapter uses the phrase as the lesser case’s identification. λόγος here is the word that arrived as address, not a later hypostatic lecture imported as the object. ἐγένετο names occurrence: the word came. Commissioning address remains commissioning address.

**καί — “and.”** The conjunction before “the scripture cannot be broken” joins the standing-clause to the condition as a parenthetical of force inside the same sentence that will reach verse 36’s application. It is not a new topic. It is the asserted character of the writing being used as case.

**ὃν ὁ πατὴρ ἡγίασεν καὶ ἀπέστειλεν εἰς τὸν κόσμον — “whom the Father hath sanctified, and sent into the world.”** ἡγίασεν (*hagiazō*, G37), in this clause, names the Father’s setting-apart of this One. ἀπέστειλεν (*apostellō*, G649) names sending. The pair is the greater case’s identification from the verse itself. It is not a reconstructed biography beyond the record. It is not yet a finished treatise on sanctification as a later doctrine. It is the text’s own greater-commission language.

**ὑμεῖς λέγετε ὅτι Βλασφημεῖς — “Say ye… Thou blasphemest.”** The application returns their charge. βλασφημεῖς is their word, already named in verse 33. The argument does not change the offence under discussion. It tests that offence by the writing.

**ὅτι εἶπον, Υἱὸς τοῦ θεοῦ εἰμι — “because I said, I am the Son of God.”** The because-clause names His speech as they prosecute it. The oneness-claim of verse 30 and the Son-claim of verse 36 stand together in the dispute, as Chapter 59 established. This chapter uses the Son-claim as the compared speech of the greater case.

The terms do not, by themselves, finish “cannot be broken.” A named writing may be used as a case. Why it cannot be broken remains later.

Thus:

> **The connectives of verses 35–36 are controlled as the argument’s joints: admitted calling, characterized addressees, joined standing, sanctified-and-sent identification, returned charge, compared Son-speech. Lexical control illuminates the logic. It does not replace it, and it does not finish Chapters 62–63.**

# 61.12 “Cannot Be Broken” as Force in the Chain, Not Finished Doctrine

The unit contains the clause:

> **“…and the scripture cannot be broken…”**

The clause belongs to the reasoning. Without it, the lesser naming could be heard as a disposable illustration: Scripture once said this; we may now say otherwise. With it, the lesser naming is treated as standing. The a fortiori requires that standing. A lesser case that may be broken at will cannot force a greater conclusion.

What this chapter must do with the clause is strictly limited and still full.

It must **include** the clause in the chain, because the chain as written includes it.

It must **locate** the clause as the asserted force of ἡ γραφή in the inference: the writing used as case is Scripture, and that Scripture is not treated as breakable for the sake of sustaining the charge.

It must **use** that force as a premise of the a fortiori: if the naming stands, the charge against the greater does not stand as a mere inference from God-vocabulary used of a man.

It must **refuse** to define λυθῆναι (*lythēnai*), to catalogue the senses of “break,” to answer why from the nature of truth and of God’s writing as a dedicated why, and to connect that why to John 17:17. Those are Chapters 62–64.

A stacked hedge would say: perhaps the clause means something, but we cannot use it until later, so the a fortiori has no standing-premise now. Sequence does not require that evacuation. The clause has force as His assertion of the writing’s standing in the dispute. The later files will examine that standing’s meaning and ground. They will not create the assertion, and they will not create the function the assertion already performs in this argument.

The positive force available at this stage is therefore real. He reasons as one who will not allow the cited wording to be treated as a broken case. That is already weight assigned to the written text. It is not yet the dedicated doctrine of the clause.

Thus:

> **“The scripture cannot be broken” functions in this chapter as the asserted standing of the writing in the chain of inference. Function in the chain is not the finished lexical and doctrinal treatment. That treatment remains Chapters 62–63.**

# 61.13 The Unit Recalled as a Chain, Not Retried as a Scene

Chapter 59 exhibited the forensic structure. This chapter recalls that structure as a chain of inference, not as a second locating of the feast, the porch, or the stones.

The chain, now used as logic, is this:

1. The charge: being a man, He makes Himself God (John 10:33), after “I and my Father are one” (John 10:30).
2. The question of writing: “Is it not written in your law…?” (John 10:34a).
3. The citation: “I said, Ye are gods” (John 10:34b), the wording of Psalm 82:6.
4. The written case: “If he called them gods, unto whom the word of God came” (John 10:35a).
5. The standing: “and the scripture cannot be broken” (John 10:35b).
6. The application: “Say ye of him, whom the Father hath sanctified, and sent into the world, Thou blasphemest; because I said, I am the Son of God?” (John 10:36).

The scene remains what Chapter 59 established: Dedication, public Temple controversy, works offered as witness, stones taken up, Scripture entered as court, works returned to after the Scripture-argument. Those facts are premises. They are not this file’s narrative task.

What is new is the following of steps 4–6 as inference. Steps 1–3 are the occasion and the exhibit. Steps 4–6 are the reasoning. The present file’s object begins in earnest at the “if” and completes its dedicated work at the application, without pretending that the “if” can be understood without the citation, and without pretending that the application can be understood without the psalm Chapter 60 has supplied.

Thus:

> **The unit is recalled as a chain of inference from charge through writing to application. Recalling the chain is not retrying the scene. The dedicated object is the movement of verses 35–36.**

# 61.14 Verse 35's “If”: Admitted Premise, Not Hypothetical Evasion

The word “if” is capable of two opposite abuses.

The first abuse treats it as doubt: perhaps He is not sure the writing exists; perhaps the argument is merely exploratory. That reading cannot survive verse 34. “Is it not written in your law” is not a scholar’s maybe. It is a question that places them under a writing they already possess. Chapter 59 established that “your law” names received Scripture they acknowledge. The “if” that follows is the drawing of a consequence from that acknowledged writing.

The second abuse treats it as a mere flourish: “if” means nothing; He is simply asserting. That reading cannot survive the grammar. A condition is a condition. It names the premise upon which the application depends. Remove the condition, and verse 36 becomes an ungrounded protest. Keep the condition, and verse 36 is a protest grounded in the writing.

The warranted sense is **admitted premise**. If — as the writing shows — He called them gods, those to whom the word of God came, then the application follows. The premise is admitted not because the opponents have spoken a concession in the narrative, but because the writing is their law, already written, already citable, already the court He has opened. A court they already possess is a premise they cannot recuse as foreign.

“If” in this argument is therefore the logic of *modus ponens* in ordinary form: if the written calling stands, then the charge against the greater is unsustainable as they have framed it. The first member is supplied by Psalm 82 as cited. The second member is verse 36. The standing-clause prevents a hidden escape: that the written calling might be admitted as text and denied as force.

Thus:

> **Verse 35’s “if” is the admitted premise of the argument: given the citation in their law, this calling has occurred. It is not doubt that the writing exists, and it is not an empty flourish.**

---

# 61.15 The Lesser Case: Those to Whom the Word of God Came

The a fortiori requires a lesser case that is real, written, and lesser.

**Real.** Chapter 60 established that Psalm 82 addresses those who judge under God, who fail the poor, who shall die like men, who are children of the Most High rather than the Most High. They are not a hypothetical class invented for a puzzle. They are the psalm’s “ye.”

**Written.** The naming “gods” is not Christ’s improvisation in the porch. It is “I said,” already written. The lesser case is a Graphē-case. If it were only His present speech, the argument would be from His authority alone, and the turn to “your law” would be unexplained.

**Lesser.** They are those to whom the word of God came — commissioning address, office under God, mortality still in force. They are not the One the Father sanctified and sent into the world. The psalm itself supplies the lesser quality: they judge unjustly; they walk in darkness; they die like men. The argument does not require this chapter to re-preach that indictment. It requires the indictment as already establishing that the named “gods” are not being presented as the Most High.

The lesser case is therefore not an embarrassment to be hidden. It is the argument’s first load-bearing member. If Scripture has used God-vocabulary of commissioned hearers who remain men, then the mere fact that a man is the subject of God-vocabulary is not, by itself, the completed proof of blasphemy. That is the lesser light. The greater will not be the same light.

The Aletheia placement of Psalm 82:6 beside Genesis 1:26 as language of man’s true nature may be reconstructed without becoming this chapter’s center. The reconstruction is: sonship-language and image-language exist in the corpus; office under God is not foreign to the writings. The limit remains Chapter 60’s limit: commission is not deification, and this argument is not a later identity-curriculum. The lesser case is office and address, not a grant that the judges are what the Son is.

Thus:

> **The lesser case is those to whom the word of God came, named “gods” in standing writing, real as the psalm’s judges under God, and lesser than the sanctified and sent Son. The a fortiori begins with that case, not with a fiction and not with identity.**

---

# 61.16 The Written Naming: He Called Them Gods

Verse 35 does not say merely that a word occurred in a psalm. It says that He **called** them gods.

Calling is naming with force. The writing is treated as an act of address that conferred a designation. Chapter 60 established that the designation is office under God, not identity as the Most High, and not a blank check. This chapter needs the further logical point: the designation is the writing’s own act, and that act is the premise.

Three consequences follow for the reasoning.

**The opponents’ charge has used God-vocabulary as if its application to a man were, as such, blasphemy.** “Thou, being a man, makest thyself God.” If every such application were blasphemy, the writing would already have blasphemed in calling them gods. The argument places their charge in collision with their law.

**The collision is not a trick.** It is the writing’s collision, because the writing is theirs. He does not invent a naming and then dare them to deny it. He cites a naming they already receive as written in their law.

**The naming is the lesser grant in the comparison.** They were called gods. He has said, “I am the Son of God.” The comparison is not “gods” as a sound matching “God” as a sound. The comparison is written designation of commissioned men, set beside the Son-claim of the sanctified and sent One. Vocabulary-overlap without the case would be wordplay. Vocabulary inside a written case is logic.

The written naming also prevents a reduction that would say: He is only claiming to be a judge. He does not say, “I too am called a god in that sense, and that is all.” He uses the naming as the lesser. The greater remains the greater. A lesser grant that is used as lesser is not a redefinition of the speaker as only that lesser.

Thus:

> **“He called them gods” treats the written wording as a naming that already stands in their law. That naming collides with a charge that treats God-vocabulary applied to a man as, by itself, blasphemy. Collision with their law is the argument, not a trick, and not a reduction of the Son to the bench.**

# 61.17 The Greater Case: Whom the Father Sanctified and Sent

Verse 36 identifies the subject of their charge by two verbs:

> **“…him, whom the Father hath sanctified, and sent into the world…”**

The greater case is this One, so identified.

**Sanctified.** The Father has set Him apart. The verb, in this clause, is the Father’s act toward this One, not a later treatise on the believer’s sanctification, and not a reconstructed temple-ritual as the chapter’s center. Setting-apart by the Father is already greater than the psalm’s commissioning address to a mortal bench that judges unjustly and dies like men. The psalm’s addressees received a word and failed it. This One is identified as the Father’s sanctified One.

**Sent into the world.** Sending is mission from the Father. The discourse has already placed His works in the Father’s name as the Father’s works (John 10:25, 32, 37–38), used as context, not retried from Chapter 45. Sending into the world is not the same sentence as “the word of God came” to judges in an assembly. The word came to them in their office. He is sent into the world. The scope of the commission is already the greater.

**The Father as the agent.** Both verbs have the Father as subject. The greater case is not self-made in the sense the charge alleges. The charge is “makest thyself God.” The identification is “whom the Father hath sanctified, and sent.” The argument thus answers the “makest thyself” as well as the God-vocabulary. He does not present a self-invented title. He presents the Father’s act.

The greater case does not require this chapter to re-prove the oneness-claim of verse 30, the works, or the later resurrection-file. Those remain premises. The present need is proportional: verse 36 names a greater commission than the lesser case, and that naming is the argument’s own.

Thus:

> **The greater case is the One the Father sanctified and sent into the world. Sanctifying and sending, as this verse’s identification, are greater than the psalm’s address to a mortal bench. The Father is the agent; the charge of self-making is already met at the level of identification.**

---

# 61.18 The Compared Speech: “I Am the Son of God”

The application’s because-clause names the speech they prosecute:

> **“…because I said, I am the Son of God.”**

Chapter 59 established that the oneness-claim of verse 30 and the Son-claim of verse 36 stand together in the dispute, and that the opponents’ “makest thyself God” is their naming of the offence. This chapter needs the compared speech as a member of the a fortiori.

The comparison is:

- **Lesser speech, already written:** “I said, Ye are gods.”
- **Present speech, now charged:** “I said, I am the Son of God.”

Both are speech. Both use God-relation language of those who are, in the hearing of the charge, men. The first is God’s written address to commissioned hearers. The second is His own claim as the sanctified and sent One. The argument does not treat the two sentences as identical. It treats the first as the written precedent that forbids treating the second as blasphemy merely because God-vocabulary has been used of a man.

Psalm 82:6 also says, “and all of you are children of the most High.” Chapter 60 established that this is sonship-language bounded by the judicial frame, not a blank check. The bounded clause is relevant here as correspondence, not as a second citation He quotes. He quotes “Ye are gods.” He claims “I am the Son of God.” The psalm’s “children of the most High” shows that son-language already stands in the same written address. Correspondence of language is not identity of persons. Children of the Most High in the psalm remain those who die like men. The Son-claim in John 10 stands in a discourse of oneness with the Father and of unique sending.

The compared speech therefore does two things at once, and the chapter must not drop either.

It **relates** the Son-claim to a writing that has already used God-vocabulary and children-language of commissioned men, so that the charge cannot treat such vocabulary as, by itself, blasphemy.

It **does not reduce** the Son-claim to the psalm’s children-language. The a fortiori would collapse if the greater were only the lesser restated. “I am the Son of God,” in this controversy, is the claim they have heard as making Himself God, after “I and my Father are one.” The argument defends that claim as not blasphemy. It does not rewrite that claim as merely “I too am a judge.”

Thus:

> **The compared speech is “I am the Son of God,” set beside the written “Ye are gods.” Relation without reduction is the rule: the writing forbids treating God-vocabulary of a man as automatically blasphemy; it does not flatten the sanctified and sent Son into the psalm’s mortal bench.**

---

# 61.19 The Charge Returned: “Say Ye… Thou Blasphemest”

Verse 36 is a question. The question is not ignorance. It is the returned charge.

> **“Say ye of him… Thou blasphemest…?”**

Three features of the return belong to the logic.

**It keeps their word.** He does not substitute a milder offence. Blasphemy is their indictment (John 10:33). The writing is brought to that indictment, not to a different quarrel.

**It names the subject of the indictment as the sanctified and sent One.** They have charged a man. He identifies that man as the Father’s sanctified and sent One. The identification is already an argumentative move: the subject is not a self-made pretender even before the a fortiori is felt as “how much more.”

**It forces a yes or a no under their law.** If they say yes — He blasphemes — they must say it while their law stands with “Ye are gods” said of those who received God’s word, and while that law is treated as not to be broken. If they say no, the stones are already an unjust court. The question is the placing of their mouths under their writing.

The return is therefore not rhetoric as ornament. It is the conclusion-form of the inference. A reconstructed argument that ended at “Scripture called judges gods” would have stopped before the dispute’s actual offence. The offence is the charge against Him. The writing is used to try that charge.

After the Scripture-argument He returns to works (John 10:37–38), and they seek again to take Him (John 10:39). Those sentences remain context, as Chapter 59 established. They show that the reasoning did not end the hostility. Failure of opponents to concede is not failure of the inference. A valid argument may be refused. Refusal is not a later file’s permission to treat the argument as wordplay.

Thus:

> **“Say ye… Thou blasphemest” returns their charge as a question under their law. The conclusion-form of the a fortiori is that question. Hostility after the argument does not convert the argument into decoration.**

# 61.20 Reconstructing the A Fortiori

The members now stand where they may be joined.

**Premise A.** It is written in their law, “I said, Ye are gods” (John 10:34; Psalm 82:6).

**Premise B.** Those so called are those to whom the word of God came (John 10:35a), the psalm’s judges under God (Chapter 60).

**Premise C.** That writing is Scripture, and the scripture cannot be broken (John 10:35b) — used here as asserted standing, not as finished why.

**Premise D.** The subject of the present charge is He whom the Father sanctified and sent into the world, who has said “I am the Son of God” (John 10:36), after “I and my Father are one” (John 10:30).

**Premise E.** The charge treats God-vocabulary used of this man as blasphemy (John 10:33, 36).

**Inferential step 1.** From A–C: Scripture has named commissioned men “gods,” and that naming stands.

**Inferential step 2.** Therefore the mere application of God-vocabulary to men is not, as such, blasphemy; otherwise the writing itself would stand as blasphemy, which the argument will not grant.

**Inferential step 3.** The present subject is not a lesser commissioned bench only. He is the Father’s sanctified and sent One. The claim “I am the Son of God” is greater, in the same order of consideration, than the written naming of those who received the word.

**Inferential step 4.** If even the lesser naming stands, the charge that the greater claim is blasphemy merely because a man has used God-relation language does not stand. That is the a fortiori.

**Conclusion.** “Say ye… Thou blasphemest…?” is to be answered no, if their law is allowed to function as court. The stones are not the court. The writing is the court.

The reconstruction does not insert πόσῳ μᾶλλον into John’s Greek. It names the comparison verse 36 forces. Explicit versus inferred will be distinguished again below. The reconstruction also does not claim that this inference, by itself, has written the whole of later Christology. Chapters 44–45 already bear the claims-and-works conjunction. This file shows that the Graphē is used as court to try the blasphemy-charge, and that the trial has this form.

Thus:

> **The a fortiori, reconstructed, is: if Scripture’s standing naming of those who received God’s word as “gods” is not blasphemy, then the sanctified and sent Son’s claim “I am the Son of God” is not blasphemy merely as God-vocabulary used of a man. The writing is the court of that conclusion.**

---

# 61.21 Why the Comparison Is Greater, Not Identical

The a fortiori dies if the two cases are the same case.

Identity would say: He is one more of those to whom the word of God came; “Son of God” means only “judge”; the argument is a plea to be classified with Psalm 82’s bench.

The text forbids identity at four inspectable points.

**The psalm’s own lesser quality.** Those called gods judge unjustly, walk in darkness, and die like men. He does not apply that indictment to Himself as the sense of His claim. He cites their naming, not their failure, as the written precedent.

**The Father’s sanctifying and sending.** Verse 36 does not say, “Say ye of one more judge.” It says, “him, whom the Father hath sanctified, and sent into the world.” Unique identification in the clause is already a refusal of mere classification with the bench.

**The prosecuted speech.** “I am the Son of God,” in this dispute, is heard with “I and my Father are one.” A later reduction that hears only “I am a child of the Most High like the psalm’s children” has not heard the controversy Chapter 59 located.

**The form of a fortiori itself.** “If even the lesser, then not the charge against the greater” requires a greater. A speaker who meant identity would not need the movement from them to Him. He could have said, “I am what they were.” He did not.

The comparison is therefore **greater in the same order**. Same order: God-vocabulary and God-relation language concerning one who stands, in their hearing, as a man. Greater: the Father’s sanctified and sent Son, not a mortal bench addressed and condemned in the same psalm. The argument defends the greater claim. It does not dissolve the greater claim into the lesser precedent.

This limit is part of the positive warrant, not a hedge against it. The reasoning is stronger, not weaker, because it does not cheat by identity. A cheat would be easy and would not need verses 35–36 as they stand.

Thus:

> **The comparison is greater, not identical. He is not pleading to be one more of Psalm 82’s judges. He is testing the charge against the Father’s sanctified and sent Son by a lesser written naming that already stands.**

---

# 61.22 Sanctified and Sent as Greater Commission Than Word-Coming to Judges

The “how much more,” reconstructed, needs a specified respect in which the greater is greater. The text specifies commission.

**Word-coming to judges** is address that constitutes office under God. It is real. It is written. It is lesser. The word comes to many in the psalm’s “ye.” They remain a plurality who die like men.

**Sanctifying and sending this One into the world** is the Father’s act toward a singular “him.” The world is the scope. The Father is the sender. The discourse’s works in the Father’s name are already the corroborating context, not retried.

The respect of comparison is therefore **commission from God concerning what may be said of the commissioned in God-vocabulary**. If commission in the lesser degree already carries the written name “gods,” commission in the greater degree cannot be treated as blasphemy merely for the Son-claim. The argument does not require a reconstructed metaphysics of degrees of deity as this file’s achievement. It requires the text’s own greater-commission language set beside the text’s own lesser naming.

Two over-claims must be refused even as the greater is affirmed.

The chapter does not claim that “sanctified and sent,” by itself, has proved every later title. It claims that verse 36 identifies a greater case than verse 35’s lesser case.

The chapter does not claim that sending into the world is a different god from the Father. Sending is the Father’s sending of this One. The oneness-claim remains the discourse’s claim, used as premise, not retried.

Thus:

> **The specified respect of the greater is commission: the Father’s sanctifying and sending this One into the world is greater than the word’s coming to the psalm’s judges. Greater commission, not reconstructed metaphysics, carries the “how much more.”**

---

# 61.23 “Gods” and “Son of God”: Related Vocabulary Without Equivocation

Equivocation would use “gods” and “God” and “Son of God” as if they were one sense in every occurrence.

The argument does not equivocate. It relates.

**θεοί in the citation** names the psalm’s addressees as Chapter 60 controlled that naming: office under God, children of the Most High, mortal, judged for injustice. The sense is the psalm’s sense.

**θεόν in the charge** (John 10:33, “makest thyself God”) is the opponents’ hearing of His oneness-claim and Son-claim. They hear identity with God in a sense they judge blasphemous for a man.

**Υἱὸς τοῦ θεοῦ in His speech** is the claim they prosecute, standing with “I and my Father are one.”

The argument’s relation is not: θεοί means the same as θεόν in the charge, therefore He is only what they were. The relation is: the writing has already used God-vocabulary of men in a standing case; therefore the opponents cannot treat the mere presence of God-vocabulary in a man’s speech as the completed proof of the blasphemy they have named. The further question — what His own claim means as oneness with the Father — remains the discourse’s question, already opened in Chapter 45 and not closed by a pun.

Related vocabulary without equivocation is the difference between a fortiori and wordplay. Wordplay would lean on the sound θεοί / θεόν. A fortiori leans on the written case and the greater commission. The sound is not irrelevant: the charge has named God, and the writing has named gods. Relevance of vocabulary is not identity of sense.

Thus:

> **“Gods” in the citation and “God” / “Son of God” in the dispute are related as God-vocabulary in a written case set beside a greater claim. They are not one sense smuggled across the argument. Relation without equivocation is logic. Identity of sense would be wordplay or reduction.**

# 61.24 The Direction of Inference: From Writing to Charge

The assignment is to identify His appeal to what is written, now as the direction of the reasoning, not merely as the fact that a citation occurs.

**From writing.** Verse 34 begins with γεγραμμένον. Verse 35 continues with the writing’s calling and the writing’s standing. The premises are Graphē-premises.

**To charge.** Verse 36 arrives at “Thou blasphemest.” The conclusion-form concerns their indictment, not a seminar about psalms.

That direction is the appeal. An appeal that ran the other way would be: I am the Son of God; therefore find me a verse. The record’s order is the opposite: find what is written; if that writing has done this, do you say of the sanctified and sent One that He blasphemes?

The direction also distinguishes this file from a later preacher’s use of John 10:35 as a poster. A poster quotes the standing-clause and may never reach the charge. His appeal reaches the charge. The writing is not a refuge from the controversy. It is the court of the controversy.

Chapter 48 established the practice of arguing from what is written, including “it is written again” as Scripture judging a use of Scripture. The present direction is that practice under a capital charge, reconstructed as logic. The present file is not a second confirmation-trial. It is the identification of this appeal as from-text-to-charge.

Thus:

> **The appeal to what is written is the direction of the inference: from Graphē-premises to the present charge. The writing is not a poster and not a search for decoration after a conclusion already reached by other means.**

# 61.25 The Graphē as the Court, Not Decoration

Court and decoration must now be distinguished at full force, because this is the chapter’s positive centre.

**Decoration** is a quoted sentence that does not bear the inference. Remove it, and the conclusion remains on other grounds. A decorated speech may be true in its conclusion and still not be a reasoning from the writing.

**Court** is that without which the inference does not hold as this argument holds. Remove the citation, the characterization, and the standing-clause, and verse 36 is no longer this argument. It might remain an assertion of identity. It would not remain an a fortiori from their law.

He had other means at His disposal in the discourse. He had works in the Father’s name. He returns to those works after the Scripture-argument. He is, by Chapters 43–44, the Truth in person. He does not, in verses 34–36, treat those facts as a reason to skip the writing. He treats the writing as the court even so. That is the weight. A speaker who is the Truth, and who nevertheless argues from their law, has assigned the written text the place of decision in the dispute’s terms.

The court is **their** law. Chapter 59 established that “your law” names received Scripture they already acknowledge. An unrecusable court is a court the parties already confess as theirs. He does not import a foreign code. He opens their code and reads the exhibit.

The court is **writing**. γεγραμμένον and γραφή name a standing text, not a mood, not an oral rumour of the moment, not an institutional decree as institution. The later gate will ask why this writing is the primary written authority. This chapter asks only what place the writing occupies in this reasoning. The place is the court’s place.

Decoration-readings fail the order of the sentences. Court-readings fit the order of the sentences. Fit to the sentences is the evidence. Reconstructed tone is not the evidence.

Thus:

> **The Graphē is the court of this dispute, not decoration around a conclusion reached by other means. He reasons from their written law as the place of decision, even as the Truth who also offers works. That is the weight this chapter is assigned to show.**

# 61.26 The Written Text as Settling-Material

Settling-material, already defined, can now be applied.

In a dispute, material settles when it determines what may and may not be said given the court’s standards. The opponents have said that a man using God-vocabulary of himself blasphemes. The writing has already used God-vocabulary of men to whom the word of God came. The writing, treated as not to be broken, therefore settles that their standard, as they have framed it, is too coarse. A coarser standard than the writing is not their law’s standard.

Settling here is **settling of the charge’s terms**, not yet the later claim that the Graphē settles every later question of life and doctrine as primary written authority. The charge’s terms are: may this speech be condemned as blasphemy because a man has been named in God-relation language? The writing answers that question’s coarse form with no. The remaining questions of His identity are not evacuated. They are placed under a court that will not allow the coarse shortcut.

The stones were an attempted court. Stones do not read Psalm 82. He replaces that attempted court with writing. Replacement of stones by Graphē is already a verdict upon both. The dedicated unbreakability-file will ask why the writing cannot be broken. The present file states that in the reasoning He treats the writing as that which the stones may not break.

Thus:

> **The written text is settling-material for the charge’s terms: it forbids treating God-vocabulary applied to a man as, by itself, blasphemy. Settling of terms in this dispute is not yet the Graphē-gate, and it is already the writing’s work as court.**

# 61.27 Positive Warrant for the Weight He Assigns

The positive warrant may now be stated without a remaining apology.

The investigator is warranted, with full force, in concluding that Christ assigns the written text the weight of court and settling-material in this dispute, because the record exhibits that weight as the argument’s structure rather than as a later slogan.

The warrant has five inspectable supports.

**Order.** He asks about writing before He returns the charge. Court-first is not decoration-last.

**Their law.** He binds them with what they already receive. Weight is assigned to the Graphē as theirs, not to a private oracle they could recuse as His invention of the moment.

**Case, not motto.** He characterizes those to whom the word of God came. A motto does not need a case. A court does.

**Standing-clause.** He will not allow the case to be treated as breakable. Force is named in the chain.

**Application.** He uses the case upon the capital charge. A decoration would not have to bear a blasphemy-trial.

These supports do not await Chapters 62–65 in order to exist. They are the reasoning-file’s own evidence. Later files will ask what “broken” means, why the writing has this character, how Word is truth, and why the Graphē is primary written authority. They will not create the weight this argument already assigns. Sequence is not delay of this warrant.

The reconstructed force corresponds to what a later formulation may call Holy Writ of unchallenged authority in this scene. Correspondence is permitted as reconstruction. “The source says” is not permitted as a substitute for the sentences of John 10. The sentences are the warrant.

Thus:

> **The investigator is warranted, with full force, in receiving the written text as the court to which He assigns the dispute. The warrant is the argument’s order, their law, the written case, the standing-clause as force in the chain, and the application to the charge.**

# 61.28 The Opponents' Own Law as Unrecusable Court

A court is unrecusable, in this argument, when the parties already confess it as theirs.

“Your law” is that confession, used against them without ceasing to be their possession. He does not say “my private writing.” He says “your law.” Chapter 59 established that this includes the psalm as written Scripture they acknowledge. Chapter 60 established that the psalm stands as Holy Writ already in force for His hearers.

The logical use of an unrecusable court is this: they cannot answer the a fortiori by saying that the writing is irrelevant, or foreign, or a later sect’s book. They can refuse the inference. They cannot, without abandoning their law, refuse the exhibit. Refusal of the inference while keeping the exhibit is the position verse 36 makes costly.

Unrecusable is not yet the Graphē-gate. A court unrecusable for these parties in this dispute is not, by that fact alone, shown to be the primary written authority by which all later truth is known, tested, reasoned through, taught, discerned, and lived. It is shown to be the court He opens upon them. The later gate will use such openings as premises. It will not be this opening under another name.

Thus:

> **Their own law is the unrecusable court of the a fortiori. They may refuse the inference. They may not recuse the exhibit as foreign without abandoning the law He has named as theirs.**

# 61.29 Reasoning From Scripture Is Not Transfer of Christ-Level Authority to Every Interpreter

This distinction belongs to the object, not to a later apology.

He reasons from what is written. That fact is this chapter’s conclusion at the level of this controversy. A later reader who also reasons from what is written does a related operation. Related operation is not transferred authority.

**His reasoning** is the reasoning of the One already identified as the Truth, who confirms Scripture, who is the faithful witness, who in this scene both offers works and opens the Graphē as court. The authority of the Person and the authority of the writing are not collapsed: He places the dispute under the writing rather than replacing the writing with a shout. Neither is the Person reduced to a later commentator.

**A later interpreter’s reasoning** is tested by the text, by context, by valid inference, and by the refusal of mishandling that Movement IX will examine. An interpreter may quote this very argument and still mishandle it — as wordplay, as identity-reduction, as a blank check, as already Chapters 62–65. Quotation of His reasoning is not automatically His reasoning.

The distinction prevents two opposite errors.

**Error of denial.** If no later reader may reason from Scripture because only He may, then the book’s own labour of reasoning through Scripture is forbidden, and Isaiah 1:18 has been abandoned. The book forbids that error. This chapter reconstructs His reasoning in order that the reader may see it, not in order that the reader may never reason.

**Error of transfer.** If every later reasoning is thereby Christ-level, then mishandling becomes impossible to name, and Movement IX has nothing to do. The book forbids that error also. Authority remains with the writing and with the Person who here reasons from it. It does not migrate into the later mouth by imitation of form.

Thus:

> **His reasoning from what is written does not confer Christ-level authority on every later interpreter who reasons from a verse. The reader is to follow the inference, not to inherit the Person’s authority by copying the form.**

# 61.35 Competing Claim: The Argument as Mere Wordplay

The first live reduction is to treat John 10:34–36 as a pun on θεοί / θεόν.

The reduction says: He noticed a vocabulary-overlap; He exploited it to embarrass opponents; there is no lesser and greater case; there is no court; there is only a sound.

The reduction fails for reasons already in the unit.

A pun does not require “unto whom the word of God came.” Characterization is case, not sound.

A pun does not require “the scripture cannot be broken” as a parenthetical of force. Standing is court, not wit.

A pun does not require “whom the Father hath sanctified, and sent into the world” as the identification of the subject. Greater commission is comparison, not a punchline.

A pun does not return a capital charge as a question under “your law.” Blasphemy-trial is not a joke.

Wordplay, as a charge against the argument, is itself a refusal to follow the joints. The joints are condition, characterization, standing, application. Followed as joints, they are logic. Ignored as joints, they can be called a pun by anyone who wishes not to be tried by the writing.

Seriousness about the word “gods” is required because He cites it. Seriousness that discards the case, the standing, and the application is not seriousness about the argument. It is capture by a sound.

Thus:

> **Treating the argument as mere wordplay fails. The unit is a written case, asserted as standing Scripture, applied to a capital charge against the sanctified and sent Son. A pun does not have those joints.**

# 61.36 Competing Claim: Ignoring the A Fortiori

The second live reduction is to treat verses 34–36 as citation plus restated identity, with no comparison of lesser and greater.

The reduction says: He quotes a psalm; He then says He is the Son of God; the middle is ornamental; there is no “if… then how much more.”

The reduction fails because verse 36 is not a new start. It is the apodosis-force of verse 35’s condition, put as a question. “If he called them gods… Say ye of him… Thou blasphemest…?” is one argument. A restated identity would not need the “if.” A citation-plus-shout would not need “unto whom the word of God came.”

The reduction also fails because it cannot explain the sanctified-and-sent identification as greater-commission language. If there is no comparison, “sanctified and sent” is colour. In the argument it is the greater case. Colour-readings are the same family as decoration-readings. They fail the same test: remove the member and see whether the argument remains. Remove the comparison, and the writing no longer tries the charge. It only sits beside it.

Ignoring the a fortiori is often a pious-sounding move: one wishes to honour the Son-claim by not “reducing” it to an inference. The honour is misplaced. He is the One who infers. To refuse His inference in order to protect His claim is to protect a claim He did not make in this form. He made the claim as the greater case of a written argument. Following Him includes following that form.

The reconstruction remains honest that “how much more” is not an adverb in the verse. Ignoring the a fortiori on that ground would confuse explicit wording with warranted structure. Structure is in the verses. The tag is later. The tag may be dropped. The structure may not.

Thus:

> **Ignoring the a fortiori fails. Verse 36 is the application of verse 35’s written case to a greater subject. Citation plus restated identity is not the argument He gave.**

# 61.37 Competing Claim: This Chapter as Already Chapters 62–65

# 61.38 Competing Claim: Reducing the Son-Claim to the Judges' Title

The fourth live reduction is identity in the opposite direction from collapse: not inflating this file into later files, but deflating the greater case into the lesser.

The reduction says: Jesus claims only to be a god in the sense of Psalm 82’s judges; the argument is a plea for that classification; oneness with the Father is thereby removed.

This chapter’s own limit remains in force: the a fortiori defends the Son-claim as not blasphemy under their law; it does not, by itself, write every later Christological file. Defense of a claim is not the erasure of the claim.

# 61.39 Reason Through the Evidence

**Premise 1.** John 10:34–36 is Christ’s appeal to what is written as decisive in a blasphemy-dispute; the cited wording is “I said, Ye are gods”; those so called are those to whom the word of God came; the unit contains “the scripture cannot be broken” and applies the case to the sanctified and sent Son (Chapter 59).

**Inference.** Therefore He reasons from what is written as the court of the dispute, assigning the written text the weight of settling-material for the charge’s terms; the logic is the a fortiori from Psalm 82’s standing naming to the sanctified and sent Son’s claim; competing reductions — wordplay, ignored comparison, collapse into Chapters 62–65, identity with the judges — fail as accounts of the unit.

The inference is not a deduction from a later creed. It is the unit plus the already-established citation plus the already-established standing of the writing as their law. Alternative global explanations fail as accounts of the conjunction, as already shown.
