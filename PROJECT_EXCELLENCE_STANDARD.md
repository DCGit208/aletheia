# Aletheia Project Excellence Standard

## Purpose

This is the non-negotiable standard for every Aletheia learning product, publication, interface, and future revision. It applies to Foundation School and to all later books, courses, programs, assessments, commerce, and reader experiences.

The aim is not merely polished copy or a functional application. The finished work must be source-faithful, intellectually rigorous, spiritually profound, literarily excellent, visually timeless, easy to use, and secure in the way it delivers access.

## Authority and source order

1. Holy Scripture governs doctrinal assertions.
2. The applicable Aletheia source manuscript governs its intended curriculum, terms, distinctions, Scripture clusters, questions, and sequence.
3. Locked owner decisions supersede known source-manuscript compression, ambiguity, and printing errors.
4. Internal source maps, Scripture matrices, distinction maps, student-charge maps, and writing standards control coverage; they are not student-facing copy.
5. Product, access, payment, and architecture decisions must remain aligned with the learner-facing experience.

Never silently correct a source ambiguity. Use the applicable locked owner decision, preserve the internal audit trail, and write the student-facing explanation cleanly.

## Preservation-first editing

Every existing passage must be classified before it is changed.

| Classification | Required action |
| --- | --- |
| Accurate, clear, and complete | Preserve it. |
| Accurate but compressed, vague, or poorly organized | Clarify it without changing the claim. |
| Source-faithful but not student-ready | Rewrite it into teachable prose while retaining every required contribution. |
| Incorrect, contradictory, or superseded by a locked correction | Correct it only through Scripture and the documented owner decision. |
| Duplicative, internal, or confusing | Remove it only after confirming it carries no unique source requirement. |

Before editing, identify a passage's Scripture anchors, doctrinal claim, word studies, distinctions, learner charge, product role, and owner-decision dependencies. No broad search-and-replace. No rewrite may erase doctrine merely because it is easier to read.

## The sacred and clear voice

The voice is solemn, active, exact, memorable, and completely intelligible.

- Write with poetic gravity, never theatrical excess.
- Use rich language only when it makes truth clearer, more memorable, or more alive.
- Prefer strong verbs, concrete nouns, and short, decisive doctrinal sentences.
- Avoid academic fog, vague slogans, motivational padding, lexical display, and internal production language.
- Let the reader pause because the thought is weighty, not because the sentence is obscure.
- Every claim must be clear enough to read aloud, understand, retain, and teach.

## Curriculum architecture

Each substantial lesson should follow this rhythm when appropriate to its subject:

1. **The Anchor — Scripture.** Establish the governing passage in its meaningful context.
2. **The Word — doctrine opened.** Explain the claim with exact language. Greek or Hebrew appears only where it clarifies the text: form, transliteration, Strong's reference where appropriate, verse anchor, plain meaning, and doctrinal consequence.
3. **The Witness — history.** Historic creeds, confessions, or Christian formulations may provide accurate intellectual context when useful. They never become a second authority, replace Scripture, or import doctrine contrary to the Aletheia source and locked owner decisions.
4. **The Distinction — error refused.** Name the specific confusion, collapse, or substitution the lesson prevents.
5. **The Altar — faithful lived response.** Show what the established truth changes in thought, prayer, worship, fellowship, conduct, and teaching. Application must follow the doctrine already established; never ask a learner to pursue in prayer what Scripture says is already theirs in Christ.
6. **The Labour — demonstrated understanding.** Use written reasoning, Scripture comparison, word drill, discussion, teaching exercises, and assessment to show comprehension.

The exact form may vary, but every lesson must remain Scripture-first, source-faithful, clear, distinguishing, assessable, and complete in itself.

## Course, book, and program roles

- A **course** is a complete guided learning experience: Scripture, teaching, distinction, practice, and assessment. A learner must not leave a lesson to make its doctrine intelligible.
- A **handbook** is a concise, durable reference that supports and deepens the course; it does not replace it.
- An **independent book** is expanded standalone reading. It is not a hidden prerequisite for a course unless the product and curriculum explicitly say so.
- A **program** gives a learner an ordered cadence, access, and journey through a shared curriculum. It is not a second doctrine or a new platform role.

For Foundation School, the Graphē is the entrance gate, the handbook supports the six complete Hebrews 6:1-2 courses, and independent doctrinal books remain supplemental reading.

## Premium learner experience

The digital experience should feel like inhabiting a serious, beautifully made theological library rather than completing a standard online lecture.

- Use an editorial hierarchy: authoritative serif headings, highly legible body copy, generous whitespace, clear section rhythm, and distinctive Scripture blocks.
- Use visual elements as teaching instruments: diagrams, timelines, word-study panels, and callouts only where they clarify a real relationship.
- Use only public-domain, licensed, or purpose-created sacred art. Never use cheap stock imagery, unlicensed work, or decoration that competes with the teaching.
- Design for calm reading, mobile use, accessibility, performance, dark/light contrast, bookmarks, progress continuity, offline reading where appropriate, and an unmistakable next step.
- Keep access, purchase, enrolment, reader, course, and success-screen states coherent. The learner should always know what they own, where they are, and what to do next.
- Paid material must be delivered through a genuine access boundary, not merely hidden by a client-side interface.

## Review gates

No unit is complete until it passes all applicable gates.

1. **Source gate:** Scripture, manuscript, and locked decisions are accurately represented.
2. **Preservation gate:** every removed or rewritten passage has been checked for unique doctrinal or curricular contribution.
3. **Literary gate:** the prose has force, clarity, cadence, and no filler or editorial debris.
4. **Pedagogical gate:** the learner can understand, retain, reason, apply, and teach the lesson.
5. **Experience gate:** visual hierarchy, responsive layout, accessibility, performance, and navigation are reviewed on desktop and mobile.
6. **Technical gate:** access, offering, entitlement, and deployment behavior remain correct; quality guards, tests, typecheck, lint, and build pass when available.

Before committing, provide a concise review summary: what was preserved, edited, rewritten, or removed; the source basis; verification results; and any unresolved risk.
