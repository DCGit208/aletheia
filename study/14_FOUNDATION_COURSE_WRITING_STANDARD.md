# Foundation School course writing standard

Foundation School lessons are student-facing teaching, not production maps. Internal source labels, cluster IDs, question IDs, and owner-decision shorthand belong in `study/` controls. They must not appear as the student's course prose.

## Standard

Every lesson should make the student able to:

1. See the governing Scripture in full or in an exact cited cluster.
2. Understand the doctrine in ordinary, teachable sentences.
3. Know which error or collapse the lesson refuses.
4. Practice the lesson through written labour appropriate to The Walk or The Intensive.
5. Answer the charge without needing the production map.

## Required student-facing shape

- **Main idea** — one clear doctrinal sentence.
- **Key Scriptures** — the exact passages the lesson will use.
- **Reading assignment** — textbook or companion reading named for the student.
- **Teaching** — Scripture, explanation, distinction, and application in consumable prose.
- **Practice** — The Walk and Intensive writing work, when both tracks apply.
- **What this lesson distinguishes** — the mistake the lesson prevents.
- **Response** — the question or charge the student must answer.

## Forbidden in student copy

- Cluster IDs such as `A1`, `J2`, `F7`, or `Q43`.
- Phrases like "owner file," "source map," "backend," "production notes," or "this file" when they mean internal organization.
- Unexplained lesson engineering such as "today J1" or "this sentence is J2."
- A string of slogans where Scripture should be explained.
- A teacher-only note presented as if it were a student charge.

## Excellence bar

The course must be more than clear. It must be hard to misunderstand.

- **Scripture-first:** the writing governs the lesson, not the instructor's shorthand.
- **Source-faithful:** the Aletheia leaflet, textbook, and locked owner decisions set the doctrine.
- **Consumable:** a student should be able to read the lesson aloud and teach its spine.
- **Distinguishing:** every lesson names the confusion it prevents.
- **Assessable:** the practice and response show whether the student understood.
- **Globally serious:** no filler, no academic fog, no motivational padding, no hidden curriculum codes.

If a lesson cannot meet this standard, it is not ready for Foundation School students.
