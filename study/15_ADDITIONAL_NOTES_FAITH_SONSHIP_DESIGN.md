# Additional Notes — Faith, Sonship, Design, Representation & Restoration

**Purpose:** This file records additional owner-supplied theological notes and context that must remain available as a standing reference while the Faith / *Faith Is Evidence* work is developed.

**Status:** These are **owner-supplied interpretive notes and developing doctrinal direction**. They are not to be treated as though every formulation below is a verbatim statement of Scripture or of the 2015 leaflet. During manuscript development, distinguish carefully between:

1. **What Scripture explicitly says**
2. **What the Aletheia source explicitly teaches**
3. **What follows by synthesis of multiple Scriptures**
4. **The owner's developing doctrinal interpretation**

The notes below should be **integrated into the argument wherever they belong**, rather than appended as disconnected footnotes or treated as optional afterthoughts.

---

## 1. Faith, Good Report and Pleasing God

A significant connection to develop is the way **Hebrews 11** associates faith with testimony, good report, and pleasing God:

- **Hebrews 11:2** — by faith the elders obtained a **good report**.
- **Hebrews 11:5** — Enoch obtained the testimony that he **pleased God**.
- **Hebrews 11:6** — without faith it is impossible to please Him.

This creates an important conceptual question for the study:

> **What is the state of the person who pleases God, and what does that state have to do with faith?**

Faith should therefore not be reduced prematurely to mental assent, optimism, or the acceptance of propositions. The Hebrews 11 sequence invites examination of faith as a lived state that bears testimony and corresponds to what pleases God.

**Important methodological distinction:** Hebrews 11 explicitly connects faith with good report and pleasing God. The further formulation that **the state of Sonship is Faith** is an interpretive synthesis and must be presented as such unless the source establishes it explicitly.

## 2. Matthew 3:17 — The Father's Declaration Over the Son

Matthew 3:17 records one of the most significant audible declarations from heaven concerning Jesus:

> “This is my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased.”

This statement should be studied in relation to Hebrews 11:5–6.

The developing line of inquiry is:

**Sonship → the Father's pleasure → faith → the state of the Son**

The point is not merely that Jesus performed something pleasing and was therefore approved. The declaration identifies Him as **Son** and simultaneously declares the Father's pleasure in Him.

This raises the deeper question:

> **Is the state of Sonship itself the state of faith — the state in which the person exists in the Father's reality and therefore pleases Him?**

This is a theological synthesis to be developed through the wider Scripture cluster, not a claim that Matthew 3:17 by itself defines faith as sonship.

## 3. Jesus as Representation — Who We Are by Design

A central developing thesis is:

> **Jesus represents who we are by Design.**

The question should therefore not be limited to:

> “What did Jesus accomplish for us?”

It should also ask:

> **“What does Jesus represent?”**

The intended investigation is that Jesus reveals, in perfect manifestation, the reality toward which humanity was designed and to which redemption restores us.

This connects:

- **Genesis 1:26–28** — humanity made in the image and likeness of God and given dominion.
- **Romans 8:17** — children, heirs, and joint-heirs with Christ.
- **1 John 4:17** — “as he is, so are we in this world.”
- **John 17:20–23** — Jesus' prayer concerning the oneness of believers with the Father and the Son.
- **Hebrews 1:2** — the Son as appointed heir of all things.
- **Ephesians 1:7 / Colossians 1:14** — redemption through Christ.
- **Romans 3:23–24** — coming short of the glory and being justified freely through the redemption in Christ Jesus.

The manuscript should investigate these passages as a connected cluster rather than allowing Jesus to function only as an isolated example of extraordinary spirituality.

---

## 4. Sonship, Heirship and Co-Heirship

The existing Aletheia study already identifies **sonship, adoption, heirship, image/likeness, righteousness, redemption, and John 17** as important components of the Faith Toward God architecture.

The additional note is to deepen the connection:

> **Jesus is not merely an exception to humanity; He is the representation of what humanity was designed to be.**

Romans 8:17 is therefore important:

- if children, then heirs;
- heirs of God;
- joint-heirs with Christ.

The question is not merely future inheritance. The study should investigate what **sonship and heirship reveal about identity, nature, relationship, and present reality**.

This should be developed alongside John 17:20–23, where Jesus speaks of believers being one as the Father and Son are one and being “in” the Father and the Son.

---

## 5. Hebrews 1:2 — The Son and the Purpose of Representation

Hebrews 1:2 describes God speaking by Son and identifies the Son as the one whom God appointed **heir of all things**, through whom also He made the worlds/ages.

This passage should be brought into the investigation of:

- why the Son is central;
- what the Son represents;
- heirship;
- creation;
- the relationship between Sonship and the intended human state.

The interpretive question is:

> **If the Son is the heir and the one through whom God made the ages, what does the Son reveal about the intended destination and identity of those who are made in God's image and are described as heirs and joint-heirs?**

Do not claim that Hebrews 1:2 by itself states the complete “Jesus represents who humanity is by design” thesis. Use it as part of the cumulative Scripture architecture.

---

## 6. Redemption — Restoration / “Back on Course”

Ephesians 1:7 and Colossians 1:14 are important because they speak of **redemption** (*apolytrōsis*) through Christ / in whom we have redemption.

The owner's working lexical formulation is:

> **apolytrōsis — riddance, ransom in full**

The conceptual direction is that redemption should be investigated as more than a narrowly reduced formula of “forgiveness so that I can go to heaven.”

Redemption is to be studied in relation to **restoration, recovery, and being brought back into the intended course/design**.

The developing conceptual movement is:

**Design → deviation → Christ → redemption → restoration → righteousness → sonship / heirship → present reality**

The exact lexical claims should remain subject to the source's word studies and any subsequent verified lexical work. Do not silently replace the owner's stated lexical emphasis with a generic theological definition.

---

## 7. Justification — “Rendered Righteous / Such as He Ought to Be”

Romans 3:23–24 is a key text:

- all have sinned;
- all come short of the glory of God;
- being justified freely by grace;
- through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus.

The owner's working lexical formulation for **dikaioō** is:

> **to render righteous, or such as he ought to be**

This should be explored alongside the idea of restoration.

The important question is:

> **If humanity has “come short of the glory” and is then justified through the redemption in Christ, what does justification mean in relation to the intended state/design of humanity?**

The phrase **“back on course”** is a conceptual formulation for the developing argument. It should be distinguished from the bare lexical definition and from what Romans 3 explicitly states.

## 8. Faith and the Original Design of Humanity

Genesis 1:26–28 must remain foundational to this investigation:

- “Let us make man in our image”
- “after our likeness”
- dominion
- blessing
- fruitfulness
- multiplication
- filling the earth
- exercising dominion

The developing thesis is that faith operates **within the design** God established for humanity.

Owner's formulation:

> **Faith is operating in our designs as Gods (Genesis 1:26–28), to operate in the state of God (Mark 11:22), to be as He is (Matthew 5:48).**

This formulation must be handled carefully.

The phrase “as Gods” reflects the owner's theological framing of Genesis 1:26–28 and should not be silently presented as though Genesis itself uses the English phrase “as Gods.” Where necessary, the study should distinguish Genesis' image/likeness/Elohim language from later passages such as Psalm 82:6 and John 10.

---

## 9. Mark 11:22 — “Have Faith in God”

Mark 11:22 is a key text for investigating the relationship between faith and God's own mode/state of operation.

The passage should not be reduced to a motivational formula.

The study should ask:

> **What does it mean to “have faith in God” when faith is being investigated in relation to God's nature, the original human design, sonship, and the operation of divine reality?**

There is a known interpretive/translation issue around the Greek construction behind “faith in God” / possible readings involving God's faithfulness or God's faith. This should be handled with source fidelity and verified lexical/grammatical evidence if it becomes a major exegetical claim.

---

## 10. Matthew 5:48 — “Be Perfect”

Matthew 5:48 says:

> “Be ye therefore perfect, even as your Father which is in heaven is perfect.”

The owner's rhetorical question is central to the developing argument:

> **Why would Jesus ask us to be perfectly something we are not?**

And further:

> **Why would we want to be something we are not?**

The command therefore drives the investigation backward into **design and nature**.

If Jesus commands a human being to be “perfect” in correspondence with the Father, the study must investigate what Scripture means by that command and how it relates to:

- original design;
- sonship;
- divine likeness;
- the Father's nature;
- love and conduct;
- restoration;
- present identity.

**Context caution:** Matthew 5:48 occurs within Jesus' teaching on love, including love toward enemies. The ontological/design interpretation is therefore a synthesis that must be demonstrated from the wider Scripture architecture rather than extracted from this verse alone.

---

## 11. 1 John 4:17 — “As He Is, So Are We”

1 John 4:17 is a particularly important representation text:

> “...because as he is, so are we in this world...”

The owner's intended connection is with the present reality of representation:

**Christ's state → our present state**

This should be studied together with:

- Romans 8:17;
- John 17:20–23;
- Genesis 1:26–28;
- Hebrews 1:2;
- Matthew 3:17;
- redemption and justification texts.

At the same time, the immediate context of 1 John 4:17 concerns **love and confidence**, so the verse should not be detached from that context while being used for the broader thesis.

---

## 12. John 17:20–23 — Oneness as Intended Reality

Jesus prays not only for the disciples immediately present but for those who will believe through their word.

The prayer includes:

- believers being **one**;
- “as” the Father and Son are one;
- believers being **in** the Father and the Son;
- the world knowing that the Father sent the Son;
- the glory given by the Father being associated with the unity of believers.

This passage is therefore important to the developing argument about:

**Sonship → oneness → representation → glory → present reality**

The question previously identified in the Aletheia study remains important:

> **How are we to liken John 17:20–23 to the rest of the doctrine being developed?**

The answer should emerge from the Scripture cluster, not from an isolated proof-text.

## 14. Core Thesis to Develop

The owner's developing formulation is:

> **Faith is not merely what the mind believes. Faith is the state of the person who exists in the reality of God's nature in Christ.**

And, more specifically:

> **Faith is operating in our designs as Gods (Genesis 1:26–28), to operate in the state of God (Mark 11:22), to be as He is (Matthew 5:48).**

These formulations should be tested and demonstrated through Scripture and the Aletheia source rather than merely repeated as conclusions.

The study should continually ask:

> **Is faith primarily a belief about reality, or is faith the person's participation in / operation from the reality revealed by God?**

This question should connect the opening investigation of *pistis* with sonship, adoption, image/likeness, righteousness, redemption, heirship, John 17, and ultimately **substance and evidence**.

## 15. Jesus: Accomplishment and Representation

The manuscript should deliberately hold together two questions:

### What did Jesus accomplish?

- redemption;
- justification;
- reconciliation/restoration themes as supported by the source and Scripture;
- the work accomplished through His blood.

### What does Jesus represent?

- Sonship;
- heirship;
- the Father's pleasure;
- divine likeness;
- oneness;
- the intended human relationship with God;
- the restored/design state.

The second question must not be swallowed by the first.

The developing thesis is that **Christ's work restores what Christ's person reveals**.

That formulation is a synthesis and must be demonstrated across the relevant Scripture cluster.

## 16. Pedagogical Direction

When these notes are incorporated into lessons or chapters, avoid beginning with abstract definitions and then forcing Scripture into those definitions.

Instead, allow the biblical sequence and the source's own architecture to generate the questions.

For example:

1. **What was humanity designed to be?**
2. **What does image and likeness mean in the source's teaching?**
3. **What is the significance of Sonship?**
4. **What does it mean to be an heir / joint-heir?**
5. **What does Hebrews 11 mean by good report and pleasing God?**
6. **Why does faith relate to pleasing God?**
7. **What does the Father's declaration over Jesus reveal?**
8. **What does Jesus represent?**
9. **What was lost / distorted / fallen short?**
10. **What does redemption accomplish?**
11. **What does justification mean in relation to the intended state?**
12. **What does John 17 reveal about oneness?**
13. **What does “as he is, so are we” mean in context?**
14. **What does faith become when understood in relation to design, sonship, and divine operation?**
15. **How do substance and evidence emerge from that reality?**

The pedagogy should therefore follow the **questions generated by the Scripture/source architecture**, rather than impose a generic “What is faith? Why do we need faith?” course structure.

## 22. Owner direction — Hypostasis is foundational to Book I

**Direction recorded: 2026-10-18.** The owner has clarified that *hypostasis* is the very basis and core of Book I, *The Doctrine of Faith*. Hebrews 11:1 and the study of *hypostasis* are not to be reserved for Book II merely because the verse also contains “evidence.” The owner's governing thesis is that faith as substance, examined through the word and the connected scriptural argument, explains faith as the person's underlying nature and state.

### Required architecture consequence

- Book I must establish Hebrews 11:1 / *hypostasis* as a foundation and then develop the Aletheia source's teaching that *pistis* / credence defines the state of children of God.
- Book II, *Faith Is Evidence*, develops faith as evidence from the foundation established in Book I. It may revisit Hebrews 11:1 but must not be the first or only book to explain *hypostasis* as foundational.
- The existing nine Book I drafts are preserved working material, not a validated final sequence. Reassess the chapter order from the foundation and its conceptual dependencies; do not merely insert a short *hypostasis* section into the old outline.
- Keep the earlier master conceptual chain in Section 13 as a map of relationships to investigate. Its placement of substance/evidence near the end must not override this later architecture direction. Distinguish the **foundational order of explanation** (start by establishing the governing *hypostasis* proposition) from the **doctrinal relationships unfolded in the book** (God's nature, image/likeness, design, Sonship, faith, deviation, Christ, redemption, righteousness, heirship, divine nature, oneness, present reality). The final synthesis can return to how the full argument illuminates faith as substance without making the foundational subject an afterthought.
- Preserve the four categories: explicit Aletheia source teaching; explicit Scripture wording and context; synthesis; owner's developing doctrinal formulation.

### Source-fidelity clarification

The 2015 *Aletheia Libraries 1* leaflet's “Faith toward God” section explicitly begins from Hebrews 6:1 and teaches *pistis* / credence as defining our state as children of God, linking it to acceptance / true nature, adoption, image and likeness, divine nature, coming short of glory, justification, righteousness, redemption, heirship, oneness, and Christ as the image of God.

The leaflet does **not** itself explicitly quote Hebrews 11:1 or use the term *hypostasis*. Therefore the manuscript must not attribute the *hypostasis* argument to the leaflet. Instead, it must develop a reasoned connection between Hebrews 11:1 and the leaflet's explicit *pistis* / state / nature teaching, with lexical and contextual evidence clearly distinguished from synthesis.

### Lexical and contextual discipline

- Examine the semantic range of *hypostasis*, including substance / actual reality / nature and confidence / assurance; do not treat etymology alone as a complete definition.
- Compare Hebrews 11:1 with Hebrews 1:3 and 3:14 in context. The same noun across passages is a reason to compare uses, not proof that every occurrence has an identical nuance.
- Read Hebrews 11:1 in the flow from Hebrews 10:32–39 and into Hebrews 11:2–6, preserving hope, endurance, testimony, pleasing God, and the unseen.
- The owner's thesis about faith as underlying nature/state must be developed and demonstrated rather than falsely presented as a direct quotation from the leaflet or as a conclusion proven by word parts alone.
